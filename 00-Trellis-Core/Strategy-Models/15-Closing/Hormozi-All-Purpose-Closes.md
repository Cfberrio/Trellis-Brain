# Hormozi — All-Purpose Closes

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
The 80/20 close library. Seven universal closes that work on **any response that is not yes**, regardless of which branch of the [[Hormozi-Closing-Definition|Blame Onion]] the prospect is casting power to. These are the operator's first-resort closes — used to (a) cut through smoke and force the prospect to surface a real obstacle, (b) tip an on-the-edge prospect into yes, or (c) handle stalls where the surface objection is unclear. After these, branch-specific scripts ([[Hormozi-Closing-Circumstances-Closes|Circumstances]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Other-People-Closes|Other-People]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Self-Closes|Self-Blame]]) handle the named obstacle.

## Core Idea
> "These are the 80/20 closes. These are the 20% of closes that work 80% of the time."

> "If there's a section of this handbook to commit to memory it would be this one. These have made me. So. Much. Money."

These seven closes are the highest-ROI memorization in the playbook. They handle the wide majority of objections without requiring the operator to first diagnose the branch. They also produce the diagnosis as a side effect: when the prospect responds to an All-Purpose close with a more specific objection, the branch is now named and the operator can deploy a branch-specific script.

## Framework / Structure

### Operator Posture (preamble)
- Use these to respond to **any objection that's not yes**.
- Stack them per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 4]] when needed.
- Each close is a complete script — verbatim wording where the words are the deliverable.
- Pair with permission-getting per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 3]] when delivering harder reframes.

### 1. The "What's Your Main Concern" Close
**They say:** *anything that's not yes*

**You say:** *"What's your main concern? What are you afraid of happening?"*

The single most-used phrase to build rapport with a prospect. If they say *"I need to think about it,"* ask what their main concerns are or what variables they're using to make the decision. This shifts to a more productive conversation.

If they give fluff answers, escalate to the more pointed version: *"What are you afraid of happening?"*

If they still don't budge, name the worst case absurdly: *"…so you're afraid of spending money and me stealing it and spending it in Vegas?"* They'll laugh and say no — and now they're back in the sale and will give a closer-to-truth answer.

**Function:** diagnostic + rapport. Surfaces the real branch.

### 2. The "Reason" Close
**You say:** *"The reason you are telling yourself not to do this is the reason you should do it."*

The reframe: the very obstacle the prospect is hiding behind is structural evidence they need the thing.

**Worked examples (verbatim from source):**

| Branch | Script |
|---|---|
| **MONEY** | *"How long do you want 'I can't afford this' to stay on your list of problems in life? The fact that you don't have money is the very reason you need to do this more than anyone."* |
| **TIME** | *"The fact that you don't have time is the very reason you need to do this."* |
| **AUTHORITY** | *"The fact that you're so dependent on your spouse is the reason you need to take this decision and own it."* |
| **AVOIDANCE** | *"The fact that you're not sure about the person that you want to be is the very reason that we're going to help you get in this program."* |

**Generalized form:** *"Whatever the reason is, usually the biggest chain that you're holding on to is the thing that you're enslaving yourself to, is the thing that you're casting your power to. If you break that chain, power comes back to you. The very reason that you're holding yourself back is typically the very reason that you need to do it."*

**Function:** universal reframe. Works because every avoidance reason is also evidence of the problem the product solves.

### 3. The "Hypothetical" Close
**You say:** *"If this were perfect, would you do it? Then what's the difference between perfect and what we've got?"*

Two outcomes:

**(a) They name a small actual detail** → fix it and close.

**(b) They can't think of anything** (because they don't know everything you can deliver on the spot):

**You say:** *"Right, well because it sounds like it's nothing to do with the program — it's something else. What are you afraid of happening? Let's talk about that."*

> "This works astonishingly well."

**Function:** strips the surface objection by testing whether it's the real one. Combines with Close #1 (Main Concern) when (b) hits.

### 4. The "Zoom Out" Close
**You say:** *"Let's zoom out for a second. You want [X]. We sell [X]. Do you think overanalyzing the details might be the reason you haven't made it happen yet? At the end of the day, you just need to decide whether you think this makes it more likely for you to hit your goal than doing this on your own. And if you've tried that already and you're here, then maybe this is just the thing you've been waiting for — it just doesn't look like you expected it to."*

**Function:** lifts the prospect out of detail-spiraling. Reframes the decision as a single-variable question (does this increase your odds vs. solo) rather than a multi-variable inspection of every program feature.

### 5. The "1 to 10" Close
**You say:** *"On a scale of 1 to 10, where 1 means you're not interested at all and 10 means you're ready to start right now, where do you see yourself?"*

**If they say 10:** *"Great. Let's rock and roll."* → close.

**If they say 9 or less:** *"What would it take to get you to a 10?"*

**They reply with a thing.**

**You say:** *"No big deal. I think we can take care of that for you. Feeling 10ish now?"*

**Function:** quantifies the obstacle. Forces the prospect to articulate exactly what's missing — which is then either solvable on the spot (close it) or reveals a real branch (deploy branch-specific script).

### 6. The "Best Case Worst Case" Close (3 variations)

**Variation A — with guarantee:**
*"Let's consider the options. Both are risk-free, only one is guaranteed to not get you what you want. Best case-worst case. Option one, you do the thing, you get the result, life is awesome. Option two, you don't do the thing, you don't get the result because you didn't do the thing. Option three, you do the thing but then you don't get the result. These are the options that can happen in front of you because we have a guarantee — they're all risk-free except for one of them. Only one of them has a true guarantee of not getting you where you want, which is walking out the door. So, which risk-free option do you want — the one that's risk-free that guarantees that you're not going to get there or the one that's risk-free and has the potential to get you where you want to go?"*

**Variation B — short form:**
*"Best case we change your life forever. Worst case you get X amount of time free and learn a bunch of stuff along the way. Either way you get closer to your goal. Ready to start?"*

**Variation C — no-guarantee alternate:**
*"Let's consider the options. Option one, you take a risk and do this. You either hit the goal or you get closer to your goal no matter what because you'll learn a ton. Option two, you take no risk but you guarantee you don't get what you want — by not starting. So, which option do you feel gets you closer to your goal?"*

**Function:** restructures the decision so doing-nothing is named as the high-risk option. Pairs structurally with [[../01-100M-Offers/Hormozi-Guarantees|Guarantees]] (when one exists) but Variation C works without one.

### 7. The "Card Not On Me" Close (special-case logistical close)
Not a standard objection close — handles the late-stage *"I forgot my wallet / don't have my card on me"* stall. Hormozi places this in the All-Purpose section because it can occur in any closing context.

**Pre-empt with this protocol** (the operator should default to this on every close):
1. Once verbal agreement is locked, **ask for their ID first** (forces them to take their wallet out).
2. Use the ID to fill in the contract — easy chit-chat about their photo, where they're from, etc., fills the time.
3. Once info is copied: *"I'll trade you for the card you wanna use,"* motioning the ID between two fingers toward their wallet. Their other cards are in plain sight when their wallet is open. (Remote: ask them to bring ID to the screen.)
4. Writing the card number ensures no mis-keyed digits.

**If the stall still happens:**

**You say:** *"No problem. Do you have your phone on you? Great. Just pull up your banking app, go to statements, and you can find the account number on the top right. I'll Google the routing number while you do that."*

This routes to ACH instead of card.

**Author Note (folded — ACH):** If you don't currently take ACH, you should. Lower costs, fewer expirations and account changes, decreased chargeback risk, and you can sign up a new client even when they don't have their wallet. *"We used to save a ton of sales with this. Very useful."*

**Function:** salvages locked-in yeses that are about to die at the payment-collection step. Hormozi's positioning is that 90% of "card not on me" instances are pre-emptable by the ID-first protocol.

## Strategic Implications

- **The 80/20 nature is real.** Operators who memorize only these 7 closes and skip the branch-specific libraries will still convert significantly above untrained baseline. The branch libraries push that further; the All-Purpose set is where most of the lift comes from.
- **Diagnostic-as-side-effect.** Closes #1, #3, and #5 produce a diagnosis whether or not they convert. Even a "fail" on those three closes is operationally valuable — the prospect surfaces the real branch, and a branch-specific script can now fire.
- **The "Reason" close is universal because every avoidance is evidence.** *"You can't afford it"* is itself the strongest argument the prospect needs the thing that fixes their cash flow. *"I don't have time"* is the strongest argument they need the thing that gives them time back. The structural logic is "evidence of need = reason to act."
- **Best-Case-Worst-Case requires a guarantee for full effect, but works without one.** Variation C is the no-guarantee form; it shifts the risk frame from "cost of doing it" to "cost of not doing it" without needing a money-back commitment. Operators without guarantees should default to Variation C and consider adding a guarantee per [[../01-100M-Offers/Hormozi-Guarantees|Guarantees]] for additional close leverage.
- **"Card Not On Me" is structural pre-emption, not just a recovery script.** The ID-first protocol prevents the stall in 90% of cases; the recovery script handles the residual 10%. Operators who skip the pre-emption and only learn the recovery still leak yeses at the payment step.
- **Memorize, don't paraphrase.** Per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 5]], the operator should *understand* — but for the All-Purpose closes specifically, Hormozi's positioning is *"if there's a section of this handbook to commit to memory, it would be this one."* The Reason close, the Hypothetical, and Best-Case-Worst-Case are short enough to be word-for-word.

## Practical Use

When to deploy each:

| Surface signal | Deploy |
|---|---|
| Stall, vague reservation, unclear obstacle | What's Your Main Concern |
| Prospect's stated objection is also evidence of the underlying problem | Reason |
| Suspect the surface objection is a smokescreen | Hypothetical |
| Prospect is detail-spiraling on features | Zoom Out |
| Need to quantify how close to yes they actually are | 1 to 10 |
| Need to reframe doing-nothing as the high-risk option | Best Case Worst Case |
| Verbal agreement secured but they say "I don't have my card" | Card Not On Me (and start using the ID-first pre-empt going forward) |

Default opening sequence for an unclear stall: **Main Concern → Hypothetical → Reason → Best-Case-Worst-Case** (stack per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 4]]). This four-close sequence handles most maybe-bucket prospects without needing branch-specific scripts.
