# Hormozi — Closing Rules

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
The 28 operating rules of closing. Constraints that govern *how* every close in the script libraries is delivered. The rules sit between the [[Hormozi-Closing-Definition|Closing Definition]] (the conceptual model) and the close libraries ([[Hormozi-All-Purpose-Closes|All-Purpose]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Circumstances-Closes|Circumstances]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Other-People-Closes|Other-People]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Self-Closes|Self-Blame]]) — they are the operator-character and tactical-discipline layer that makes the scripts work. Skipping a rule will collapse a perfectly-executed script.

## Core Idea
> "You don't need to memorize. You need to understand."

The 28 rules are not a checklist to recite in order. They are the operating discipline that lets a closer use *any* script naturally, recover from a missed line, and stack multiple closes inside one conversation without sounding mechanical. Rules cluster into four functional groups: **conversation hygiene** (how to listen and respond), **closing mechanics** (how to deploy and stack closes), **operator character** (who to be at the close), and **observation discipline** (how to learn from each close).

## Framework / Structure

### The 28 Rules — verbatim ordering, grouped by function

#### A. Conversation Hygiene (Rules 1–3)
1. **When in doubt, repeat back what they said.** Buys you time to think + bonus active-listening signal. Don't be a parrot — but most salespeople don't listen at all, so repetition is a net upgrade.
2. **Acknowledge or agree, but never disagree.** Disagreement triggers hostility. Buy time with: repeat their last point + nod + one of *"totally agree"* / *"totally understand"* / *"that's a great point"* / *"thank you for bringing that up"* / *"you're totally right, I forgot to mention…"* — then transition to the close. Closes are reframes or alternative perspectives, never contradictions.
3. **Before getting real, get permission.** Three favored ask-permission frames: *"Can I be a coach instead of a friend for a second?"* / *"Would it be totally overstepping for me to put my coach hat on for a second?"* / *"Can I tell you what I told someone in your exact position just yesterday that helped her make this decision?"* Then deliver the harsh truth.

#### B. Closing Mechanics (Rules 4–9)
4. **Learn to stack closes.** If one close works well, a second often works better. Sometimes they don't bite at the first; another angle or analogy gets through. Stacking is additive, not redundant.
5. **You don't need to memorize. You need to understand.** When you understand the logic behind each close, you can use them in your own words and move between them smoothly.
6. **Two or three closes per obstacle is enough.** The book provides many; you don't need them all. Pick the two-or-three per main obstacle that you like, and drill those.
7. **Use nudges when someone is right on the edge.** Nudges are one-liners woven into closes — not full overcomes. They tip a deal that's already sitting on the edge of yes. (See note below.)
8. **Yellows are golds. Be a trashman. Practice on unqualified leads.** The more you close, the better you get. Get as many reps as possible. Even less-qualified prospects give a bigger challenge than lay-downs and produce faster skill growth. (Caveats: time is already blocked, prospect lied through STAR, you might make a sale anyway.)
9. **Volume negates luck.** The way to guarantee world-class closing is to do so many sales it would be unreasonable that you aren't.

#### C. Tactical Discipline (Rules 10–18)
10. **Don't assume objections mean they won't buy.** Statements like *"It's expensive"* aren't always actual objections — sometimes they're observations. Respond *"I agree."* Or just let them process out loud. Assume nothing until they explicitly say they don't want to buy.
11. **Never change your price on the spot to get a sale.** Assuming the value is real, a price drop sends you down a sad, dark, ethically questionable path of getting bullied by your own customers. Avoid.
12. **If you feel like you're always in a battle, look at what happens before they show up to the call and at your scripting before you ask for the sale.** The fix is upstream, not at the close.
13. **Only ask for the sale when you think you've got 'em.** If they need more convincing, keep probing. Asking too early forces a no that didn't have to happen.
14. **People want to believe you. They want to buy.** Your job is to help their brains justify the decision they already want to make.
15. **Selling happens before you ask for the sale. Closing happens after.** This separates the two operational phases. Don't conflate them.
16. **It's easier to handle problems before you ask for money than after.** Diffuse as much as you can before the ask.
17. **Expect and plan for no. It's not failure; it's expected.** No is the median outcome of a maybe-bucket prospect with un-restored decision-making power.
18. **Price shock isn't a "no."** Unless someone explicitly tells you there's a problem, assume they're processing thoughts out loud.

#### D. Operator Character (Rules 19–25)
19. **Selling properly is the first step to becoming a coach.** Helping someone is helping them make decisions to help themselves. That's exactly what a coach does.
20. **The person who cares the most about the prospect wins the sale.** Keep the prospect, not the sale, as the priority. They will hear it in your tone. It also divorces you from needing to close. Sometimes the best sales we make are the ones we don't.
21. **Seek to understand, not to argue.** Diffuses tough situations. Ask questions, don't accuse.
22. **Selling is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust.** Trust is built only when you genuinely want to help. Rules 11, 12, 13 create the environment that increases sale likelihood.
23. **Belief and trust are a continuum, not binaries.** You can always get better at getting people to believe and trust you. The question isn't whether they believe you — it's how much.
24. **Closers ask hard questions because they genuinely care.** Be **kind, not nice**. Nice guys don't want to offend. Kind people want to genuinely help — which sometimes means calling out BS in a tactful way that helps the prospect realize it themselves.
25. **If you're going to say something offensive, don't say it about them — say it about someone like them or your past self.** Self-deprecating or third-person reframes deliver the same message with less ego friction. The prospect connects the dots.

#### E. Observation Discipline (Rules 26–28)
26. **Once the prospect says yes, shut up.** Don't sell yourself out of a sale. Rule of law. Everything else after the yes has the potential to disrupt what's working.
27. **Record all your sales to learn from them.** Champions watch game tape. Champion salespeople treat each sale like a performance. See what worked, what to improve, focus on improving one thing at a time.
28. **Power is the ability to direct or influence people. If you want to be powerful, you must understand how to close.** The capstone — closing is not just a sales skill; it is a generalized influence skill.

### The Nudge sub-mechanic (folded from p. 27 Author Note)
A **nudge** is a single sentence appended to a close, used when the operator can see the prospect is right on the edge. Not a full overcome. Inline examples in the script libraries:

- *"And besides, this is geared to give you time back, not cost you time."*
- *"And the first thing we're going to do is actually eliminate 90% of the stuff you're doing that's not working."*
- *"That helps you too because we have this fear of perfectionism."*

Nudges appear inside scripts (not as standalone closes) because they only work when paired with a substantive close that just landed. Solo nudges feel hollow.

## Strategic Implications

- **The Rules layer is invisible to the prospect, structural to the operator.** A closer who knows the script library but ignores the rules will execute scripts mechanically and lose conversations. A closer who internalizes the rules can paraphrase any script and still close.
- **Two-or-three closes per obstacle = the trained-closer minimum.** Operators who try to memorize the entire close library produce worse results than operators who drill 2–3 per obstacle until they're automatic. Expertise here is depth, not breadth.
- **"Kind not nice" is the closer's character thesis.** Niceness is the reason untrained salespeople fail to close — they're afraid to push past the polite-deflection layer. Kindness is the discipline that lets you ask the hard question because the prospect's outcome matters more than the operator's comfort.
- **"Yes — shut up" (Rule 26) is non-negotiable.** Most blown closes after a yes are the operator continuing to sell. Once the prospect commits, the only remaining job is logistics (collect the card, schedule, hand off). Any further selling is downside-only.
- **"Record everything" (Rule 27) is the differentiator.** The compounding benefit of reviewing recorded calls is the structural reason elite closers exist. Without recording, every close is the closer's first; with recording, every close is informed by the last one's tape review.
- **Permission-getting (Rule 3) is the safety on hard truths.** *"Can I be a coach instead of a friend?"* converts a potentially-hostile reframe into a welcomed one. The same words without the permission ask trigger defensiveness.
- **The rules contradict each other in places, deliberately.** Rule 10 (don't assume objection = no) tempers Rule 13 (only ask when you've got 'em) — both are correct in their respective moments and require operator judgment to reconcile in real time. The judgment is the skill.

## Practical Use

Operator drill protocol when training a new closer (synthesized from Rules 5, 6, 8, 9, 27):

1. **Pick 2–3 closes per obstacle** from the script libraries. Don't try to memorize the whole book.
2. **Drill the chosen closes daily.** 70-20-10 split also applies here: 70% drilling proven closes, 20% practicing variants, 10% experimenting with new ones.
3. **Practice on unqualified leads** when possible (Yellows-are-golds rule). Lower stakes, higher reps, faster skill growth.
4. **Record every sales call.** Review with one improvement focus per session — don't try to fix five things at once.
5. **Internalize the conversation-hygiene rules first** (Rules 1–3). These are the foundation; without them, no script lands.
6. **Add the closing-mechanics rules next** (Rules 4–9). Stacking, nudges, picking 2–3-per-obstacle.
7. **Hold the operator-character rules at all times** (Rules 19–25). Especially "kind not nice" and "care most about the prospect."
8. **After yes, shut up.** Memorize Rule 26 as a hard stop.
9. **Re-watch tape weekly.** Fix one habit per week. Sustained for a year, this produces a top-quartile closer.
