# Hormozi — Closing Definition

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
Foundation of the *Closing* playbook. Defines what closing actually is (not "convince to buy" — "give the prospect enough decision-making power to make a genuine yes-or-no"), why most prospects fail to decide (decision-avoidance is the disease, not the symptom), and the structural model the entire close library operationalizes (the Blame Onion). Sets the conceptual frame that the Rules note operationalizes and the close libraries (All-Purpose, Circumstances, Other-People, Self) execute against. Distinct from `01-100M-Offers/Hormozi-Offer-Creation` — that note builds the offer the close is selling; this note defines the mechanism that finishes the sale once the offer is on the table.

## Core Idea
> "The money is in the maybes."

Advertising splits the audience into three buckets, not two. **Yes** (lay-downs — they buy regardless), **No** (unqualified — wrong fit), and **Maybe** (on-the-fence — value the thing, have reservations). Most businesses only convert from the Yes bucket and assume Maybes are Nos. They aren't. Maybes are prospects who lack the decision-making power to commit either way and therefore avoid deciding. **Closing is the mechanism that returns decision-making power to the Maybe so they can make a genuine choice — yes or no, both acceptable.**

> "Closing gives the prospect enough power to overcome decision avoidance. To make a decision one way or the other. And you should feel OK about yeses and nos."

The reframe inverts what most operators believe closing is. It is not persuasion. It is power restoration. The prospect already has a decision they want to make; blame is what's blocking them from making it.

## Framework / Structure

### The 3-Bucket Maybe Model
Advertising sorts every prospect into one of three buckets:

| Bucket | What they are | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** (lay-downs) | Advertising itself closed them. Show up, accept payment. | Don't mess it up. |
| **No** (unqualified) | Wrong fit (e.g., person in Ohio opting into a Florida gym offer). Caused by confusion in advertising about who/what/how. | Filter out. |
| **Maybe** (on-the-fence) | Value the thing. Have reservations about exchanging money for it. Largest bucket. | This is what closing exists for. |

The closing operator's frame: **the maybe-bucket is where revenue lives, and it is invisible to operators who only count yeses-vs-nos**. A "no" from a maybe-bucket prospect was never a real no — it was decision-avoidance dressed up as a no.

> "Better ads make the pie bigger, and closing gets you a bigger slice of it."

The corollary: closing scales with advertising. The better your advertising, the more maybes you produce, the more closing leverage exists. Closing is not a substitute for advertising — it's the multiplier on it.

### Power: The Missing Ingredient
A maybe-bucket prospect lacks **decision-making power**. They think "maybe yes" and "maybe no" simultaneously, which means neither — so they avoid deciding entirely. The operator's job is not to push them toward yes; it is to give them enough power to choose. Three steps to give somebody decision-making power:

1. **Get a clear understanding of what they want** — earlier in the sales conversation, not at the close. (If you don't know what they want, you can't put your offer next to it.)
2. **Put what they want next to the options they have to get there** — your offer being one of them, doing nothing being another.
3. **Make sure they come to a genuine decision** — not a polite deflection, not a "let me think about it." A real yes or a real no.

> "Bottom line: People with enough power can decide. And if you've given someone all the right stuff to make a decision, you've won. Purchase or not, you have the mission to get a genuine decision."

This is the operator's measured outcome. **Genuine decisions, not closed sales,** is the closing skill's KPI. Closed sales follow.

### The Blame Onion (3 Layers + 5 Surface Forms)
When a prospect avoids a decision, decision-making power doesn't disappear — it transfers to whatever they blame for not deciding. The Blame Onion is the structural map of where that power gets cast:

```
                    SELF (innermost — hardest to surface)
              OTHER PEOPLE (decision-makers, past burns)
        EXTERNAL CIRCUMSTANCES (time, money, fit — outermost)
```

The onion shape is operationally important: **prospects say no more than once**, peeling off outer layers and exposing inner ones. Expect them to swap blame as you neutralize each one. Swapping blame is a positive signal — it means the close is working and you're moving toward the real obstacle.

The three blame categories defined:

1. **Circumstances** — *"I must get what I want when I want it. I must not get what I don't want. Or else… XYZ circumstance is to blame."* External-world objections.
2. **Other People** — *"I must get treated fairly and kindly. Or else… XYZ person is to blame."* Permission, trust, decision-maker objections.
3. **Self** — *"I must be perfect, I must do well, I must get what I want, or else my XYZ character trait is to blame."* Fear, avoidance, identity objections — the hardest layer because the prospect has to confront themselves.

These three categories surface in sales as five recognizable forms:

| Surface form | Sounds like | Branch |
|---|---|---|
| Time | "I'm too busy right now" / "I don't have time" | Circumstances |
| Money | "It's too expensive" / "I can't afford it" | Circumstances |
| Fit | "I don't like this detail" / "Not right for me" | Circumstances (also overlaps Self) |
| Authority/Permission | "I need to check with X before deciding" (partner doesn't trust me to buy stuff) | Other People |
| Trust/Skepticism | "I've been burned before" / "Let me think about it" / "What makes you different?" (I don't trust you enough to buy stuff) | Other People |
| Fear | "It might work for them, but it won't work for me" / "I'm scared to repeat my past mistakes" | Self |
| Avoidance | "I need to think about it" / "Send me a brochure and I'll email you tomorrow" | Self |

**Routing rule for the operator:** identify which branch the surface objection lives on, then deploy the close library for that branch. Use the All-Purpose closes first (they cut through the smoke and force the prospect to surface a real branch); then go branch-specific.

### Ethics Guardrails (Author Note)
The Closing playbook is explicit about its own power. It comes with three operating assumptions that gate legitimate use:

> "This objection-handling handbook is incredibly powerful. Using it you can get just about anyone to do just about anything."

Therefore the operator must believe, before deploying any close:

1. **The product will get them to their goal the way they want to go.**
2. **You and others will support them.**
3. **It will work for them, not just everyone else.**

> "The difference between help and manipulation is intention."

This is not optional framing. The same close, applied to the wrong prospect (one of these 3 conditions is false), is manipulation. Applied to the right prospect, it is help. The operator carries this responsibility, not the script.

### STAR Qualification (Prerequisite Filter)
Before any close fires, the prospect must already be qualified. STAR is the prerequisite filter:

| Letter | Means | Operator's job |
|---|---|---|
| **S** Situation | Has the problem your product solves | Confirm in discovery |
| **T** Timing | Has the time to solve it now | Confirm in discovery |
| **A** Authority | Has decision-making authority (or has the decision-maker on the call) | Confirm in scheduling |
| **R** Resources | Has the money | Confirm in discovery |

> "Trying to sell somebody a product well beyond their means wastes everyone's time. You simply sell an unqualified prospect. And unqualified prospects are not a sales problem — they're an advertising problem."

Closing **does not solve the unqualified-prospect problem**. The closing playbook's value applies only when STAR is satisfied. When it isn't, the issue routes upstream to advertising (wrong audience), lead nurture (insufficient pre-qualification), or offer (priced beyond market).

The exception (per Rule #8 — Yellows are golds): once a prospect is on the call with the time blocked, the operator can attempt to close them anyway. Worst case = practice; best case = a sale that wouldn't have happened otherwise. But the qualification filter is still the upstream truth.

## Strategic Implications

- **Sales teams routinely double close rates with effective closing training.** The lever is real and unusually concentrated. There are very few skills where one operator decision (training the team on closes) produces a 2× output change in a measurable KPI.
- **Closing scales with advertising, not against it.** Better ads → bigger maybe-bucket → more leverage on closing skill. The operator who is "good at sales" but "bad at marketing" produces less revenue than the one who runs ads at scale and trains a competent closer team.
- **Yes and no are both acceptable outcomes.** A genuine no from a closed conversation is a clean exit. The cancerous outcome is the unqualified yes — a prospect pushed past their real reservations who later refunds, charges back, or stays unhappy. The closer's KPI is *genuine decisions*, not *closed sales*.
- **The maybe-bucket is invisible by default.** Operators who only count yes/no miss the entire closing-leverage opportunity. The three-bucket model is a pre-condition for caring about closing as a discipline.
- **Blame swaps mean the close is working.** When the prospect says "well it's not the money, it's that I need to talk to my spouse," they have peeled an outer layer (circumstances) and exposed an inner one (other people). This is closer-positive behavior, not closer-negative.
- **The operator's character is part of the close.** Hormozi explicitly frames it: "Selling is a transference of belief over a bridge of trust." If the operator does not believe (or doesn't earn trust), no script saves the close. The Rules note operationalizes this as "Care most about the prospect, not the sale" — a character rule that the script library presupposes.
- **Champions close.** The athletic analogies (top-5 red zone offenses make playoffs 90% of the time; Federer won 80% of matches but only 54% of sets because he won the points that mattered) are not motivational decoration. They name the structural truth: *the points scored at the moment-that-matters disproportionately determine the outcome*. Closing is the moment-that-matters.

## Practical Use

When the operator engages a maybe-bucket prospect, the closing-frame protocol:

1. **Confirm STAR is satisfied.** If not, this is not a closing problem — escalate upstream (advertising, lead-nurture, offer) instead of attempting a close on an unqualified prospect.
2. **Hold the maybe-bucket frame.** Default-assume the prospect's reservation is decision-avoidance, not a real no. Treat the surface objection as a blame casting, not a reason.
3. **Identify the branch.** Map the surface form (time / money / fit / authority / trust / fear / avoidance) to the branch (Circumstances / Other People / Self).
4. **Open with All-Purpose closes** if the surface objection is unclear or sounds like a stall. (See [[Hormozi-All-Purpose-Closes|All-Purpose-Closes]].)
5. **Branch-specific scripts** when the obstacle is named. (See [[Hormozi-Closing-Circumstances-Closes|Circumstances-Closes]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Other-People-Closes|Other-People-Closes]] / [[Hormozi-Closing-Self-Closes|Self-Closes]].)
6. **Expect blame to swap.** After the first script lands, the prospect surfaces the next layer. Repeat — peel until the prospect either (a) has decision-making power and genuinely chooses, or (b) reveals an unqualified status (STAR was wrong) and the conversation ends honestly.
7. **Run all closes through the ethics gate.** If any of the three ethics conditions is false (product won't get them there, you/others won't support them, won't work for them specifically), do not close — that's manipulation, not help.
