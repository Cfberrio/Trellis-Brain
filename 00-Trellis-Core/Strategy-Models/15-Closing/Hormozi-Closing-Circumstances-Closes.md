# Hormozi — Closing: Circumstances Closes

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
Branch 1 of the [[Hormozi-Closing-Definition|Blame Onion]] — script library for prospects who blame **external circumstances** (time, money, fit) for their decision-avoidance. Two sub-categories: **Time** and **Money** (the largest category in the book, split into 4 flavors). Operator deploys these after the branch is identified — typically following an All-Purpose close that surfaced the named obstacle. Each script preserves verbatim wording where the words are the deliverable.

## Core Idea
> "I avoid buying your thing because of stuff out of my control."

Circumstances-blame is the outermost layer of the onion. The prospect points at an external constraint — time, money, fit — and uses it to absolve themselves of the decision. The closer's job: shift the locus of control back to the prospect by demonstrating that the "constraint" is in fact a choice, and that the choice they're currently making (don't buy) is the one with the higher cost.

> "Saying 'I can't make the wedding because (something outside of my control) came up' absolves the person of responsibility. Our goal is to get them to say 'I'm not going to your wedding because I'm choosing to do something more valuable to me.'"

## Framework / Structure

### Operator Posture (preamble)
**Repeated for emphasis:** Every script in this library begins with an acknowledgment frame ([[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 2]] — *"acknowledge or agree, never disagree"*). Default openers before the close: *"Totally get it,"* *"Heard,"* *"Totally understand,"* *"That's a great point,"* *"I'm right there with you."*

**Frame:** *"You sit on the same side of the table. You're not competing with them. Validate what they say before responding to it. You can never convince someone. We can only guide them through a series of questions and statements to convince themselves."*

## SECTION A — TIME ("I'm too busy" / "I don't have time")

### Operator framing for Time
> "People get busy. Then they get unbusy. Then they get busy again. Life works that way. But here's the thing — people make time for stuff either way."

Two operations: (1) help them see where their hours go, (2) discuss how they can better use them. Bridge: *if our thing saves them time vs. what they do now / makes their life easier / supported = better use of their time = win-win = busy is the BEST time to start (more value when they're not busy).*

### Time Closes (5)

#### "Better To Start When You're Busy"
*"Totally understand. Honestly, here's some good news: the busier you are, the better time it is to start. Because life is always going to have busy seasons, and if you can't figure out how to do it during busy seasons, then this isn't going to last. And you want this to last, right? Then what better time to do it than when you need it most?"*

#### "You're Gonna Get Busy Again"
*"Totally understand. Even if you did wait until it wasn't busy, you'd get busy again right? Well, then even if you did start when you had time, you'd stop again the moment you were busy. So, if you learn how to make this work when you're busy, then you'll be able to stick with it no matter what life throws at you. And you want this to be permanent right? Right…"*

**(Nudge)** *"And besides, this is geared to give you time back, not cost you time. The first thing we're gonna do is get you to stop doing all the stuff that isn't working."*

#### "It's Not About Timing, It's About Priorities"
*"Totally understand it's a busy time right now. Can I ask you a real question? What are you doing that's more important than this right now? … okay, well, either you're never going to have time, or something needs to become less important so this can be more important. It sounds like it's more a question of priorities than it is timing. Because let's face it, stuff is always going to come up. So either [goal] is more important than these interruptions, or it's not. And if it's not, that's totally okay. I'd rather you leave knowing you're never going to achieve [goal] rather than suffering all the time hoping it's going to happen. It's either important enough to change your schedule for, or it's not. And if it is, then let's do it."*

#### "The Smartphone Close"
*"Totally understand. I honestly thought the same thing when I started my business. And I told that to my wife. And instead of saying 'sure baby, do it later,' she had had enough of my whining and said 'give me your phone so we can…' She pulled up my time usage and it showed 20 hours per week on social media. She said 'guess I found you some time,' and handed it back to me.*

*Because at the end of the day, you, me, and Jeff Bezos all had the same 24 hours when we started out. It's all about how we allocate them. And up to this point, would you say you've allocated your time well to get to where you want? If not, then that's exactly what we're going to show you how to do."*

**Extra nudge:** *"And the first thing we're going to do is actually eliminate 90% of the stuff you're doing that's not working to make room for what does."*

#### The "When/Then" Close
*"Totally understand. This is a classic example of a when/then issue. I used to do this all the time. Can I explain why this thought kept me stuck for almost a decade? Well, it's a false premise. When you say 'I'll do it when I have more time' it's like saying 'When I get healthy, then I'll go to the doctor' or 'When I'm rich, then I'll start saving money.' It flips the order."*

## SECTION B — MONEY ("It's too expensive" / "I can't afford it")

### Operator framing for Money
Money objections come in **4 flavors**, each with a different operator response:

| Flavor | Surface form | Operator's frame |
|---|---|---|
| **B1. Not Enough Value** | *"It's too much"* | They have the money. They don't see the value. Translate "price too high" → "value too low." |
| **B2. Can't Afford** | *"I can't afford it"* (rarer if STAR-qualified) | They see the value. Genuinely beyond means. Appeal to **resourcefulness, not resources**. |
| **B3. Cheaper Elsewhere** | *"The guy down the street does it for less"* | Highlight the delivery difference. Ask why they didn't already buy from that other guy. |
| **B4. Haggling** | *"Can you do it for less?"* | Offer to charge MORE. Ends the haggle and forces decision. |

> "If something really did have value then, so long as you have a qualified prospect, they would figure out how to get it. Remember, nothing is inherently 'too expensive.' If somebody says 'the price is too high' they aren't saying they don't value your thing. But, they are saying they'll avoid deciding until they see the value of your product meet or exceed the value of their dollar. For that reason, you should immediately translate 'price too high' into 'value too low.'"

The general operator move: **shift focus from the price to the value.** Put all the painful stuff they have to deal with now next to all the awesome stuff that replaces it after they buy.

**STAR Qualifying note (Author Note p. 28, folded):** Money objections from prospects without **R**esources are an *advertising problem*, not a closing problem. STAR-qualify before closing. The closes below assume STAR is satisfied.

### B1. Not-Enough-Value Closes (10)

#### "It's Good That It's A Lot"
*"I understand that it feels like a lot of money, and that's actually why I believe this is the right step for you. Most of our biggest success stories started exactly where you are — seeing this as a big investment.*

*And honestly, that's a good thing. The fact that this feels like a big investment means you're more likely to take it seriously. Just imagine: if you follow all the steps I outlined, do you have any doubt you'd succeed? Of course not. The commitment of your hard-earned money is there to hold you accountable. If this were just $1, it might be easy to overlook, just like other things you've tried in the past.*

*Now, let's think about the worst-case scenario. You mentioned losing $XXX feels significant. That tells me you wouldn't take this lightly. The real risk isn't in investing the money — it's walking out the door right now and staying where you are, without achieving the results you want."*

**(Nudge)** *"If this means a lot to you, I know you will succeed. So, are you ready to take the next step and get started?"*

#### "Good Things Aren't Cheap — Cheap Things Aren't Good"
*"Totally understand. Let me ask you a question. How many free PDFs have you downloaded and made no more money? We had two choices: give the bare minimum for people to be successful and control costs to make it as easy as possible on your part, OR, rather than starting with price, start with value, and put so much help, support, and resources to stack the chips in your favor so much it would be unreasonable not to be successful. So yes, it's not cheap, but would you rather be less likely to hit your goal? Right — us neither."*

#### "Would You Even Believe Me If It Were Less?"
*"Totally hear where you're coming from. Let me ask you a question… Would you even believe it could do what I say it could do if it were one third the price? …"* (They'll say no.) *"Then what are we talking about? (joking tone) Let's get you going."*

#### "It's Not A Lot When You Think About It"
*"Totally understand. Let me ask you a question… Hypothetically, if this got you an extra $10,000/mo would it be worth it? If so, then it's not the price, it's whether you believe you can do it, right? Well, if I show you success stories of people like you, at what point does it become more unreasonable to believe? 10, 20, 50? Okay, well here's 200. At some point you just have to make the jump.*

*If this only [outcome], would it be worth it? If all it does is [outcome], would it be worth it? Right, then the only thing more expensive would be not doing it."*

#### "Not What You Make But What It's Worth"
*"Totally understand. But I think it's not about how much you make today. It's about how much you're gonna make and how much that's worth to you. Right?"*

#### "You're Gonna Spend The Money Either Way"
*"Totally understand. And let's be honest, in the next 12 months, you're gonna spend this amount of money either way. So the money is already spent. All we're doing is deciding where it's going. Are you gonna spend it on stuff that doesn't matter, or on something that 12 months from now you can look back and say 'that was the best decision of my life?'*

*After all, what is money good for anyways? It only has value when it is exchanged. Your goal is to exchange it for something better than doing nothing with it, or spending it on something else. But what better to spend money on than the skill to increase your ability to generate money and do work you find meaningful?"*

#### "You Pay The Price Either Way"
*"Totally understand. Mind if I share a different way of thinking about it? …"* (they nod) *"You're gonna pay the price either way. With years of your own life to figure it out, or with a payment today to use the time other people have already put in for you. We just pay with the currency we value least. Your time or your money.*

*As long as you value your time above minimum wage, you come out way ahead buying your time back at a steep discount and dragging your future earning power into the present."*

**(Nudge)** *"And at the end of the day, that's why we reverse all the risk and provide you a XX guarantee or your money back. So best case, you accomplish everything we said, or worst case, you learn tons of stuff for free. Either way, you win."*

#### "Some Now or More Later"
*"Totally understand. If that's the case this is probably the best time to do it then because it's only gonna become more expensive. Doing this costs some now but not doing this will cost more later. The amount of time and money will only increase as you put it off. So let's get it done now while it's the cheapest and fastest it's gonna be."*

#### "Future Favor"
*"It totally is a lot of money, but that's because of the amount of time it will save you. How long have you been wanting this? As long as you can remember, right? If 10 years ago, you could have made a decision that would have made the last 10 years of your life the fittest and best, would that be worth what we're asking? Of course, right?*

*So then let's do future you a favor and have her look back on these next 10 years really excited about having made the decision that changed it for good."*

### B2. Can't-Afford Closes (3)

#### "Resourcefulness Not Resources"
*"So you don't have the money? In other words, the resources to start, is that right? Can I tell you something that blew my mind when I heard it?*

*Do you know the difference between a self-made millionaire when they had nothing and someone who has nothing? Nothing financially. The only difference is that it proves that if they were successful, it wasn't about the resources they had; it's about how resourceful they were."*

**(Optional — Phil Knight addition):** *"Phil Knight, in the story of Nike, was about to lose everything multiple times. He needed a tremendous amount of money and didn't have it. His people already worked for free. He went to his vendors and said, 'You need to pay my payroll for me.' He used his resourcefulness, not his resources.*

*When you have nothing, it's the easiest excuse to give yourself because you're like, 'Well, I have nothing, that's why I can't.' But every self-made millionaire and every self-made billionaire started with nothing, which puts them in the exact same seat you are. The question is, how much control do you want over your life? When you have power, you focus on the things you can control — how resourceful you are, not the resources you have."*

#### "Had It Worse And Done Better"
*"Can I tell you something my dad told me when I told him I couldn't make ends meet to move out here to take this job? Before I tell you — I wanna brace you — my dad was being a little harsh to me (nod). Yea… so he asked me, do you think there's anyone else who's been in your situation who's achieved this? If the answer is yes — then what's the difference between them and you?"*

#### "Everyone Starts At Zero"
*"Mind if I share something that blew my mind when I heard it? This is a fact… all self-made millionaires have something in common — they all started at zero. Which means where you are right now, is where many self-made millionaires started. So when they didn't have resources, they had something else — resourcefulness. So if you want to become wealthier, do you think you want less resourcefulness?*

*Let today be the first day where you let your resourcefulness be stronger than your excuses. And you let your resourcefulness turn into resources."*

**(Nudge)** *"Let me prove it to you right now: If you needed life-saving surgery, would you find a way to pay for it? Of course you would. So the question is, is saving yourself from the life you're living worth it?"*

### B3. Cheaper-Elsewhere Closes (3)

#### "Cheap Comparison Close"
*"Super reasonable. So let me ask you… if we were the same price, which one would you pick?"* (they say you) *"Why?"* (they list out all the reasons you're better) — wait until they finish, then say: *"I totally agree! Those are all awesome reasons. Couldn't have said it better myself. So now you understand why we aren't the same price, right?"* Then laugh and ask for the sale again.

#### "Good Fast Cheap"
*"Totally hear you. But let me share something I've learned… the way I see it, you can have it good, fast, or cheap. But you can only ever pick two — you can have it good and fast, but it ain't cheap; you can have it cheap and fast, but it won't be good; or you can have it cheap and good, but it won't be fast. So… which two do you want?"*

(Variant continuation:) *"Honestly, sometimes the cheapest option ends up costing more because you have to come to me anyway — a day late and a dollar short. If you start with me you save the time and money you're gonna waste with the other guy not getting what you want the first time, done right."*

#### "Cheap Or What You Need"
*"I hear you. Maybe think about it this way. At the end of the day, you have two choices — you can buy something cheap or what you need. In other words, you can get yet another thing that doesn't hurt to buy because you and I both know it won't do anything, or you can invest in yourself and solve this problem for good. If you look at the value of your time and what [being in amazing shape for every picture, event, summer with the kids, and other events in your life] don't you think this would be worth it?"*

### B4. Haggling Closes (2)

#### The "We Could Do It For More" Close
*"Uhh… we could do it for more (ha)."*

OR

*"We could do it for $XXX (price higher than you quoted)."*

> Note: If someone ever asks for a discount, this works insanely well.

#### "It's Not, Not The Money"
**Used when prospect haggles indirectly (*"it's not the money, it's [some other thing]"*)**

*"If this were free, would you do it?"* (they say yes)

*"I tell you what. If you're gonna do it either way, you might as well lock in the savings now, then we can push out your start date / ship date as far as you like. Because if you like a discount / bonus now, you're gonna like one later."*

**Note A:** As soon as someone says they'd do it for less, they give up all objections unrelated to price and payment. This allows you to focus on only one obstacle then dismantle it.

**Note B:** As soon as you say you'd do it for less, you give up all appeals to your product's value. This takes you from closing to haggling. Gross.

## Strategic Implications

- **"Value too low ≠ price too high" is the load-bearing translation.** Every Money close in this library presupposes the operator translated the surface objection. The translation rule is the structural operator move; the script is the delivery vehicle.
- **Time and Money closes share an underlying frame: shift "constraint" → "choice."** Every Time close reframes time as a priority, not a constraint. Every Money close reframes price as a value-perception gap, an investment trade-off, or a haggle the operator declines to participate in.
- **The "We Could Do It For More" haggle close is asymmetric power.** It signals the operator is not desperate, ends the haggle in one move, and forces the prospect to either accept the original price or walk. Per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 11]] — "never change your price on the spot" — this is the discipline expression.
- **Resourcefulness-not-resources closes only work on STAR-qualified prospects who lied about R.** A truly unqualified prospect (genuinely without resources) is not a closing problem — it's an advertising problem. The Can't-Afford library is for prospects who *can* find the money but haven't because they haven't decided the value is real.
- **Future-self framing is the operator's most repeated structural move.** "Some Now or More Later," "Future Favor," "You're Gonna Spend the Money Either Way" — all three are versions of the same structural move: *let the prospect look back on this decision from a future where they did it.* The frame is more powerful than any single close.
- **Cheaper-Elsewhere closes work because the prospect already chose you.** If the competitor's price were enough, they would already be the competitor's customer. The fact that the prospect is on the call with you proves they want what you're delivering, not what the competitor delivers. The close surfaces this implicit choice.
- **Drill 2–3 per flavor.** Per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 6]], pick 2–3 from each section that fit the operator's natural voice. Drill those. Don't try to memorize all 23 closes in this library — Hormozi's own recommendation.

## Practical Use

Recommended drill set per flavor (operator picks 2–3):

| Flavor | High-leverage closes |
|---|---|
| Time | Better-To-Start-When-You're-Busy + Smartphone + It's-Not-About-Timing-It's-About-Priorities |
| Not-Enough-Value | It's-Good-That-It's-A-Lot + You-Pay-The-Price-Either-Way + Future-Favor |
| Can't-Afford | Resourcefulness-Not-Resources (with Phil Knight addition) |
| Cheaper-Elsewhere | Cheap-Comparison-Close |
| Haggling | We-Could-Do-It-For-More |

Default sequence for a money objection where the flavor is unclear: **Open with Hypothetical (All-Purpose) → diagnose flavor from response → deploy the matching script → if blame swaps, route to the next branch ([[Hormozi-Closing-Other-People-Closes|Other-People]] or [[Hormozi-Closing-Self-Closes|Self]])**.
