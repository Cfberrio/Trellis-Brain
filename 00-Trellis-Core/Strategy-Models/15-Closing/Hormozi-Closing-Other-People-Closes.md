# Hormozi — Closing: Other-People Closes

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
Branch 2 of the [[Hormozi-Closing-Definition|Blame Onion]] — script library for prospects who blame **other people** for their decision-avoidance. Two sub-categories: **Decision Makers** (the spouse / business-partner / boss / family member they "need to check with") and **Bad Experiences with Other Products** (trust transferred from a past burn). The Decision-Maker library uses a 4-step diagnostic sequence rather than parallel scripts — each step has its own off-ramp where the prospect can agree, and the operator handles whatever they reveal at that branch point.

## Core Idea
> "I don't want to buy your thing because SOMEONE ELSE prevents me."

Other-People-blame is the middle layer of the onion. The prospect deflects power to a third party — typically a spouse or business partner — to avoid owning the decision. The closer's job: **help the prospect see they're asking for support, not permission**, that there are no social obligations (only social consequences), and that ceding life-decisions to a third party guarantees long-term resentment toward that party.

> "There are no social obligations, only social consequences. They don't need permission, they need support."

The Bad-Experiences sub-branch is structurally similar but the third party is in the past — a vendor or product that burned them. The reframe is: don't let one bad decision burn you twice.

## Framework / Structure

### Operator Posture (preamble)
**The single most important pre-empt:** *"The best way to avoid this is to have decision makers on the call ahead of time."* During the qualification process, confirm the decision-maker is present. If they aren't, **reschedule** rather than try to close — this is upstream of the close, not a closing problem.

**When the decision-maker is missing and you must close anyway:** the 4-step sequence below works best for cheap stuff relative to the prospect's buying power. Each step has its own off-ramp; the prospect can agree and close at any point.

> "It's best for selling cheap stuff relative to the buying power of the prospect. Instead of being a couple lines, this is a series of questions and responses with their own off ramps and destinations. Meaning, someone can agree at any point in this sequence."

> "I have only had one series of questions that has worked to close these consistently. I'll also note that if you are asking someone to make a life-changing decision, there's no point in forcing it. You'll need partner buy-in if the purchase materially affects their household."

The 4 diagnostic steps in order: (1) What if they say no? (2) What would they object to? (3) If they know you're not happy, why would they stop you? (4) Ask for support, not permission.

## SECTION A — DECISION MAKERS ("I have to talk to my spouse / business partner / etc.")

### The 4-Step Sequence

#### Step 1 — "What if they say no?"

**They say:** *"I have to talk to my spouse / business partner / grandma / employees / dog / etc."* OR *"I can't make this decision alone."*

**You say:** *"What if they don't give you permission? Or, what if your spouse says no?"*

**(a) If they say "I'd do it anyway":**

**You say:** *"Awesome. Then let's get you started."*

(In Hormozi's experience, **1 in 3 prospects** says this. Close immediately.)

**(b) If they say "Then I wouldn't do it":** route to Step 2.

#### Step 2 — "What would they object to?"

**You say:** *"What part specifically do you think they wouldn't approve of? The price? The program? Or me? haha."*

**(a) If they give an answer** → off-ramp: handle the named obstacle. (The prospect just surfaced a real branch — typically Money or Trust. Route to [[Hormozi-Closing-Circumstances-Closes|Circumstances]] or back through [[Hormozi-All-Purpose-Closes|All-Purpose]].)

**(b) If they say "I just don't make decisions without my spouse":** route to Step 3.

#### Step 3 — "If they know you're not happy, why would they stop you?"

**You say:** *"Does your spouse approve of your current struggle? If they don't, then why do you think they'd be against you fixing something they already don't approve of?"*

**(Nudge — Role Reversal):** *"If the roles were reversed and your husband needed this to make his dreams happen, or your wife needed this program to make her dreams happen, would you support her? Then why wouldn't you support you?"*

**If they still say "My partner and I always make decisions together":** route to Step 4.

#### Step 4 — Ask for support, not permission

**You say:** *"I totally get it. Can I offer a potentially different perspective? (yes)… I think you're asking for support not permission. Because… let's play it out. It's your life, not theirs. What ends up happening is if you give that power to them and then two years from now [SAME THING BUT MORE], who are you going to blame? Them. That's when resentment sets in. And that's not fair to you or them.*

*I'm not saying you shouldn't explain the decision to them, you just need to ask for their support."*

#### Off-Ramp: Three-Day No-Sweat Guarantee (lock-in mechanic)

**If they still say no after Step 4:**

**You say:** *"I tell ya what… sign up, I'll delay the payment and if your partner says you should stay poor, fat, stupid and lazy… you give me a call and — first — I'd like to talk to them. And — second — I'll tear up the contract. This way you lock in your spot but you still get time to talk to your partner. Fair enough?"*

**Pro Tip (folded — Three-Day No-Sweat Guarantee, p. 38):** Most states require this anyway, so you're not changing much. The downsell version is to put a card down and delay payment for 24–72 hours so they can "lock in the spot" but still have time to chat it over.

#### Final Off-Ramp: Humorous Hypothetical Extreme (B2C example)

**If they still say no after the lock-in:**

**You say:** *"NAME… hear me out… if you sign up and you tell (YOUR PARTNER's NAME) and he's upset, and when he sees you, he's like, 'Girl, I don't want you to get in shape. I want you to stay overweight. I want you to put those sweatpants on. I want you to sit on that couch, reach your hand into a bag of Cheetos, wipe the Cheetos fingers on your sweatpants. And create generational unhealth. I want you to live ten years less long. I want you to not see your grandkids. I don't want to marry a younger, better you sooner because you have a heart attack.' If he says that to you, you have him call me."*

(They're cracking up normally.)

*"Okay, okay, listen. Sign up. Three-day no-sweat. If he does tell you that he just wants you to be out of shape and never leave him because you're going to get too hot… if he says that, just let me know and I'll tear this up no problem."*

**(Nudge):** *"Sometimes it's better to ask for forgiveness than permission, right?"*

**Function:** breaks tension via absurdity. The humorous hypothetical surfaces the obvious truth that no spouse would say that — which means the prospect has been hiding behind a fictional objection. Combined with the lock-in mechanic, this often closes the sale even after Step 4 fails.

## SECTION B — BAD EXPERIENCES WITH OTHER PRODUCTS

### Operator framing for Bad Experiences
> "People who blame others for their decision avoidance can be a pain to deal with. But, it's totally doable. I've learned over the years that how you deliver your message matters as much as the message itself. You can have the perfect script, but unless you've got big rapport and correct tonality you will flop."

> "They're not the prisoners they make themselves out to be. So if you can get them to laugh at the silliness of blaming others, and that they really can make decisions for themselves, you'll get a genuine close."

The operator's tonal frame matters more here than in the Decision-Makers branch. The reframe is structural and short — but it lands only with rapport and humor, not with confrontation.

### "Don't Let It Burn You Twice" Close

**They say:** *"I've been burned before…"* OR *"I tried something like this already and it didn't work."*

**You say:** *"Totally understand your reservations. I'd feel the same way too if the same thing happened to me. But maybe this is worth considering. If you don't do this, then you let that bad decision burn you twice. It burned you once when you made the bad investment but it'll burn you twice if that bad decision prevents you from making a good one.*

*It would be like having a bad eighth-grade boyfriend or girlfriend and being like, you know what, men aren't for me. All coaches suck, right? There are good coaches and bad coaches. There are crazy-ass chicks and crazy-ass dudes. There are also good ones. The only thing worse than making a bad investment is letting one bad investment stop you from making a good one. Don't let that bad decision burn you twice."*

**Function:** reframes the past burn as evidence the prospect needs *better discrimination*, not avoidance. Pairs the eighth-grade-relationship analogy with the operator's tonal warmth — tone carries the message; the words alone won't land.

## Strategic Implications

- **The 4-step Decision-Makers sequence is structurally a diagnostic, not parallel scripts.** Each step ends with an off-ramp that either closes the sale or surfaces the next layer. Operators who try to deploy steps as standalone closes ("I'll just use Step 4") will leak yeses that would have closed at Step 1's "I'd do it anyway" branch.
- **Step 1 closes 1 in 3 prospects.** Hormozi's published number. The "what if they say no?" question is the highest-leverage single line in the entire Decision-Makers library because it surfaces the prospect's actual autonomy.
- **The lock-in mechanic (3-day no-sweat) is non-optional.** Without it, the 4-step sequence has no terminal off-ramp for prospects who genuinely won't decide alone. With it, the sequence converts even the high-resistance cases by offering a low-commitment "yes for now."
- **Tonal frame carries the Bad-Experiences close.** This is the close most likely to fail when the operator has the words but lacks the tone. Per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 24]] (kind not nice), the "burn you twice" reframe must come from genuine warmth — not gotcha tone.
- **The humorous-hypothetical extreme is an asymmetric move.** Operators worry it's too risky. In Hormozi's experience it's specifically chosen for the moment when *every other approach has failed* — at that point, absurdity-based humor either breaks the impasse (and closes) or breaks rapport irreparably (and the close was already lost). The expected value is positive.
- **Other-People-blame is the layer where the prospect is most likely to be honest about a real obstacle.** When Step 2 produces an actual answer ("they wouldn't approve of the price"), the prospect has revealed the real branch — and the operator can route back to [[Hormozi-Closing-Circumstances-Closes|Circumstances]] (Money flavor) and close on the actual obstacle.
- **The "support not permission" reframe is the close's load-bearing thesis.** Steps 3 and 4 both rest on it. The framing produces the resentment-prevention argument (cede the decision → blame the third party → resent them) which is the operator's strongest ethical argument for the prospect deciding on their own.

## Practical Use

Pre-empt protocol (always, before closing):
1. **Confirm decision-maker presence** during qualification / scheduling. If absent, reschedule.

Mid-call protocol (when decision-maker absent and you must close):
1. **Step 1** — *"What if they say no?"* Close on "I'd do it anyway."
2. **Step 2** — *"What would they object to?"* Off-ramp to the named obstacle's branch script.
3. **Step 3** — *"If they know you're not happy, why would they stop you?"* + Role-Reversal nudge.
4. **Step 4** — *"You're asking for support, not permission."* Resentment frame.
5. **Lock-in** — 3-day no-sweat with delayed payment.
6. **Humorous hypothetical** — only when steps 1–5 all failed; high-variance close that breaks the impasse.

For Bad-Experiences: deploy "Don't Let It Burn You Twice" with deliberate warm tone. Prepare to follow with a [[../01-100M-Offers/Hormozi-Guarantees|Guarantee]] (when one exists) — guarantees pair structurally with bad-burn objections because they reverse the prospect's actual fear (losing money to another bad vendor).
