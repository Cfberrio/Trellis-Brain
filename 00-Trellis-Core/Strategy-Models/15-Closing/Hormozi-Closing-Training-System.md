# Hormozi — Closing Training System

## Parent
- [[00-Book-Home|Closing — Book Home]]

## Purpose
The training mechanism — *how an operator gets a sales team from untrained to top-quartile in closing*. Distinct from the [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Closing Rules]] (the operator-character constraints that govern any individual close) and the script libraries (the closes themselves). This note covers the operator-economics case for training closing as the highest-leverage skill investment in a sales org, and the practical drill cadence + observation discipline that produces trained closers.

## Core Idea
> "The sales guys weren't lazy. They weren't dumb. They weren't unmotivated… They didn't know how to sell."

Closing is a teachable skill, not an innate talent. The "Start Here" narrative (Gym Launch, January 2017) is structurally evidence: a team that was 0-for-7 to 0-for-18 on calls, going $412/hr into debt, became a $7.4M-revenue operation in 12 months — not by hiring better salespeople, but by training the existing ones. The lever is the training system, not the hire.

> "Sales teams routinely double close rates with effective closing. I can hardly think of a better use of time. How else can you reliably double the close rates of an entire team?"

The operator's framing: closing is the skill that compounds the fastest into revenue. There is no other single training investment with comparable ROI on operator time.

## Framework / Structure

### The Trained-vs-Untrained Operator Economics (p. 53, verbatim list)

When you learn how to close the right way, you…
- **Decrease your cost of getting customers** (close more from the same lead pool)
- **Improve your Return On Advertising Spend (ROAS)** of all campaigns
- **Get better customers** who are more skeptical (because you can close them, you don't have to filter them out)
- **Improve cash flow** (more Paid In Fulls and fewer payment plans)
- **Get better customer results** because they are fully bought in
- **Out-execute competitors** in the same market with the same services
- **Keep closers longer** (they make more money → they stay)
- **Get better-paid salespeople** because they make you more money — everyone wins

When you close the wrong way, you…
- **Ward off good customers** (they smell weakness)
- **Pay more for every customer** (worse close rate → more ad spend per acquired customer)
- **Make it harder to stay profitable** on advertising campaigns
- **Create more friction to scaling** (every dollar of growth requires more capital)
- **Get customers worse results** because they're not bought in (low conviction = low effort)
- **Extend out your cash flow** — what should've been a prepayment becomes an extended payment term (and sometimes results in non-payment)
- **Lose good salespeople** because they aren't making enough

> "Closing is the big game. It's where you either win or lose. It's how you play when the game is on the line. Do great — and become a business legend. Do poorly — and have your business fizzle into obscurity."

These are not motivational bullets. They are the cycle's downstream effects on the *unit economics of the entire business*, not just the salesperson's commission.

### The Drill Cadence (synthesized from Rules 6, 8, 9, 27 + "Start Here" narrative)

The Gym Launch case (pp. 3–7) is structurally the playbook's training-system prototype:

1. **Identify the most common obstacle in current calls.** (Gym Launch: "the stall.")
2. **Drill that one obstacle daily** until close rate improves.
3. **Move to the next most common obstacle.** (Gym Launch: price.)
4. **Drill that one daily** until close rate improves.
5. **Iterate.**

> "Sales started to tick up. They were handling stalls, but now they struggled the most with price. So, we drilled the price for a few days. Lo and behold, sales ticked up again."

The cadence is **single-obstacle-at-a-time**, not encyclopedic. Per [[Hormozi-Closing-Rules|Rule 6]] — *2-3 closes per obstacle is enough.* The drill set per closer is small but deeply rehearsed.

### The 4 Training Rules (extracted from Closing Rules + applied here)

#### 1. Practice on unqualified leads (Rule 8 — "Yellows are golds")
> "The more you close the better you get. So you want to get as many reps as possible. I train my teams to be equal opportunity closers. As long as the person has the problem to solve, we try to close. Less qualified prospects give you a bigger challenge than lay-downs. So I see this as the perfect opportunity for practice."

The operator's frame: every booked call is a training rep, regardless of qualification. Worst case, the closer practices on a low-stakes call. Best case, they make a sale that wouldn't have happened.

#### 2. Volume negates luck (Rule 9)
> "The best way to guarantee that you'll be world class is to do so many sales that it would be unreasonable that you aren't."

Training time on calls > training time on simulations. The system isn't built around classroom drills — it's built around real call volume with structured post-call review.

#### 3. Record everything (Rule 27)
> "There's a reason champions watch game tape. And champion sales people treat each sale like a performance. See what you did well. What you could improve. Focus on improving one thing at a time until you've corrected the things you suck at so much that you're actually good."

Recording is the differentiator between learning-by-doing (slow) and learning-by-deliberate-review (fast). Without recording, every close is the closer's first; with recording, every close is informed by the last one's tape review.

**One-thing-at-a-time** is critical. Reviewing tape with five things to fix produces zero fixes. Reviewing tape with one thing to fix produces incremental improvement that compounds weekly.

#### 4. Memorize the All-Purpose closes; understand everything else (Rules 5, 6 + [[Hormozi-All-Purpose-Closes|All-Purpose-Closes]] preamble)
The 7 [[Hormozi-All-Purpose-Closes|All-Purpose Closes]] should be word-for-word in every closer's repertoire. The branch-specific libraries should be *understood* — closers paraphrase them naturally in their own voice.

> "If there's a section of this handbook to commit to memory it would be this one. These have made me. So. Much. Money."

### The Operator's Position in the Training System
> "Just remember you've closed this sale more times than they've even been in sales appointments… combined."

The operator (founder / sales lead / closing trainer) is structurally the most-trained closer in the organization. The training system depends on the operator running it — daily huddles, tape review, drill design. Hormozi explicitly framed it: *"Alright boys, this is on me. I should've prepared you better."* When the team isn't closing, that's an operator-training problem, not a salesperson-quality problem.

## Strategic Implications

- **Closing is the highest-ROI training investment available to a sales org.** There is no other single training investment with comparable revenue impact per operator-hour. Doubling close rates compounds against every dollar of ad spend, every booked call, every retained customer.
- **Untrained closing leaks revenue at every stage.** The cycle list above (left column = trained, right column = untrained) shows that untrained closing isn't just a sales problem — it degrades CAC, ROAS, cash flow, customer quality, and team retention simultaneously. Operators who treat closing as "the salesperson's job" miss that the leak is structural across the business.
- **The Gym Launch case proves teachability.** A team that was 0-for-18 became a $7.4M operation in 12 months. The variable that changed was the training, not the headcount. The cycle is repeatable in any sales org.
- **Daily drill > weekly classroom.** The Gym Launch huddles were daily. Drilling one obstacle per day for 3-5 days produces measurable close-rate movement. Weekly trainings don't.
- **Tape review is the operator's compounding edge.** A closer who reviews their own calls weekly with one improvement focus reaches top-quartile in 6-12 months. A closer who never reviews calls plateaus at the level they hit in their first year.
- **Practice on unqualified leads is counterintuitive but structurally correct.** Most sales orgs treat low-quality calls as time-wasters. Hormozi treats them as training reps — calls where the closer can attempt high-stakes scripts at low actual stakes. The reframe accelerates skill acquisition.
- **The operator must close more than they think they should.** Per the Gym Launch narrative — Hormozi was the most-experienced closer on his team and was the one running training. Operators who delegate closing entirely to junior salespeople before they themselves have closed thousands of times are setting up the org to plateau at the operator's own (limited) closing skill.

## Practical Use

Operator install protocol for a closing-training program:

1. **Record every sales call.** (Most CRMs and meeting platforms support this. Get permission per legal jurisdiction.)
2. **Run a daily 15-minute sales huddle.** Cover one obstacle per session. Drill 2-3 closes for that obstacle.
3. **Pick the obstacle by frequency** — review the prior day's calls, identify what objections came up most, drill those. Don't drill what *should* matter; drill what *did* fail to close yesterday.
4. **Assign each closer a tape-review homework** — review one of their own calls this week, identify one thing to improve. Bring it to next huddle.
5. **Stack-rank close rates monthly.** Promote the best closers to mentor the others — peer instruction outperforms top-down once a baseline is set.
6. **Practice on unqualified leads** as a default policy. Don't pre-cancel low-quality calls; treat them as paid training reps.
7. **Operator (founder / sales lead) closes occasionally** to stay current and to model the behavior. The most-experienced closer in the org should not stop closing entirely — they should close enough to keep skills current and credibility intact.
8. **Track unit economics weekly.** Close-rate × call-volume × avg deal size = revenue from closing. Close-rate × CAC delta = ROAS lift. The numbers compound visibly within 4-8 weeks of starting the training cadence.

The Gym Launch month-1 outcome ($0 → break-even within 30 days; $7.4M annualized within 12 months) is the case-study benchmark for what a properly-installed training system produces.
