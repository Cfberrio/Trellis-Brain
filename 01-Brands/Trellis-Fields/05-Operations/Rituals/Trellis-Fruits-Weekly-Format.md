# Trellis Fruits — Weekly Format

## Purpose
The recurring weekly recap format for Trellis Fields internal work. Every Sunday, a Trellis Fruits page is created for the prior Sun–Sat window. It evaluates each team member across 5 dimensions + provides a preliminary narrative summary.

## Reporting Window
**Sun → Sat (Eastern time).**

Page is created on Sunday for the prior week (e.g., May 17 page covers May 10–May 16).

## Document Structure

### Section 1 — Preliminary Weekly Summary
A narrative paragraph (3–6 sentences) describing the dominant theme of the week:
- What kind of work dominated (creative shipping / strategy / pipeline-building / etc.)?
- Where was the most strategic load carried?
- What was the main friction?
- Which brand(s) absorbed the most attention?

### Section 2 — Bullet Highlights Per Person
Concise person-by-person bullets calling out the key task links from the week. Format:

> Charlize helped push multiple pieces into final or published states, including [task link 1], [task link 2], [task link 3].

Used as a quick scan if the reader doesn't want to read the full table.

### Section 3 — Trellis Fruits Table (5 columns)

| Column | What it captures |
|---|---|
| **Person** | Team member name |
| **Communication** | How they showed up in async + sync — specificity, channel choice, threading style |
| **Delays / Progress** | What moved, what stalled, what was rescheduled or rescoped |
| **Highlights / Wins** | Concrete deliveries, breakthroughs, judgment calls |
| **Content Produced** | Main visible outputs (with task links) |
| **Meetings / Notes** | Documents created, notes captured, syncs attended |

### Section 4 — Narrative Placeholder
Waiting for Luis's outline. Once received, Trellis Fruits Weekly Writer turns it into the polished weekly narrative.

## Tone Guidance
- **Specific, not generic.** "Charlize helped push [exact task] into publish-ready state" — not "Charlize did good work."
- **Task-linked.** Every claim should link to a ClickUp task or doc.
- **Honest.** If someone's footprint was light, say so. If revision pressure was heavy, name it.
- **Strategic, not emotional.** Identify patterns, not feelings.

## How To Generate
1. **Sun**: pull all activity from previous week (ClickUp, docs, Drive, etc.)
2. **Sun**: classify each person's work against [[../Team/Team-Roles|Team Roles]] dimensions
3. **Sun**: draft preliminary summary + bullets + table
4. **Sun**: post draft to designated location (currently the `𐦂𖨆𐀪𖠋𐀪𐀪` ClickUp space → `TRELLING FRUITS` doc → new dated page)
5. **Mon**: Luis adds narrative outline
6. **Mon**: writer (currently automated agent — Trellis Fruits Weekly Writer) finalizes narrative

## Example — May 17 Weekly (excerpt)

> The week leaned heavily toward creative shipping plus pipeline-building. Charlize and Julian kept Trellis, Discipline Rift, and Cheese To Share assets moving through review and into publish-ready states, while María José carried the clearest strategic load through approvals, scheduling, content-inventory work, and meeting follow-through.

> The main friction was not a lack of output, but the amount of revision and sequencing work happening at the same time. CTS menu/sign work kept absorbing detail-heavy review, several new Trellis and DR pieces were queued or re-scoped to protect quality, and Cristian's visible footprint was lighter in task activity even though technical planning and meeting follow-up stayed in motion.

## Why This Ritual Matters
Without weekly written recap:
- Leadership loses visibility into who shipped what
- Patterns of overload / underload stay invisible
- Strategic decisions get made without weekly evidence
- Wins don't get recognized; bottlenecks don't get named

Trellis Fruits **converts a week of distributed work into a single source-of-truth document** the team can review, learn from, and use to recalibrate.

These feed the weekly Trellis Fruits recap.
