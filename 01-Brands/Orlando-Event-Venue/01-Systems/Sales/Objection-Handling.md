# OEV Objection Handling

## Parent
- [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/01-Systems/Sales/Sales-Home|OEV Sales Home]]

## Handling Principle
Clarify fit, clarify cost, clarify rules, and clarify next step. Do not over-negotiate against the brand's economics.

## Standard Reframe
Ask: "What is your main concern right now?"

Then resolve according to bucket:
- price,
- rules,
- timing,
- tour,
- A/V,
- approval from another decision maker.

## Update Log

### 2026-05-20 — Objection-handling patterns from Luis × Non-Profit Inquiry call

Source: [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/04-Training/Source-Calls/2026-05-20_Luis_NonProfit_Inquiry|Source Call 2026-05-20]]

#### Objection: "Can we bring our own alcohol?" or "I thought I could bring beer"

**Canonical response (identity-framed, not policy-framed):**

> "Because we are a non-profit, we're strict on alcohol. We have to stand by our principles and our structure. Beer or wine has to run through our bar service so it's done a specific way. If you'd rather skip the bar service, non-alcoholic drinks are totally fine — water, sodas, no problem."

**If they push:**
- Don't debate. Don't apologize.
- "I hear you. The structure is what it is for us — we have to honor it. If bar service doesn't fit, non-alcoholic is wide open."
- If they insist or claim Luis approved it, escalate. Do not make exceptions.

**Banned responses:**
- "We don't allow alcohol" (too cold, triggers debate)
- "Sorry, our policy is..." (apologetic, gives them an opening)
- "Let me check with Luis" (don't stall on a known rule)

See [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/00-Brand-Core/Language-Rules#Rule 2 Alcohol policy identity-framed not policy-framed|Language Rule 2]].

---

#### Objection: "Do you charge a deposit?"

**Canonical response:**

> "It's not really a deposit — we don't charge deposits. We mainly work with community and trusted individuals and organizations. The 50% just locks your date. Half now, half before the event."

**Why this works:**
- Reframes the financial structure as trust, not collateral
- Removes the negative connotation of "deposit"
- Aligns with the non-profit/community identity

**Banned responses:**
- "Yes, we require a 50% deposit"
- "Our deposit is..."
- "It's non-refundable" (we don't take deposits, so this framing is wrong)

See [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/00-Brand-Core/Language-Rules#Rule 1 Never say deposit|Language Rule 1]].

---

#### Objection: "Pricing seems high" or "Can you do anything on the price?"

**Workflow:**
1. **Pause and reflect.** Don't immediately discount.
2. **Re-anchor on value.** Walk them through what's included: 90 chairs, 10 tables, prep kitchen, two bathrooms, free parking, 24-hour daily access.
3. **Identify discount eligibility:**
   - Non-profit → offer matching discount
   - Multi-day workshop → offer 25% off base rental
   - Otherwise → offer $100 off as a closer
4. **Use Luis's frame:** "Anything I can do to lower the cost, I would do it." Then deliver the discount that fits.
5. **Don't over-discount.** Cap at 20% unless management-approved.

---

#### Objection: "Can you hold the date without payment?"

**Canonical response:**

> "Dates are held with a 50% payment. Once that's in, the date is locked for you. Without that, the calendar stays open and someone else can grab it. The 50% is just to make it real on the calendar."

**Why this works:**
- Names the structure without making it punitive
- Implies real scarcity (someone else can take it)
- Avoids saying "we require a deposit"

---

#### Objection: "Can we use our own caterer?"

**Canonical response (proactive):**

> "Yes — zero restrictions on catering. Bring any caterer, any food, any setup. We're flexible on that side."

Surface this even before they ask — it's a differentiator.

---

#### Objection: "What about outdoor space?"

**Canonical response:**

> "We have an outdoor patio coming — about 20 to 30 people, decked-out space. We're approved for permitting, build hasn't started yet. For your event right now, that won't be available. If you need outdoor for a breakfast or gathering, there are restaurants nearby that work."

**Rule:**
- Don't promise the patio for current bookings.
- Don't show photos (there are none).
- Don't oversell — let them know it's coming, not here.

See [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/00-Brand-Core/Constraints#Outdoor Patio — Under Construction|Constraints — Outdoor Patio]].

---

#### Objection: "We need a custom setup / something not on the website"

**Workflow:**
1. Don't refuse on the call.
2. "Let me check the specifics with Luis and get back to you within 24 hours."
3. Document the request in GHL.
4. Escalate to Luis.
5. Follow up with the customer within the promised window.

**Banned:** approving custom requests on the spot without Luis's sign-off.

---

#### Objection: "We need to think about it"

**Canonical response (no pressure):**

> "Take the time you need. The date is still on the calendar — heads up though, we're booked through August so the closer we get the harder it is to hold. If you decide today or tomorrow, you'll have plenty of options. If anything comes up, I'm here."

**Why this works:**
- Respects their decision process
- Surfaces real scarcity without manufactured pressure
- Keeps the door open without being needy
- Follows up the call with the right text/email cadence (see [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/04-Training/Text-and-Email-Communication-Approach#Template 5 — Warm Follow-Up Customer Went Quiet|Warm Follow-Up template]])

---

#### Anti-patterns (don't do)

- Apologizing before stating a fact ("Sorry, but..." / "Unfortunately...")
- Debating with the customer over policy
- Manufacturing urgency that isn't real
- Promising the outdoor patio
- Saying "we don't allow alcohol" without the identity frame
- Saying "deposit" anywhere
- Discounting before live-quoting (you lose the anchor)
- Stalling routine pricing questions by saying "let me check with Luis"
