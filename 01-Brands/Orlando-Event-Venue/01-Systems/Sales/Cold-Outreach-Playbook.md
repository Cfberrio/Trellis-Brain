# OEV Cold Outreach Strategy & Email Playbook

Corporate Clients · Event Planners & Agencies · Non-Profits

---

## 1. Positioning: What OEV Actually Sells

OEV does not sell a room. It does not lead with square footage, a photo wall, or a prix-fixe catering menu. OEV sells **execution confidence** — the certainty that a host who books this venue will not have to troubleshoot the day of their event.

That framing shapes every sentence of every cold email. The prospect we are reaching is not browsing venues for fun. They have been burned before, or they are afraid of being burned. They need the event to go right — and they are willing to pay for a venue that is engineered around that outcome.

### Core Positioning Statement

**OEV is built for hosts who cannot afford a chaotic event.**

- Clear rules and expectations upfront — no surprises on the day
- Online booking, invoicing, and payment in a single flow — no manual back-and-forth
- Deposit-first path locks the date the moment a decision is made
- Production packages that handle AV — the client does not need to figure it out
- Bar service coordinated by OEV — one package selection, vendor handled
- Non-profit pricing: 50% off base rental Monday–Friday for qualified organizations

The brand is **corporate-first, celebration-second**. Every email should feel like it was written by someone who understands operations, not someone trying to fill calendar dates.

## 2. Target Segments

Three segments receive tailored outreach sequences. Each has a distinct primary concern and a different version of the execution-risk argument.

### Segment A — Corporate Clients

- **Who they are:** In-house ops leads, executive assistants, HR managers, or department heads responsible for planning offsites, team meetings, workshops, or product launches. Their job is on the line if the event fails. They are not shopping for the cheapest room; they are buying the right answer.
- **Primary concern:** Something goes wrong and it reflects on them personally.
- **OEV angle:** The entire booking process is engineered to prevent that. No vendor-hunting, no day-of surprises, no payment ambiguity.

### Segment B — Event Planners & Agencies

- **Who they are:** Independent event planners or boutique agencies that book venues on behalf of corporate or high-net-worth clients. Their reputation is their business. A venue that creates problems for them loses a referral source permanently.
- **Primary concern:** A venue that makes them look bad in front of their client.
- **OEV angle:** OEV is a low-friction venue partner. The booking process is fully online, the rules are clear, and production is handled — which means planners can confidently put OEV on a proposal and know it will not surprise them.

### Segment C — Non-Profits & Community Organizations

- **Who they are:** Executive directors, program managers, or development staff running weekday workshops, fundraisers, board meetings, or community programs. Budget pressure is real, but so is the need for a professional environment.
- **Primary concern:** Stretching a tight budget without sacrificing the impression the event makes.
- **OEV angle:** 50% off base rental Monday through Friday for qualified non-profits. Professional venue, online booking, AV handled — at a price point that makes sense. This is a segment that other venues do not actively pursue; OEV has a structural advantage here.

## 3. Sending Cadence

Each prospect receives a 3-email sequence. Emails are spaced to respect attention without going cold. **Do not compress the gaps** — cold outreach that arrives too quickly reads as desperation.

| Day      | Action                       | Goal                                                |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Day 1    | Email 1 — Initial outreach   | Establish the problem, introduce OEV                |
| Day 4    | Email 2 — First follow-up    | Add one concrete detail; restate the ask            |
| Day 9    | Email 3 — Final follow-up    | Soft close; remove friction to reply                |
| Day 10+  | Move to long-term nurture or close | No response = remove from active sequence     |

### Personalization Minimums

Every email should include at minimum: the prospect's first name, their company or organization name, and one sentence that demonstrates you know what kind of event they run. Generic outreach gets deleted. One specific detail — a recent event, a program name, the type of work their organization does — raises reply rates meaningfully.

### Sender Best Practices

- Send from a personal inbox, not a marketing alias (e.g., `firstname@oev.com`, not `hello@oev.com`)
- Subject lines should be lowercase — formal capitalization signals mass email
- Keep emails under 150 words. Prospects skim; earn the meeting before explaining the venue
- One ask per email. Multiple CTAs split attention and reduce replies
- If using a sequencing tool (Apollo, Instantly, Lemlist), set reply-detection to stop the sequence immediately when someone responds

## 4. Email Sequences

### Segment A — Corporate Clients

Use for: In-house corporate event organizers, operations leads, executive assistants, HR teams booking company events, offsites, workshops, or product launches.

#### Email 1 · Initial Outreach

**Subject:** your next [Company] event — without the execution risk

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Most venue conversations start with photos and square footage. That is not what OEV leads with.
>
> OEV is built for corporate hosts who need the event to go right — not just look good in a pitch deck. That means: online booking and payment in a single flow, AV handled through a production package so you are not coordinating vendors the week of, and a deposit-first process that locks the date the moment you decide.
>
> No manual back-and-forth. No day-of surprises. Clear rules from the first confirmation email.
>
> If you have a corporate event coming up in [City] in the next 60–90 days, I would like to show you the booking flow — it takes about 10 minutes and you will know exactly what you are getting.

**CTA:** Reply to this email or book a 10-minute call: [Calendar Link]

#### Email 2 · Follow-up — Day 4

**Subject:** re: [Company] events

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Quick follow-up on my note from last week.
>
> One detail I should have led with: bar service is coordinated through OEV. Your team picks a package — we handle the vendor. That is one less vendor relationship to manage for the event.
>
> If you are planning anything for Q[X] and want to compare options, I am happy to walk you through what OEV handles so you can make a fair comparison.

**CTA:** 15 minutes this week or next? [Calendar Link]

#### Email 3 · Final Follow-up — Day 9

**Subject:** last note on this

> Hi [First Name],
>
> I will keep this short — last note from me on this.
>
> If the timing is not right or you already have a venue locked in, no problem at all. If an event comes up later and you want a venue where the logistics are handled upfront, OEV is worth a look.
>
> Either way, good luck with whatever is on your calendar.

**CTA:** One-line reply is enough — happy to resurface this when the timing is better.

## 6. Recommended Tooling

For **low volume** (under 50 outreach contacts per week): manage sequences manually in Gmail or Outlook using a simple spreadsheet to track status. The follow-up discipline is more important than the platform.

For **higher volume or automated follow-up**: the following tools support sequence automation with reply-detection, meaning the sequence stops automatically when a prospect replies.

- **Apollo.io** — includes prospecting database + outreach sequencing in one platform. Good starting point.
- **Instantly** — strong deliverability tooling, good for warming new sending domains.
- **Lemlist** — best for personalization at scale; supports image and video personalization.
- **Close CRM** — if OEV eventually wants a full sales CRM, Close handles sequences natively without a separate tool.

### Deliverability Notes

- Do not send more than 50 cold emails per day from a new domain.
- Warm the sending domain for 2–4 weeks before launching sequences at volume.
- Use a subdomain for cold outreach (e.g., `team.oev.com`) to protect the main domain reputation.
- Plain-text emails outperform HTML-heavy templates in cold outreach. Keep formatting minimal.
