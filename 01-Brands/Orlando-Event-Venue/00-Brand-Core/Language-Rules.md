# OEV Language Rules

## Parent
- [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/00-Brand-Core/Brand-Home|Orlando Event Venue - Brand Home]]

## Related

## Rules
- Say what the next step is.
- State restrictions cleanly, not emotionally.
- Use concrete numbers when useful.
- Prefer clarity over persuasion tricks.
- Do not hide mandatory fees or requirements.
- Use "beer & wine only" exactly and consistently until policy changes.
- Use "50% deposit to hold the date" consistently until reconciled otherwise.
- Say "up to 90 guests" consistently.
- When talking about production, present it as optional support or required vehicle depending on the ask.

## Words To Favor
professional, smooth, clear, flexible, supported, compliant, straightforward, optional, held, confirmed, included.

## Words To Avoid
cheap, party room, luxury destination, stress-free magic, all-inclusive fantasy language, no-rules language.

### 2026-05-20 — Canonical language rules from Luis × Non-Profit Inquiry call

Source: [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/04-Training/Source-Calls/2026-05-20_Luis_NonProfit_Inquiry|Source Call 2026-05-20]]

These rules are non-negotiable. Reps train against them. Claude applies them when drafting any OEV customer-facing communication.

#### Rule 1: Never say "deposit"

OEV does not charge deposits. The 50% upfront is **holding the date.**

**Banned:** deposit, non-refundable deposit, put down a deposit, your deposit, deposit required.

**Use instead:**
- "50% holds the date"
- "Half now, half later"
- "Pay half to lock it in"
- "50% to lock"

**Why:** the word "deposit" implies distrust. OEV's positioning is community + trust. The 50/50 split is a logistical split, not collateral. This is a deliberate brand voice choice.

#### Rule 2: Alcohol policy = identity-framed, not policy-framed

When explaining the no-outside-alcohol rule, the canonical frame is **the non-profit identity**, not a venue policy.

**Banned:**
- "We don't allow alcohol" (incomplete, and customers hear it as a wall)
- "Our policy is no outside alcohol"
- "Sorry, we can't allow it"
- "It's the rules"

**Use instead:**
- "Because we are a non-profit, we're strict on alcohol — we have to stand by our principles and our structure"
- "Beer or wine has to run through our bar service so it's done a specific way"
- "Non-alcoholic — water, sodas — totally fine"

**Why:** the identity frame is the truth (OEV operates as a non-profit) and it gives the customer a reason that respects them. The policy frame feels arbitrary and triggers debate.

#### Rule 3: Catering = zero restrictions (say it proactively)

When the topic of food comes up, surface the policy actively as a differentiator.

**Use:**
- "We have zero restrictions on catering"
- "You can bring any caterer"
- "Bring whatever food you want"

**Why:** customers expect venue restrictions. Saying it proactively builds trust and differentiates OEV.

#### Rule 4: Identity framing for OEV

When introducing OEV on a call or email (especially to mission-aligned or non-profit customers), lead with identity:

**Use:**
- "We're a non-profit ourselves"
- "We operate for non-profits"
- "We do a lot of community work — women's groups during the week, community gatherings, local Orlando work"

**Why:** customers buy from organizations whose identity they recognize. Identity framing first changes the entire conversation tone.

#### Rule 5: Production = transparent cost pass-through

When explaining the production package fee, frame as paying the producer.

**Use:**
- "It's hourly because we have to pay the actual producer"
- "Not because I want to charge you — that's our cost"

**Don't:**
- Frame as an upsell
- Use marketing language ("amazing production team")
- Bury the cost reasoning

#### Rule 6: Use customer titles and identity respectfully

If a customer introduces themselves with a title (Doctor, Pastor, Coach, Founder, Prophet), use it back.

**Use:** "How's it going, doctor?" / "What other questions, prophet?" / "Coach, here's how we can do this."

**Don't downgrade** to a first name unless the customer invites it.

#### Rule 7: Micro-permission language

OEV reps give customers verbal permission, not approval to ask for permission.

**Use:**
- "Feel free."
- "You guys can."
- "We're good with that."
- "Anything I can do to lower the cost, do it."
- "Sure, that's fine."

**Why:** flips the dynamic from "asking the venue's permission" to "the venue helping you."

#### Rule 8: Urgency = real scarcity only

Never manufacture urgency. Only surface real calendar truth.

**Use:**
- "We're booked through August — those dates are still open."
- "All those dates are available right now."

**Banned:** "This won't last," "Dates are going fast," "I can't hold this much longer" — unless they are literally true at the moment of saying them.

#### Rule 9: Closing line matches customer's frame

Match their energy. Don't force one voice across all customers.

| Customer type | Close style |
|---|---|
| Religious / mission-aligned | "I love the heart you have for [community/this work]. Amen." |
| Corporate | "Let's lock it in. I'll send the link." |
| Community / personal | "We've got you. Book online when you're ready." |
| Non-religious mission | "Excited to host this. Let's get it on the books." |

#### Rule 10: Never apologize for things that aren't problems

The alcohol policy, the 50% structure, the cleaning fee — none of these are problems to apologize for. Frame them as facts inside the OEV identity.

**Banned:** "Sorry, but..." / "Unfortunately, our..." / "I wish we could but..."

---

#### How Claude applies these rules

When drafting OEV customer-facing communication:
1. Scan the draft for any banned word/phrase from this list.
2. Replace with the canonical alternative.
3. Apply identity framing where relevant.
4. Match closing to the customer's type.

When in doubt about a phrase that isn't on this list, default to **the host voice**: clear, respectful, operational, community-aligned.
