## Purpose
This is the canonical guide for OEV text messages and emails written by sales reps (not transactional automation). It carries the **host voice** from the call into the inbox and the phone.

For transactional system emails (booking confirmed, payment received, reminders), see [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/02-Communication/OEV-Communication-Manual|Communication Manual]]. That manual handles automation. **This note handles human-written sales communication.**

---

## The Core Principle: Carry the Call Voice Into Writing

The voice that wins on the phone is the voice that wins in text and email.

A customer who just talked to OEV and felt **hosted, not pitched** should feel the same thing when they open the follow-up.

If the email reads colder than the call, the customer drops 20%.

---

## Voice Translation: Call → Text → Email

| Pattern on the call | How it shows up in text | How it shows up in email |
|---|---|---|
| Host posture | Conversational, no formality | Warm subject line + clean structure |
| Live-quote on website | Send the website link with the date pre-filled | Embed the total + breakdown in the email |
| Identity framing | "We're a non-profit too, here's what we can do" | Lead the email with one line on who OEV is |
| Reframe "deposit" | "50% holds your date" | "50% to lock the date — half now, half before" |
| Soft-sell production | "Producer runs $89/hr — that's our cost" | Itemize production add-on cleanly |
| Micro-permissions | "Feel free to..." | "You're welcome to..." |
| Real urgency | "Booked through August, those dates still open" | Same line in email, with date stamp |
| Mirror values | Match their energy in 1–2 words | One warm closing line that reflects their mission |
| Close warm | "Got you. Send the link your way now." | "Excited to host this — link below whenever you're ready" |

## Text (SMS) Rules

### Core
- **Open with their first name.** No exceptions on direct outreach.
- **One ask per text.** If there are two, send two texts (spaced) or use email.
- **Under 160 characters when possible.** Long texts feel like a wall.
- **No marketing language.** No "Excited to share!" or "Don't miss out."
- **Include the link if action is needed.** Don't make them dig.
- **Sign off with your name** on the first contact.

### Structure
`Hi [First Name] — [what happened / why I'm reaching out]. [what to do next]. [how to reach you].`

### When to use SMS
- Within 5 minutes of a call (recap + link)
- Same-day follow-up after a tour
- Time-sensitive availability check
- Payment link resend
- Reminder of an action they said they'd take

### When NOT to use SMS
- Long quotes or breakdowns → use email
- Multi-day quote details → use email
- First-touch outbound prospecting → use email
- Sensitive policy explanations → use email

### Things to Avoid in Text
- "Just following up" → say what you're following up on
- "Hope you're well!" → skip the filler
- Two questions in one text
- All-caps anywhere
- Emojis (unless customer used them first)
- "Per our conversation..." → too corporate

## Email Rules

### Core
- **Subject line states the thing.** No clever lines, no clickbait.
- **Open with the useful thing, not filler.** Their name + the next sentence should answer "why am I getting this?"
- **One main action per email.** Booking link OR tour confirm OR quote — not three.
- **Cleanly structured.** Use sub-headers or bullets for any list over 3 items.
- **Close with a real next step.** Not "let me know if you have questions" — name what's next.
- **Standard signature.** Use the canonical block from [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/02-Communication/OEV-Communication-Manual#Standard Signature Block|Communication Manual]].

### Subject Line Patterns
For sales rep-written emails (not transactional automation):

- `[Their first name] — your venue quote` (post-call follow-up)
- `Your tour at OEV on [date]` (tour confirmation rep-written)
- `Quick check on your [event type] date` (warm follow-up)
- `Holding [date] at OEV — your link` (sending the booking link)
- `Re: [original subject]` (replying to their inquiry)

For transactional emails (booking confirmed, payment received, reminders), see [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/02-Communication/OEV-Communication-Manual#Subject Line System|Communication Manual Subject Line System]].

### Structure
```
Subject: [clear, specific]

Hi [First Name],

[One-sentence reason for the email. What happened, what's next.]

[Body — quote / breakdown / details / answer to their question.]

[Clear next step. What you want them to do.]

[Warm but operational close. Match the call energy if there was one.]

[Signature block.]
```

### When to use Email
- Quote breakdowns
- Multi-day pricing summaries
- Non-profit discount offers
- Tour confirmations with logistics
- Anything they need to keep for records
- Anything over ~3 lines of content
- First-touch outbound

### Things to Avoid in Email
- "Just reaching out..." → say why directly
- "I hope this finds you well" → unnecessary
- "Per our conversation..." → too cold
- "As discussed..." → too cold
- Long paragraphs without breaks
- Multiple CTAs ("Book here OR call us OR email us OR DM us")
- Repeated subject + preview text
- Generic "Best regards" closes
- Sending access details before the 1-day mark

## Template Library

### 1. Post-Call Text (Send Within 5 Minutes)

```
Hi [First Name] — great talking just now. Sending your booking 
link here so you can lock in [date]: [link]. 50% holds the date. 
Anything you need, text or call me back.

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

**Variant for live-quoted total:**
```
Hi [First Name] — recapping: [event type] on [date], total comes 
to $[total] with [add-ons]. Booking link: [link]. 50% holds the 
date. Here whenever you're ready.

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

---

### 2. Post-Call Email (Send Within 1 Hour)

```
Subject: [First Name] — your OEV quote for [date]

Hi [First Name],

Recap from our call. Here's your quote for [event type] on [date]:

QUOTE
Venue rental:    $[amount] ([daily / X hours @ $139/hr])
Cleaning fee:    $199
[Production package, if relevant]: $[amount]
[Bar service, if relevant]: $[amount]
[Setup & breakdown, if relevant]: $199
[Tablecloths, if relevant]: $[amount]
[Discount, if applied]: -$[amount]
                ─────────
TOTAL:           $[total]

50% holds the date. Half now, half before the event.

[Insert any non-pricing context the customer asked about — 
catering, alcohol policy, outdoor patio status, etc.]

Book here when you're ready: [booking link]

Anything you need, I'm here.

[Signature block]
```

```
Subject: [First Name] — non-profit option for [date]

Hi [First Name],

letter / EIN documentation] and I'll get the code on your booking 
before you pay.

[Warm close — mirror their mission.]

[Signature block]
```

### 5. Warm Follow-Up (Customer Went Quiet)

For a customer who got a quote but hasn't booked in 2–4 days.

**Text version:**
```
Hi [First Name] — circling back on [date] for your [event type]. 
Date's still open. Anything I can clear up to help you decide? 

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

**Email version:**
```
Subject: Quick check on your [date] at OEV

Hi [First Name],

Following up on your [event type] inquiry. [Date] is still 
available, and your quote is still good.

If something's on the fence, tell me — I'd rather help you decide 
than have you wonder.

If you're ready to lock it in: [booking link]

[Signature block]
```

---

### 6. Soft Urgency (When Calendar Is Tight)

Only use when scarcity is real. Cross-check the calendar.

**Text:**
```
Hi [First Name] — heads up, we're booked through [month]. 
[Date] is still open but moving. Want me to hold it for you?

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

**Email:**
```
Subject: [First Name] — calendar update for [date]

Hi [First Name],

Quick update — we're booked through [month] right now, and 
[date] is one of the last open dates in that window.

If you're ready to move on it: [booking link]

If you need a couple more days to decide, no pressure — just 
wanted you to know where the calendar stands.

[Signature block]
```

### 7. Connect-Call Setup (Founder Loop-In)

When the customer needs Luis on the line.

**Text:**
```
Hi [First Name] — going to loop Luis in so he can answer your 
[multi-day / partnership / sensitive] question directly. Best 
time today or tomorrow?

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

**Email:**
```
Subject: Looping in Luis on your [event] question

Hi [First Name],

For [the multi-day setup / the partnership question / the 
specifics on X], I want to bring Luis in directly. He'll be 
able to give you the clearest answer.

When works for a quick call today or tomorrow?

[Signature block]
```

### 8. Tour Follow-Up (Sales Rep Written)

After an in-person or remote tour. Different from transactional automation.

**Text (same day):**
```
Hi [First Name] — thanks for touring today. Anything come up 
after you left? Booking link is here when you're ready: [link]

— [Rep Name], OEV
```

**Email (next day):**
```
Subject: [First Name] — about your tour at OEV

Hi [First Name],

Thanks for coming through yesterday. [One specific thing you 
remember from the tour — what they liked, what they asked about, 
who they were with.]

If you want to move forward on [date]: [booking link]

If you're still weighing it, tell me what's on your mind — happy 
to talk it through.

[Signature block]
```

---

### 9. Decline / Escalation Holding Pattern

When a customer asks something a rep should not decide alone.

```
Subject: Quick hold on your [event type] question

Hi [First Name],

I want to give you a clean answer on [specific question], so let 
me confirm a couple of details with Luis. I'll come back to you 
within 24 hours.

Talk soon,
[Signature block]
```

**Rule:** never use this template to stall a normal booking. Only when there's an actual escalation.

## Audience Tuning

The voice doesn't change. The vocabulary does.

### Non-Profit / Mission-Aligned
- Identity-frame OEV early ("we're a non-profit too")
- Mention the $199 cleaning-fee-waiver as a real offer
- Mirror mission language ("the work you're doing matters")
- Warm closes are appropriate

### Corporate
- Skip the mission framing
- Lead with precision: dates, total, what's included
- Mention multi-day workshop discount if applicable
- Cleaner closes, less warmth

### Community / Personal (Birthday, Baby Shower, Family)
- Lighter touch, less formal
- Emphasize ease ("we make this easy")
- Setup & breakdown service is often the right add-on to mention
- Warm closes, casual

### Religious / Faith-Based
- Identity-frame OEV honestly (the venue serves faith-based work)
- Mirror their warmth without performing
- Closing line can match their energy
- Do not fake faith language if it isn't authentic

## What Claude Should Default To

When Claude is drafting an OEV text or email for a sales rep, default to:

1. **Match the call context.** If there was a recent call, mirror its tone.
2. **Use the host voice.** Not the agent voice. Not the marketer voice.
3. **Surface pricing transparently.** Never bury cost, never inflate add-ons.
4. **Reframe "deposit" → "50% to hold the date."** Always.
5. **Identity-frame the alcohol policy.** Never apologize for it.
6. **Default to email for anything over 3 lines of content.**
7. **Default to text for follow-ups within 24 hours of a real conversation.**

## Quality Bar

A strong OEV text or email should feel like:
- the rep remembers the customer
- the rep knows what stage the customer is in
- the rep is making the next step obvious
- the rep is reducing friction, not adding to it
- the rep is hosting, not selling

If any of those is missing, the message needs review before sending.
