# OEV Sales Onboarding System — Training Intelligence v1

Built from the uploaded onboarding transcript between Luis/Trellis Fields and Sebastian.

---

## 1. Core Teaching Points

### A. OEV sales is not "hard selling"

The salesperson's job is to help interested people finish the booking process. The tone should feel natural, familiar, and helpful.

**Core idea:**
"We're not selling insurance. We're helping people book a venue if it fits their event."

The salesperson should:

* Answer quickly.
* Check availability first.
* Guide the person through the website.
* Explain pricing clearly.
* Help them book online.
* Use discounts strategically.
* Follow up through Go High Level.
* Tag the customer correctly for commission tracking.

---

### B. First filter: availability

Before giving a full explanation, check the date and time.

**Rule:**
Do not waste time selling a date that is already unavailable.

If unavailable, offer alternatives:

> "Unfortunately, we don't have that exact time available, but we may be able to do Friday, Sunday, or a slightly different time on Saturday."

---

### C. Second filter: send them to the website

Luis emphasized this as a key sales behavior.

**Key question:**

> "Have you been to our website yet?"

Then guide them live:

1. Pricing section.
2. Hourly vs. full-day booking.
3. Production packages.
4. Bar services.
5. Gallery / tour option.
6. Book Now button.

The website becomes the salesperson's visual sales tool.

---

### D. OEV pricing basics

From the transcript:

* Hourly rental: **$139/hour**
* Minimum booking: **4 hours**
* Full-day special: **$899**
* Included: **90 chairs and tables, prep kitchen, two bathrooms, main venue space**
* Production packages exist as add-ons.
* Bar service can be added.
* Setup/breakdown can be added as a service.
* Customer setup and breakdown happen inside the booking window unless they purchase setup service.

**Needs clarification:** exact current pricing for every production package and bar package should be confirmed in the OEV website/admin before training new reps.

---

### E. Production packages are revenue opportunities

Production is not just operational. It increases the ticket size.

The trainee should learn to ask:

> "Will you need microphones, speakers, screen support, or any kind of presentation setup?"

If yes, walk them through production packages.

---

### F. Bar service / alcohol rule is strict

Outside alcohol should not be allowed unless managed through the approved vendor/staff process. The transcript explains that beer/wine must be served through the approved vendor because of venue/nonprofit rules, and water/soda/non-alcoholic drinks are fine.

**Training point:**
Do not debate the policy. Explain it calmly and redirect to the approved bar service option.

---

### G. Discounts are a closing tool

Discounts should feel personal and helpful, not automatic.

Transcript guidance:

* Basic offer can be around **$100 off**.
* Multi-day or larger bookings may receive up to **20% off**.
* Do not exceed 20%.
* Fixed dollar discounts can feel better than percentages.

Example:

> "I can help you out with a discount code for this booking. Use GRAD20 and it'll take $150 off your reservation."

**Needs clarification:** create official discount approval rules by booking size.

---

### H. Admin panel controls the booking workflow

The trainee must learn:

* How to search bookings.
* How to view customer details.
* How to identify paid vs. partially paid.
* How to send payment links.
* How to move completed events to post-event.
* How to cancel if needed.
* How to assign staff/cleaning.
* How to check event details and add-ons.

Transcript note: OEV does not treat the first payment as a separate "deposit." The customer pays half now and the remaining half later.

---

### I. Go High Level is the sales communication hub

Go High Level handles:

* Calls.
* Missed calls.
* Texts.
* Emails.
* Conversation history.
* AI transcriptions.
* AI-drafted replies.
* Customer tagging.
* Commission tracking.

The transcript says the Go High Level mobile app is where calls come in, and if setup disconnects, the rep must reconnect inbound calls in settings.

---

### J. Every handled customer must be tagged

If Sebastian handles a customer, he tags the customer with his name. That tag is later used to calculate commission.

**Rule:**
No tag = hard to track commission.

---

### K. AI is a support layer, not a replacement for sales judgment

Claude + Obsidian can help with:

* Drafting emails.
* Answering policy questions.
* Explaining setup/breakdown.
* Prospecting lists.
* Finding companies like clinical research groups, realtors, speakers, workshops, etc.
* Creating outreach campaigns.

But the human still needs to:

* Review the answer.
* Make judgment calls.
* Confirm policy.
* Escalate unclear situations.

## 3. OEV Sales Onboarding Curriculum

### Module 1: OEV Sales Role and Revenue Goal

**Objective:** Understand the role: answer leads, guide bookings, increase revenue.

**Must learn:**

* OEV sales is service-based.
* The rep helps customers book.
* Revenue comes from venue rental, production, bar services, add-ons, and multi-day bookings.

**Workflow:**

1. Answer inquiry.
2. Confirm event type/date/time.
3. Check availability.
4. Walk through website.
5. Explain options.
6. Help customer book.
7. Follow up.
8. Tag customer.

**Common mistakes:**

* Talking too much before checking availability.
* Forgetting to tag customer.
* Not upselling production/bar when relevant.

**Practice:** Roleplay a baby shower inquiry.

**Checklist:**

* Can explain role.
* Can describe revenue opportunities.
* Can identify when to upsell.

---

### Module 2: Required Tools and Logins

**Objective:** Get the trainee fully operational.

**Must learn:**

* ClickUp = credentials and internal docs.
* OEV Chrome profile = customer/admin communication.
* Ministries Chrome profile = calendar/Pure Space.
* Go High Level = calls/texts/emails.
* Claude/Obsidian = knowledge and drafting.
* Whisper = faster dictation.
* Camera/AC apps = venue access support.

**Workflow:**

1. Log into ClickUp from personal Chrome.
2. Set up Trellis profile.
3. Set up OEV profile.
4. Set up Ministries profile.
5. Install Go High Level mobile.
6. Install Google Authenticator.
7. Install Claude, Whisper, Obsidian/Cursor if required.
8. Confirm all logins on laptop and phone.

**Common mistakes:**

* Opening ClickUp inside the wrong Chrome profile.
* Looking for OEV credentials inside Trellis instead of OEV profile.
* Forgetting phone setup.

**Practice:** Trainee explains which account they would use for each task.

**Checklist:**

* Laptop access complete.
* Phone access complete.
* GHL calls active.
* Calendar visible.
* Admin panel accessible.

---

### Module 3: Calendar and Availability

**Objective:** Learn to check and manage availability.

**Must learn:**

* OEV and Ministries calendars are connected.
* Edits happen through Ministries.
* Website blocks unavailable dates.
* Internal events may sometimes be moved, but only with approval.

**Workflow:**

1. Ask customer for date and time.
2. Open calendar.
3. Check if paid booking, internal event, or partial conflict.
4. If available, continue sales process.
5. If unavailable, offer alternatives.
6. If big opportunity conflicts with internal event, escalate.

**Common mistakes:**

* Assuming a date is free without checking time blocks.
* Deleting internal events without approval.
* Not offering nearby alternatives.

**Practice:** Review five dates and classify: available, booked, partial conflict, internal event, needs escalation.

**Checklist:**

* Can read calendar.
* Can identify paid vs. internal events.
* Can offer alternatives.

---

### Module 4: Customer Call Flow

**Objective:** Handle incoming calls confidently.

**Must learn:**

* Friendly opening.
* Gather key info.
* Check availability.
* Move to website walkthrough.
* Close toward booking.

**Workflow:**

1. "Hi, this is Sebastian with Orlando Event Venue. How can I help you?"
2. Ask: event type, date, time, guest count.
3. Check calendar.
4. Ask: "Have you been to our website yet?"
5. Walk through pricing and add-ons.
6. Ask what they need.
7. Send booking/payment link if needed.
8. Tag customer in GHL.

**Common mistakes:**

* Not capturing event date/time.
* Not checking calendar.
* Not asking website question.
* Forgetting follow-up.

**Practice:** Simulated call: birthday party for 75 guests.

**Checklist:**

* Opens call professionally.
* Gets required info.
* Checks calendar.
* Guides to next step.

---

### Module 5: Pricing and Website Walkthrough

**Objective:** Use the website to explain OEV clearly.

**Must learn:**

* Hourly vs. full-day.
* Four-hour minimum.
* Included items.
* Add-ons.
* Production packages.
* Bar services.
* Booking button.

**Workflow:**

1. Ask customer to open website.
2. Guide them to pricing.
3. Explain hourly rate.
4. Explain full-day option.
5. Explain what is included.
6. Show add-ons and packages.
7. Help them select booking option.

**Common mistakes:**

* Explaining everything from memory.
* Forgetting to mention included tables/chairs.
* Not identifying whether full-day is better.

**Practice:** Customer wants 6 hours; trainee explains hourly vs. daily.

**Checklist:**

* Can explain $139/hour.
* Can explain $899 full day.
* Can explain four-hour minimum.
* Can explain included items.

---

### Module 6: Booking Admin Panel

**Objective:** Navigate booking records.

**Must learn:**

* Search bookings.
* View customer information.
* View payment status.
* View add-ons.
* Send payment links.
* Move statuses.
* Schedule cleaning/staff.

**Workflow:**

1. Open OEV admin.
2. Search customer name.
3. Open booking details.
4. Review event info.
5. Confirm payment status.
6. Check add-ons.
7. Assign cleaning/staff if needed.
8. Move event status after completion.

**Common mistakes:**

* Confusing half-payment with deposit.
* Forgetting to schedule cleaning.
* Not checking add-ons before answering customer questions.

**Practice:** Find a booking and explain what the customer paid for.

**Checklist:**

* Can search booking.
* Can explain payment status.
* Can identify add-ons.
* Can assign staff/cleaning.

---

### Module 7: Discounts and Closing Strategy

**Objective:** Use discounts to help close bookings.

**Must learn:**

* Discounts should feel helpful.
* $100 off can be used as a simple incentive.
* Up to 20% may be used for larger/multi-day bookings.
* Fixed amount can feel stronger than percentage.
* Do not over-discount.

**Workflow:**

1. Confirm customer is serious.
2. Choose discount type: fixed or percentage.
3. Create code in admin.
4. Apply to correct booking type.
5. Give code to customer.
6. Encourage booking.

**Common mistakes:**

* Giving discount too early.
* Exceeding 20%.
* Creating wrong discount type.
* Forgetting to delete test codes.

**Practice:** Create a mock graduation discount.

**Checklist:**

* Can create discount code.
* Knows max discount.
* Knows when to escalate.

---

### Module 8: Bar Service and Alcohol Policy

**Objective:** Explain alcohol rules without creating conflict.

**Must learn:**

* Outside alcohol is not allowed.
* Beer/wine must go through approved vendor/staff.
* Non-alcoholic drinks are fine.
* Food/catering does not require the same restriction, based on transcript.
* Customer may misunderstand or claim prior approval.

**Workflow:**

1. Check if booking indicates alcohol.
2. Confirm if they plan beer/wine.
3. Explain approved vendor rule.
4. Show bar service on website.
5. If they decline, confirm no alcohol will be brought.
6. Document/confirm in writing when needed.

**Common mistakes:**

* Saying "sure, beer is fine" without explaining vendor requirement.
* Debating with customer.
* Not documenting clarification.
* Letting customer bring alcohol outside the process.

**Practice:** Customer says, "Luis told me I could bring beer."

**Checklist:**

* Can explain policy.
* Can redirect to bar service.
* Can stay calm.

---

### Module 9: Tours and Venue Access

**Objective:** Manage venue tours.

**Must learn:**

* Tours usually happen Monday–Friday after 5 PM.
* Some days have recurring internal events.
* Tours can be remote.
* Rep can walk guest through access by phone.
* Camera access may help monitor tour experience.

**Workflow:**

1. Confirm availability for tour.
2. Schedule tour.
3. Give access instructions.
4. Stay on call while they enter.
5. Let them walk through.
6. Follow up after tour.
7. Move toward booking.

**Common mistakes:**

* Scheduling tour during booked event.
* Not following up.
* Not confirming venue access.

**Practice:** Schedule and guide a mock remote tour.

**Checklist:**

* Can schedule tour.
* Can explain access.
* Can follow up.

---

**Must learn:**

* Calls, texts, emails, and missed calls live in GHL.

**Practice:** Respond to a missed call using a draft.

**Checklist:**

* Can receive calls.
* Can send text/email.
* Can review AI draft.
* Can tag customer.

**Objective:** Use AI to support sales and operations.

**Must learn:**

* Claude drafts emails.
* Obsidian stores OEV knowledge.

**Checklist:**

* Can identify prospect type.
* Can draft message.
* Can track outreach.

### Module 13: Commission Tracking

**Objective:** Make sure sales activity is trackable.

**Must learn:**

* Customer must be tagged in GHL.
* Tags identify who handled the sale.
* Payments are reviewed later to calculate commission.
* Commission applies to customer payment handled/closed by rep.

**Workflow:**

1. Open customer profile.
2. Go to Actions.
3. Add tag with rep name.
4. Save.
5. Confirm tag appears.
6. Check booking/payment later.

**Common mistakes:**

* Forgetting to tag.
* Tagging wrong customer.
* Tagging before meaningful interaction.

**Practice:** Tag three mock customers correctly.

**Checklist:**

* Can tag customer.
* Knows why tagging matters.
* Can explain commission tracking.

---

### Module 14: Red Flags, Cancellations, and Escalations

**Objective:** Know when not to decide alone.

**Must learn:**
Escalate when:

* Event conflicts with internal calendar.
* Customer requests alcohol outside vendor process.
* Customer disputes prior communication.
* Event type may conflict with venue/nonprofit policy.
* Cancellation/refund situation is unclear.
* Large discount request.
* Multi-day corporate booking needs internal event moved.

**Important legal/policy note:**
The transcript mentions moral/religious restrictions. This should not be turned into a casual sales script. OEV should create a legally reviewed policy and train reps to escalate unclear event-type questions instead of making independent decisions.

**Workflow:**

1. Pause.
2. Do not approve or deny impulsively.
3. Ask Luis or approved manager.
4. Use neutral language with customer.
5. Document decision.

**Common mistakes:**

* Giving a direct denial without review.
* Saying the wrong reason.
* Making exceptions to alcohol policy.

**Practice:** Customer asks to host controversial event. Trainee writes escalation message to Luis.

**Checklist:**

* Knows escalation triggers.
* Uses neutral language.
* Does not make legal/policy decisions alone.

# 4. Scripts and Talk Tracks

## Incoming Call Opening

> Hi, this is Sebastian with Orlando Event Venue. How can I help you?

---

## Checking Availability

> Perfect. What date and time are you looking for, and what type of event are you planning?

> Let me check the calendar real quick to confirm availability.

---

## If Available

> Good news, we do have availability for that date and time. Have you already had a chance to visit our website?

---

## If Unavailable

> Unfortunately, we don't have that exact time available. But we may be able to do another time that day, or we can check Friday or Sunday if you're flexible.

---

## Asking About Website

> Have you been to our website yet? It's usually easier if we look at it together because the pricing, packages, and add-ons are all there.

---

## Website Walkthrough

> Once you're on the website, scroll to the pricing section. You'll see the hourly option and the full-day option. Then below that, you'll see the production packages and bar service options.

---

## Hourly vs. Full-Day

> The hourly rate is $139 per hour with a four-hour minimum. If you need the venue for most of the day, the full-day special is usually better because it gives you access for the day at $899.

---

## What's Included

> The venue includes the main space, 90 chairs and tables, a prep kitchen, and two bathrooms. Setup and breakdown need to happen inside your booking window unless you add the setup service.

---

## Production Packages

> We also have production packages if you need microphones, speakers, projectors, LED wall, streaming, recording, or a tech assistant. Depending on what your event needs, we can help you choose the right package.

---

## Bar Service

> We also offer bar service as an add-on. If your event will include beer or wine, that has to go through our approved vendor/staff process.

---

## No Outside Alcohol Rule

> Because the venue is connected to a nonprofit, we have specific rules around alcohol. Beer or wine is allowed only when it is served through our approved vendor/staff. Guests can bring water, sodas, and non-alcoholic drinks, but outside alcohol is not allowed.

---

## Handling "I Thought I Could Bring Beer"

> I understand the confusion. To clarify, beer and wine can be part of the event, but they have to be served through our approved vendor/staff. That helps us stay compliant and avoid issues during the event. If you prefer not to use the bar service, that's completely fine — you can still bring non-alcoholic drinks.

---

## Offering a Discount

> Since you're looking to book soon, I can help you with a discount code for the reservation. Use this code at checkout and it'll take $100 off.

For larger/multi-day bookings:

> Since this is a multi-day booking, I can check if we can apply a larger discount. We can usually help a bit more on those bigger reservations.

---

## Moving Toward Booking

> The easiest next step is to complete the booking online. Once you select the date, time, and any add-ons, the system will show your total and allow you to reserve it.

---

## Follow-Up After Missed Call

> Hi, this is Sebastian from Orlando Event Venue. I saw we missed your call and wanted to follow up. Are you looking for availability for a specific event date?

---

## Asking for Final Payment

> Hi [Name], I'm reaching out about your upcoming event on [Date]. Your remaining balance is ready to be paid, so I'm sending you the payment link now. The link may expire after a couple of days, so if that happens just let me know and I can resend it.

---

## Setup/Breakdown

> Setup and breakdown are included inside your booked time. That means your booking window should cover your setup, event time, and breakdown. If you'd rather not handle that yourself, you can add the setup/breakdown service.

---

## Tour Script

> We can schedule a tour for you. Most tours happen during weekday afternoons or evenings depending on availability. Some tours can be done remotely, where we guide you through accessing the venue and you walk through the space while we stay available by phone.

### New Module 15 — No-Deposit Language Rule

**Objective:** Train reps to never use the word "deposit."

**Must learn:**
- OEV does not charge deposits. The 50% upfront is "holding the date."
- Reframe is intentional — positions OEV as community/trust-based.
- The structure is the same (50% upfront, 50% before event) — only the language changes.

**Customer-facing language:**
- ✅ "50% holds your date"
- ✅ "Half now, half later"
- ✅ "Pay half to lock it in"
- ❌ "Deposit" in any form

**Reasoning (from Luis):**
> "It's not really a deposit — we don't charge deposits. We mainly work with community and trusted individuals and organizations."

**Common mistake:** mirroring the customer when they say "do I need to pay a deposit?" Instead, reframe: "It's not really a deposit. The 50% just locks your date."
