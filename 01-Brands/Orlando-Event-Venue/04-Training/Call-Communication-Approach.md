## Purpose
This is the canonical pattern for how OEV reps talk on inbound and outbound calls. It is built from real Luis Torres calls and reflects the actual closing posture that wins multi-day, mission-aligned, and corporate bookings. New reps train against this until it becomes muscle memory. Claude uses this to draft call notes, follow-ups, and roleplay scripts.

---

## The Core Posture: Host, Not Salesperson

OEV reps are hosting the customer through a decision. The venue exists. The customer is interested. The job is to make the next step easy.

**The customer should feel:**
- talked with, not talked at
- helped, not pitched
- trusted, not qualified
- respected, not processed

**Operator rule:** if at any point on the call you sound like you're trying to "close them," you've left the posture. The close happens because the booking makes sense, not because you pushed.

---

## The Call Flow (Inbound)

1. **Open warm.** First name, calm tone, no script feel.
2. **Get the basics.** Event type, date, time, guest count, day(s).
3. **Check availability live.** Don't pitch unavailable dates.
4. **Use the website as the visual.** Ask "have you been to our website?" — guide them through it together.
5. **Live-quote.** Book a random date in the system to surface their actual total. Show, don't describe.
6. **Stack the relevant add-ons.** Production if they need AV. Bar if alcohol. Setup if they don't want to manage it.
7. **Identity framing when it fits.** Non-profit, mission, community, corporate — match the frame.
8. **Surface real urgency.** Calendar truth, not pressure.
9. **Give the next step clearly.** Book online, send the link, schedule a tour.
10. **Close warm.** Acknowledge who they are. Mirror their energy.

## The 12 Patterns That Define the OEV Call

### 1. Live-Quote on the Website
Don't explain pricing from memory. Don't read a list. **Open the website, pick a placeholder date, walk it to checkout, and read them the actual number on screen.**

> "What I'll do is — I'm going to our website right now. I'll pick a random date, book it like you would, and show you the total so you can see it. The number you see is for the daily."

**Why it works:** removes friction, kills "is that the real price?" doubt, makes the website feel like the source of truth.

---

### 2. Identity Framing First
Before pricing, before policy, name who OEV is in language that connects to who they are.

> "We operate for non-profits — we are a non-profit. We do a lot of community work. Women's groups during the week, community gatherings, local Orlando work."

**When to use:** mission-aligned, religious, community, non-profit, or corporate-with-purpose customers. Match the frame — don't over-extend.

**Rule:** identity framing is honest reflection of who OEV is. Don't bend it to flatter customers.

---

### 3. Address the Customer by Their Identity, Respectfully
Use the title, role, or honorific they carry. Doctor. Pastor. Coach. Founder.

> "How's it going, doctor?"
> "Okay, master prophet, what other questions you have?"

**Rule:** if they introduce themselves with a title, use it back. Don't downgrade them to a first name unless they invite it.

---

### 4. Reframe "Deposit" Away
The word "deposit" implies distrust. OEV does not take deposits. The customer pays 50% to **hold the date.**

> "The 50% is made through the website. It's not really a deposit — we don't charge deposits. We mainly work with community and trusted individuals and organizations. The 50% just locks your date."

**Never say:**
- "We need a deposit"
- "Put down a deposit"
- "Your deposit is..."

**Always say:**
- "50% holds your date"
- "Half upfront, half before the event"
- "Pay half to lock it in"

See [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/00-Brand-Core/Language-Rules|Language Rules]] for the canonical phrasing.

---

### 5. Soft-Sell Production as Cost Pass-Through
Production is not an upsell. It's the cost of bringing a producer on site.

> "If you need a producer running AV during the event, you'd select that package on the website. The producer handles it on the day of. You're paying hourly — not because I want to charge you, but because we have to pay the actual producer."

**Why it works:** removes salesy energy, makes the price feel fair, lets the customer choose.

---

### 6. Reference Real Events for Social Proof
Drop in concrete proof you're not making it up.

> "We're actually hosting a worship night this Friday, our first one for UCF college students. LED screen, full production, the works."

> "I did exactly this yesterday with another customer who was trying to figure out a multi-day."

**Rule:** must be real. Never fabricate. If you don't know the event, don't reference one — say "we host events like that regularly."

---

### 7. Catering: Zero Restrictions (Say It Proactively)
Customers expect venue restrictions on food. OEV doesn't have them. Surface this — it's a differentiator.

> "We have zero restrictions on catering. Bring any caterer, any food, any setup. You're free."

---

### 8. Alcohol: Identity-Framed, Not Apologetic
The alcohol policy is grounded in the non-profit identity. Don't apologize, don't debate.

> "Because we are a non-profit, we're strict on alcohol. We don't allow outside alcohol — we have to stand by our principles and our structure. If you want beer or wine, that runs through our bar service so it's done a specific way."

**Never say:**
- "We don't allow alcohol" (incomplete and wrong)
- "Sorry, we can't allow it"
- "It's the rules"

**Always say:**
- "We have to stand by our principles and our structure"
- "It has to run through our bar service so it's done a specific way"
- "Non-alcoholic — water, sodas — totally fine"

---

### 9. Mirror the Customer's Values (Without Performing)
If the customer is religious, hold space for that. If they're corporate, sharpen up. If they're family-focused, soften.

**Religious / mission-aligned:**
> "Amen, amen, absolutely."
> "I love the heart you have for community."
> "Stir up the creativity God's put in people."

**Corporate:**
> "Got it — let's make sure your team has what it needs."
> "We can lock that in cleanly for you."

**Community / personal:**
> "We've got you. Let's make this easy."

**Rule:** mirror what's authentic to you and to OEV. If you don't share the customer's faith framing, don't fake it — hold respectful neutrality.

---

### 10. Give Micro-Permissions
Verbal phrases that lower friction and make the customer feel in control.

> "Feel free."
> "You guys can."
> "Anything I can do to lower the cost, do it."
> "Sure, that's fine."
> "We're good with that."

**Why it works:** flips the dynamic from "asking permission of the venue" to "the venue helping you."

---

### 11. Real Urgency from Real Scarcity
Don't manufacture urgency. Surface real calendar truth.

> "We're booked through August. You guys still have time, but the closer we get the harder it gets."

> "All those dates are open right now on that weekend."

**Rule:** scarcity statements must be true at the moment they're said. Cross-check the calendar before quoting capacity.

**Stale date stamp:** the "booked through August" line was true as of 2026-05-20. Reps should refresh this monthly. Don't keep using stale scarcity.

---

### 12. The Connect-Call Play
For higher-stakes inquiries — multi-day, large-spend, mission-aligned, corporate — offer to bring the founder live onto the call.

> "You can call him and connect the call. I did that yesterday with another customer trying to figure out a multi-day."

**When to deploy:**
- Multi-day booking
- Inquiry over ~$2,000 total
- Customer asks for the decision-maker
- Mission-aligned or partnership-tone conversation
- Pricing-sensitive non-profit asking for help

**When NOT to deploy:**
- Simple single-day birthday party
- Quick tour scheduling
- Standard pricing question

## Operational Patterns

### Multi-Day Bookings
Each day is its own reservation in the system. Don't try to combine them.

> "If you want Thursday, Friday, and Saturday — those go in as three separate bookings. You'd do them individually. You'd have access for the full day on each one if you do the daily rate."

### Live-Quote Technique (Step-by-Step)
1. Tell the customer what you're about to do: "Let me show you the actual total."
2. Open the OEV website in front of you.
3. Pick a placeholder date close to theirs.
4. Build the booking — daily or hourly, plus their add-ons.
5. Walk them to the total in real time.
6. Say: "That number is what you'd pay for [the daily / X hours]."
7. Text or email them the breakdown after the call.

### Discount Conversation
- Offer the right discount for the right customer. Don't lead with discounts.
- Non-profit, weekend → $199 cleaning-fee-waiver framing.
- Non-profit, weekday → 50% off base rental (proof of status required).
- Multi-day workshop → 25% off base rental.
- Always frame as "what we can set up for you," not "I'll throw in a discount."

### Closing
Match their energy. If they were warm, be warm. If they were transactional, be precise.

**Mission-aligned close:**
> "I love the heart you have for community. We're glad to host this. Let's get you locked in."

**Corporate close:**
> "Let's lock it in. I'll send the link now. Reach out if anything comes up."

**Community / family close:**
> "We've got you. Book online when you're ready — I'm here if you need anything."

## Things to Say (Greatest Hits)

- "Have you been to our website yet?"
- "Let me show you the actual total."
- "We have no restrictions on catering."
- "We're a non-profit ourselves, so we operate around community."
- "It's not really a deposit — we don't charge deposits."
- "We have to stand by our principles and our structure."
- "Not because I want to charge you — we have to pay the actual producer."
- "Feel free."
- "Anything I can do to lower the cost, do it."
- "We're booked through August, but those dates are still open."
- "I love the heart you have for [community / your team / what you're building]."

---

## Things to Avoid

- "Deposit" (use "hold" or "50% to lock")
- "We don't allow alcohol" (incomplete and feels like a wall)
- "Our policy is..." (cold; use identity framing instead)
- "Let me check with my manager" before checking if it's a real escalation
- Reading pricing from memory when the website is right there
- Apologizing for the alcohol policy
- Manufactured urgency ("this date will go fast")
- Discounting before the customer asks or before establishing value

## Scenarios

### Multi-Day Non-Profit Inquiry
1. Open warm, get the days and event type.
2. Identity frame: "We're a non-profit ourselves."
3. Confirm availability live.
4. Live-quote the daily rate for each day.
5. Offer non-profit discount ($199 cleaning waiver or 50% Mon–Fri base, whichever fits).
6. Soft-sell production if they have a teaching/worship/AV element.
7. Catering: zero restrictions, proactive.
8. Alcohol: identity-framed.
9. Close warm, mirror their language.
10. Connect-call founder if the deal is large or mission-aligned.

### Quick Single-Day Birthday Party
1. Open warm, get the date and guest count.
2. Confirm availability.
3. Live-quote daily vs. hourly — recommend whichever is better.
4. Mention setup & breakdown service ($199) as an option.
5. No alcohol identity framing unless they bring it up — keep it light.
6. Send the booking link.

### Corporate Workshop Inquiry
1. Open warm but precise.
2. Get day count, guest count, AV needs, food needs.
3. Live-quote daily rate + production package they need.
4. Confirm the date.
5. If multi-day, mention the 25% workshop discount.
6. Send a clean email summary with the total. Use [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/04-Training/Text-and-Email-Communication-Approach|Text and Email Approach]] for the post-call follow-up.

### "Luis Told Me I Could Bring Beer"
1. Don't argue. Don't blame.
2. "I understand the confusion. To clarify — beer and wine can be part of the event, they just have to run through our bar service so it's done a specific way."
3. "If you'd rather skip the bar service, non-alcoholic drinks are totally fine — water, sodas, no problem."
4. If they push, escalate to Luis. Don't make exceptions.

### Sensitive Event Type
1. Don't approve or deny on the call.
2. Use neutral language: "Let me confirm a few details with Luis and get back to you within 24 hours."
3. Document the inquiry in GHL.
4. Escalate to Luis with the specifics.
5. See [[01-Brands/Orlando-Event-Venue/04-Training/OEV-Sales-Onboarding-System-v1|Training Module 14 — Red Flags]].

## Anti-Patterns (What Breaks the Voice)

- **Salesperson voice:** "Let me tell you about our amazing packages..." → cut it. Just answer the question.
- **Apology spiral:** "I'm so sorry, our policy is, unfortunately..." → identity-frame instead.
- **List-reading:** rattling off all packages without asking what they need.
- **Cold qualification:** "What's your budget?" too early in the call.
- **Manufactured urgency:** "this won't last" without scarcity to back it up.
- **Over-promising:** describing the outdoor patio as if it's available now.
- **Faking identity:** non-religious rep performing religious language. Hold neutral respect instead.

---

## Quality Bar for a Strong OEV Call

A trained rep should leave the customer feeling:
- they got real information, not a sales pitch
- they understood the actual cost
- they were treated like a guest, not a lead
- their identity was respected
- the next step is clear and easy
- the venue is reliable

If any of those is missing, the call needs review.
