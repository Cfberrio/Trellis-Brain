## 2\. Deep Reflection Prompts
### Humble
*   When was the last time I stood up for a good idea even though it made me uncomfortable? (Real humility means speaking truth, not shrinking.)
*   Do I tend to exaggerate my contributions, or minimize them? Both are violations of humility.
*   When someone else gets credit for something I helped with, how do I honestly feel?
*   Am I more concerned about being right or about getting to the right answer?
*   C.S. Lewis check: Am I thinking about myself less, or just thinking less of myself?
### Hungry
*   Do I consistently go above and beyond, or do I coast once I hit "good enough"?
*   When there's unglamorous work that needs doing, am I the one who picks it up?
*   Would my teammates say I have the highest standards on the team, or that I sometimes cut corners?
*   Lencioni's warning: hunger is hardest to develop later in life. Am I building this muscle now?
*   What's one area where I know I'm doing the minimum and rationalizing it?
### Smart (People Smart)
*   Can I read a room? Do I notice when someone is checked out, frustrated, or hurt?
*   Do people come to me when they need to navigate a difficult conversation?
*   Have I ever accidentally created a mess by saying the wrong thing at the wrong time? How often?
*   Do I adjust my communication style based on who I'm talking to, or do I have one mode?
*   Would my teammates call me emotionally intelligent, or would they say I'm "a lot"?

## 5\. Resources
### The Source Material
*   **Book:** _The Ideal Team Player_ by Patrick Lencioni (the full framework, includes a fable and the practical model)
*   **Book:** _The Five Dysfunctions of a Team_ by Patrick Lencioni (the predecessor: team trust, conflict, commitment, accountability, results)
*   **Book:** _Built to Last_ by Jim Collins and Jerry Porras (referenced in the talk, on core values in great companies)
### On Humility
*   **Book:** _Mere Christianity_ by C.S. Lewis (Chapter on "The Great Sin" is the humility deep-dive Lencioni references)
*   **Book:** _Ego Is the Enemy_ by Ryan Holiday (modern, practical take on keeping ego in check)
*   **Book:** _Good to Great_ by Jim Collins (Level 5 Leadership = humility + fierce resolve, directly complements Lencioni)
### On Hunger (Work Ethic & Drive)
*   **Book:** _Grit_ by Angela Duckworth (the science behind sustained passion and perseverance)
*   **Book:** _Atomic Habits_ by James Clear (building the daily systems that make hunger a habit, not a mood)
*   **Book:** _The War of Art_ by Steven Pressfield (on overcoming resistance and showing up to do the work)
### On Smart (Emotional Intelligence)
*   **Book:** _Emotional Intelligence 2.0_ by Travis Bradberry (practical EQ assessment and improvement strategies)
*   **Book:** _Nonviolent Communication_ by Marshall Rosenberg (the gold standard for interpersonal awareness and communication)
*   **Book:** _How to Win Friends and Influence People_ by Dale Carnegie (classic, still the best intro to reading people)
### For Ongoing Development
*   **Assessment:** The Ideal Team Player assessment at [tablegroup.com](http://tablegroup.com) (official self and team assessment tool)
*   **Talk:** Patrick Lencioni's keynotes on YouTube (search "Lencioni ideal team player" for several versions of this talk)
*   **Podcast:** _At the Table with Patrick Lencioni_ (weekly, covers all his frameworks in conversational format)
