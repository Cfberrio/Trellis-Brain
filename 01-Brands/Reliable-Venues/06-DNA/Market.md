## Market / submarket

- **Market:** Private event venues
- **Submarket:** Underutilized venues selling **date-based inventory** (weddings, corporate events, buyouts, milestone parties) with a booking + deposit flow as the starting unit.

## Why this market now

- Deliberately picking a **"starving crowd"** — markets with high demand make selling easier even with a simpler offer.
- Avoid the "Lloyd problem" (great offer, wrong market). Shrinking markets kill businesses even when product/offer/sales are strong.
- Mechanism leans into **speed + reduced effort** — core value drivers (people pay for speed; DFY commands more because it removes effort/sacrifice).

## Competitor set (mental anchors prospects use)

Prospects mentally compare RV against:

1. **"Normal agency work"** — commoditized, price-compared.
2. **DIY / DWY / DFY alternatives** — they do it internally, hire/consult, or outsource.
3. **"Cheaper version of the same thing"** pressure — the default competitive-pricing trap.

## Decision constraints (non-negotiables)

1. Pick a market with demand + ability to pay — don't be "romantic"; serve those who can pay what we're worth.
2. Be incomparable, not "competitive." If we look like everyone else, we get price-shopped.
3. Choose ONE reliable acquisition path first, then max it out (Core Four: do more / better / new).
4. Reach must be executable via Core Four channels (public: content / ads; private: outreach).

## Minimum buying power

- Must be able to **pay what we're worth** (market selection rule).
- Must have some ability to fund growth actions (e.g., minimum money to run ads if ads is the channel).

## Minimum urgency / pain

- Prospect must be in a "desperate need" / starving-crowd situation for the outcome we provide.

## Where they can be reached

Use **Core Four** framing:

- **Private 1:1 cold outreach:** cold calls, cold emails, cold DMs, voicemails.
- **Public content / paid ads:** warm/cold audiences; time vs money trade.

## "We win here because…"

**We win in private event venues because we're targeting a "starving crowd" market that can pay, and we structure a DFY outcome-driven offer that is hard to compare to commoditized agencies — so we avoid the "competitive pricing" trap and can reliably acquire customers via the Core Four (especially 1:1 outreach).**
