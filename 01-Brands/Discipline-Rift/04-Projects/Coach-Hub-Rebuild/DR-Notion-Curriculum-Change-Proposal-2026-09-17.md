# Discipline Rift — Notion curriculum reconciliation and change proposal

Prepared September 17, 2026 · For Luis’s review and subsequent Claude / Notion MCP implementation

**Status: proposal only. No Notion pages have been changed.**

## 1. Recommendation

Keep the current six-hub navigation and the core method: **Practice Design → Progression Ladder → Coach Rules**. The September redesign already implements much of the philosophy. The next change should make its content consistent and usable, rather than introduce another competing curriculum structure.

The essential correction is to make every practice page support the same behavior: **teach the skill, provide controlled repetitions, correct, add a meaningful layer when ready, connect skills, and verify independent application.** Preserve depth within skills and bridges between skills. A week number, a game score, or a child saying “yes” does not establish readiness.

The highest-priority work is:

1. Correct the live contradictions in teaching instructions, progression, timing, and practice versus skill-page structure.
2. Add explicit progression and regression choices to existing skills and activities, preserving useful current content.
3. Make evidence of learning drive the next practice and the parent update.
4. Consolidate outdated and duplicated material without discarding unique content.
5. Complete the other sports one at a time, using volleyball as the corrected pilot.

## 5. Priority change register

### C01 — Make “teach before testing” unmistakable

**Type:** content correction · **Priority:** first pass · **Sources:** T §§1–3, C §§1–2, M §§5–9,19.

**Where:** PRACTICE DESIGN → “Retrieve / Show” and “Build the Progression Ladder”; COACH RULES → rule 1; all week headers; WEEK PAGE TEMPLATE.

The week pages already require demonstration before a new challenge, but their repeated instruction **“Demonstrate everything. Don’t explain.”** can be read as omitting instruction. Replace it with:

> **Teach and demonstrate before asking players to perform.** Name the goal, show the movement with one or two clear cues, give controlled repetitions, observe and correct, then add the next layer.

Add the distinction:

> Retrieval checks a skill already taught to these players. A new skill, new position, or unfamiliar role needs instruction first. A player demonstration may reinforce teaching; it does not replace the coach’s teaching.

Preserve the useful Week 1 sentence that showing the sport is a preview and the first skill is taught separately. Preserve Week 2’s fallback if setting was not reached and Week 5’s warning about unfamiliar locations. Extend these to returning and newly enrolled players in every week.

**Acceptance:** no active page directs coaches to assess unfamiliar technique before teaching it; retrieval remains available for known skills.

### C02 — Reconcile the different DR sequences without creating another method

**Where:** PRACTICE DESIGN and PROGRESSION LADDER; glossary after live inspection.

The sources describe different scales, not necessarily rival methods:

| Scale | Keep or use |
|---|---|
| Whole session | Open → Retrieve / Show → Build the Ladder → Test Transfer → Close |
| Within a learning sequence | Teach → Repetition → Target → Variability → Connection → Application (T) |
| Short coaching explanation | Teach → Rep → Challenge → Apply → Connect (M) |

Explain that application and connection can recur. Do not require coaches to finish every stage once, in an inflexible order, or wait until the end to connect skills. Preserve the source formulations in training context; use one concise operational formulation consistently on practice pages.

### C03 — Plan the next layer and regression, while keeping the ladder adaptable

**Where:** PROGRESSION LADDER → blank ladder and final callout; HOW WE COACH; COACHING TODAY → preparation; WEEK PAGE TEMPLATE.

Current instruction to plan one or two realistic starting rungs is useful. T also requires knowing the next layer, success, regression, and eventual connection before arrival. Replace “two starting rungs are enough” implications with:

> Prepare your starting rung, what success will look like, an easier version, the next likely layer, the bridge it enables, and how you will check it. Adjust the route after observing the players.

Keep **Skill + Problem + Gamified Challenge** as the visible core. Add concise planning fields rather than a massive table on every coach page:

| Planning field | Required information |
|---|---|
| Starting point | Last reliable skill under the recorded conditions; new-player needs. |
| Teaching | What must be demonstrated; one or two cues. |
| Evidence | What action shows control; distinguish a prompted rep from independent execution. |
| Regression | Which demand to reduce if players cannot perform. |
| Next layer | One change in target, distance, movement, entry, role, or decision. |
| Bridge | Next skill or teammate action; instruction needed for it. |
| Transfer | One changed condition for CFU. |
| Transition | Where players move, who feeds, where balls go, and what is already set up. |

The exact number of successful reps is not established as universal policy by these MDs. Existing game scores may remain game rules, identified as starting examples rather than validated mastery thresholds.

### C04 — Clarify repetition, variability, and serial practice

**Where:** PROGRESSION LADDER → “Serial Practice”; PRACTICE DESIGN; coaching feedback page; historical science reference.

T permits stable repetitions to learn a movement and rejects staying in identical repetitions indefinitely. Preserve that distinction. Do not replace “too much unchanged practice” with “never use blocked repetitions.”

Proposed wording:

> Start with enough predictable repetitions to teach control. When players show control, keep the skill recognizable and add a purposeful layer. Revisit it later under another condition. If they struggle, teach again or simplify; do not keep repeating failure.

The archived PRACTICE STRUCTURE distinguishes blocked, serial, interleaved, and randomized practice. Preserve that reference rather than redefining every variation as technically “serial.” DR’s coach-facing emphasis can remain connected, progressive practice without making an unsupported research claim that one format is always best.

### C05 — Protect time without turning the sample into a rigid clock

**Where:** PRACTICE DESIGN; three volleyball banks; WEEK PAGE TEMPLATE; COACH EXPECTATIONS §10; all sports’ week pages.

Actual conflicts:

- Practice Design rejects a fixed seven-block clock.
- Warm-up bank says “about 10 minutes.”
- Challenge bank says “two or three” challenges in a “30 minute” block.
- CFU bank says “the last 15 minutes,” although Close also needs time.
- Individual challenge pages repeat “Do not run four of these in a session.”
- COACH EXPECTATIONS says every part of the lesson plan should be covered, conflicting with progression by player readiness.

Propose planning time deliberately while treating allocations as examples. Budget opening, teaching, connected repetitions, transfer, close, and transitions against the actual session duration. If a group stays on an early rung, use an appropriate simple transfer check instead of abandoning CFU. Remove arbitrary challenge-count caps and requirements to finish every sample step.

Replace expectation §10 with:

> Use practice time intentionally. Keep transitions short, respond to readiness, and protect time to check learning and close. Progress players rather than completing a list.

Do not convert the coaching incident timestamps in C into a universal “3:40” deadline.

### C06 — Reconcile active feedback with independent learning checks

**Where:** COACH RULES → rules 6–7; repeated headers in all volleyball weeks; CFU template and games.

The weekly header says **“Coach at all times. Don’t stop speaking.”** CFU pages correctly say to observe before speaking and remove coach-directed destinations. Resolve the conflict explicitly:

> During learning repetitions, stay actively engaged: observe, teach, praise, correct, and adjust. Give one actionable cue for the next attempt. During an independent check, allow players to act before prompting; then correct and replay if needed.

Keep direct and frequent coaching. Avoid replacing it with passive supervision or mandatory silence during teaching. The observable aim is useful feedback, not uninterrupted speech.

### C07 — Make environment and transitions actionable

**Where:** COACH RULES; PRACTICE DESIGN → Open; COACHING TODAY → setup; Different Levels / Co-Coaching.

M requires attention, energy, respect, trust, and excitement before technical teaching. Add a short opening check: group together, coach visible and audible, clear attention signal, boundaries demonstrated, goal stated. Keep enthusiasm specific: greet players, use names, recognize effort, and maintain pace.

Add a transition plan: next task setup, next group position, ball collection and distribution, stop signal, and coach roles. Preserve the current three-lines-per-coach qualification: roster, space, supervision, and activity must allow it. A supervised attacking lane or small-sided game should not be forced into a three-line format.

### C08 — Separate depth within a skill from bridges between skills

**Where:** PROGRESSION LADDER and SKILL PAGE TEMPLATE; all seven volleyball skills.

Add two explicit sections to the skill template:

1. **Within this skill:** control → target → movement/angle/entry → appropriate decision or pressure.
2. **Bridge to the next action:** what the player must produce so a teammate can perform the next skill.

Do not imply every item must be mastered before trying a supported connection. A usable pass and a taught set can be connected while both continue improving. “Functional enough for the next supported task” is more useful than undefined perfect mastery.

### C09 — Preserve useful role upgrades and individual progression

**Where:** PROGRESSION LADDER → Golden Ticket; Different Levels / Co-Coaching; challenge template and activities.

T supports moving capable players into harder targets, movement, connected skills, or special roles. The current bank already states that a Golden Ticket adds one demand, is temporary, and does not control who gets the ball. Keep those safeguards and the optional mechanic.

Make role instruction explicit: a successful passer does not automatically know how to set. Teach the new role before assigning its performance. Let players work at different constraints within one learning objective; do not make everyone wait for the slowest or force everyone to the fastest rung.

## 6. Volleyball skill-page changes

### C10 — PASSING: correct the progression ceiling and add angles

**Existing location:** “HOW PASSING PROGRESSES OVER TIME” says **“Only the movement before the pass changes.”** “Application Phase (Around Week 4)” imposes a calendar association inconsistent with the sample ladder. The page does not contain the explicit passing-angle/seam distinction developed in M and P.

**Proposed edits:**

- Keep the foundational numbered teaching sequence and familiar cues as the beginner reference.
- Replace the “only movement changes” claim with: “Keep the foundational cues consistent while developing control, destination, movement, platform angle, entry, coordination, and connection.”
- Rename calendar phases to **Starting version** and **Progress when ready**.
- Add **Passing angles: where the ball goes**. Preserve the cue: “Face the incoming ball and angle the platform toward the target.” Explicitly distinguish angle adjustment from swinging the arms sideways.
- Add a short **Seam responsibility: who takes the ball** definition and link to the canonical communication/defense activity.
- Add the bridge: produce a playable ball for a setter. Do not score a first pass only by whether it crosses the net.
- Link the internal ladder to Deliver the Package, Target Hoops, Beat the Ball, Build the Bridge, and the missing games after live inspection.

**Proposed runnable progression example:**

| Layer | Teaching / task | Evidence | If it fails | Likely next layer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled contact | Demonstrate platform; predictable feed | Controlled upward ball | Easier feed or self-toss support | Direct toward a target |
| Targeting | Same feed, teammate target | Teammate can play the next ball | Larger/closer target | Change target angle |
| Angles | Demonstrate platform direction to side target | Chooses the named left/right destination | Smaller angle; repeat demonstration | Movement before contact |
| Movement | Move into a reachable feed | Arrives and redirects with control | Reduce movement or feed uncertainty | Less predictable entry |
| Connection | Teach setter role; pass → set → catch | First contact supports second | Declared catch/self-toss support | Controlled third action |
| Application | Change one condition in CFU | Skill appears independently | Cue, replay, simplify | Next observed problem |

This is an adaptable example, not a mandatory sequence or a new universal mastery rubric.

### C11 — SETTING: broaden progression and connect it earlier

**Existing location:** “HOW SETTING PROGRESSES OVER TIME” says only movement changes; “Application Phase (Around Week 4)” repeats the calendar issue.

Preserve the numbered beginner instruction, short cues, balanced arrival, and controlled contact. Add height, destination, distance, changing first-ball quality, movement, and a playable ball for the next sender. Link Window Delivery → Build the Bridge → a taught attacking connection. Explicitly label catch/self-toss as temporary support rather than the intended final skill.

Window Delivery already tells coaches to watch coordinated extension, “not just leg movement.” Reconcile that wording with the skill page’s absolute “legs create / fingers only guide” claims through a focused technical review. Do not silently rewrite mechanics from outside sources in this structural pass.

### C12 — SERVING: teach first and respond to repeated failure

Keep the open-palm underhand beginner method. Add the explicit “teach → controlled contact → achievable serving distance → target → changed condition” path and a regression decision. A repeated failed full-distance serve triggers teaching and scaling, not more identical failed attempts.

Align the skill page’s “Hold” description with Serve to Unlock’s supporting-hand release at contact. Keep distances, net height, and equipment scaling subject to the actual setup. Connect serving to reception and a playable continuation; keep the receiving task achievable too. Rename “Week 1 Focus” to “Starting version” to avoid conflict with the Week 2 sample.

### C13 — ATTACKING: convert the old practice plan into a true skill page

**Current finding:** this is still a full seven-block DR-UPS session, with fixed tier labels, numeric exit criteria, embedded drills, a “14” set call, and optional quick timing (“All the Steps Before”). TIMING TICKET explicitly excludes quick attacks from this beginner pathway.

**Proposed changes:**

1. Preserve the original as a dated historical reference before restructuring.
2. Rebuild the same canonical skill page using SKILL PAGE TEMPLATE: purpose, numbered demonstration, cues, problems/corrections, internal progression, bridge, evidence, linked activities.
3. Preserve standing controlled contact as a valid earlier level, consistent with Week 3 and High Hand Target.
4. Use the complete handed approach already written in both sources when teaching it: left–right–left for right-handed, reversed for left-handed. Clarify any shortened cue rather than presenting it as the whole approach.
5. Move drill execution to existing High Hand Target, Timing Ticket, Build the Bridge, and CFU pages where equivalent. Preserve unique drill material in resources until mapped; do not lose it during deduplication.
6. Remove quick-attack timing and unexplained “14” calls from the beginner coach route; retain only in clearly separate advanced reference pending DR technical review.
7. Replace legacy tier numbers with observable constraints. Existing 8/10, 3/5, and 4/6 figures are sample criteria unless DR confirms otherwise.

### C14 — DEFENDING, MOVING, COMMUNICATING: teach roles and avoid week gates

| Page | Current material to retain | Change |
|---|---|---|
| DEFENDING | Movement first; known passing platform; ready/read/move sequence; court awareness | State that positions, responsibility, and rotations must be demonstrated before expected. Teach seam ownership separately from platform angle. Let playable defense connect to setting when ready. |
| MOVING | Step/shuffle patterns, balance, movement before contact | Describe these as the current foundational repertoire; do not imply movement development ends with speed and distance alone. Connect to destination, entry, recovery, and decisions. |
| COMMUNICATING | Short shared calls, names, group voice, early responsibility calls | Add a brief starting-point check for already-capable players, practical teaching for those struggling, and early links from all weeks. Communication begins with the first practice; Week 6 is integration, not its first introduction. |

The statement “Silence is not personality” and the absolute refusal to continue until every response occurs should be reviewed against M’s emphasis on confidence, trust, adaptation, and low-pressure participation. Proposed replacement: teach the expected words, model, rehearse at an achievable level, and give specific feedback without labelling the child. This is a proposed clarification, not an inferred new behavior policy.

## 8. Parent communication and coach development

### C18 — Make the parent update show actual development
