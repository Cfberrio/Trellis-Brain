## 02. Lead Funnel — SMS + Email Cadence

### Guardrails
- Only send between **9:00 AM–6:00 PM local time** (America/New_York)
- If lead arrives after 6:00 PM → schedule next touch for 10:00 AM next day
- 3 SMS total + 5 emails total

### SMS sequence

**SMS #1 — Immediate (0 min)**
> Here's your link to finish your DR Franchisee application: {link}

**SMS #2 — Next day (18–24h) at 10:30 AM**
> Quick reminder — your application isn't complete yet. Takes <1 min: {link}

**SMS #3 — Day 3 (48–72h) at 4:30 PM**
> Last reminder today — if you still want to be considered, finish here: {link}

### Email sequence

**Email #1 — Immediate (0 min)** — Same link + "what happens next" in 2 lines.

**Email #2 — Same day (2h later)** — 2 bullets: benefits + what the license opportunity leads to.

**Email #3 — Next day at 9:15 AM** — "Common questions" (2–3 Q/As) + link again.

**Email #4 — Day 3 at 1:00 PM (48–72h)** — Social proof / clarity + link.

**Email #5 — Day 6 at 11:00 AM** — "Closing note / last chance this week" + link.

### Capture form (Step 1 — Quick Info)
1. First name
2. Email
3. Phone number
4. Sport experience — *What sport do you have experience in, and for how many years?*

### GHL implementation rule
- 9:00 AM–6:00 PM: run schedule as-is
- 6:01 PM–8:59 AM: treat "start time" as 10:00 AM next day, then run

## DRF Avatar — Full

### Best-fit cluster (MBTI lens, not hard rule)

1. **ENFJ — Best overall fit**
   Mission-driven, natural leader, good with people, persuasive, comfortable carrying responsibility, wants to build impact.

2. **ENTJ — Very strong fit, especially for scale**
   Ownership, decisive, organized, growth-oriented, comfortable with outreach + structure + metrics.

3. **ESTJ — Strong operational fit**
   Reliable, structure-friendly, follows playbooks, consistent.

4. **ESFJ — Strong people-and-community fit**
   Warm, responsible, community-oriented, good with families.

**Primary archetype:** ENFJ with execution discipline.

### Real avatar — plain English

> **"A student leader who genuinely likes kids, but is not fulfilled by coaching alone."**

They want: real leadership, real responsibility, ownership optics, something local to build, structure + support, resume leverage, income upside, a path bigger than a side hustle.

### High-probability traits
- Socially confident
- Responsible
- Proactive
- Coachable
- Comfortable with structure
- Comfortable leading peers + kids
- Okay with outreach
- Not scared of follow-up
- Willing to own results
- Energized by building something locally

### Lower-probability fit (red flags)
- Only motivated by coaching moments
- Deeply service-oriented but low-ownership
- Highly relational but avoid sales/outreach
- Thoughtful but slow to commit
- Impact-driven but not execution-driven
- Allergic to structure, accountability, or performance standards

### DRF ideal formula

**People-first + ownership-minded + system-friendly + action-oriented**

MBTI tendencies that matter most:
- **E** over I (outreach, local leadership, parent/school interaction)
- **J** over P (weekly execution, standards, consistency)
- F or T can both work (F for mission/kids/parents/trust; T for decisions/accountability/growth pressure)

### Worst mismatch

Someone who says:
- "I love the kids"
- "I'll help when needed"
- "I want balance"
- "I'm open to involvement"

But hesitates around:
- ownership
- outreach
- growth responsibility
- committing to local execution

### Behavior-based filter (better than MBTI)

The application should filter for the 4 behaviors:
1. **Ownership**
2. **Consistency**
3. **Outreach comfort**
4. **Builder mindset**

### Recommended application question to expose ownership

**Which sounds more like you?**

- A. I mainly want to coach and help kids directly → likely contributor/coach
- B. I want to coach, but I also want to help build and grow something locally → possible operator-in-training
- C. I'm most excited by leading, growing, and owning outcomes in a local program → strongest DRF fit

## 03. Application Background — OKR

**Objective:** Build one short, clear application that filters the right people without scaring good candidates away.

**Key Results:**
- **KR1:** Students complete full application in **under 5 minutes**.
- **KR2:** Students complete swipe section in **under 2 minutes**.
- **KR3:** Confirm top 3 swipe prompts that best reveal fit:
  1. "I can commit to running sessions weekly and following a playbook."
  2. "I'm comfortable doing outreach to schools/communities to grow."
  3. "I want something I can build locally (not just try once)."
- **KR4:** Identify 3 best overall questions + remove confusing/weak.
- **KR5:** After submitting, students can explain the opportunity in one sentence.
- **KR6:** Final version: 3–5 items default; max cap 8–12 if needed, no fluff.

### Pilot version (applicant-facing)

#### Step 2 — Swipe Fit Check (✅ That's me / ❌ Not me)
1. *I can commit to running sessions weekly and following a playbook.*
2. *I'm comfortable doing outreach to schools/communities to grow.*
3. *I want something I can build locally (not just try once).*

#### Step 3 — 2-minute Fit Questions

1. **Time commitment** — *How many hours per week can you realistically commit to building this?*
   - Options: 0–3 / 4–6 / 7–10 / 10+

2. **Transportation / local execution** — *Do you have reliable transportation to run programs locally?*
   - Options: Yes / No

3. **Start window** — *If accepted, when could you start?*
   - Options: 0–2 weeks / 3–4 weeks / 1–2 months / 2+ months

#### (Optional) Step 3B — "Seriousness" question (one only)

- 11A) *Why are you interested in this?* (1–2 sentences)
- OR
- 11B) *What's your main goal?*
  - Options: Extra income / Build something long-term / Leadership-community impact / Coaching & development

#### Final screen
**"Thanks — we'll text you the next step shortly."**

## KnightShadow validation questions (used to test application)

### A) Timing + friction (KR1, KR2)
- "From start to submit, did this feel under 5 minutes?"
- "Which step felt slowest?"
- "If you were seeing this from an Instagram ad, would you finish it?"
- "What's the first thing you'd remove to make it faster?"

### B) Swipe prompts quality (KR3)
- "Which swipe statement felt most accurate for filtering the right people?"
- "Which swipe statement felt least useful / confusing?"
- "Did any swipe feel like a 'trap' question?"
- "What swipe statement would you add that would filter better?"

### C) Best questions overall (KR4)
- "Pick the top 3 questions in the whole application that reveal fit."
- "Which question felt pointless?"
- "Which question felt unclear?"
- "What info do you think we're missing?"

### D) Clarity check (KR5)
- "In one sentence: What is this license / opportunity?"
- "What part of the application made that more clear?"
- "What part made it less clear?"

### E) Final version decision (KR6)
- "How many total items should this application have to feel easy?"
- "Would you rather answer: swipes only, short multiple choice, or 1–2 short typed answers?"
- "What would make you trust this more before submitting?"

## Application Official — 15 Swipe Questions

Top instructions: **Swipe right if this feels more like you. Swipe left if it does not really sound like you. Go with your natural tendency, not the answer you think sounds best.**

| # | Statement | Best-fit | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | When something is unclear at first, I usually prefer to watch how others handle it before stepping in. | Left | Initiative / ownership instinct |
| 2 | I'm usually more comfortable improving an existing plan than creating one from scratch. | Left | Builder vs supporter identity |
| 3 | If I believe in something, I naturally start mentioning it to other people without needing much prompting. | Right | Outreach comfort / persuasion instinct |
| 4 | Once a routine becomes familiar, I usually perform better rather than getting bored with it. | Right | Consistency / operational stamina |
| 5 | I do my best work when expectations and standards are clearly defined. | Right | Structure fit / playbook comfort |
| 6 | Being responsible for one clear outcome usually appeals to me more than being lightly involved in several things. | Right | Ownership appetite |
| 7 | In unfamiliar settings, I usually wait for someone else to break the ice first. | Left | Social initiative / local leadership |
| 8 | I tend to notice small problems in how things are organized, even when other people seem fine with them. | Right | Systems awareness / operator instinct |
| 9 | I try to avoid enforcing standards if I think it might create tension with people. | Left | Leadership maturity / standards under pressure |
| 10 | I'm usually more energized by contributing to a mission than by representing it publicly. | Left | Ownership visibility / face-of-program readiness |
| 11 | When I commit to something recurring, I usually think first about whether I can sustain it week after week. | Right | Consistency / realism / discipline |
| 12 | I'm more comfortable being known for dependable execution than for having big ideas. | Right | Execution identity / reliability |
| 13 | If a conversation could lead somewhere useful, I usually don't mind following up more than once. | Right | Follow-up tolerance / outreach persistence |
| 14 | I'm usually willing to make a decision with incomplete information if waiting would slow things down too much. | Right | Decisiveness / action bias |
| 15 | I prefer roles where success is shared broadly rather than roles where my results are easy to measure. | Left | Accountability comfort / ownership of results |

### Scoring

For 15 swipes:
- Best-fit direction = 1 point
- Opposite = 0 points

**Max score = 15**

| Score range | Classification |
|---|---|
| 8–10 | Strong fit |
| 6–7 | Possible fit |
| 4–5 | Likely better as coach/intern path |
| 0–3 | Low fit for DRF operator |

### Short Answer Questions

1. **Describe your experience with the sport.** Include anything that applies, whether as a player, coach, volunteer, or leader.
2. **Tell us about a time you had to keep something moving when others were counting on you. What did you do?**
3. **What part of building something local would interest you most:** leading people, growing it, running it well, or something else? Explain.
4. **Describe a time you had to work within a system or clear set of standards.** How did you handle it?
