## Purpose
**Project Play UCF** is a UCF Registered Student Organization designed as a **coach pipeline + community service vehicle** for DRF. Develops UCF students as youth coaches → some convert to paid DRF operators outside the RSO via a separate, optional pathway.

> [!warning] Strategic boundary
> The RSO is NOT "the student branch of Discipline Rift." It is a UCF student organization focused on youth sports access + coach education + community service. DRF business activity stays **separate**.

## Names To Consider
**Recommended:** **Project Play UCF**

Other options:
- Knights for Youth Sports
- UCF Youth Sports Access Club
- Play Forward UCF
- Coaches for Kids UCF
- Sport for All UCF

> Avoid using "Discipline Rift" in the RSO name. Keeps the organization mission-first and avoids looking like a business arm.

## Mission Statement
> Project Play UCF exists to train student coaches and expand access to safe, fun, beginner-friendly youth sports programs for children in the Orlando community.

The organization develops UCF students as youth coaches, mentors, and community leaders while creating free or low-cost sports opportunities aligned with the belief that **every child deserves a safe place to play, learn, belong, and grow.**

This fits **Aspen Project Play** because it focuses on helping stakeholders build healthy communities through sports + identifying access and quality gaps.

## Strategic Frame

### The RSO is NOT a sports club
It is a **coach development + community service organization.**

Not gathering students who like volleyball/tennis/pickleball/flag football. Building a system where UCF students learn to:
- Coach children safely
- Create beginner-friendly practices
- Use sports for confidence, belonging, and character
- Serve local schools and youth organizations
- Bring access to kids who may not otherwise join organized sports

Aspen's **Children's Bill of Rights in Sports**: every child has the right to play sports, safe and healthy environments, qualified program leaders, developmentally appropriate play, equal opportunity for growth, dignity, and enjoyment.

## The Coach Funnel Strategy (5 stages)

### Stage 1 — Awareness
Attract UCF students who care about sports + kids + coaching + leadership + service + education + health + mentorship.

Recruit from:
- Kinesiology students
- Education majors
- Health Sciences students
- Psychology students
- Pre-med / pre-PT / pre-OT students
- Student athletes or former athletes
- Students who need volunteer / service experience
- Students interested in youth development

### Stage 2 — Training (Workshops)
- **Workshop 1:** How to coach beginners
- **Workshop 2:** Child safety and supervision
- **Workshop 3:** Practice structure and age-appropriate play
- **Workshop 4:** Positive correction and confidence-building
- **Workshop 5:** How to run a free youth sports clinic

Aspen's youth sports playbook strategies: ask kids what they want, reintroduce free play, encourage sport sampling, design for development, train all coaches, emphasize prevention.

### Stage 3 — Field Experience
Students volunteer at:
- Free youth sports clinics
- School-based play days
- Community center events
- Beginner sports camps
- One-day "try a sport" events
- Local outreach with churches / schools / nonprofits / city programs

### Stage 4 — Leadership Track
Consistent students become:
- Lead Coaches
- Sport Captains
- Safety Leads
- School Partnership Leads
- Training Coordinators
- Event Directors

### Stage 5 — Separate Career Pathway (DRF connection)
Only AFTER they are already involved as students + volunteers, create an **optional, separate pathway**:

> "Students who want paid coaching opportunities outside the RSO may ask about independent opportunities with local youth sports providers."

**Do not make the RSO's purpose "recruit coaches for the business."**

Make it: **develop coaches for the community.** That protects the RSO's integrity.

## RSO ↔ Business Boundary (must be clean)

### Clean boundary statement
> "We are a UCF student organization focused on youth sports access, coach education, and community service."

NOT: "We are the student branch of Discipline Rift."

### What you CAN use from the business
Experience can shape:
- Practice structure
- Coach training
- Safety expectations
- Youth development philosophy
- Beginner-friendly sports curriculum
- Community programming model

### What you must NOT do
- Use RSO meetings to sell Discipline Rift
- Use UCF funding for business operations
- Require RSO members to work for the business
- Present business programs as RSO programs
- Use UCF rooms/resources for private business activity
- Mix RSO funds and business funds

> UCF SG funding requires: org registered through OSI before seeking funding, complete financial training, submit funding forms, plan ahead (allocation requests = 4–6 weeks; bill requests = 8–10 weeks before funding needed).

## Program Model

### Main Program: Free Youth Sports Access Days
One Saturday or weekday afternoon per month.

Sports rotate:
- Volleyball
- Flag football
- Tennis
- Pickleball
- Multi-sport games

### Target Kids
Ages 6–12.

### Format (60–90 minutes)
1. Welcome + name game
2. Warm-up game
3. Skill station
4. Small-sided play
5. Team challenge
6. Reflection: "What did you learn?"
7. Parent/community closeout

### RSO Promise
> **Every child gets to play. Every student coach learns how to lead.**

## Best Pitch To UCF

> Project Play UCF will create a student-led coaching and service organization that trains UCF students to deliver safe, beginner-friendly youth sports experiences for children in the Orlando community. The organization will support youth access to sports, leadership development for UCF students, and community engagement through free clinics, school partnerships, and coach education workshops.

Stronger than "we want to create teams." "Teams" sounds competitive-club. Use:
- programs
- clinics
- play days
- community sports experiences
- student-led youth development programs

## RSO Constitution Purpose Statement

> The purpose of this organization is to expand access to safe, inclusive, beginner-friendly youth sports opportunities for children in the local community while developing UCF students as trained coaches, mentors, and service-minded leaders. The organization will host coach education workshops, youth sports clinics, community play days, and partnership-based service events aligned with youth development, health, inclusion, and access.

## Officer Structure
- **President** — vision + UCF compliance + partnerships + officer meetings
- **Vice President of Coach Development** — coach workshops + training standards
- **Director of Community Programs** — free clinics + school outreach + event logistics
- **Director of Safety & Operations** — waivers + supervision plans + check-in/out + emergency plans + coach ratios
- **Director of Marketing & Recruitment** — recruit coaches + promote events
- **Treasurer** — RSO funding requests + budgets + purchasing

## First 90-Day Launch Plan

### Days 1–15: Foundation
- Pick RSO name
- Recruit 10 UCF student members
- Choose President + second officer
- Draft constitution
- Schedule KnightQuest consultation
- Identify potential advisor (optional)

### Days 15–30: Positioning
Build one-page doc:
- **Name:** Project Play UCF
- **Mission:** youth sports access + student coach development
- **Activities:** coach workshops + free clinics + school/community partnerships
- **Audience:** UCF students + local kids ages 6–12
- **Impact:** more kids playing + more students trained to coach

### Days 30–60: Recruitment
Recruitment message:
> "Love sports? Want to work with kids? Project Play UCF trains students to become youth coaches and helps create free sports programs for local children. No coaching experience required."

### Days 60–90: First Pilot
Run one pilot event:
- **Free Youth Sports Play Day**
- 20–30 kids
- 8–12 student volunteers
- 2–3 sports stations
- 90 minutes
- Parent check-in/out
- Photos/testimonials if approved
- Post-event reflection for coaches

## Strategic Advantage (why this works for DRF)

1. **Coach pipeline** — Students discover coaching through service
2. **Training environment** — Teach DR method without making it feel like business onboarding
3. **Credibility** — UCF student organization + Aspen-aligned mission + youth access
4. **Community impact** — Free local programs for kids who may not join paid sports
5. **Partnership leverage** — Schools, nonprofits, churches, community centers say yes faster to student-led service vs private business pitch
6. **Long-term talent development** — Best student coaches can choose paid DRF opportunities separately

## One-Liners (pitch options)

**Simple:**
> We train UCF students to become youth coaches and create free beginner-friendly sports programs for local kids.

**Stronger:**
> Project Play UCF develops student coaches and expands access to safe, fun, beginner-friendly sports experiences for children in Orlando.

**Most strategic (build around this):**
> Project Play UCF is a student-led coach development and youth sports access organization that prepares UCF students to serve children through safe, inclusive, developmentally appropriate play.

## Operational Implications

- RSO funnel feeds **DRF Operator pipeline** but only via Stage 5 (separate pathway).
- All workshops can be staffed by DR coaches + Luis as guest trainers without making the RSO a DR business asset.
- Free clinics generate visible community impact → strengthens future DRF licensee pitches.
- Track **conversion rate** RSO members → DRF operator applicants quarterly (without making it the RSO's purpose).
