## Purpose
Defines the ideal Discipline Rift Franchise operator. This is **not** the parent payer — this is the person who runs a local DRF program in their community.

## Plain English
> "A student leader who genuinely likes kids, but is not fulfilled by coaching alone."

They want:
- Real leadership
- Real responsibility
- Ownership optics
- Something local they can build
- Structure and support
- Resume leverage
- Income upside
- A path bigger than a side hustle

## Best MBTI Fit
> [!note] MBTI is a **filtering lens**, not a hard rule.

| Tier | MBTI | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | **ENFJ** | Mission-driven, natural leader, persuasive, inspires parents/kids/schools, builds impact |
| Secondary | **ENTJ** | Likes ownership, decisive, organized, growth-oriented, comfortable with outreach + metrics |
| Secondary | **ESTJ** | Reliable, structure-friendly, follows playbooks, consistent, manages standards + logistics |
| Conditional | **ESFJ** | Warm, community-oriented, good with families, strong on trust + consistency |

**The shape that matters most:** Extraverted + Judging. F or T both work.

## DRF Ideal Formula
> **People-first + ownership-minded + system-friendly + action-oriented**

## High-Probability Traits
The operator should be:
- Socially confident
- Responsible
- Proactive
- Coachable
- Comfortable with structure
- Comfortable leading peers and kids
- Okay with outreach
- Not scared of follow-up
- Willing to own results
- Energized by building something locally

## Lower-Probability Fit (red flags)
Be careful with people who are:
- Only motivated by coaching moments
- Deeply service-oriented but low-ownership
- Highly relational but avoid sales/outreach
- Thoughtful but slow to commit
- Impact-driven but not execution-driven
- Allergic to structure, accountability, or performance standards

These traits matter because DRF operators must hit standards, use DR systems, follow pricing/scripts, and can lose territory if they fail to execute.

## Filtering Behaviors (use in the application)

Instead of asking applicants directly for MBTI, build the application around the 4 behaviors that map to the best-fit personality:

1. **Ownership** — willing to be the face of a local program
2. **Consistency** — show up week after week
3. **Outreach comfort** — comfortable contacting schools and families
4. **Builder mindset** — excited to grow something, not just maintain

## Application Question — Ownership Differentiator

> Which sounds more like you?
> A. I mainly want to coach and help kids directly
> B. I want to coach, but I also want to help build and grow something locally
> C. I'm most excited by leading, growing, and owning outcomes in a local program

- A = likely contributor/coach (not operator)
- B = possible operator-in-training
- C = strongest DRF fit

## Reference Anti-Example — Julian
Julian shows the difference between a high-value coach and a high-fit operator.

**What he has:** cares about kids, values mentorship, thoughtful about responsibility, open-minded, emotionally connected to mission, willing to help, not lazy, not flaky.

**What he lacks for DRF fit:** strong desire to own/grow a territory, natural pull toward operator identity, comfort with "I will be the face of this locally", instinctive excitement around business building, clear appetite for outreach + weekly growth pressure.

His biggest quote (paraphrased):
> "I still want the meat and potatoes to be around the kids, not computer stuff, paperwork, meetings, and admin."

That is the key difference. Julian is a great mission-aligned **coach** candidate, but DRF needs a **builder who also coaches**, not a **coach who tolerates building**.

## Worst Mismatch
Someone who says:
- "I love the kids"
- "I'll help when needed"
- "I want balance"
- "I'm open to involvement"

But hesitates around:
- Ownership
- Outreach
- Growth responsibility
- Committing to local execution
