# School Outreach SOP — Private + Charter + Christian + Catholic + Montessori

## Purpose
SOP for contacting private schools, Christian schools, Catholic schools, academies, preparatory schools, and similar non-public schools.

> [!warning] Critical difference vs Public SOP
> Public schools start with **Facilitron / facility rentals**. Private schools start with **after-school programming, clubs, lower school activities, athletics, enrichment**. Do **not** lead with Facilitron unless the school specifically uses it.

## 1. Main Objective
Not to "sell the school" on the first call. Goal is to sound familiar with how schools work and need to confirm who handles after-school programs or lower school activities.

Posture:
- Not strangers
- Not aggressively pitching
- Trying to find the correct person
- Understand schools are busy
- Want to make sports accessible for younger students
- Bringing something useful, simple, easy to say yes to

## 2. Core Difference From Public Schools
**Do NOT lead with Facilitron.**

Instead of: "Who handles Facilitron?"

Ask: "Who handles after-school programming?" or "Who would be the best person to speak with about lower school clubs or enrichment programs?"

Use Facilitron only if the school specifically mentions it.

## 3. Core Tone
Friendly, calm, familiar, confident, warm. Not salesy, corporate, robotic, or over-explained. Like we already know how school programs work.

> "We need to be besties with these private schools."

That does not mean fake. It means approachable, respectful, easy to talk to.

> [!warning] Do not pretend to be a parent. Do not lie. Do not say anything misleading.

## 4. CRM Source Of Truth — Go High Level
Every private school needs:
- School name, school type, phone, email
- Contact person + role
- Notes from call attempts, current stage, next follow-up action
- Sport discussed
- Space availability notes
- Whether they have lower school / elementary students
- Whether they have middle school athletics
- Whether they have existing after-school clubs

If "Follow up in July" → create reminder/appointment in GHL. Do not rely on memory.

## 5. Who To Call
Only private schools ready or assigned by Luis. Before calling check:
- School name, type, phone, contact, email, notes
- Comeback or new school
- Lower school / elementary grades present
- Middle school volleyball or athletics
- Existing after-school clubs
- Sport opportunity (volleyball / flag football / tennis / pickleball)
- Old history from Como CRM if Pipeline 1

Do not restart from zero if we already have history.

## 6. How To Identify Private School Opportunities

**Private school keywords:**
- Christian / Catholic / Academy / Preparatory / Episcopal / Montessori / Classical / Foundation / Private School / Lower School / Upper School / K–8 / K–12

**Important caveats:**
- Not every K–8 is private (some are public with facility restrictions)
- "Elementary" in name usually = public (verify website)
- Schools with middle school + elementary often have an issue: middle school takes most facilities after school. Use private-school space conversation, not Facilitron approach.

## 7. Best Time To Call
**9:00 AM – 12:00 PM**. Avoid 8 AM (arrival chaos).

Use this block for:
- Reviewing CRM notes
- Checking old Como notes
- Calling schools
- Sending follow-up emails
- Logging notes + creating reminders
- Moving stages + drafting replies

> Outreach is not only calling new schools. **The follow-up is where the opportunity is created.**

## 8. First Call Script — Front Office

### Main opening
> "Hi, this is [Name]. How's it going?"
> *Pause.*
> "I'm trying to confirm who handles after-school programs or clubs for lower school students."
> "Who would be the best person to speak with about that?"

Keep it short.

## 9. If We Have A Contact Name
> "Hi, this is [Name]. How's it going? I wanted to confirm if Ms. [Last Name] is still the right person for after-school programming or lower school activities. Is she still handling that, or is someone else in charge now?"

If previous person left:
> "Got it. Who would be the best person to speak with now?"

If old history exists:
> "We had spoken with Ms. [Last Name] a while back about bringing a sports program to campus, but I understand she may no longer be there. I just wanted to confirm who handles that now."

## 10. If They Ask What It's About
> "We have a beginner-friendly volleyball program that we can bring directly to campus for lower school students. I just wanted to make sure I'm speaking with the right person before sending anything over."
> "Would that usually go through athletics, after-school programs, or lower school administration?"

If needed: "It's one day per week, after school, and we bring the coaches and equipment."

## 11. Keywords To Use

**Primary:**
- After-school programs / Clubs / Lower school / Elementary students / Enrichment / Athletics
- Volleyball / Beginner-friendly / Developmental
- One day per week / One hour after dismissal
- We bring the coaches / We bring the equipment / Directly on campus

**Use carefully:**
- "Developmental" — when person interested or clarifying it's not a competitive team
- "Methodology" / "programming" / "curriculum" — only with athletic director or admin who values professional program language

Don't use too much professional language too early.

## 12. If Transferred To Decision-Maker
Don't restart. Stay warm and familiar.

> "Hi Coach [Last Name], this is [Name]. How are you? I was asking because we have a volleyball program we can bring to campus for lower school students."

Then discovery question:
> "Quick question — what do you guys currently have volleyball-related for elementary-age students?"

If they mention middle school volleyball:
> "That's great. Do you have anything that introduces the sport before they get to middle school?"

Then connect value:
> "That's where we can be helpful. We bring the sport directly to campus so younger students can start learning earlier and be more prepared by the time they reach middle school."

## 13. Decision-Maker Call Structure
1. Introduce by name
2. Mention lower school / elementary students
3. Ask what they currently offer
4. Connect program to their school
5. Clarify: one day/week, one hour after dismissal
6. Explain we bring coaches + equipment
7. Ask about interest + available space
8. Ask to send info by email

### Core version
> "Hi Coach [Last Name], this is [Name]. How are you?"
> "We have a beginner-friendly volleyball program we can bring directly to campus for lower school students."
> "I wanted to ask what you guys currently offer for elementary students who may want to learn volleyball before middle school."
> "We only need one day per week, about one hour after dismissal, and we bring the coaches and equipment."
> "Do you think that could be useful for your students?"

## 14. If They Ask "What Do You Guys Do?"
> "We make sports more accessible for kids by bringing beginner-friendly programs directly to schools."
> "For private schools, we usually help lower school students try sports like volleyball in a simple after-school format."
> "We bring the coaches, the equipment, and the structure. The school just helps us identify a space and the best day."
> "Would that be something your lower school students may be interested in?"

## 15. Lower School Angle (the unlock for private schools)
Many private schools already have middle/upper school volleyball but students don't learn the sport until they're old enough to join. Students not already in club sports miss the early development window.

Our angle:
> "We help introduce the sport earlier so students have a chance to learn before middle school."

Use when school says:
- "We already have volleyball."
- "Our middle school has a team."
- "Athletics handles that."
- "We don't need another volleyball team."

Response:
> "That makes sense. What we usually focus on is the younger group — the lower school students who may not have a way to learn the sport yet. It's more developmental than competitive."
> "Do you think there would be interest from that age group?"

## 16. Space Conversation
Private schools may assume volleyball requires a full gym, full court, referees, competitive setup. Clarify: this is younger students, developmental, we bring equipment, doesn't need a full court.

> "What spaces do you usually have available after school?"
> "Do you have a gym, court, covered area, grass field, outdoor space, or concrete area that could work for a beginner group one day per week?"

If gym full:
> "Totally understand. Since this is developmental for younger students and we bring the equipment, it doesn't necessarily have to be a full gym. Is there any smaller space that could work?"

If middle school uses everything:
> "That makes sense. Does middle school take over all indoor and outdoor spaces, or is there any smaller area lower school could use one day per week?"

If still no:
> "No worries. Could I send you the information anyway in case something opens up later or another space becomes available?"

## 17. If Interested But Unsure About Space
> "No problem. Would it be okay if I send you the information and the flyer? You can take a look and see if there's a space that could make sense."
> "Who else should be included — athletics, lower school, or administration?"

Log: space uncertain, needs internal review, email sent, follow-up date. Move to **Follow-Up Needed** or **Interested**.

## 18. If They Say "Send Me An Email"
This is a good outcome. Do not keep pitching.

> "Absolutely. What's the best email for you?"
> "I'll send the flyer and a quick overview so you can review it or pass it to the right person."

If admin approval needed:
> "Should I copy anyone else, or would you prefer to forward it internally?"

> "Perfect. I'll send that over. Thank you."

Update GHL immediately after the call.

## 19. If They Are Short, Rushed, Or Dismissive
Do not argue. Ask for the email.

> "No worries at all. Could I send you the information by email so you have it in case it's useful later?"

If refused:
> "Totally understand. Thank you for your time."

Log clearly. Do not mark Not a Fit unless the right person confirms no interest, no space, no path forward.

## 20. If Not Interested In Volleyball
Don't give up immediately. Private schools have different student interests.

> "Totally understand. Volleyball may not be the best fit for every school."
> "Do you think another sport, like flag football, tennis, or pickleball, would be more interesting for your students?"

If positive:
> "That's helpful. I can send the information and mention that we can adjust the program based on what would fit the school best."

Log sport preference in GHL.

## 21. If They Already Have Another Program
Don't attack the competitor. Stay professional.

> "That makes sense. Are they mostly working with middle school students, or do they also offer something for lower school?"

If only older students:
> "Got it. Our program may still be useful for the younger students who are just starting out."

If same students:
> "Totally understand. Would it be okay if I send our information in case you ever want an additional option or a future alternative?"

Log: existing program, sport, age group served, contact response, follow-up worth it?

## 22. Email Rules
- No all-caps subject lines
- Question mark when appropriate
- Attach the flyer
- Personalize the name
- Verify school name, grade level, sport
- Friendly + professional tone
- Mention call if just spoke
- Easy to forward internally
- Don't make it too long

## 23. Subject Line Rules

**Good:**
- Volleyball for lower school students?
- After-school volleyball at [School Name]?
- Lower school sports program?
- Quick question about after-school programs
- Volleyball or another sport for lower school?
- Beginner-friendly sports program at [School Name]?

**Bad:**
- VOLLEYBALL PROGRAM
- FACILITY REQUEST
- URGENT
- PLEASE RESPOND
- PROGRAM APPROVAL NEEDED
- SPORTS OPPORTUNITY FOR YOUR SCHOOL

## 29. CRM Stages

| Stage | When to move here |
|---|---|
| **High Priority** | Private schools ready to be contacted |
| **First Outreach** | Spoke to someone / sent first email / got right contact / confirmed who handles after-school programming / lower school activities / athletics / enrichment |
| **Follow-Up Needed** | No answer / person not available / need to call back / sent email + waiting / they need to check admin/space/athletics/lower school / said to follow up later / contact not identified |
| **Interested** | Asked for flyer / asked for more info / agreed to review / open to volleyball or another sport / asked about space or scheduling / lower school interest mentioned / asked to include admin / specific follow-up date |
| **Not a Fit** | Right person confirmed: no interest / no space / no day / no lower school students / no path / has competing program / not eligible |

> [!warning] Do not move to Not a Fit just because the front office was confused or rushed.

## 31. GHL Reminders
If a school says:
- "Follow up in July"
- "Call me next week"
- "Send it and check back later"
- "We need to speak with admin"
- "We need to check space"

Do not rely on memory. Create a reminder or appointment in GHL on the assigned outreach calendar. Add follow-up date in the CRM note.

## 32. Gmail / Email Organization
Do not only star emails. Use the correct folder/label so follow-ups don't get lost.

After sending or receiving:
- Put in correct folder/label
- Update GHL
- Add clear note
- Create follow-up reminder if needed

If a draft needs review: leave as draft + notify Luis. Do not send sensitive/uncertain replies without review.

## 33. Draft Review Process
If unsure how to respond:
1. Draft the reply
2. Save as draft
3. Notify Luis ready for review
4. Luis edits/sends or approves

Use especially when school is warm, reply is complicated, admin approval mentioned, another program mentioned, facility issues, prior history with us, or tone needs care.

## 34. Call Recording
Record when possible and approved (GHL / ClickUp / Whisper / phone recording if approved). Purpose: improvement — listen for language that works, recurring objections, better SOP updates.

## 35. Call Quality
Before calling: AirPods, close to computer, clear mic, GHL open, school website open, old notes open if available, no multitasking.

## 36. What To Listen For
Who handles after-school programs / clubs / lower school activities / enrichment / athletics. Whether they have lower school / middle school volleyball / anything for elementary. Existing provider. Space usable / middle school takes over. Email/flyer request. Admin approval needed. Follow-up date.

Write it down immediately.

## 37. What Not To Do
- Lead with Facilitron unless school uses it
- Start with corporate pitch
- Sound like stranger or salesperson
- Pretend to be a parent
- Lie about why calling
- Over-explain the program
- Make volleyball sound like full competitive team
- Assume they need a full gym
- Argue if space is limited
- Keep pitching after "send me an email"
- Forget to attach flyer
- Forget to update GHL
- Forget to create follow-up reminder
- Use only stars instead of folders/labels
- Mark Not a Fit too early
- Speak negatively about competitors

## 38. Daily Outreach Goal
Realistic goal per outreach block:
- Contact 20 schools / people
- Get 2 people to hear us out
- Get 1 productive conversation
- Create clear follow-up actions from that conversation

> Goal is not perfection. Goal is to keep the fire going. Follow-up is what turns interest into onboarding.

## 39. Best Private School Call Flow
1. Open the private school contact in GHL
2. Check school type and notes
3. Check old Como notes if available
4. Check whether they have lower school / elementary grades
5. Call the school
6. "Hi, this is [Name]. How's it going?"
7. "I'm trying to confirm who handles after-school programs or clubs for lower school students."
8. "Who would be the best person to speak with about that?"
9. Get transferred or collect the email
10. If transferred, ask what they currently offer for lower school sports
11. Explain the program simply
12. Ask about interest
13. Ask about space
14. If they say send email, get the email and stop pitching
15. Update GHL
16. Send the existing email with flyer
17. Move the school to the correct stage
18. Add clear notes
19. Create a follow-up reminder
20. Follow up later

## 40. Final Standard

> "Hi, this is Yassine. How's it going? I'm trying to confirm who handles after-school programs or clubs for lower school students."

Then:
> "We have a beginner-friendly volleyball program we can bring directly to campus. I wanted to see what you guys currently offer for elementary students who may want to learn volleyball before middle school."

Then:
> "We bring the coaches, equipment, and practice structure. We only need one day per week, about one hour after dismissal."

Then:
> "Do you think this could be useful for your lower school students?"

If space is the concern:
> "Since this is developmental for younger students and we bring the equipment, it doesn't have to be a full gym. Is there any smaller indoor or outdoor space that could work one day per week?"

**Posture: friendly, familiar, relationship-first, direct, and easy to say yes to.**
