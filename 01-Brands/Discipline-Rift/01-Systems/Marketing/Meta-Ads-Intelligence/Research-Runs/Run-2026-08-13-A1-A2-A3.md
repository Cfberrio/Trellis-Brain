## Cross-source analysis

**Agreement (real):** three independent sources — Gardideh, Kerhoas, and Loomer via his Engagement route — converge on *defining your own qualified event* rather than accepting a generic one. Three assertions, not three bodies of evidence, but arrived at from different directions.

**Disagreement (unresolved, and central):** Haus finds mid-funnel optimization works *particularly well* below its low-volume boundary; Loomer holds that top-of-funnel optimization buys volume with no regard to quality. **This is precisely the A2 question and external evidence does not settle it.** The framework resolves it structurally rather than evidentially — by introducing the qualified-event rung (R2), which is neither pure up-funnel nor unreachable deep-funnel — and flags that only DR's own data can settle it properly.

**Context dependence:** every threshold in the batch assumes 10–1000× DR's volume. Haus's *low* bucket is 5,000 purchase events/week; the lowest budget guidance found anywhere in discovery was $50/day. DR ran ~$2.70/day.

**Weak / unevidenced:** Kerhoas's "massive impact" and server-side-tracking dismissal; Gardideh's 50% CAC reduction (no client, baseline or period); Loomer's identical-delivery-across-objectives claim.

**Strongest presented evidence:** Haus, by a clear margin — 640 controlled incrementality experiments, the only `self_reported_test` in the batch and the only source that measured rather than asserted. Discounted for vendor alignment and an enormous scale gap, but it is the one source that did an experiment.

**Framework-independence warning:** the "50 conversions per week per ad set" rule appears in nearly every source found, substantive and rejected alike. It traces to Meta's own learning-phase framing and must be counted as **one claim from one origin**, never as corroboration across many sources.

## Saturation check

**A1 — CONTINUE_RESEARCH (low priority).** Meta mechanics are now understood first-hand and are structurally decisive. But the stop_condition also requires practitioner evidence from *comparable registration businesses*, and discovery found none — only one general practitioner (Loomer), weakly evidenced, on a feature-availability argument. Another credible source could firm this, though it is unlikely to overturn a documented structural fact. **Does not block the gate.**

**A2 — ENOUGH_EVIDENCE (external).** The ladder is ranked with two-directional switch conditions anchored to Meta's own published figures. The Haus/Loomer conflict over up-funnel value is genuine and remains open — but it is a question about *outcomes at DR's scale*, which no external source can answer. Further practitioner sources would re-shuffle details, not the ladder. Correctly settled by DR data in Half 2.

**A3 — ENOUGH_EVIDENCE (external), with a capability dependency.** Concrete methods exist (ratio monitoring, correlation check, qualified-event gating, don't-trust-platform-CPA). The stop_condition's *"checkable with data DR can actually obtain"* clause is **not yet satisfied** — but the missing piece is DR extraction and registration data, not more external evidence. Recorded as a Half 2 requirement rather than an open research question.
