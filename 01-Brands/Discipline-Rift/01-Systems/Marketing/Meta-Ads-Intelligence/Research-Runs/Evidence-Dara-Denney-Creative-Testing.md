## 2. Evidence map

### D-1 — At low budget, one campaign; all of it is creative testing

- **Claim:** For brands spending ~$5k–$15k/month "or even a little bit lower," use **one campaign
  only**; there is no separate testing structure because everything in it is effectively a test.
- **Timestamp:** 0:32 — *"at this stage we are only using one campaign So in theory it's all
  creative testing especially if you're just getting started"*
- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_METHOD` (supported by 0:44: *"I've actually set up a few first
  campaigns for some new brands recently and this is exactly how I do it"* — an unquantified
  `REAL_ACCOUNT_EXAMPLE` with no results attached).
- **Account context:** ecommerce; $5k–15k/month or lower; sales objective optimizing purchases;
  new/early brands; conversion volume `NOT_DISCLOSED`.
- **Principle:** Below some budget level, splitting spend across structures buys nothing, because
  there is not enough spend for a separate structure to be readable — so consolidation is the
  correct default and the testing *is* the campaign.
- **Implementation:** One Advantage+ Shopping campaign; ~10 creatives inside it.
- **DR transferability:** **HIGH (principle) / LOW (implementation).** DR runs one campaign, one ad
  set, at ~$2.70/day — orders of magnitude below her floor, which makes the consolidation argument
  *more* forceful, not less. But her vehicle is **Advantage+ Shopping**, a sales/catalog product
  DR cannot use for season registrations.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `REINFORCES_EXISTING` — DR's current one-campaign /
  one-ad-set / no-separate-testing-campaign hypothesis. Adds an independent operator who reaches
  the same conclusion from budget logic rather than from DR's constraints.

### D-2 — The budget level at which a dedicated testing campaign becomes justified

- **Claim:** At ~**$1,000/day**, or **$30,000–$50,000/month**, add a **second, manual (non-ASC)
  campaign dedicated to creative testing**, in which **every test gets its own ad set**, holding
  **3–6 creatives**, funded at **$100–$200 per ad set per day**.
- **Timestamps:** 6:42 (budget threshold) · 7:22 (*"every single test gets its own ad set"*) ·
  7:46 (3–6 creatives) · 8:00 (*"I typically recommend $100 to $200 per adset per day"*).
- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_METHOD` with `OPERATOR_OBSERVATION` about the wider market
  (*"many ad Accounts at this stage actually just have two core campaigns"*).
- **Account context:** ecommerce; $30k–50k/month; two campaigns (ASC + manual testing);
  conversion volume `NOT_DISCLOSED`.
- **Principle:** A dedicated testing structure has a **funding floor** — it only becomes readable
  once each testing ad set can be given enough daily spend to produce a verdict in days.
- **Implementation:** Separate manual testing campaign; one ad set per concept; $100–200/ad set/day.
- **Numbers:** `PRACTITIONER_SPECIFIC` — `$1,000/day`, `$30k–$50k/month`, `$100–$200/ad set/day`,
  `3–6 creatives/ad set`. **No evidence is offered for any of them.** Per the domain's numeric
  threshold rule these are **not** adopted, converted, or scaled into a DR number.
- **DR transferability:** **HIGH as a directional gate / LOW as a number.** DR's entire daily spend
  is roughly **1/37th of her *minimum single testing ad set*.** The useful DR read is qualitative:
  DR is nowhere near the level at which a dedicated testing structure becomes fundable.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `REINFORCES_EXISTING` — "dedicated testing structures require
  enough budget/data." Adds an operator's own funding floor, quantified for *her* accounts.

### D-3 — Kill rule: unequal spend alone is not a verdict; the trigger is spend **and** CPA miss

- **Claim:** Do not turn ads off merely because they under-deliver. Turn one off when it is
  **both** missing the CPA goal **and** the algorithm is spending a lot on it. Otherwise let it run.
- **Timestamp:** 2:39 — *"I wouldn't worry too much about turning ads off unless they really aren't
  meeting your CPA goal and for some reason the algorithm is spending a lot on them otherwise I
  would just let them run"*
- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_METHOD`.
- **Account context:** ecommerce; low budget tier; purchase optimization; volume `NOT_DISCLOSED`.
- **Principle:** Low or zero spend on an ad is not information about that ad. The only ad you can
  judge is one the system has actually funded — so the kill condition needs a spend precondition.
- **Implementation:** Leave low-spend ads running; kill only high-spend + CPA-missing ads.
- **DR transferability:** **HIGH.** This is directly usable reasoning for an account where most ads
  receive little spend, and it does not depend on budget size or business type.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `REINFORCES_EXISTING` and **sharpens it** — the project holds
  "unequal spend is not automatically a creative verdict"; this supplies the missing operational
  converse (**what does** constitute a verdict) as an explicit two-condition rule.

### D-4 — Creative cadence: an initial batch, then 1–2 additions per week

- **Claim:** Start with **10 ad creatives** at low budget, then **add one or two more every week**.
  Lead with statics, not video, until messaging learnings exist.
- **Timestamps:** 1:36 (*"limit the ad creative to 10"*, statics-first) · 2:12 (static types:
  benefit call-out, us-versus-them, headline, "ugly ad", testimonial) · 2:39 (*"add another ad
  creative or two every single week"*).
- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_METHOD`.
- **Account context:** ecommerce; low budget tier; new brands.
- **Principle:** Introduce creative in a **continuous small trickle** rather than in large periodic
  drops, and learn message before investing in production-heavy formats.
- **Implementation:** 10 to start; +1–2/week; statics-first.
- **Numbers:** `PRACTITIONER_SPECIFIC` — `10`, `1–2/week`. No supporting evidence given.
- **DR transferability:** **MEDIUM.** The *cadence shape* (small, frequent, continuous) transfers.
  The *count* does not: 10 simultaneous creatives at DR's spend would divide an already thin
  signal. Statics-first has independent DR appeal on production cost, not proven here.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `REINFORCES_EXISTING` — small provisional creative batches,
  no fixed calendar gate. Adds a concrete continuous-trickle cadence rather than a review calendar.

### D-5 — ⚠ Adding a test into a performing campaign has repeatedly hurt performance

- **Claim:** Isolating testing from scaling keeps data cleaner and avoids "muddying performance with
  unproven tests." She reports having seen performance **tank** repeatedly after adding a creative
  test to an otherwise performing campaign.
- **Timestamp:** 10:21 — *"I can't tell you how many times I've added a creative test to an
  otherwise performing campaign and I saw performance tank"*
- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_OBSERVATION` — repeated personal observation, **no account, no
  numbers, no before/after shown**. Not a test.
- **Account context:** ecommerce; growth tier ($30k–50k+/month); `NOT_DISCLOSED` otherwise.
- **Principle:** Introducing unproven creative into a performing structure carries a **real risk of
  destabilising that structure**, not merely a risk of the new ad failing.
- **Implementation:** Separate testing campaign; duplicate winners into the ASC scaling campaign.
- **DR transferability:** **MEDIUM.** The risk is real and DR-relevant — DR's single ad set *is* its
  performing structure, so it has nowhere to absorb a bad test. But her mitigation (a second
  campaign) is the one DR cannot fund, per D-2, **from the same speaker**.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `CHALLENGES_EXISTING` — this is direct counter-pressure on
  DR's current existing-ad-set creative-introduction hypothesis.
  **The contradiction is preserved, not resolved.** Note that the *same source* prescribes
  existing-structure introduction at low budget (D-1) and warns against it at growth budget (D-5).
  She does not reconcile these herself. The honest reading is that the risk is real and the
  affordable mitigation is budget-gated — which leaves DR holding the risk with no funded exit.

- **Evidence type:** `OPERATOR_OBSERVATION` / `OPINION`.
- **Account context:** `NOT_DISCLOSED` for the referenced brands.
- **Principle:** Testing methodology is a decision under constraints, not a best practice.
- **Implementation:** n/a.
- **DR transferability:** **HIGH** as a framing discipline; nothing to implement.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `REINFORCES_EXISTING` — "no universal creative testing

### D-7 — Start at the real objective, not an intermediate one

- **Claim:** Even a brand-new brand seeking purchases should launch a **sales campaign optimizing
  for purchases** — not traffic, engagement, or add-to-cart.
- **Timestamp:** 1:04 — *"there is no way even if you are just starting off as a brand new brand…
  we are not starting with traffic campaigns we are not starting with engagement campaigns"*
- **Evidence type:** `UNSUPPORTED_ASSERTION` — the stated reason is *"meta's algorithm is so
  advanced in 2025 that you just have to tell what you want to go after."* That is a claim about
  platform behaviour with nothing behind it.
- **Account context:** ecommerce; new accounts; volume `NOT_DISCLOSED` — and volume is exactly the
  variable this claim turns on.
- **Principle:** Optimize for the outcome you actually want rather than a proxy.
- **Implementation:** Sales/purchase objective from day one.
- **DR transferability:** **LOW / UNKNOWN.** DR is a sparse-signal, geo-constrained registration
  business; the whole difficulty is whether the true outcome fires often enough to optimize toward.
  Her assertion carries no volume context, so it cannot speak to that.
- **Existing evidence relationship:** `NOT_RELEVANT` to a decision — Wave 1A already settled DR's
  objective/optimization-event framework from **first-party Meta documentation**, which outranks
  this. Recorded only so the claim is not silently lost.
- **Platform note:** per §16 of the run brief, *"meta's algorithm is so advanced"* is a
  practitioner claim and is **not** promoted to a platform fact.

## 3. What is genuinely new

1. **A budget-tiered map of when structure changes** (D-1, D-2), from an operator who runs all three
   tiers, with an explicit funding floor for a dedicated testing ad set. The project held
   "dedicated structures need enough budget" as a qualitative belief; this is the first source that
   states where *its own* floor sits and why.
2. **A two-condition kill rule** (D-3): spend **and** CPA miss. The project had the negative half of
   this ("unequal spend is not a verdict") but not the positive half.
3. **A recorded counter-observation** (D-5) that adding tests to a performing structure has
   repeatedly tanked performance — from the same source that recommends doing exactly that at low
   budget.
4. **Consolidation is not only a beginner posture** — six/seven-figure operators running the
   one-campaign method (D-6).
