## Repeated patterns inside this advertiser

**One creative message, eleven ads.** All 11 ads carry byte-identical body copy: "🏃‍♂️ACCEPTING NEW MEMBERS🏃‍♂️ KidStrong is a science-based training program designed for kids ages 1–11. Our certified coaches lead 45-minute classes… 96% of kids experience a boost in self-confidence." Same title on all 11: "Schedule a class today".

**What actually varies is the CTA and the media.** CTA splits `Book now` (6) / `Learn more` (3) / `See details` (2) on identical copy, with 9 VIDEO and 2 IMAGE. The observable structure is a CTA-and-media variation set with copy held constant. What it is *testing for* is not observable, and no result of it is observable.

**Three proof elements repeat in every ad:** a quantified claim (96% confidence boost), a credential (certified coaches), and a format spec (45-minute weekly classes).

**No offer and no urgency language anywhere in the HQ set.**

**Single national destination** (kidstrong.com, "Find your local KidStrong") on all 11.

## Potential relevance to DR

- **Observable:** copy held constant while CTA and media vary across 11 ads. **Why it may matter to DR:** it is a clean way to isolate one variable, and DR's account is small enough that muddled tests are a real risk to learning conditions. **Limit:** we can observe the structure and nothing about its result. Adopting the *structure* is defensible; assuming it worked is not.
- **Observable:** a quantified proof point (96% confidence boost) carried in every ad. **Why:** proof density is a lever DR can use. **Limit:** DR's claim rules are explicit — confidence-type outcomes may only appear as supported byproducts, and DR must not publish a measured claim it does not actually track. This transfers only if DR has its own real number.

*No recommendation is made here. These are observations for later evaluation against DR's own performance data.*
