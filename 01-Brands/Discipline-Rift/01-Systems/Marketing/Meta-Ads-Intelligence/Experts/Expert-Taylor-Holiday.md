## Contradictions within this author

- **Benchmarks vs. their own range data.** The same source publishes channel-level median iROAS figures *and* shows that one channel spans 0.5x-2.4x across brands. The medians are presented as usable starting points while the range arguably shows they are not. CTC resolves this with the confidence-weighting method ("progressive truth"); recorded here because a reader taking only the medians would be taking the weaker half of their own argument.
- **Volume as the answer.** Activation economics says launch more ads to buy more outliers; the offer discussion in `tIMQIWMd6LM` argues the constraint is often the offer and customer economics, not creative volume — and that a better offer raises the hit rate so each ad can carry more spend. These are not incompatible but they point production budgets in different directions, and the author does not reconcile them.

## Open questions from this author

1. The cost-control study's headline (reported elsewhere as $200M across 253 accounts, Mar 2025-Jul 2026) does not appear in the passages captured here. What is the published methodology — how were accounts selected, and were the bid strategies randomized or self-selected by the operators? Without that, `moderate` is the ceiling this evidence can reach.
2. Does the activation threshold ($1,000 lifetime spend) come from anything, or is it a convention? The entire volume model is built on it.
3. Every incrementality factor is derived from geo holdouts on multi-market ecommerce brands. Is there any published analogue for a single-metro service business, where a geo holdout would mean turning off the only market?
4. Their iROAS work says platforms over-report near the bottom of the funnel. DR's exposure is the reverse — under-reporting of offline/branded-search completion. Nothing in this source addresses that direction.

### TH-F4 — The Meta Canon: structure, and "never turn off an ad"

`-BYAC0ALW78`, 2026-07-07, Tony Chopp. The complete stated architecture:

- **Mechanism:** Andromeda (creative-first retrieval, Meta-claimed 10,000× model capacity) + **GEM**
  (generative evaluation model, the ranking layer at LLM scale). Targeting selections no longer drive
  delivery; creative attributes do.
- **Breakdown effect:** historical ROAS does not predict future ROAS. Chopp cites Meta's own docs —
  *"relationship between historical poor performance and future delivery is not deterministic."*
- **Campaign:** sales objective in ~95% of cases; **minimum ROAS** as the starting control; ASC or CBO;
  **inflated budgets with cost control** so Meta can buy cheap moments (Meta calls this *liquidity*);
  day-to-day spend variance is wanted, not suppressed.
- **Ad set:** 7-day click default; broad targeting with acquisition/retention exclusions;
  **ROAS target set at the ad set, not the campaign.**
- **Ad:** AI enhancements where the brand allows; dynamic creative test; multiple-URL testing.
- **Structure — two arms only.** *Evergreen* (acquisition + retention; ad sets filled sequentially to
  Meta's current **150-ad cap**, then a new ad set; acquisition also carries a dedicated DABA/catalog
  ad set). *Marketing moments* (time-bound: sale, launch, anything with a start and an end).
- **Signal:** CAPI non-optional; **Event Match Quality ≥7/10**; Shopify native integration preferred;
  **audit monthly** that server purchase events reconcile to actual order count. *"If Meta's receiving
  80% of your actual purchase events, the algorithm is optimising on incomplete data."*

**Underspend ladder, in order:**
1. Creative expansion — *"prioritise the diversity of concepts. Not minor variations. Big swings."*
2. **Bid-surface expansion** — duplicate evergreen campaigns into lowest-cost / bid cap / cost cap with
   the **same creative pool**. A different bidding profile reaches a different audience; no new
   segmentation required.
3. **Underspend expansion campaign** — one ASC, single ad set, value-optimised, same tROAS as
   evergreen, containing only ads whose spend is **< 3× the CPA target**. Pull from evergreen only;
   introduce no new creative; exclude anything that already scaled.
4. Constraint relaxation — change the ROAS target. Last resort, *"be very careful."*

**And the rule that contradicts standard practice:** *"never actually turn off an ad… If you're running
a CBO with cost controls and an ad has poor historical ROAS, the system has already reduced allocation
to it. Turning it off removes optionality without improving the outcome."* Backed by TH-F2 Study 2.

Media buyer's entire job, reduced to three items: budget allocation unconstrained at campaign level;
bid matches the business objective; launch new creative.

### TH-F5 — The creative-volume math, and the numbers that changed inside the window

Two datasets, four months apart, with different definitions. Both are recorded; the July one is larger
and supersedes April where they conflict.

**April 2026 (`8lAouUczg88`, single-brand and portfolio views):** outliers = ads spending ≥1 standard
deviation above account mean = **3.5% of ads driving 66% of spend** (194 of 5,595); typical range
2–7%. **79% of ads never reach $1,000 lifetime spend.** Time to discover an outlier: fastest quartile
18 days, **mean 39 days**, up to **81 days** — *"we can't call it early. The two-week timeframe is too
slow."* Top-10 creative turnover ~70% per quarter. Named account benchmarks (Nike, best in CTC's
portfolio): 38% zero-spend rate, 22% top-5 concentration, +58% ROAS *improvement* after week one, 61%
evergreen share → needs only 58 new ads/month; *"most of the time it's north of 100+ ads a month."*

**July 2026 (`xIc-Z9sKws0`, Creative Canon): 504 stores, $3.35B Meta spend, 7-day click, trailing 365
days.**

| Threshold | Share of all ads |
|---|---|
| got any spend at all | 74% |
| cleared $100 | 23.8% |
| **≥ $1,000 ("real contributor")** | **7.2%** |
| ≥ $5,000 | 1.8% |
| "whale" | **0.9%** |

**Raw hit rate from creation to whale ≈ 1 in 100.** Independently echoed by Motion's 2026 benchmark
(550,000 ads / $1.3B / 6,000 advertisers): ~5% become real winners, ~half get minimal or zero spend,
and top advertisers shipping **12–19+ new evergreen creatives per week** achieve more than double the
hit rate of smaller accounts. Motion is a separate data set and counts as corroboration.

⚠ **Contradiction to carry, not resolve:** April says 21% of ads reach $1,000; July says 7.2%. Single
brand vs 504 stores, and possibly different denominators (launched vs active). Do not average them.

**Creative demand formula:** `new ads needed = (target spend − current spend × carry rate) ÷ expected
spend per new ad`.
- Carry rate: **~70–75% of current spend carries forward on its own**; observed range 65%–122%; brands
  that scale budgets on proven winners before producing new creative carry 85–100%+.
- Whales: 82% survival, 70% spend retention. Winners: 71% survival, 90% spend retention.
- Of *new* ads: whales are 1% of them but **41% of new-ad spend**; winners 5% of them but **34%**.
- Five creative-score metrics. Three drive carry rate — **evergreen share** (→survival), **spend
  degradation** (→retention), **ad concentration** (→spend mix). Two drive expected $/new ad —
  **zero-revenue rate**, **ROAS degradation**. A 30% zero-revenue-rate brand needs 2–3× fewer ads than
  a 65% one. Two brands both spending $200k/month: good score needs **40** new ads, poor score needs
  **200**.

**Three levers, in order:** (1) **mine historical winners first** — revive last year's and last 30
days' top performers, re-enable paused winners, **point existing creative at new landing pages**;
(2) make better ads via **product × persona × pain point × URL**, diverse across format *and* entity
ID; (3) **carve the catalog layer out of the formula entirely** — catalog carry rate ≈100%, it is
routinely the highest-ROAS line item, and it is under-used as an *acquisition* lever (broad +
Advantage+ catalog).

### TH-F6 — Entity ID: the platform mechanism that makes low-volume "more ads" fail

`lq3ExjrrBEo` (Ian Jordan) and `UmfikyCo0rQ` (Holiday). Meta groups materially-similar creative under

ID, which is why Holiday flags catalog as an under-exploited diversity source.

### TH-F7 — Joy Sharma's offer-market-fit thesis. The most DR-transferable argument in the corpus.

`tIMQIWMd6LM` (2026-05-07) and `RmXJMrEq7E8` (2026-06-11). The one place this corpus argues *against*
the industry's creative-volume consensus, from inside an agency that sells creative volume.

**The auction escalates with your spend.** At $500/day you are bidding against mom-and-pop operators.
At $5,000/day you are bidding against operators with better margins, better bundles, better retention
and a higher tolerable CAC. *"When you spend more money, you play with more sophisticated players.
That is the reality of the game."* A growth plateau is therefore usually **not** a creative problem, an
account-structure problem, or a new-channel problem — it is the point at which your offer economics
stop clearing the auction. *"When businesses try to solve for a business problem with a marketing
solution, that's where businesses go to die."*

**The diagnostic that tells you creative is not your problem:** check CPM, then CTR, then CPC. If CTR
is above ~2% and CPC is near $1, *"you're probably good… your ceiling potential might be 10–20%
improvement."* Then plot your **AOV against conversion rate** on a log curve and find where you sit
versus the industry. Low AOV demands high CVR (a $40 product should convert nearer 6%, not 2%); high
AOV tolerates low CVR. Being above the curve is the win condition — *"you just need to be above
average"* — but you can be above the AOV/CVR curve **and still lose** if the industry's CAC exceeds
what your unit economics let you bid.

**Hit rate is downstream of the offer, not the creative.** Without offer-market fit, hit rate sits near
**2%** and spend-per-ad near **$20**; with it, hit rate rises toward **7%** and spend-per-ad toward
**$150** — same production cost, so production becomes affordable. *"When you have offer-market fit,
statics will work."* CTC's Marketing Moments service ships **statics only** and guarantees revenue.

**Volume-by-variation is the trap:** *"they're going to take the same image, they're going to change
the headline, they're going to make very small changes… you made 10 times more ads and it 1/10'd
because you basically made the same ad."*

**The framework:** `(landing pages + offers + creative) ^ customer journey ^ moment`. Not linear. The
moment is the exponent.

**Say no to brand awareness below scale:** *"Brand awareness is not a real thing when you're spending
two grand a day. You're aiming for product-market fit, let alone branding."* Also: do not open new
channels before you have won the one you are on.

### TH-F8 — Marketing moments as the testing container. The single most portable idea for DR.

Joy Sharma, `tIMQIWMd6LM`; Holiday, `agsLE7078qI`. **Never test evergreen. Never split-test traffic.**

Why moments work mechanically: *"Your best ROAS day is never an evergreen ad. It's a moment of urgent
purchase demand where conversion rate spikes and you get outsized value return."* A conversion-rate
spike is an arbitrage on the auction's expected-action-rate term.

**Cadence: four moments a month — two public, two unlisted.** Public ones: find the brand for whom this
calendar date is the biggest day of their year, and learn from them. Unlisted ones (announcement bar
only, no hero banner, no all-list email, unlisted landing page) are where you test evergreen offers.
*"When you can attach something to a marketing moment, you will just not lose money on testing."*
Then **graduate the winner into evergreen** by stripping the moment and keeping the offer, page and
angle. Do not split-test — launch a *new* campaign to a *new* page so the spend is incremental.

*"To you as a brand, you're doing something every day. To the end customer, you're doing one thing a
month."* And: 80% of a sale week's revenue is from customers who have never seen your previous
promotions, so repeating an offer is fine.

**Launch sequence for a moment** (`dHjLl3tJCjc`): email VIP early access at T−48h → email + SMS at
T−24h → **ads to prospecting only for the first 48 hours** → open to remarketing after. Extract the
existing file by email first; ads exist to acquire.

**Planning discipline** (Tyler McCann, Taste Salud, `zeJF6EeicwA`): a **weekly all-hands roadmap call**
walking the calendar from this week to next January, every week. Work backwards from the launch date
through every lead time. Their World Cup / Mexican National Team launch was in motion 10+ months ahead.
The launch was allowed to break even or lose money because the halo lifts the whole catalog. *"Keep
options on the bench"* — spare concepts ready so you react rather than scramble.

### TH-F9 — Measurement: the incrementality benchmark table, and the two numbers that moved

`jBWJWsXFJd0` (2026-07-02), Luke Austin. Hundreds of geo-holdout tests, results accepted at **≥90%
confidence (p ≤ 0.1)**, measured across every distribution point and every customer cohort.

Median incrementality factors — the ratio of true incremental revenue to platform-reported revenue:

| Channel | Median iROAS factor |
|---|---|
| **Facebook acquisition** | **1.14** (range 0.5 – 2.4) |
| YouTube | 1.10 |
| Google non-brand | 0.67 |
| Facebook non-acquisition | 0.60 |
| Google branded search | 0.27 |

Restated three weeks later (`COAQnHl-f34`, Steve Reekock) as **Meta acquisition 113.4%**, with the
observed spread running **from 25% to above 200%**.

⚠ **Two figures moved inside the window and the later ones supersede.** (a) Meta acquisition was quoted
as "120%" in May–June (`MZGF5uBL308`, `MpGIB6hTTV8`) and as 1.14 / 113.4% in July. (b) **YouTube was
quoted in May as "2× to 2.5× what the platforms are reporting" and lands at 1.10 in the July Canon** —
a very large revision, and neither is acknowledged as a change on air.

**The mechanism that decides where a brand lands in that 25%–200% range,** and it is the most useful
sentence in the measurement material: *"if Meta's attribution is making up 60 or 70% of your new
customer revenue, you probably are not running above 100% on incrementality… because there's no more
additional customers for it to grab."* Second driver: **AOV as a proxy for consideration length.** High
AOV → longer deliberation → purchase falls outside the 7-day click window → Meta *under*-reports → the
factor goes up.

**Method:** progressive truth. Start on the aggregate benchmark; weight the first brand-specific test
against it by confidence rather than jumping to it; converge on the median as tests accumulate. Then
normalise every channel to iROAS so a platform-reported 3.2× on Meta and 12.5× on Google brand become
a comparable 3.7 and 3.1. *"iROAS is subordinate to reality"* — when iROAS improves and contribution
margin does not, the measurement system is what is wrong.

Holiday's own self-criticism at the client summit (`bDJRINefLok`): *"Right now, and this is our
responsibility, we are testing way too little. Every one of you should have an expectation that there
are one to two geo-holdout incrementality studies running every single month."*

### TH-F10 — The floor DR sits under, stated three times

- **Geo-holdout incrementality is unavailable at DR's scale.** Holiday, `tlNHaUkZsTI`: *"if your store
  is spending 10 to $15,000 a month, you likely aren't creating enough conversions in any specific geo

- **But conversion lift studies (CLS) are not gated the same way.** `MpGIB6hTTV8`, Meta Summit May

  best option I have available to myself, 7-day click as the largest window of optimisation, is

**Against Meta, inside CTC's own material**

Meta's stated position (via Chopp) is that historical performance does not determine future delivery —
CTC uses that to justify never pausing an ad. Meta simultaneously ships Mona with no methodology
(TH-P3-22) and pushes conversion-lift studies as the measurement complement. CTC's read is that Meta
optimises for spend volume, not for the advertiser's contribution margin (`1VKwf5iUE-A`).

**Against other panel members**

- **Never turn off an ad** (TH-P3-04) is the sharpest disagreement this panel contains. Almost every
  other operator prescribes kill rules. CTC is the only one who tested one and published the null.
- **39-day mean to find an outlier, 81 days at the tail** (TH-P3-10) versus the short creative-test
  windows used elsewhere in the panel, including Denney.
- **Structure:** CTC's evergreen-plus-moments two-arm split with sequential ad sets is a *third*
  position, distinct from both Heath's late-window one-campaign/one-ad-set consolidation and the
  multi-campaign structures Heath dismisses. Note that Heath's gate — under 50 conversions/week, do not
  split — would put DR on the consolidated side, while CTC's moments arm requires splitting by
  definition.
- **Creative volume:** Joy Sharma is the only voice in the panel arguing that the volume prescription
  is downstream of an offer problem and that *"statics will work"* when the offer is right. Ben Heath
  breaks from the 20+ consensus on feasibility grounds; Sharma breaks from it on causal grounds. Those
  are different objections and both should survive into the cross-expert synthesis.
- **Independence:** Holiday and Faris are one cluster. Their agreement on cost controls is one voice.
  Their disagreement (bid cap vs cost cap; dynamic bidding at low volume) is the informative part.
