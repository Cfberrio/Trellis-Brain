## How to read this

Every problem carries two tags.

**Confidence**
- `Stated` — Luis said it directly, or it is documented in the repo. Traceable.
- `Inferred` — a pattern drawn from what was said, not said outright. Argue with these first.

**Citation caveat.** These Q numbers are cited from the transcript but are not reproduced in `founder-voice-insight-bank.md` or `dr-marketing-context-from-qa.md`, so they have not been cross-checked: **Q2, Q6, Q8, Q10, Q16, Q19, Q21, Q43, Q45, Q49.** Verify against `Founder-QA-Library-Full.md` before treating any of them as settled.

**Type** — this is the one that decides what you do about it.
- `DR gap` — we are not doing something well. Fixable internally. This is the backlog.
- `Market condition` — the industry created it. Not ours to fix, ours to position against.
- `Design constraint` — a true fact about how elementary-age children (K through 5th) and their parents behave. Not a problem to eliminate. The method exists to absorb it. If a coach is surprised by one of these, that is a training gap, not a kid problem.

Confusing a design constraint for a DR gap is how programs end up blaming children for being children.

## The three that carry the rest

Most of this list is downstream of three problems. Solve these and a dozen entries below get smaller on their own.

**1 · Coach alignment and method adoption (C1 + C2).** Luis names this as the biggest friction in the organization. The method is DR's entire thesis, and it is unevenly run. Everything students struggle with is supposed to be handled by the method, so partial adoption means partial product. Hardest of the three, highest ceiling.

**2 · Invisible progress (P1).** The infrastructure to fix it already exists — the parent dashboard and the weekly update are built. This is the cheapest large win on the board and it unlocks four other parent problems.

**3 · The method is invisible outside the building (X1).** A pure content problem, cheapest of all to fix, and it directly neutralizes the two market conditions that cost DR the most. Scope note per Luis (2026-08-09): making the method visible means telling parents a method *exists* and what it does — never teaching them its names or mechanics. The science stays internal.

**One reconciliation to settle.** `dr-marketing-context-from-qa.md` §1 concludes the binding constraint is purchase-moment invisibility — "the whole marketing job is to make the teaching visible before the parent pays." This list ranks that third and puts coach alignment first. Both cannot drive the roadmap. The ops read: C2 makes the marketing claim conditional — if the method's delivery rate is unknown, marketing scales an unverified claim, and the Value Guarantee is written on that same claim. **Ranking pending Luis's review.** Whichever way it lands, update both files in the same pass.

# A · PARENTS

### P1 · Progress is invisible to parents, obvious to coaches, and the child is a bad narrator.
`Stated` · `DR gap — infrastructure now exists`

Parents are not at practice. Asking the child returns "fine." So the parent evaluates a season on the only signal available to them, which is whether their child seemed happy that afternoon.

**Cost:** re-enrollment gets decided on mood instead of progress. Real development goes unpaid for.
**Closest lever:** the parent dashboard — per-practice coach updates, progress, photos (cadence per Luis's approved campaign copy, 2026-08-09). Consistency of send is now the whole job.
**Boundary (Luis, 2026-08-09):** the dashboard supplements the parent-child conversation, it never replaces it. Its job is to hand the parent the coach's perspective so they can ask more and deeper questions — not to make asking unnecessary. Copy or product framing that says "you won't have to ask" is wrong.

### P2 · Parents pull the child at the exact moment the growth was starting.
`Stated (Q20)` · `Design constraint + education gap`

Every learning curve has a stretch that is not fun. The child says they want to stop. The parent reads that as evidence the child isn't suited to it, when it is the ordinary middle of learning something.

**Cost:** the highest-value moment in a season is also the most common exit point.
**Closest lever:** tell parents this is coming before it arrives. A single pre-season line — "somewhere around week three it stops being easy, and that is the part that counts" — converts a churn trigger into a milestone they were expecting.

### P3 · Parents read a mental problem as a physical one.
`Stated (Q24)` · `Design constraint`

A discouraged child looks like an uncoordinated child from the sideline. The parent concludes their kid isn't athletic. The actual state is defeated, and it is reversible.

**Cost:** children get written off by the person paying for the season.
**Closest lever:** name the distinction in parent comms. It costs one sentence and it changes what a parent thinks they are watching.

### P4 · Parents compare programs on the two variables that differentiate least.
`Stated (Q51)` · `Market condition`

The two questions Luis gets every season are how much and when does it start. Nobody asks what method you use. So programs get compared on price and calendar, which is exactly where DR's advantage does not live.

**Cost:** DR competes on the ground where it is weakest by default.
**Closest lever:** teach the question. Give parents the criteria before they shop. Directly connected to X1 — and bounded by it: the criteria is "does a method exist," not a methods curriculum for parents.
Worth holding against this: price and start date are already published per school. Part of P4 may be a surfacing problem on the per-school page rather than parent behavior, which changes the lever. Marketing implication is owned by `dr-marketing-context-from-qa.md` §1.

### P5 · Parents see the coordination gap and not what it means.
`Stated (Q27)` · `Design constraint`

Parents notice their child is clumsy. Very few connect that to perception, spatial awareness, and body control, or know that it is trainable and that this is roughly the last easy window to train it.

**Cost:** a real reason to enroll gets read as a personality trait.
**Closest lever:** parent-facing content that explains the developmental window in plain language.

### P6 · Sideline pressure removes the thing that keeps a child in the sport.
`Stated (Q37)` · `Design constraint`

When a parent leans too hard, the sport stops being fun. Fun is what the environment, the coaches, and above all the teammates supply, and pressure from home overrides all three.

**Cost:** DR loses children for reasons that happen in the car, not on the court.
**Closest lever:** coaches are the only ones positioned to see this early. Give them language for it.

### P7 · A missed practice costs more than a missed practice.
`Stated (Q52)` · `Education gap`

Three absences reads to a parent as three skipped sessions. What is actually lost is the breakthrough moment that was building, and the child's place in a group that keeps forming without them.

**Cost:** attrition that neither side recognizes as attrition until the child has quietly dropped out.
**Closest lever:** attendance in the weekly update already exposes this. Attach one line of meaning to the number.

### P8 · The parent lever that works best is the one nobody tells parents about.
`Stated (Q30, Q36)` · `DR gap`

Luis gives two answers. Q30 — three things to say: there is always a chance to start again, we can always give a bit more, losing is also winning. Q36 — ask a question after practice that surfaces progress the child cannot see themselves. *Inferred:* most parents do not know that is the lever, so they default to advice or correction the child did not ask for.

**Cost:** the partnership DR wants with parents has no instructions attached at the moment a parent would use them.
**Closest lever:** one observation cue per weekly update. Not advice — a specific thing to ask about this week.

### P9 · Parents buy the promise that is furthest away and least verifiable.
`Stated (Q53)` · `Market condition`

Clubs sell college pathways, connections, and exposure (Q53). Luis calls it smoke. *Inferred:* the promise is effectively unfalsifiable for years, which is part of why it sells.

**Cost:** DR's near-term, verifiable claims compete against a distant unfalsifiable one. Whether DR is actually losing parents to it is unmeasured.
**Closest lever:** do not argue with the promise. Make the near-term result concrete enough that it outweighs a distant maybe. The Tier certificate and the weekly update are the artifacts that do this.

### P10 · The season that gets sold and the season that gets delivered are not described the same way.
`Inferred (from Q35)` · `DR gap`

Marketing says fun-first and beginner-friendly, which is true. Luis says plainly that the journey has stretches that are not fun and are not easy, which is also true. Only one of those is in the funnel.

**Cost:** the gap between the two shows up as mid-season disappointment and it lands on the coach.
**Closest lever:** put the honest version in the pre-season email, where it builds trust, instead of letting a parent discover it in week three, where it reads as a bait and switch. This is the same fix as P2.

# B · COACHES

### C1 · Coach supply is not the bottleneck. Coach alignment is.
`Stated (Q3)` · `DR gap` · **load-bearing**

Luis names this as the largest friction in the organization: getting every coach to be present, to be conscious of the impact they are having, and to accept that being a good coach starts with being a good teacher of the sport.

**Cost:** the ceiling on every other initiative. A dashboard, a curriculum, and a season structure all deliver through a coach or they do not deliver.
**Closest lever:** this is not a memo problem. Alignment came from the coach-proximity initiatives that ran last season, and those worked. Make them a permanent cadence rather than a season push.

### C2 · The curriculum is written. It is not uniformly run.
`Stated (Q7, Q9)` · `DR gap` · **load-bearing**

New teaching methods went into the curriculum and are the most significant advance of the last season. Luis is also direct that not all coaches apply them equally and that it takes time to land in practice.

**Cost:** the method is DR's entire differentiator and its actual delivery rate is unknown. A parent at one school buys a different product than a parent at another.
**Closest lever:** measure adoption before pushing more curriculum. You cannot improve a rollout you are not tracking. See also C3.

### C3 · How fast a coach reaches the correct method is the real performance metric, and nothing measures it.
`Stated (Q23)` · `DR gap`

Luis's own answer for what makes the biggest difference in a child's progress: the longer a coach takes to apply the right method, the longer the child's learning is delayed. That is a coach performance variable, it is the one that matters most, and there is no instrument for it.

**Cost:** coach quality gets assessed on vibe, results, and whoever Luis happened to watch that week.
**Closest lever:** a short observation rubric used on site visits. Three to five items, scored the same way every time. This is the smallest change on this list with the largest downstream effect.

### C4 · The product is one person's energy on a given afternoon.
`Stated (Q39)` · `DR gap`

Luis is explicit that the enthusiasm starts with the coach — the energy, the initiative, the love for the sport all originate there and then transfer. That is accurate, and it means the delivered product varies with an unmanaged human variable.

**Cost:** quality variance that no curriculum can flatten on its own.
**Closest lever:** structure absorbs some of it. A session format that carries energy by design asks less of a tired coach on a Thursday.

### C5 · Coaching quality transfers by proximity to Luis.
`Inferred (Q2, Q6, Q16, Q45)` · `DR gap`

The strongest coach development stories in the interview all involve Luis being physically present — supervising a practice, visiting a school, taking a team himself. That is real mentorship and it works. It also does not scale past his calendar.

**Cost:** growth is capped by one person's travel schedule, and it is the same person who has to run everything else.
**Closest lever:** make the transfer reproducible. What Luis does on a site visit needs to be written down before it can be delegated.

### C6 · One coach down and the team has no second option.
`Inferred (Q19)` · `DR gap`

Sebastián got injured and Luis covered the team himself, in a sport he had never coached, learning it inside a week. It worked. It worked because the founder was available.

**Cost:** every team is one injury away from a cancelled session or an unqualified substitute. At 55 schools that is not a rare event.
**Closest lever:** a named backup per team, and a session plan a substitute can actually run.

### C7 · What happens on the court travels up slowly, and mostly through site visits.
`Inferred (repo: communication-rules §6)` · `DR gap`

Coach communication is a WhatsApp group where operations sends directives down. There is no structured channel carrying observations back up. The organization learns what is happening in the field when Luis goes and looks.

**Cost:** problems surface late and only at the schools someone visited.
**Closest lever:** the coach dashboard already handles schedule and attendance confirmation, so it is the nearest surface for a structured post-session field. Confirm it supports coach-to-operations input before scoping.

# C · STUDENTS

Almost everything in this section is a `Design constraint`. These are not failures. They are the conditions the method was built for, and a coach who treats them as obstacles is working against the design.

### S1 · The fun of a drill competes with the teaching inside it.
`Stated (Q8)` · `Design constraint`

The most common error is that children cannot stay on the simple thing. Teaching a volleyball pass means the form is the whole point, and the child drifts, complicates it, or gets pulled by the game wrapped around it.

**Closest lever:** teaching a skill and holding attention on it are two separate jobs. Coaches who only plan the first one lose the second.

### S2 · The first time a skill fails, the conclusion is "I can't."
`Stated (Q43)` · `Design constraint`

Children go straight to being no good at it and it being impossible. That reaction is immediate and near-universal at this age.

**Closest lever:** DR's answer is already explicit — there is always another try, you can ask a question, you can use the feedback. It has to be taught out loud, repeatedly, as content. Not assumed.

### S3 · Kids want the parts that feel good and want out of the parts that don't.
`Stated (Q20)` · `Design constraint`

Not every moment of a sport is satisfying. The wanting-to-quit reflex when it stops being satisfying is normal, and it is the upstream half of P2.

**Closest lever:** coaches see it before parents do. What the coach says in that moment determines which way it goes.

### S4 · Attention is the prerequisite skill and most children do not arrive with it.
`Stated (Q12, Q34)` · `Design constraint`

Luis's read is that most kids today have some degree of attention deficit, and the method is intense specifically because of it. High energy, rotating formats, no dead repetition, whole-group resets when someone drifts.

**Closest lever:** already designed for. Depends entirely on C2 — the method only absorbs this where the method is actually run.

### S5 · Children arrive certain they are already good.
`Stated (Q49)` · `Design constraint`

They come in thinking they can take on the world. What the program needs from them is a learner's posture and an open ear for the coach.

**Closest lever:** handled in week one or not at all. This is part of why Q21's week-one list is non-negotiable.

### S6 · Asking for help reads as admitting failure.
`Stated (Q46)` · `Design constraint`

Luis flags a child asking a coach for help as a genuine marker, precisely because most people find it hard to admit they need it. Adults included.

**Closest lever:** make asking visibly safe and visibly rewarded. It is one of the few behaviors here that a coach can reinforce in the moment at no cost.

### S7 · The shared-space instinct is not there yet.
`Stated (Q29, Q41)` · `Design constraint`

Picking up a teammate's ball, cheering the team with shouts and applause, encouraging instead of yelling at them, listening instead of interrupting. These arrive later than the physical skills and they are the clearest signal of real growth. (Q41 counts loud cheering as the positive behavior — the contrast is encouragement vs. yelling *at* someone, not loud vs. quiet.)

**Closest lever:** name them out loud when they happen. Which runs directly into X2 and X3.

# D · SYSTEM

### X1 · DR's real differentiator is invisible outside the building.
`Stated (Q4) + Inferred (Q18)` · `DR gap` · **load-bearing**

DR has named, deliberate teaching methods — blocked practice, serial practice, retrieval practice — being implemented on a scientific basis. Luis says it himself: a flyer does not do justice to what happens on that court, and parents cannot picture a practice they have never seen.

**Cost:** the one thing no competitor can copy quickly is the one thing prospective parents never hear about. It is also the direct cause of P4.
**Closest lever:** put the method on the site and into ads. It is written down already. It just lives in an interview instead of in front of a parent.

### X2 · The most valuable thing in a practice is the thing least likely to be written down.
`Stated (Q10)` · `DR gap`

A good practice gets called a good practice. What actually happened — the child who stopped interrupting, the one who started collaborating, the shift in the room's energy — goes unnamed.

**Cost:** the value DR creates is real and unrecorded, so it cannot be shown to a parent, credited to a coach, or used as proof in marketing. This is the supply-side twin of P1.
**Closest lever:** one structured field after each session. Same field as C7. One input, three problems.

### X3 · Nothing measures what DR says it actually values.
`Inferred (Q29, Q41, Q46 against the Tier system)` · `DR gap`

The Tier system measures sport skill, and it does that well. Everything Luis identifies as the real point — asking for help, lifting a teammate, listening, doing the right thing regardless of how you feel — is measured by nothing.

**Cost:** DR cannot prove its own thesis, improve against it, or sell it without sounding like every other program making unbacked character claims. It is also why the honest marketing rule blocks those claims: they are true and they are uninstrumented.
**Closest lever:** pick two behaviors, track them the way tiers are tracked. Two is enough to start. This is the entry that would change the most about what DR can credibly say.

### X4 · The value accrues over seasons. The product is sold in six-week units.
`Inferred` · `Structural`

Habits, discipline, reasoning under pressure, and the shift from self to team are multi-season outcomes. The unit of sale is six weeks. The girl who still says DRVC a year later was not made in six weeks. **(Consent-gated: that story involves a minor. Luis's and the family's sign-off before any public use; can run without identifying detail.)**

**Cost:** DR is structurally required to undersell its own thesis at the point of purchase, and re-enrollment quietly matters more than acquisition. Worth confirming whether the business is currently resourced that way.
**Closest lever:** treat season one as the acquisition product and season two as where the actual promise starts being kept. Then check whether anything in the funnel is built for that handoff.

### X5 · The site speaks two languages. It is unconfirmed whether the comms layer does.
`Inferred (site snapshot: ES toggle in nav)` · `DR gap — open question`

The public site carries an ES toggle. Central Florida's parent base is heavily bilingual. Whether the weekly update, the parent dashboard, and the coach-to-parent messages are available in Spanish is not documented anywhere in this repo.

**Cost:** if they are English-only, the exact system built to fix P1 excludes part of the paying base, and it excludes them silently.
**Closest lever:** confirm the answer first. If it is no, that is a top-three item, not a nice-to-have.
