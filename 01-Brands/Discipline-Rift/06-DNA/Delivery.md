## Purpose
How DR proves the promise + drives retention. Activation point + onboarding + in-season delivery engine + save plays + planned ascension.

## A. Critical Path To Results (never break)

### 0. Define the activation point (leading indicator of retention + re-enroll)
Template: **"Every customer that does X thing or gets Y result stays longer than customers who don't."**

Activation-point candidates — pick one and measure for a season:
- **AP1 (attendance):** "Every family whose child attends first session + at least 1 more in the first 7 days finishes the season at a higher rate."
- **AP2 (confidence):** "Every family whose child reports 'I want to come back' by session #2 enrolls next season more."
- **AP3 (parent engagement):** "Every parent who completes the 'setup' homework (calendar + pickup plan) shows up more consistently."

How to finalize: analyze longest-staying / highest-spending customers → find common actions → update onboarding to drive to that activation point.

### 1. Enrollment → confirmation → "tell them where to start"
Onboarding = teach customers how to hit the activation point.

Onboarding quality rules: **custom > generic, personal > group, live > recorded, carrots > sticks, some beats none.**

### 2. First session attended (activation attempt #1)
Don't "hope" they show. Force the first attendance milestone — show rate is the first bottleneck.

### 3. Track attendance every session (leading indicator + intervention trigger)
Attendance is a proven churn predictor. When attendance drops, intervene early.

### 4. 2x/week proactive touchpoints (progress + praise + problem-solving)
Praising participation + progress and fixing small problems = core retention lever.

### 5. End-of-season "next step" (ascension planned, not improvised)
Customers buy more over time. Plan ascension — it's more likely to happen if you plan for it than if you don't.

## B. Onboarding (Day 0–7) — Force the early win

> **Principle:** faster wins reduce churn. Updates ARE wins. Customers form lasting impressions within the first 48 hours.

### Day 0 (same day as purchase) — Welcome + Where-to-start
Email/text includes:
- Exact dates + times + location
- Pickup/dismissal flow
- What to bring (keep minimal)
- "Beginner welcome / safe-to-learn" expectation
- **Homework (carrot):** "Reply 'READY' and I'll send your child's first-day intro + what to expect."

### Day 1–2 (48-hour forced win window)
- **Progress/clarity update** even if nothing changed ("You're all set. Here's what week 1 looks like.") because **updates = wins**.
- **Activation-driving homework (carrot-based):**
  - Add sessions to calendar
  - Confirm pickup plan
  - Child chooses 1 goal ("make 1 friend" / "learn 1 skill")

### Day 3–7 (drive to first attendance)
- Reminder cadence focused only on actions that predict success — don't add fluff
- If no confirmation → personal nudge (text/call) = **personal > group**

## C. In-Season Delivery Engine (every week)

### Session structure (non-negotiable, 60 min)
| Time | Block | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 5 min | Arrival + name/connection ritual | Belonging |
| 10 min | Warmup game | Fun-first |
| 20 min | Skill ladder | Safe-to-learn progression |
| 20 min | Guided play / scrimmage | Use the skill |
| 5 min | "Win moment" + preview next session | Anchor + anticipation |

### Attendance tracking SOP (every session)
- Mark attendance live
- **Trigger:** if attendance drops (or they miss session #1) → intervene immediately. Reaching out right when attendance declines can "rescue" the customer.

### 2x/week parent touchpoints (fixed schedule)
- **Touchpoint A:** praise participation ("show rate" recognition)
- **Touchpoint B:** progress update ("what they improved")

> Consistent praise + solving little problems is a retention driver.

## D. Save Plays (when things start to slip)

### If they miss the first session
- Personal outreach within 24 hours
- Reframe: "no problem — here's exactly how to jump back in next session" (remove embarrassment)

### If attendance drops
- Reach out **at the drop**, not after 2–3 missed sessions
- Solve friction (pickup confusion, kid anxiety, schedule conflict)
- Re-sell the value in the context of their original goals

## E. End-Of-Season "Next Step" (planned ascension)

Weeks 4–6 (or final 2 weeks):
- Show progress (simple before/after)
- Present next season or sport swap as the obvious continuation
- Customers buy more over time, and buying again makes them less likely to churn → **ask them to buy**

## Roles Required

### Program Operator (Admin)
- Enrollment confirmations
- Onboarding messages
- Parent comms cadence
- Attendance reporting

### Head Coach
- Session quality
- Safe-to-learn delivery
- "Win moments"
- Kid confidence

### Assistant Coach / Floater
- Equipment flow
- Beginner support
- Keeps experience stable as headcount rises

## Quality Control (Weekly Dashboard — minimum viable)

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| Show rate session #1 | Activation #1 |
| % attendance by week | Retention leading indicator |
| Intervention outreaches sent | Save-play execution |
| Re-enroll intent (Y/N) final week | Future season pipeline |

This keeps the team centered on **activation + attendance** — the highest-leverage retention drivers.
