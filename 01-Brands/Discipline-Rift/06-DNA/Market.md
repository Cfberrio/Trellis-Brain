## Purpose
Where DR plays. Submarket, winnable wedge, competitor set, decision constraints, reach.

## Market / Submarket
- **Market:** Youth sports participation for kids ages **6–12**.
- **Submarket:** **On-campus, after-school, parent-paid sports seasons** where schools provide access (space + permission + distribution) and parents enroll per season (fixed term).

## Winnable Wedge (where we win first)

**School type (first rollout):**
Elementary schools with (1) after-school traffic already in place (car riders + aftercare), (2) space availability (gym/field), and (3) openness to enrichment vendors.

**Parent type (first rollout):**
Busy parents who want their kid active + social **but won't drive to off-campus sports** and don't want "club life." They value: convenience, beginner safety, predictable commitment.

**Sport spearhead (first rollout):**
Pick **one** default spear per school cluster (e.g., **volleyball** OR **flag football**) based on local demand + facility fit. Other sports stay as **swaps**, not headline positioning.

> Why this matters: it tells you where you win first and prevents "generic enrichment" positioning.

## Why This Market Now

**The "why now" isn't "parents are busy". It's:**
- The biggest blocker isn't interest — it's **friction** (driving, timing, signup complexity).
- On-campus after-school removes the friction by default, so the "yes" is easier than clubs/parks.
- Parents are increasingly choosing options that feel **safe-to-learn** and not "elite-only", especially for first-time sports.
- Schools want more participation, but only if it's **low-lift, low-risk, and operationally clean** (dismissal + supervision + communication).

## Competitor Set

**Direct sports alternatives**
- Local youth sports clubs / academies (including "elite")
- City recreation leagues / parks programs
- YMCA / community centers / church leagues
- Private coaches / small group training
- School-run clubs / intramurals

**True substitute alternatives (often the real comparison)**
- **Aftercare programs** (structured supervision)
- **Other enrichment vendors** (STEM, tutoring, music, martial arts, dance)
- **Babysitter / family pickup solutions**
- **Do nothing** (screens, homework, unstructured time)

## Internal Comparison Table

| Option | Logistics | Intensity | Cost / Commitment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DR (on-campus season)** | On-campus after school; minimal driving; clean pickup/aftercare flow | Beginner-friendly; fun-first; safe-to-learn | Fixed per-season commitment; predictable; "everything included" framing |
| Club / academy | Off-campus travel; weekends/tournaments | Higher intensity; can feel intimidating | Higher cost; longer cycles; add-ons + travel |
| Rec league / parks | Off-campus fields; weekend games; transport required | Mixed coaching; less consistent progression | Usually cheaper but still time-heavy; gear varies |
| Aftercare / enrichment | Convenient + supervised | Not sports-specific skill progression | Similar spend, different outcome |
| Do nothing (default) | Easiest now | No guided progress | $0 now; "cost" shows up later (inactivity, confidence, connection) |

## Decision Constraints (non-negotiables)

**Parents + Schools:**
- Safe environment + safe-to-learn structure
- Coach quality + consistency
- On-campus convenience (logistics reduction)
- Inclusive participation (not elite-only)
- Clean dismissal/pickup coordination

**Minimum buying power:** Parents can pay per player, per season without it becoming a lifestyle (time + travel + weekends).

**Minimum urgency:** Parents feel logistics/cost/intensity barriers with off-campus options and want a low-friction way for their child to try sports and keep playing.

## Reach (where they can be reached reliably)
- Backpack flyers + QR at pickup
- School newsletters / email blasts
- Front office referrals
- PTA groups, parent WhatsApp/Facebook groups, Nextdoor
- On-campus demos / try-it days / interest forms

## "We Win Here Because…"
We win first at elementary schools that want low-lift enrichment because we remove the biggest blocker (logistics) with an on-campus, after-school, beginner-friendly sports season — making it easier than clubs/parks and more outcome-driven than aftercare/enrichment, so more kids start and stick with sports.
