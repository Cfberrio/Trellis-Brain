## Develop Session — Full Content
*Presenter: Luis*

**Cold Open:** "DR Team Training — Here is where this template begins" — 2024, Welcome!

**Our Journey**
- **01 — Discipline Rift:** the rift, or starting point, to a sport as a discipline. Formation of life lessons and values through teachable moments found in the sport.
- **02 — Est. 2021:** First season in DR history
- **03 — Challenges:** Growth, resources, and development

**Coach → Player**
- **01 — Coach:** What we do matters
- **02 — Core Values:** Humility, Perseverance, Adaptability
- **03 — Connection:** Coach-to-player relationship

**Moving Forward**
- Interaction with players · Mutual respect · Adaptability · Purpose · Motivation · Achievements & consequences · Accountability

**Coach: You Have Two Goals**
1. **Teach Life Lessons** — by recognizing and being intentional; emphasize a teachable moment
2. **Develop Talent** — by communicating complex ideas in specific ways; perform a move and break it down into its actions

*Values vs. Life Lessons framework:* (1) leads to better performance (2) not "happy talk" (3) setting high standards and helping through encouragement (4) allows a coach to see growth in athletes

**Talent = Skill + Passion**
- **Skill:** Singular process that grows through deep practice
- **Passion:** What ignites the unconscious energy for that growth
- We teach the skill with exactitude and inspire with passion through information

**Developing Talent — the M+/M-/M+ Pattern**
1. Demonstration (M+): show the correct way
2. Explanation: breakdown the how and why
3. Imitation (M-): show the incorrect way
4. Correction (M+): remodel the correct way
5. Repetition: encourage and reinforce the skill
*(seeking small improvement, one day at a time)*

**Research Basis — "Is This Possible?"**
- 1980s Study, University of Chicago: studied the initial education of world-class tennis players, pianists, mathematicians, neurologists, and sculptors
- Result: many started with average instructors (average by conventional teaching-ability measures)
- Conclusion: their success was driven by igniting, creating, and sustaining passion — "teaching love," which keeps the learner involved, captivated, and hooked

**Creating an Irresistible Environment — How**
1. Make initial learning pleasant and rewarding
2. Include more playful activities
3. Give positive reinforcement (critique performance sometimes; set standards expecting progress)
4. Adjust performance
5. Set high standards

**DR Coach Approach (Have → Apply)**
- **Perceptiveness:** trained eyes to notice mistakes with curiosity
- **GPS Reflex:** provide information in short, specific, high-definition bursts (avoid "what if / what about / maybe")
- **Motivation Man:** "Got another one in you!"
- **Probing Strategy:** stay flexible, change approach if A doesn't work

**All Together (Talent Formula):** Passion → Ignition → Irresistible Environment → Deep Practice → DR Coach Approach → Coach → **Talent**

**Activity: The Golf Ball**
- Dynamic: in 15 seconds, write three experiences that have impacted your life on the golf ball
- Metaphor: early golf balls were smooth and traveled poorly; players noticed dents ("bad" at first) made the ball travel farther. Manufacturers eventually added dimples on purpose — modern golf balls have 300–500 dimples.
- Meaning: the experiences written on the golf balls are your "dimples" — reflecting on past experiences, hardships, achievements, coaches, intensive practices, and life lessons is what propels you forward, just as dimples propel a golf ball.

**Activity: Row the Boat**
- Row with direction, power, and speed
- **The Oar** = our source of energy: purpose, mission, vision
  - **Purpose:** to add value and make a positive impact on players
  - **Mission:** teach life lessons while developing skills and inspiring passion for a sport
  - **Vision:** players achieve personal growth and skill development
- The oar represents your story — experiences, hardships, achievements, and passion that propel the boat
- We're all in the same boat, each with a unique oar
- Every day you coach, you decide: row or don't row. Not rowing costs the whole boat direction, power, and speed.

**How Do We Row? — Being FUELED**

**F.U.E.L.E.D.** (Collaborative Breakdown):
- **F**riendly
- **U**pfront
- **E**nthusiastic
- **L**eader
- **E**fficient
- **D**riven

- Being fueled is contagious — the energy you invest and share with others determines your quality of life
- Why it matters: it's how we make a positive impact on players. Coaching isn't about ourselves — hence "the boat" represents sacrifice.
- The more we help and serve others, the bigger the boat gets, and the more people we can carry — the more fulfilled our role as coaches becomes.

**The Boat:** Not about us. Sacrifice.

**Culture & Closing**
> "Never throughout history was there a man who lived a life of ease that left a legacy worth remembering." — Teddy Roosevelt

- **Culture** = the way we do things, how we connect as people
- Being fueled is how we do things; our core values define how we connect
- "A coach can never tell where their influence stops. It's a gift that keeps on giving."
- Even when the waves of life rock your boat: row, row, row the boat.

**Reference Reading: "Conscious Coaching" — Chapter Notes**

- **Chapter 1 — Understanding the Value of Conscious Coaching:** Addressing the psychological and emotional needs of an individual. People have unique personalities and different experiences; coaches must adapt. Coach–athlete relationship develops through mutual respect, trust, and effective communication. Athletes must feel like they belong and have a purpose to be there; coaches must feel the same.
- **Chapter 2 — Motivation:** Motivational strategies must be tailored to individuals. Intrinsic motivation pushes passion and long-term sustainability; extrinsic motivation pushes short-term goals and milestones. Intrinsic motivation is nurtured through the coach's attitude and behavior toward the class. Setting clear, achievable goals gives players a horizon to strive for. Consistent, constructive feedback is necessary. Encourage players to support and motivate each other.
- **Chapter 3 — Adaptability:** All athletes are unique. Coaches must adapt to different groups. Reflect on classes and adapt continuously.
- **Chapter 4 — Trust:** Without trust, athletes won't fully immerse themselves in the lessons or the coach. Be on time, be specific, be prepared. Athletes expect their coach to be a reliable source of knowledge and understanding. Progress builds trust — for both parents and players. Show empathy — proves you connect with and understand them.
- **Chapter 6 — Values & Standards:** Instill values; the main purpose is to teach fundamental values for life, applicable to anything. Responsibility, perseverance, and discipline. Shared mission and vision. Every athlete/coach should understand their role and what's required of them. Hold everyone to a high standard. Use assessments, feedback, performance reviews. Failures are opportunities for growth, not endpoints.
