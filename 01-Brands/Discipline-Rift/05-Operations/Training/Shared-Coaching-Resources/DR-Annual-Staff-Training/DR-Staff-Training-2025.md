## Session 1: DR 101
*Presenter: Diego Rivero*
**Session Goal:** Ensure everyone leaves clear on who we are, why we exist, and how we operate. A fast, energizing primer framing the whole conference around relationship-first youth sports.

**Identity in One Breath:** "Relationships win, and performance follows connection." — "Discipline Rift is an irresistible environment for 6–12-year-olds to explore sport, develop skills, and grow in values."

**The DR Approach (three relationship pillars):**
- **Coach ↔ Player Trust** — Feedback is fuel that builds confidence, competence, and buy-in for long-term growth.
- **Player ↔ Team Unity** — Team before talent creates accountability, unity, resilience, and genuine joy in the sport.
- **School ↔ Family Bridge** — We extend the school's mission by connecting practice, classroom, and home/real-life environments.
- These relationships form the foundation of the organization and boost both athletic performance and character development.

**Our Name, Our Promise:**
- **Discipline** — Constancy, focus, and commitment to growth on and off the court.
- **Rift** — A positive breakthrough of the comfort zone where real transformation begins.
- When combined, young players can tap into their potential for excellence in a supportive environment.
- *Student Story:* a 30–45 second live example of a child "crossing the rift" to illustrate relationship-powered growth.

**Our Promise to Families:**
- **Age-Appropriate Specialization** — Focus on Volleyball, Tennis, and Pickleball differentiates the program and accelerates learning through dedicated skill development.
- **Mission-Led Coaches** — Coaches don't just teach drills; they live the mission and model core values.
- **Purposeful Teaching** — Skill + values = growth that extends beyond the season into all of life.

**Our Story:** "We inspire more active, motivated, and connected players." Timeline: **EST. 2021** → First Season → Growth Moments → Today. Every chapter has been powered by relationships, not luck — real, tangible impact in Central Florida.

**Meet the DR Team — the "FUELED" method** (full acronym, matches 2024):
**F**riendly · **U**pfront · **E**nthusiastic · **L**eader · **E**fficient · **D**riven

## Session 2: No 100% Solution, But ONE HUNDRED 1% Solutions
*Presenter: Luis Torres*

**Core thesis:** Great coaching isn't about finding one perfect approach — it's about consistently applying many small improvements that compound. The DR Training Day = a toolbox of 1% improvements addressing the complexity of becoming an exceptional coach. Coaching is a socio-behavioral craft built on **knowledge, communication, and leadership.** Prompt: How can we grow 1% in any of these areas?

**What Makes a Great Coach? — Etymology of "Coach"**
- From Hungarian *kocsi* (carriage) → "coach" → buses → transport
- A coach takes you where you want to go, when you can't get there alone — our job is to take players where *they* want to go
- **1% Daily Improvement → 37x Mid-Season Growth** (compounding-improvement framing)

**What If? (reflection prompts)**
- What if coaches could make players 1% better after every session?
- What if coaches were more attentive in how they managed time? Gave feedback? Built relationships? Assessed how much everyone was learning?

**Building Relationships Through Excellence**
> "To build a long-term and substantive relationship, you have to help people get better. You have to start teaching first. You have to teach better and use that as a tool to connect."
- Most coaches prioritize relationships over technique — DR's competitive edge is building relationships *through* technical teaching.
- "Teaching better is technical; it requires sustained focus on things that are often mundane." The difference is in the details.

**The DR Coach: Next-Gen Role Models**
- **Humble** — constantly learning, growing, open to feedback
- **Hungry** — driven by excellence, not ego
- **Smart** — emotionally intelligent, adaptive, tactical
- High-level impact: coaches cultivate a hunger for excellence, teamwork, and character development — teaching players to strive, sacrifice, win, and belong.

**The First Day of Practice: A Walkthrough** *(5-step walkthrough referenced; steps not detailed in source — see gap flag below)*

**DR Team Non-Negotiables** *(referenced with a Google Form link placeholder for a QR code on the slide — content of the non-negotiables themselves not detailed in source)*

**POV for a Coach — See → Decide → Act**
- Coaching is perception: eyes in the right place, know your cues
- **Reality:** messy groups, low attention spans
- **Actions:** clarify, simplify, repeat, push, smile, re-explain
- **Repeat:** decide quickly & effectively

**Understanding Motivation — Engaging Every Age**
- **Funny Joe (K–2):** learning happens through playful challenges and positive reinforcement — the joy of discovery and being seen
- **Cool Jess (3rd–5th):** learning happens through goals and the satisfaction of skill development — cool friendships and growing competence

**Inside-the-Box Thinking**
- Be creative by adding value to what has already been invented
- The creativity DR looks for usually isn't "outside the box" — it's an unexpected or inventive *inside-the-box* solution: an unexpected application or adaptation of a common idea, not an entirely new discovery

**Toolbox Mindset**
- There's no single "best" method — there's a toolbox
- We teach diverse groups of players, so methods must vary
- 1% improvement = picking the right tool for the right job

**The Discipline Rift Curriculum**
- Skill Acquisition — focused technical development with measurable progression
- Game-Based Activities — learning through play to increase engagement and retention
- Tactical Activities — decision-making skills that translate across sports and life
- Challenge: to "gamefy" every aspect of training while maintaining deliberate-practice principles
- Live prompt: How can we gamefy a targeting drill for skill acquisition?
- Most important piece: understand the curriculum and how it applies *dimensionally* — for different players, ages, and personalities

**Closing line:** PLAN your game PLAN — and gamefy all of it.

## Session 3: Practice with Purpose, Developmental by Design
*Presenter: Michaela Franklin*

**What Makes a Great Practice?**
1. **Intentional & Structured** — goal-oriented, clear plan for progression; plans sometimes sent to players in advance to foster engagement
2. **Diverse Styles** — retrieval, spaced, blocked, serial, and randomized practice, used to optimize learning and retention
3. **Feedback-Driven** — continuous, constructive feedback integrated throughout
4. **Planned & Organized** — 1–2 hours of planning for a 3-hour practice ensures efficiency, pace, and involvement for every athlete

**Diverse Practice Styles for Optimal Growth**
- **Retrieval Practice** — actively recalling learned material to strengthen memory. *Ex: players explain a game rule mid-drill, or a coach asks "what does proper passing body posture look like?"*
- **Spaced Practice** — distributing sessions over time rather than concentrating them. *Ex: practicing setting Mon/Wed/Fri instead of one intense session.*
- **Blocked Practice** — focusing on one skill for an extended period; ideal for initial acquisition. *Ex: repeatedly shuffling to pass multiple passes in an unbroken sequence.*
- **Serial Practice** — interleaving multiple distinct activities in one session to build adaptability. *Ex: alternating passing and hitting drills, with or without a ball.*
- **Randomized Practice** — interleaving diverse skills with unpredictable timing/patterns to promote real-game decision-making. *Ex: "Drill #10 Scramble," unpredictable scenarios requiring varied responses.*
- **Live activity:** take 5 minutes to design a drill using any of the above styles.

**Intentional & Structured: 6-Week Unit Plan (DR Curriculum)**
- **Week 1:** Passing & Setting Basics — introduce fundamentals
- **Week 2:** Passing & Setting Review — deepen and reinforce
- **Week 3:** Hitting & Skill Integration — introduce hitting while reviewing passing/setting
- **Week 4:** Serving & Multi-Skill Review — introduce serving alongside passing/setting/hitting review
- **Week 5:** Serial Practice Integration — apply all skills in integrated sequential drills
- **Week 6:** Assessment & Randomized Practice — evaluate skill application in unpredictable game-like scenarios

**Our Daily Practice Structure** (consistent flow every session, to reduce anxiety and foster development):
1. **Pre-Practice Brief** — huddle outlining the day's full plan drill-by-drill and its goal; players can ask clarifying questions before starting (credit: communication coach Betsy Butterick)
2. **Dynamic Warm-Up** — sport-specific, ball-incorporated movements to build early contacts and inject fun
3. **Skill Training** — focused positional work in groups (e.g., setters, middles, outsides)
4. **Combination Training** — integrating positions to practice collaborative skills/transitions (e.g., setters with middles, outsides with defensive specialists)
5. **Game Play** — structured 6-on-6 drills targeting specific team needs; rarely just "games to 25"
6. **Cool Down** — player-led when possible, promoting leadership and self-care; even young kids ("little tikes at camp") are capable of leading with guidance
7. **Post-Practice Debrief** — huddle to reflect: 3 things learned / 2 things that went well + 1 to work on; ideally loud enough for parents to hear; be intentional even in casual goodbyes (e.g., *"Bye Tommy, way to nail your shuffling today!"*)

**Planning for Impactful Practices**
- **Collaborative Design** — build the plan in advance (date, time, drills), with input from all coaches
- **Assign Roles & Drills** — designate specific drills/segments to specific coaches, leveraging their expertise
- **Share the Blueprint** — every coach gets a copy of the finalized plan
- **Maintain Records** — keep past plans as references for future planning
- **Goal-Oriented Focus** — if the goal is passing, the majority of drills should reinforce passing

**Pace of Drill: Ensuring Every Athlete Is Engaged**
- High-intensity, high-repetition drills minimize downtime and maximize involvement
- **Passing Drill example:** continuous ball contact, players constantly moving/receiving/passing, multiple balls in play (referenced video linked in source deck)
- **Fan Setting example:** rapid sequence of sets from various angles simulating game scenarios; feeders distribute balls quickly for high-rep, fast decision-making (referenced video linked in source deck)
