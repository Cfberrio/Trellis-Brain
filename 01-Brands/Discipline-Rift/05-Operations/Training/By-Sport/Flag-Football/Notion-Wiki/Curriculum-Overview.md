# START — Flag Football Curriculum Overview

## Overview
**Flatfootbook Flag Football Curriculum** — a progressive, decision-based flag football program built on intentional teaching and skill development.

## Core Pillars
- **Decide** — Teach kids what to look at so they choose better actions
- **Plan** — Practices and units are intentionally designed, not improvised
- **Feedback** — Coaches give short, specific corrections that change the next rep
- **CFU** — Coaches actively check that learning happened
- **Culture** — Routines create safety, belonging, and effort
- **Growth** — Everything is age-appropriate and progressive

## Teaching Philosophy
Flag football is taught as a **space-and-decision game**, not a playbook sport. Players learn to move, protect space, create space, and make simple decisions under pressure — without tackling or complex schemes.

**Core Decisions Players Make:**
- Find open space
- Protect the ball while moving
- Choose who is open
- Stop space on defense by angles and flag pulls

## Age Groups

### Funny Joes (K–2)
**Coaching Approach:** movement-heavy, playful, highly structured practices; one cue at a time; short, fast reps; focus on basic movement, confidence, and effort.
**Player Development:** run, stop, change direction, play simple chase games; carry the ball safely; pull flags with control; listen, start on signal, reset quickly.

### Cool Jess (3–5)
**Coaching Approach:** introduce decision-making and responsibility; ask players what they saw; add simple constraints that force choices; begin tracking progress through tiers.
**Player Development:** throw and catch on the move; learn when to run vs. pass; create space without being told exactly where to go; work in small groups with shared responsibility.

## Skill Development System

### Six Core Skill Domains

**1. Ball Handling + Carrying**
- Hold the ball securely; keep eyes up while running; change direction without stopping; protect the ball when defenders are close

**2. Throwing** *(focus: consistency and decision timing, not distance)*
- Step toward a target; release the ball smoothly; throw to open teammates; throw at the right moment

**3. Catching** *(focus: readiness and confidence)*
- Watch the ball all the way in; catch with hands, not trapping; secure the ball before running; progress from standing to moving

**4. Getting Open** *(create space without routes or plays)*
- Move away from defenders; vary speed to get open; show hands to the thrower; find empty space naturally

**5. Defense + Flag Pulling** *(focus: control and angles, not aggression)*
- Stay balanced and face the ball carrier; take good pursuit angles; pull flags safely; work together to stop space

**6. Team Play + Communication**
- Talk to teammates; reset quickly after plays; encourage effort; rotate roles so everyone participates

### Sub-Skill Breakdown
Each skill is broken into small, teachable pieces. Coaches focus on **one sub-skill at a time**.
- Throwing → grip, step, release, follow-through
- Catching → eyes on ball, hands ready, secure then run
- Defense → stance, angle, reach, pull

### Tier Progression System
Players progress through 3–4 tiers per skill based on what the coach observes. Each tier shows: what a player can currently do; what comes next; clear signs they're ready to move up.

## Practice Structure (60 Minutes)

1. **Arrival & Routine** — kids get equipment and start moving immediately; sets focus and eliminates wasted time
2. **Skill Focus Block** — one main skill is introduced or reinforced; quick demonstration and cueing; fast, high-rep practice
3. **Decision Block** — add constraints that force choices: who to pass to, where to run, how to defend space
4. **Game Segment** — modified game reinforces the learning goal; scoring rewards good decisions, not just points
5. **Feedback & CFU** — coach pauses briefly to ask "What did you see?" and "Why did that work?"; kids show or explain the answer
6. **Wrap-Up** — highlight effort, progress, and one takeaway; give parents one clear message at pickup

## Supporting Systems

### Drill Library
Every drill includes: what skill it teaches; how to keep reps high; specific cues to use; age-appropriate adjustments.

### Game Library
Games designed to force learning through: limited space; extra points for spacing or passing; everyone must touch the ball. *No playbooks. No scrimmage chaos.*

### CFU (Check for Understanding) System
Learning is checked constantly through: asking kids to explain decisions; watching for repeated behaviors; quick demonstrations as proof. If many kids miss the same thing, the coach adjusts teaching immediately.

### Coach Scripts
Consistent language across all locations: short cues; quick corrections; simple questions.

### Culture Routines
Intentional culture-building in every practice: clear start routines; fast transitions; effort praise; safety reminders.

## Parent & Home Connection

### Parent Updates
After each practice, parents receive: what their child worked on; where they're progressing; one simple thing to practice at home.

### Home Playbook
Optional at-home activities: 10–15 minutes; minimal equipment; reinforces the week's skill.

## Program Quality Systems

### Coach Training
Coaches are trained on: how to run practices; how to give feedback; how to check learning; how to manage energy and transitions.

### Metrics
Each week, coaches record: attendance; skill progress; engagement; effort. This keeps quality consistent across the program.
