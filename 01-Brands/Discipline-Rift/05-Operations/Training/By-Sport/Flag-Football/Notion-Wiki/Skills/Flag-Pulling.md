# Flag Pulling

Defense might not be the most glamorous part of flag football, but it's just as important as offense. A great defender anticipates what will happen on the field and stops the ball-carrier using speed and sound fundamentals.

## How to pull a flag

The biggest mistakes players make: grabbing the flag at the wrong spot, not aligning their body correctly, and overrunning the ball-carrier.

- **Correct stance.** Align yourself in front of the ball-carrier — this gives you more time to pull their flags and makes it difficult for them to get around you. Square up your shoulders and slightly bend your knees, keeping your **eyes on the flags, not the player**.
- **Break down.** As you reach your opponent, break down defensively by shuffling your feet as you slow your speed. Keep your body aligned and positioned in front of the ball-carrier.
- **Two hands, two flags.** Always use both hands to go for both flags. Pulling one flag with one hand — or one flag with two hands — drops your odds of a successful stop tremendously.
- **Aim for the base of the flag.** Organizations use different flags (Velcro, pop socket, Triple Threat belts). In every case, pull as close to the base of the belt as possible. Grip firmly and yank straight down in a swiping motion.

## 3 best flag pulling drills

### Tunnel drill
**Setup:** no equipment. Create a tunnel — three defensive players in one vertical line, three in another about 5 feet across. All face forward in the same direction and remain stationary. An offensive player faces the tunnel with room for a running start.

**Directions:** the offensive player runs through the tunnel as fast as they can without losing their flags. Defensive players stay stationary and may only use their **inside hand** to remove a flag — no both hands, no turning to use the outside hand.

**Tips:** keep your eyes on the flags, not the player. It's harder to grip the flag when you're stationary and the carrier is moving — practice grabbing firmly at the base and swiping straight down.

### Zig zag drill
**Setup:** defensive players line up in two staggered vertical lines about 5 yards apart, stationary, facing forward with hands on hips. An offensive player faces the defenders with room for a running start.

**Directions:** the offensive player runs to the first defender. As they reach their target they **break down, square up, and quickly remove both flags using both hands**. Then sprint to the next staggered player in the opposite line and repeat. Complete after reaching every player.

**Tips:** great for practicing breaking down as you meet the ball-carrier. Keep knees bent and stay low so you can slow your speed and shuffle as needed.

### Cone drill
**Setup:** competitive 1-on-1. Place 4 cones 10 yards apart in a square (or any visible markers). At least one offensive and one defensive player.

**Directions:** the offensive player (ball-carrier) stands at the lower-left cone; the defender stands diagonally opposite at the upper-right. On start, both sprint around the cone in front of them — offense rounds the upper-left moving upward, defense rounds the lower-right moving downward. Both meet in the middle for a 1-on-1. The defender wants to pull the flag(s) before the carrier escapes the square. The carrier practices moves like sliding or spinning.

**Tips:** easy to scale. For younger players make the square smaller. Add an extra defender to challenge the carrier more.
