# Throwing

## Step-by-step guide

### Hand placement
When you pick up or receive the football, spin it so that the laces are on top. Cup your thumb beneath the ball and place your four fingers on top by the laces.

The number of fingers on the laces is personal preference — quarterbacks vary between two, three, and four, with the remaining fingers placed behind the laces, closer to the body. Choose by comfort and control. There is no wrong answer; just keep the laces on top and use them to grip and control the ball. Try various grips during pre-season practices.

### Motion
Once you find the grip, lift the ball above your shoulder. This height allows a quick release. Too low limits range of motion and makes it easier for defenders to knock down passes.

**Pro-tip:** don't use a sidearm motion — it's bad for the shoulder and less accurate.

Square your shoulders to your target. Take a step with the foot opposite your throwing arm toward your intended receiver, building kinetic movement and momentum. That foot also acts as a buffer from the defense as you prep your throw.

As you step, pull the ball behind your head — laces still on top — then forward past your head. Follow through until your throwing arm is around a 90-degree angle before releasing. Don't stop the motion of your arm at release.

As you release, flick or pop your wrist downward, almost like shooting a basketball. This creates the spiral. Your release point changes with distance: shorter passes release closer to the ear with a greater follow-through; long, deep passes release further back behind the head to create an arch.

**Pro-tip:** think of the follow-through like a golf swing — you wouldn't stop the club at the ball.

### One knee drill
- **Objective:** focus on fundamentals — grip, posture, release.
- **Set up:** two players, 10–15 yards apart, each kneeling on one knee.
- **Directions:** throw back and forth while remaining on one knee. Focus on fundamentals; test different grip positions and release angles. After ~10 throws, alternate knees.
- **Tips:** move your torso back and forth while throwing to mimic in-game movement.

### Two knee drill
- **Objective:** same as the one knee drill, but grounding both knees requires more force behind the ball since range of motion is limited.
- **Set up:** two players kneeling on both knees, 10–15 yards apart.
- **Directions:** pass back and forth. Focus on grip, release, and targeting. Shift shoulders and body back and forth during the drill.
- **Tips:** also builds receiving skills.

### How to throw a football farther
Perfect your form first — grip, stance, release. Consistent form builds the core and arm strength needed for distance.

Practice moving — both stepping and running — into a pass. Momentum puts more kinetic energy into the ball. Even when limited in a game, always try to step into a pass. Practice all playbook routes; for throw length, emphasize fly routes.

### Perfecting the spiral
The spiral is about follow-through. Don't stop the arm at release — continue forward in a full swinging motion. At release, flick the wrist downward; your fingertips should be the last thing in contact with the ball. Those two movements together create the spiral.

Not every throw will be perfect. Learning takes time.
