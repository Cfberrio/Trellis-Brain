# Catching

## 3-step guide to catching a football

Not every pass is the same — some are thrown directly to you, some to the side. Sometimes you're facing the quarterback, other times you're running full speed downfield. In every scenario there are basic fundamentals you can use to make the catch.

**1. Align your body.** The easiest way to catch the ball is to square your shoulders and frame your body so you're facing the quarterback. That isn't always possible in a game. Whether you're catching a low ball to the side or a Hail Mary over your head, focus on the ball's trajectory and align your body directly in the path of the ball.

**2. Hand positioning.** Hand positioning changes depending on where the ball is thrown. If a pass is above your waist, extend your arms and create a diamond shape with your pointer fingers together — use this for high balls even if thrown to your side. For low passes, cup your hands in front of you with your pinky fingers together, again for both straight and side throws. Catch the ball with your **fingers, not your palms**. Keep elbows slightly bent and use big, soft hands.

**3. Tuck the ball.** After the catch, protect it from defenders. Use your hand, forearm, and elbow to firmly press the ball tight into your body. As you run your route, keep the ball tucked high and tight.

## Drills

### High and low pass drill
**Setup:** two players and a ball, lined up about 10 yards apart, gradually getting farther apart as the drill continues.

**Directions:** two players throw and receive with proper hand positioning. First focus on high passes thrown directly to each other, then to the side — the catcher creates a diamond and maintains it every catch. After several throws, change to low passes, straight and to the side, cupping hands with pinkies together. To advance: increase distance and throw harder.

**Tips:** if the ball is bouncing off your hands, you're catching with hard palms. Use fingertips to grip. It's easier to catch with elbows slightly bent.

### Over the shoulder drill
**Setup:** many routes (post, corner) force receivers to catch over their shoulder while running. Two players and a ball.

**Directions:** two players stand about 10 yards apart, one receiver and one quarterback. The receiver moves forward looking over their shoulder as the quarterback throws to a designated position in front of them. The receiver catches while continuing to move forward, then tucks it in. Increase speed and distance as it gets comfortable. Switch sides and practice over both shoulders. Bonus: quarterbacks improve accuracy throwing to a designated spot.

**Tips:** the key is to wait until the last minute to put your hands up. The defender guarding you also has their back to the ball — don't give them time to block it. You also want to run at full speed, and running with hands out slows you down.
