# Conditioning

All drills below use the same setup: place a cone to mark where the player starts and another cone 5 to 10 yards away where they finish.

### High knees
**Objective:** a calisthenic exercise to build endurance. Best used as a warm-up before practice or a game to get the blood flowing.
**Directions:** jog while bringing your knees up to your chest (aim for 90 degrees or higher). Let your feet touch the ground only for a moment before quickly switching legs. Look straight ahead, keep your chest up, and pump your arms as you move downfield. Faster, sharper movements = more cardio work.
**Tips:** don't do this drill if you're already fatigued. Conditioning drills prepare the body and improve endurance — if you're exhausted before you start, your muscles will be too strained to build strength.

### Lunges
**Objective:** targets the complete lower body — quads, glutes, calves. In repetition, increases flexibility, shapes and strengthens leg muscles, engages the core.
**Directions:** start with your right leg in front and left behind, enough space between them for stability. Putting all the weight in your front foot, lunge straight down, dropping your back knee so it almost touches the ground; front leg bends to 90 degrees. Slowly straighten your front leg to return to a stance. Take an oversized step with the opposite foot and lunge on that side. Keep the opposite arm forward with every step. Alternate legs to the finish.
**Tips:** don't let your back knee contact the ground. Both knees should stay aligned and never collapse inward — wobbly knees are a fast way to get hurt. Start slow and perfect the form before adding pace.

### Butt kicks
**Objective:** strengthens and builds flexibility in the glutes and hamstrings. Warms up the lower body and raises heart rate.
**Directions:** keep your upper body straight and eyes up as you run, but instead of traditional running form, drive your heel to your glutes at each step. Bring the heel all the way back, stretching out your quad. Pump your arms with each step.
**Tips:** this is not a race. It's less about getting from A to B quickly and more about completing the full movement of reaching the heel back. Technique first, then speed.

### Quick steps
**Objective:** intensive cardio that builds quick feet.
**Directions:** take the proper stance — knees slightly bent, weight on the balls of your feet, eyes up, arms in front. Then as quickly as possible take small, choppy steps (almost like running in place) while pumping your arms at the same high speed. Run all the way through the cone.
**Tips:** the faster and quicker the steps, the better the footwork. To add a challenge, throw and catch a football with a teammate during the drill — it forces you to react to a play while still focusing on quick feet.

### Sumo squats
**Objective:** targets the glutes and inner thigh muscles. The wide stance and 45-degree toe angle work inner leg muscles more than a traditional squat.
**Directions:** face forward toward the opposite cone. In one smooth motion take an oversized step, swinging your back leg forward while rotating your hips 90 degrees. When fully sideways, plant your leg about 6 inches wider than your shoulders with your toe at 45 degrees. Bend into a lunge on the planted foot, weight in that foot, back leg extended straight. Lower your hips until parallel with the ground. Press back up through your heels, core tight, then shift weight to the other leg and lunge on that side. As you return to a stance, swing your back leg up while rotating 180 degrees to face the opposite side. Continue to the finish.
**Tips:** count down out loud the number of squats to the finish line, or add music and squat to the rhythm.

### Karaoke
**Objective:** loosens the hips and improves agility and speed. Practices the quick-feet skills essential in flag football.
**Directions:** turn sideways — say your left foot is closest to the finish line. Arms out to the sides, take your back (right) foot and cross it over and in front of your left foot, turning your pelvis as you do. Then step and reach all the way behind you, opening your hips and crossing your right foot behind your left leg. Pick up speed as the form settles. Keep your core tight and fully rotate your hips in both directions. Repeat evenly on both sides.
**Tips:** very versatile — slow for beginners, add a kick for advanced players, or start slow and build pace for a full cardio workout.

### High knees + scoop
**Objective:** fully stretches the lower muscles before practice or a game. The initial movement targets lower back, hips, and glutes; the scoop works hamstrings and calves.
**Directions:** walking pace. Step with your left foot as you grab the knee or shin of your right leg with both hands and pull it to your chest — the higher and tighter the pull, the better the stretch. Then, balancing, plant your right heel on the ground with the toe pointing up. Reach down and scoop the grass on either side of your foot, keeping your leg extended, for a full calf and hamstring stretch. Switch sides to the finish.
**Tips:** requires a lot of balance. To make it easier, place one hand on your knee and the other on your shin.

## Quick feet — additional methods
- **Jump rope.** Quick feet require balance and focus; jump roping builds both. Many coaches have players practice at home until movements are quick and rapid.
- **Lateral drills.** Require players to accelerate, stop, and burst without losing balance. Excellent for coordination, quick direction changes, and acceleration. A ladder works, or set up small cones.
- **Agility drills.** Quick feet, speed training, and agility overlap — see [Agility-Drills](Agility-Drills.md). Many agility drills include an actual football, which changes up the repetition of short choppy steps.
