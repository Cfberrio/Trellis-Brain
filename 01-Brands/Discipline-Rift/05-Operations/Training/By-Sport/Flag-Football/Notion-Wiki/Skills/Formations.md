# Formations (5-on-5 Playbook)

Nine formations, three plays each. Every play can be mirrored left/right and adapted for 7-on-7.

## Single Back Formation
One receiver on either side of the center and quarterback, and one a few yards behind the pair.

- **Play 1:** on hike, Receivers X and Z run post routes cutting toward the center. Receiver Y runs a corner route once past the line of scrimmage. If no receiver is open, the center releases and runs a corner route too.
- **Play 2:** Receiver Z sidesteps the defender then runs a fly route (long yardage). Receiver Y runs a slant then cuts toward the sideline, occupying the space opened by the defender shifting back to cover the fly. Receiver X and the center run post and corner routes to cut off their defenders.
- **Play 3 (short yardage near the first down line):** the right receiver runs 10 yards then cuts in at 90 degrees, pulling their defender and leaving the right side open. The back receiver runs a dragging slant into that space. The other side receiver and center run crossing in/out routes at short yardage.

**Pro-tip:** always decide which receiver moves first when paths cross. You don't want anyone bumping heads.

## Spread Formation
All three receivers essentially on the line of scrimmage, one starting a couple yards behind, allowing a slight route delay that catches defenders off-guard.

- **Play 1 (defense weak on the right):** Receiver Z jukes their defender and runs a fly (long yardage). Receiver Y runs an option route toward the right sideline into the space vacated as the defense pushes back. The center also pushes toward the right sideline. If the defense has shifted right, use Receiver X on a post route.
- **Play 2 (defense weak on the left):** Receiver X runs a quick option route toward the left sideline, pulling the defensive line far left. Receivers Y and Z run in and post routes for long yardage. If the defense shifts left, the center releases on an out route toward the right sideline.
- **Play 3 (crossers):** Receiver Y runs a five-yard out, crossing the far right receiver who runs a hitch. Receiver X and center also cross, both running slants.

**Pro-tip:** receiver placement is a recommendation, not a rule. Adapt any of these for 7-on-7 and personalize them.

## Bunch Formation
The offensive line starts close together then spreads out, clumping the defense so they're ill-prepared to move at hike. More advanced — set an order on who moves first to avoid collisions.

- **Play 1:** the center and Receiver Y run corner routes, drawing the defense back. The other two receivers run staggered out plays at shorter distance, so receivers are open both short and long.
- **Play 2 (simpler version):** Receivers X and Z run corner routes, drawing the defensive line back and out. Receiver Y and the center run crossing out routes toward the five-yard line.
- **Play 3:** get the defensive line caught up with each other in the center, then spread out. Receivers X and Y both run seven yards then a corner route (long yardage options). Receiver Z and the center run five-and-out routes toward the sidelines.

## Trips Formation
All three receivers on one side of the quarterback at the start. Shown as right trips; adaptable to the left.

- **Play 1:** Receiver X runs an option route at five yards cutting toward the sideline. Receiver Y runs an angled out at the five-yard line. Receiver Z runs a fly. The center releases on an angled corner route. Staggers players at varying yardage.
- **Play 2:** Receiver Z runs a fly, Receiver Y a corner at seven yards. Receiver X and the center run out and in routes respectively, opening the inside receiver on the left side as the defense covers the heavy-yardage right.
- **Play 3 (trick play):** Receiver Z runs a hitch around nine yards where Receiver Y runs a long corner — two long-yardage options for a first down. Receiver X runs a quick option route around five yards, tricking the defense into thinking they're going left when they're going right. The defense follows right, leaving the center wide open for a five-yard out. **Pro-tip:** have the center wait a couple seconds before running their route.

## Twins Formation
Think of the non-quarterbacks as two pairs, or twins, standing close to each other and complementing each other.

- **Play 1:** the two right receivers run angled post routes at staggered lengths, drawing the defensive line toward the center. Receiver X and the center run quick in and out routes at low yardage. Perfect for closing a small gap for a first down.
- **Play 2:** Receiver Z runs a fly, Receiver Y a five-and-out, Receiver X a slant into a fly. These draw the defense away from the middle, allowing a quick throw to the center on an option route.
- **Play 3:** Receiver Z runs a post, Receiver Y a chair route. Receiver X runs a corner and the safety releases into a five-and-out. The chair route is especially effective — two changes of direction instead of the usual one.

## I Formation
All three receivers stacked behind the quarterback, giving the defense no indication where they plan to go.

- **Play 1:** Receiver X runs an angled out after hitting the line of scrimmage. Receiver Y runs an angled fly. Receiver Z runs a quick hitch after crossing the line. The center releases on a corner. Draws the defense in all directions, leaving the middle vulnerable.
- **Play 2:** Receivers X and Y both run angled out routes. Receiver Z runs an angled hitch around the five-yard line. The center releases on a corner. **Pro-tip:** have Receiver Z stall their route a few seconds so the others draw the defense away from the middle.
- **Play 3:** Receiver X runs a chair route at shallow yardage. Receiver Y crosses Receiver X on a five-and-out. Receiver Z runs two slants and the center releases on a fly for heavy yardage. **Safety pro-tip:** X and Y cross paths twice — decide who runs first and practice ahead of time.

## Double Back Set Formation
Two receivers on either side of the quarterback a couple yards back; the last receiver farther out at the line of scrimmage. Shown starting right; adaptable to the left.

- **Play 1:** Receiver X runs an out at four yards toward the sideline. Receiver Z runs a post, the center a five-and-out. Leaves an opening in the center for Receiver Y on a shallow hitch.
- **Play 2:** Receiver X runs a post for deep yardage, Receiver Y an angled chair route equally deep — drawing the defense backward. Receiver Z runs an angled in and the center an angled out; these are your short-yardage targets.
- **Play 3:** Receiver X runs an angled corner, Receiver Z a wide five-out, Receiver Y an angled hitch, and the center releases for a five-and-out. Spreads players across the field for throwing variety.

## Trips Stack Formation
Three receivers on one side of the quarterback, stacked vertically. Shown far right; the stack can go anywhere along the line of scrimmage.

- **Play 1:** Receiver X runs eight yards then a corner. Receiver Y an angled post, Receiver Z an angled fly. All three drive the defense deep, allowing the center a quick five-and-out for short yardage.
- **Play 2:** Receiver X runs a short post, Receiver Y a shallow out toward the sideline, Receiver Z a hitch at the 10-yard line for a deeper option. If nobody's open, the center releases into a fly for heavy yardage.
- **Play 3 (against a weaker right defensive side):** Receiver X runs seven yards then cuts in. Receiver Y runs an angled corner, Receiver Z an angled in. The center runs a deep corner.

## Twins Stack Formation
Very similar to trips stack. Two receivers stack on one side of the quarterback (shown right), with the final receiver on the opposite side.

- **Play 1:** Receiver Y runs a fly, Receiver Z a five-and-out. Receiver X runs a fly and the center a five-and-out. Two short-yardage and two long-yardage options.
- **Play 2:** Receiver Y runs a seven-yard corner, Receiver Z a fly. Receiver X runs a shallow post crossing the center, who runs a seven-yard out. Staggers yardage for varied length options — a good beginner play.
- **Play 3:** Receiver Y runs an eight-yard in, Receiver Z a deep post (both heavy yardage). Receiver X runs a shallow option across the center while the center releases for a five-and-out (shallow options).

## Single Set Formation
Two receivers on the line of scrimmage, equal distance apart. The final receiver a few yards behind the quarterback. Distances are customizable.

- **Play 1 (deep yardage for a first down):** Receiver Y runs a deep corner, Receiver Z an angled fly after juking their defender — pulling the defense deep and right. Receiver X runs an angled fly to the left. For short yardage, the center releases on a five-and-out.
- **Play 2:** Receiver Z runs the farthest on a fly. Receiver Y runs a five-and-out. Receiver X starts with a slant before crossing the middle on an out route. The center runs a mid-length corner.
- **Play 3:** Receiver Z runs a post, Receiver Y a corner, both deep. Receiver X runs an angled fly, opening the shallow left side where the center runs a five-and-out.

**Safety pro-tip:** whenever players cross in their routes, practice the play ahead of time. It eliminates hesitation on the field and decreases injury risk.
