# Agility Drills

## 5 youth football agility drills

### 4-corner drill
**Objective:** a beginner speed and agility drill strengthening the ability to change direction at a moment's notice — especially important on defense.
**Setup:** four cones in a square, five yards apart (adjustable, but keep them equidistant).
**Directions:** backpedal from the first cone to the second, pumping your arms, not looking backward. Once you backpedal past the second cone and can see it in front of you, cut at a 90-degree angle and shuffle toward the third. Once past the third, sprint forward to the fourth. After passing the fourth, cut 90 degrees again and shuffle back to the start to complete the square.
**Tips:** bend your knees and stay low for smoother transitions. Don't cross your feet when shuffling — it makes mid-play adjustments very difficult.

### Zig zag drill
**Objective:** refines the ability to quickly change direction when running a route. Most common for wide receivers but adaptable to other positions.
**Setup:** a row of three cones, each 10 yards apart. A second row of three cones placed three yards away from the first, staggered five yards, creating the zig zag shape.
**Directions:** run quickly from the first cone to the staggered first cone of the second line. Cut and run to the next cone of the first line. Repeat through the remaining cones.
**Tips:** run at a controlled, fast pace rather than an all-out sprint so your footwork stays in control. Don't cross your feet when cutting — plant and pivot off the outside foot.

### Foot fire drill
**Objective:** a simpler drill boosting cardio and endurance while preparing players for quick footwork.
**Setup:** for each player, place two cones on the outsides of their feet, slightly wider than shoulders — enough room to shuffle while keeping movements centered and controlled.
**Directions:** move your legs and feet back and forth in a tight, cutting fashion. Pump your arms to complete the full motion. Ankles should not kick back as if running in place — feet shuffle back and forth on the ground. Increase speed as confidence builds.
**Tips:** a great start to practice — raises heart rate without being overly complex.

### Lateral box drill
**Objective:** similar to foot fire — fast footwork and cardio — and improves sideways shuffling, a key defensive move.
**Setup:** four cones in an equidistant square, recommended one yard apart (adjustable by skill).
**Directions:** start just left of the square, facing the side you've designated as the finish. Shuffle left quickly into the center of the square, both feet stepping in. Immediately shuffle right back to the starting point. Then shuffle through the center to the other side. Without stopping, shuffle back to the center. Once in the direct center, sprint forward through the front of the square to finish. Never stop when changing directions — shift quickly.
**Tips:** bend your knees and stay low to keep your center of gravity stable. Pump your arms for max acceleration.

### "T" drill
**Objective:** improve acceleration, sprinting, and rapid direction change. Sprint-focused rather than shuffle-focused, so it applies to both offense and defense.
**Setup:** three cones in a line, each five yards apart. A fourth cone lined up with the middle cone, five yards away — this is the start.
**Directions:** start at the solo cone and sprint to the middle cone. Pivot 90 degrees right and sprint to the next cone, squatting slightly to tap it. Sprint to the furthest cone in the line and touch it. Finish by sprinting past the middle cone.
**Tips:** create tight pivots when changing direction rather than rounding out turns.

## How to improve speed and agility

Speed and agility help players change direction at a moment's notice and adapt to plays as they unfold. These five drills are a good start but shouldn't be the only ones. Build cardio routines into practice — running laps, sprinting exercises, and scrimmage games all work. Stamina and endurance keep you quick and nimble for the full duration of the game.
