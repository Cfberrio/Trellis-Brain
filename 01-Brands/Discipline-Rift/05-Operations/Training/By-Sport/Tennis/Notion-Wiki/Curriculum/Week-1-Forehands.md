## Introduction (10 min)

**WELCOME**

- Greet all students and introduce them to the tennis season.
- Coaches introduce themselves.
- Briefly explain the mission of DR Sports & Athletics:
    - **DR = Discipline Rift**.
    - **Discipline Rift**: Introduction and development in tennis and introduction to the life lessons we can learn from the sport.
    - Ask: What is discipline?
      Players may think discipline is receiving punishment; clarify that discipline is the power to commit to a decision and follow through.

**TEAM EXPECTATIONS**

- Arrive on time for tennis class, held every (insert day) of each week.
- Be respectful and supportive of fellow players by helping each other improve.
- Listen to your coaches and follow instructions.

**ACTIONS & CONSEQUENCES**

- Every action has consequences:
    - Positive actions lead to good consequences.
    - Negative actions lead to bad consequences.
- 3 Call-Out Rule:
    - **First Callout**: Coaches will speak to the player.
    - **Second Callout**: Player will be placed in timeout during class.
    - **Third Callout**: Parent talk.
- Program Overview:
    - The program includes 6 weeks of tennis classes, held on (day of practice) each week.
    - Remind students to remember and attend each week.

### ICE BREAKER / TEAM BONDING (OPTIONAL)

1. **Pass Your Name Around**
    - **Activity**: Make a circle of students and have them pass a tennis ball to each other while saying their name as they pass it.
    - **Goal**: Memorize their names.
2. **Team Building Circles**
    - **Activity**: Have players stand in a circle and take turns sharing something positive about a teammate, such as a skill.
    - **Goal**: Affirmation and encouragement.
3. **Team Handshake**
    - **Activity**: Create a unique team handshake or high-five routine that players can do together.
    - **Goal**: Team bonding and unity.
4. **Team Chants**
    - **Activity**: Create simple and energetic team chants or cheers that players can do together before practice or during drills.
    - **Goal**: Build energy and momentum.
5. **Clap Challenge**
    - **Activity**: Players stand in a circle and take turns throwing a ball in the air. While the ball is in the air, the player claps a set number of times (starting with 5) before catching the ball. The challenge increases to 10, 15, and so on.
    - **Goal**: Coordination and focus.

### TENNIS SIMON SAYS

1. **Setup:**
    - Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
    - Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
    - **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
    - Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
2. **Game Rules:**
    - The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
    - If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
3. **Commands:**
    - **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
    - **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
    - **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
    - **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
    - **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
    - **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
        - Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
        - **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
        - Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
    - **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
        - Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
        - Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
    - The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
5. **Elimination:**
    - Any player who follows a command that doesn't begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
    - Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
6. **Winner's Reward:**
    - The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.

## Skill (5 min)

**BREAKDOWN**

1. **Introduction (Introduce all tennis skills)**
    - **Forehand**: The basic groundstroke hit with the dominant hand.
    - **Backhand**: A stroke hit from the opposite side, using either one or both hands.
    - **Volleys**: Shots hit near the net before the ball bounces.
    - **Serving**: The starting stroke used to begin a point.
    - **Explain that today's focus will be on Forehand & Backhand.**
2. **Demonstration (Forehand Breakdown)**
    - Stance and Alignment: Start in the ready position (feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent). Turn toward your dominant hand side.
    - Hand Position:
        - Dominant hand grips the racket
        - Non-dominant hand supports at the neck.
    - Arm Position: Bring the racket back, non-dominant hand points at the ball.
    - Posture: Stay athletic with knees bent, ready to shift weight.
    - Contact: Make contact at waist height, in front of the body.
    - Directional Control: Slightly close the racket face and aim towards your target.
    - Follow Through: Finish the swing over your opposite shoulder.
3. **Imitation (Players Imitate the Coach)**
    - Stance and Alignment
    - Hand Position
    - Arm Position
    - Posture
    - Contact with the Ball
    - Directional Control
    - Follow Through
4. **Correction (Coach corrects form of player)**
    - **Stance and Alignment**: Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent.
    - **Hand Position**: Dominant hand grips the racket, non-dominant hand on the neck for balance.
    - **Arm Position**: Align the racket with your body before swinging.
    - **Posture**: Stay stable, only swing the arm while maintaining your stance.
    - **Contact with the Ball**: Make contact at waist level, in front of your body.
    - **Directional Control**: Swing towards or through the net.
    - **Follow Through**: Finish with the racket above your opposite shoulder

## Forehands (30 min)

**FORM**

- **Start in the Ready Position:** Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, facing the net.
- **Turn Body Toward the Dominant Hand:** Rotate your torso to the side of your **dominant hand**.
- **Racket Alignment:** Bring the **racket back**, aligning it with your torso.
- **Point with the Secondary Hand:** The **secondary hand** points at the ball, helping maintain balance.
- **Swing:** Swing the racket **towards the net**, aiming to make contact at waist level.
- **Return to Ready Position:** After the swing, return to the **ready position** to prepare for the next shot.

### ADVANCED PLAYER CUES

- **Not going through the full movement**: Remind players to complete the entire swing, finishing with the racket above the shoulder for better power and control.
- **Not brushing enough**: Encourage players to brush up on the ball to generate topspin, ensuring the ball clears the net with accuracy.
- **Brushing too much but not hitting the ball**: Emphasize finding the right balance between brushing and solid contact, ensuring the racket makes firm contact with the ball.
- **Supinating the wrist too much and making impact with the frame**: Instruct players to keep their wrist stable and aligned, avoiding excessive rotation that can cause off-center hits with the frame of the racket.
- **Keep your feet moving**: In a match, your opponent will aim to hit the ball away from you. Always stay **light on your feet** and ready to move in any direction.
- **Recovery to the middle**: After hitting a shot, **return to the middle** of the court (or a good recovery position) to be prepared for the next ball. This improves your ability to cover both sides of the court.
- **Anticipate your opponent's shot**: **Read your opponent's body language** and shot patterns to anticipate where the ball will go. This helps you react faster and position yourself effectively.

**DRILLS**

- **Set Cones as Targets:** Place cones on the opposite side of the net, in different spots for players to target.
- **Baseline Position:** Line the players up at the baseline and feed balls to them one by one.
- **Hit Targets:** Players aim to hit the ball towards the cones with their **forehand stroke**.
- **Scoring:** Players who hit the targets earn **points**, which can lead to rewards.
- **Vary the Target Locations:** Place some cones down the line and others cross-court to improve accuracy and simulate match scenarios.

### TARGET DRILL

1. **Instructions**:
- **Set Cones as Targets**:
    - Place cones on the opposite side of the net in different spots, targeting areas where players would attack in a real match (e.g., down the line, cross-court).
- **Player Line-Up**:
    - Line players up at the baseline. Feed the ball to each player one by one.
- **Hitting Execution**:
    - Players aim for the cones using a **forehand stroke**.
2. **Scoring**:
- Each time a player hits a cone, they earn **points**.
- Points can be tallied, and players who accumulate the most points receive **rewards**.
3. **Format Options**:
- **Beginner Level**:
    - Use larger target areas and feed slower balls.
- **Advanced Level**:
    - Use smaller targets and faster feeds to challenge accuracy.
4. **Coaching Tips**:
- Focus on **technique**: Encourage players to use proper **forehand form** while maintaining control and aim.
- Provide feedback on how to improve **accuracy** and **footwork** during the drill.
5. **Purpose**:
- Improve players' ability to **aim** and **hit targets** in different match scenarios, developing their accuracy and strategic thinking.

### FOREHAND CHALLENGE

1. **Instructions**:
    - **Divide into Two Teams**:
        - Players are split into two teams, each team coming up with a **team name** and **cheer**.
    - **Line Up Players**:
        - Players line up behind their respective starting points.
    - **Hitting Execution**:
        - Players take turns hitting the ball across the net.
        - After hitting, players pick up their own ball and return to the line.
2. **Scoring**:
    - Each successful hit that crosses the net earns the team **X points**.
    - **Bonus points** are awarded for hitting cones placed on the opposite side of the net.
3. **Format Options**:
    - **Format 1 (For Beginner Players)**:
        - Teams start closer to the net for easier hits and quicker success.
    - **Format 2 (For Advanced Players)**:
        - Teams start farther back near the baseline to challenge their accuracy and power.
        - Group A starts closer to the attack line.
        - Group B starts further back, closer to the baseline.
4. **Coaching Tips**:
    - Correct players' **forehand** and **backhand form**.
    - Encourage players to focus on **accuracy** and hitting the ball **into target zones**.
5. **Winning Condition**:
    - The first team to reach the **pre-determined point total** wins the challenge.

1. **Line Up Players**:

5. **Repeat:**
    - Continue the drill, encouraging players to focus on their **accuracy**, **aim**, and **shot placement** in different court zones.
    - Adjust the cones to practice **cross-court**, **down-the-line**, and other strategic shots to simulate various match scenarios.
6. **Purpose**:
    - Improve players' ability to **aim** and **hit targets** in different match scenarios, developing their accuracy and strategic thinking.

### FOREHAND APPROACH (ADVANCED)

1. **Line Up Players**:
    - Players start lined up behind the **baseline**.
2. **Cone Setup**:
    - Place one **cone** on the right-hand side near the **baseline**, and another cone **closer to the net** on the same side.
3. **Drill Execution**:
    - Players will **shuffle** towards the first cone, **go around it**, and hit a forehand.
    - After hitting, they continue to the second cone (closer to the net) and execute a **forehand approach** shot.
    - The goal is to hit the approach shot toward **targets** (cones or zones) placed on the opposite side of the court.
4. **Purpose**:
    - This drill trains players to use the **forehand approach** effectively by improving **footwork**, **shot placement**, and **court awareness**, while focusing on transitioning to the net to close out points.

### RECOVERY FOREHANDS (ADVANCED)

1. **Line Up Players**:
    - Line players up behind the **baseline**.
2. **Set the Cone**:
    - Place a **cone** on the **right-hand side** of the court near the sideline.
3. **Drill Execution**:
    - Each player starts in the **middle of the court**.
    - Players **shuffle** toward the cone, **circle around** the outside of it, and then hit the ball as if returning a forehand.
    - After hitting, players **shuffle back to the middle**, ready for the next repetition.
4. **Repeat**:
    - Continue this drill, focusing on quick movement, positioning, and recovery.

## Game of the Day (10 min)

In the **Game of the Day** for forehands, players will compete in a fun, competitive format that emphasizes forehand stroke development.

### HANGMAN

1. **Setup**:
    - Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
    - Players will take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (e.g., forehand) or previously learned skills.
2. **Game Rules**:
    - **Objective**: Players must hit the ball **over the net** and within the **court boundaries**.
    - **Body Parts**: If a player succeeds, they "lose" a body part (start with one arm, then a leg, etc.). The progression continues until they are hitting from the ground.
    - If a player **misses** their last attempt (after all body parts are "lost"), they go to the other side of the net, also known as **jail** (or become a shark or go into detention).
3. **Escaping Jail**:
    - To escape from jail, players must catch a ball, either after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
    - Players continue until only one player remains, or the coach decides to end the game. Those who avoid missing shots and "losing body parts" will keep going.

### BATTLESHIP

1. **Setup**:
    - **Divide players** into two teams.
    - Each team positions themselves on **opposite sides** of the net.
2. **Cone Placement**:
    - Each team sets up **cones** (representing ships) on their side of the court, strategically placing them in various locations.
3. **Game Rules**:
    - Players will take turns using the **skill of the day** (e.g., forehand, backhand) to try and hit the cones on the opposite side of the net.
    - The goal is to **hit and "sink"** the opposing team's ships (cones) by knocking them down.
4. **Winning Condition**:
    - The first team to destroy all of the other team's ships (cones) **wins** the game.

### JAILBREAK

1. **Setup**:
    - Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
    - Players take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (or previously learned skills if it's past Week 1).
2. **Game Rules**:
    - **Objective**: Players aim to hit the ball over the net and into the court.
    - **Losing Lives**: If successful, the player **loses a life** (starting with a body part).
    - If they **miss** their shot, they go to the **other side of the net** (jail, become a shark, or detention).
3. **Escaping Jail**:
    - To escape from jail, players must **catch a ball** from a rally after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
    - Players continue hitting until only one player remains, or until the coach ends the game. Players who successfully keep the ball in play without missing stay in the game longer.
5. **Purpose**:
    - This game helps players practice their consistency and recovery while keeping things fun and competitive.

### SKITTLES

1. **Setup**:
    - Line all players up on **one side** of the court.
2. **Game Rules**:
    - When the coach calls out the word **"skittles"**, all players must **run** from one side of the court to the other.
    - As they run, the coach will **throw balls** at the players.
    - If a player is **hit by a ball**, they are eliminated and must step aside to help the coach throw balls at the remaining players.
3. **Winning Condition**:
    - The game continues until only **one player** is left standing, and that player is the **winner.**
4. **Purpose:**
    - Improve agility, quick reflexes, and dodging skills. It encourages players to stay alert, react quickly, and move with speed

### FIREBALL

1. **Setup**:
    - Players spread out across the court, ready for the coach to hit balls at random spots.
2. **Game Rules**:
    - The coach will hit **fewer balls** than the number of players to **random locations** on the court.
    - Players must quickly move to **catch or hit** the balls.
    - Any player who **fails to get a ball** will be **eliminated** and must step aside to help the coach toss balls for the remaining players.
3. **Progression**:
    - Each round, the number of balls decreases, ensuring that there are always **fewer balls than players**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
    - The game continues until only **one player** remains, who is declared the **winner**.
5. **Purpose:**
    - The purpose of this game is to enhance players' **speed**, **anticipation**, and **court coverage**.

### FULL SET (ADVANCED)

1. **Set Up:**
    - Divide players into two groups.
    - Players from each group face each other one-on-one.
2. **Match Play:**
    - Play in a standard scoring format (15, 30, 40, game) for 6 games, which equals one set.
    - Ensure serves are diagonal and must land in the serving box.
3. **Winning Condition:**
    - The first player to win 6 games wins the set.
    - Accurate ball calls and proper court boundaries must be followed.
4. **Repeat:**
    - Continue the match format until all players have faced each other or time ends.

### KING OF THE COURT (BEGINNER / ADVANCED)

1. **Set Up**:
- Choose **one or two players** to be the **king/queen** on one side of the net.
- The rest of the players line up on the **opposite side** of the court.
2. **Challenger Rotation**:
- Players from the opposite side challenge the king/queen **one at a time** by playing a point against them.
3. **Winning Conditions**:
- **First Point**: If the challenger wins the first point, they earn the right to play a second point against the king/queen.
- **Second Point**: If the challenger wins **two consecutive points**, they become the **new king/queen**.
4. **Automatic Victory**:
- If a challenger hits a **winner** (a shot that the king/queen cannot reach or return) during the **first point**, they **immediately** become the new king/queen, skipping the need for a second point.
5. **Repeat**:
- Continue the drill, with challengers rotating through the line to take on the reigning king/queen.
- The game keeps going until a new king/queen is crowned or the drill time ends.

## Life Lesson (Ongoing)

**Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.

1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
    - Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
2. **Explain the Meaning**:
    - Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
        - **Word**: **Integrity**
        - **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it's about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
    - Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
        - Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
        - Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
4. **Assign a Task**:
    - Ask the players to think about the word during the week and find examples in their own lives or in the stories they read or hear about. They should come back next week ready to:
        - Share their understanding of the word.
        - Give an example of how they or someone they know demonstrated this life lesson in sports or everyday life.
5. **Wrap-Up**:
    - Encourage players to practice the life lesson throughout the week, both in their sports activities and in their personal lives. Remind them that these lessons help them become better athletes and better people.

This activity emphasizes the importance of applying sports values to real-life situations, fostering personal growth and character development in young athletes.
