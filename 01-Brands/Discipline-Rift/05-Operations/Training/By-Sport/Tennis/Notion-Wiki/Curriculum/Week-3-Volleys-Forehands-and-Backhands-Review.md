### TENNIS SIMON SAYS

1. **Setup:**
	- Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
	- Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
	- **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
	- Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
2. **Game Rules:**
	- The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
	- If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
3. **Commands:**
	- **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
	- **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
	- **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
	- **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
	- **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
	- **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
		- Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
		- **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
		- Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
	- **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
		- Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
		- Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
	- The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
5. **Elimination:**
	- Any player who follows a command that doesn't begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
	- Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
6. **Winner's Reward:**
	- The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.

### SKITTLES

1. **Setup**:
	- Players line up in a single horizontal line along the baseline.
	- The coach stands in the middle of the court with a supply of tennis balls.
2. **Gameplay**:
	- The coach calls out different candy names (e.g., "Sneakers," "Starburst," "M&M's").
	- When the coach says "Skittles," all players must run across the court to the opposite baseline.
	- As they run, the coach tosses tennis balls towards the players. The goal for the players is to dodge the balls.
3. **Elimination**:
	- If a player is touched by a ball, they are out and join the coach in the middle.
	- Eliminated players can help the coach by calling out the candy names but cannot toss the balls for safety reasons.
4. **Winning**:
	- The game continues until only one player remains untagged. This player is the winner.
5. **Purpose**:
	- Improve players' agility, reaction time, and dodging skills in a fun and engaging way.

### TENNIS TAG

1. **Explain:**
	- Define the boundaries on the court within which the game will be played.
	- Select one player to start as "it," holding a tennis ball.
2. **Gameplay**:
	- The player who is "it" must tag other players by touching them with the volleyball (no throwing, just a soft touch).
	- Players must stay within the boundaries; if anyone steps out, they become "it."
	- The tagged player then becomes "it" and continues the game.
3. **Team Variation**:
	- If there are many players, divide them into two teams:
		- **Chasers**: A team of players with volleyballs who try to tag the runners.
		- **Runners**: The other team, without volleyballs, must avoid being tagged.
	- The chasers have a set time (e.g., 30 seconds) to tag as many runners as possible. After time is up, teams switch roles.
4. **Purpose**:
	- Develop agility, quickness, and spatial awareness while adding an element of fun and teamwork.

## Skill — Volleys (5 min)

**BREAKDOWN**

1. **Briefing**
	- **Review of Previous Skills:**
		- Review the forehand & backhand form learned last week.
		- Ask a few players to demonstrate proper forehand form, focusing on footwork and follow-through.
	- **Introduction of New Skill:**
		- Explain that today's focus will be on volleys, both forehand and backhand volley.
2. **Demonstration (Breakdown of Backhand)**
	- **Stance and Alignment**
		- **Ready Position**: Feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on balls of feet. Hold the racket at chest height, facing the net.
		- **Alignment**: Body faces the net, feet slightly angled. Step forward with the left foot for forehand (right-handed players) or right foot for backhand.
	- **Grip**
		- **Continental Grip**: Used for both volleys, offers control and flexibility.
		- Place the base knuckle on the second bevel (as if shaking hands with the racket).
		- Maintain a firm, relaxed grip for quick transitions.
	- **Body Rotation**
		- Minimal rotation is needed.
		- For forehand, turn slightly towards the dominant side.
		- For backhand, rotate towards the non-dominant side, keeping your head steady.
	- **Swing Mechanics**
		- **Short and Controlled**: Use a compact, direct motion, "punching" the ball with a slightly open racket face to lift over the net.
	- **Follow-Through**
		- Minimal follow-through.
			- Forehand: Racket finishes in front/right.
			- Backhand: Racket finishes in front/left. Avoid long follow-throughs for better control.
	- **Footwork**
		- **Step In**: Step forward with the opposite foot while hitting. Return quickly to the ready position, shuffling to prepare for the next shot.
3. **Imitation (Players Imitate the Coach)**
	- **Body Position**
		- Players imitate the coach's ready stance: feet shoulder-width, knees bent, racket in front.
		- Practice stepping forward with the opposite foot (left for forehand, right for backhand), keeping the upper body steady.
	- **Swing Practice**
		- The coach shows the forehand volley; players copy the short, controlled "punching" motion with an open racket face.
		- Players replicate the backhand volley, focusing on a compact swing and minimal follow-through.
		- Practice both volleys, stepping in and returning to the ready position after each swing, following the coach.
4. **Correction (Coach Corrects Player Form)**
	- **Stance and Alignment**
		- Adjust players' stances to ensure feet are shoulder-width apart, knees bent, and weight balanced on the balls of the feet.
		- Correct any misalignment; make sure they're facing the net with proper foot placement for forehand and backhand volleys.
	- **Grip**
		- Check that players are using the continental grip, adjusting hand placement if needed for better control and quick transitions.
	- **Foot Position**
		- Ensure players step forward with the correct foot: left for forehand, right for backhand volleys.
		- Correct any hesitations or improper stepping to maintain smooth, decisive movement.
	- **Swing Mechanics**
		- Address any issues with swing length; emphasize keeping it short and controlled with a stable racket face.
		- Encourage players to "punch" the ball rather than taking a full swing.
	- **Follow-Through**
		- Correct players who overextend, reminding them to keep the follow-through short and stop the racket in front of their body.

## Volleys + Forehand & Backhand (30 min)

**FORM**

- **Start in the Ready Position:** Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, racquet held with both hands, facing the net.
- **Turn Foot Positioning to the Secondary Hand's Side:** Rotate your body so that your non-dominant hand leads the motion.
- **Grip:** Place both hands on the handle of the racquet, ensuring proper grip (Eastern or Semi-Western for two-handed, Continental for one-handed).
- **Racquet Alignment:** Align the racquet with your body, keeping it close to your torso.
- **Swing:** Use a low-to-high motion to swing the racquet towards the net, aiming to make contact with the ball around waist height.
- **Follow Through:** Finish the swing above the opposite shoulder to ensure proper mechanics.
- **Return to Ready Position:** After the swing, return to the ready position to prepare for the next shot.

### PLAYER CUES

- **What is a Volley?**
	- A volley is a shot hit before the ball bounces, typically performed at the service line or closer to the net. It's an aggressive move designed to disrupt the opponent's rhythm or win the point quickly.
- **Grip**
	- Use the **Continental Grip**: The racket should be held perpendicular to the ground, with the face slightly pointing to the sky. This allows for control and the ability to impart backspin.
- **Footwork**
	- **Forehand Volley**: Step forward with your opposite foot (left foot for right-handed players).
	- **Backhand Volley**: Step forward with the same foot (right foot for right-handed players).
- **Common Mistakes to Watch For:**
	- **Not stepping forward** with the opposite foot during a forehand volley.
	- **Failing to move forward** towards the ball while volleying.
	- **Bringing the racket back** and swinging instead of using a short, controlled punch.
	- **Overhitting or smashing** the ball instead of maintaining control.
	- **Confusing hand movements** during the volley, leading to improper execution.

**DRILLS**

- **Divide Groups as Needed**: Split players into smaller groups to ensure more focused practice if there are many participants.
- **Set Cones as Targets**: Place cones on the opposite side of the net in different spots for players to aim at.
- **Volley Position Instruction**: Instruct players on the correct volley stance while they are in front of the net, emphasizing proper footwork and grip.

### FEEDING DRILL

**Instructions:**

- **Feeding Drill (Both Strokes)**:
	- Players practice both forehand and backhand volleys in the same drill.

1. **Player Line-Up**:
	- Line players up in front of the net. Feed balls to each player, alternating between forehand and backhand volleys.
2. **Hitting Execution**:
	- Players aim to return the ball using both strokes, focusing on proper grip, stance, and footwork.
3. **Format Options:**
	**Beginner Level**:
	- Feed balls at a slower pace from the same side of the net for each stroke.
	**Advanced Level**:
	- Feed balls at a faster pace from the opposite side of the net or use racquet feeding for more challenge.
4. **Coaching Tips:**
	- Encourage switching between forehand and backhand smoothly.
	- Provide feedback on transitioning between strokes and maintaining correct form.
5. **Purpose:**
	- Improve players' ability to switch between strokes quickly, enhancing overall control and versatility at the net.

### TARGET DRILL

**Instructions:**

1. **Set Cones as Targets**:
	- Place cones on the opposite side of the net in various spots (down the line, cross-court) to simulate match scenarios for volleys.
2. **Player Line-Up**:
	- Line players up at the service line. Feed balls to each player one by one, alternating between forehand and backhand volleys.
3. **Hitting Execution**:
	- Players aim to hit the cones using both forehand and backhand volleys, focusing on precise targeting.
4. **Format Options:**
	- **Beginner Level**:
		- Use slower feeds, alternating forehand and backhand volleys from the same side of the net.
	- **Advanced Level**:
		- Increase feed speed, alternate sides, or use racquet feeding for a more challenging and game-like experience.
5. **Winning the Drill:**
	- **Scoring**:
		- Players earn points for each cone they hit with their volley. Assign different point values based on cone difficulty (e.g., down-the-line cones might be worth more than cross-court ones).
	- **Rewards**:
		- Players with the most points win rewards (e.g., fewer conditioning drills, small prizes).
6. **Coaching Tips:**
	- Encourage proper volleying form, focusing on a short, controlled swing.
	- Provide feedback on aim, footwork, and consistency during volleys.
7. **Purpose:**
	- Enhance volley accuracy and consistency while developing strategic targeting in match-like scenarios.

### PUTTING IT ALL TOGETHER

**Instructions:**

1. **Player Line-Up**:
	- Players start lined up at the baseline, ready to go one by one.
2. **Hitting Execution**:
	- **Step 1**: Each player starts with a **forehand groundstroke** at the baseline.
	- **Step 2**: Immediately switch to a **backhand groundstroke**.
	- **Step 3**: The player then runs forward to the net to perform a **forehand volley**.
	- **Step 4**: Finish with a **backhand volley** at the net.
3. **Format Options:**
	- **Beginner Level**:
		- Use slower feeds, allowing the player time to adjust between groundstrokes and volleys.
		- Focus on consistency, ensuring players can complete all four shots accurately.
	- **Advanced Level**:
		- Increase feed speed and vary ball placement, challenging players to react quickly.
		- Introduce more challenging targets for each stroke, both at the baseline and the net.
4. **Footwork**:
	- Emphasize quick and efficient foot movement.
		- Players should side shuffle between the forehand and backhand groundstrokes.
		- Encourage explosive movement when transitioning from the baseline to the net.
		- Ensure players split-step when arriving at the net before executing each volley.
5. **Coaching Tips**:
	- **Forehand and Backhand Groundstrokes**:
		- Ensure players maintain proper racket positioning and swing mechanics.
		- Remind them to follow through on each stroke.
6. **Volleys**:
	- Keep the volleys compact with a short, controlled motion.
	- Remind players to use the continental grip for both volleys and to step into each shot.
7. **Purpose**:
	- This drill is designed to improve players' ability to transition between baseline and net play, developing agility, footwork, and shot accuracy for both groundstrokes and volleys.

### OVERHAND SMASH (ADVANCED)

**Teaching the Overhead Smash**

- **Focus on Technique**: The overhead smash requires precise technique, as players often mistake it for a regular forehand.
- **Grip**: Use the **Continental Grip**. Place the 8th pebble of the grip against the hard part of your index finger.
- **Racket Position**: Bring the racket behind your head, as if scratching your back.
- **Ball Tracking**: Point your non-dominant hand at the ball to maintain focus and accuracy.
- **Contact Point**: Decide whether to hit the ball in the air or let it bounce before smashing.

**Overhead Smash Drill**

1. **Player Line-Up**:
	- Have players line up at the baseline.
2. **Execution**:
	- The coach feeds a lob to each player, one at a time.
	- Depending on the height of the lob, instruct players to either hit the ball in the air or let it bounce first.
3. **Target Practice**:
	- Set targets on the opposite side of the court for players to aim at when hitting their overhead smash.

**Objective:** This drill helps players develop accuracy, power, and confidence in executing the overhead smash.

### TRI-STROKE DRILL (ADVANCED)

**Setup**:

1. **Player Line-Up**:

## Game of the Day (10 min)

In the **Game of the Day** for forehands, players will compete in a fun, competitive format that emphasizes backhand + forehand stroke development.

### POP-UP VOLLEYS

**Instructions:**

1. **Player Setup**:
	- Have players squat in a ready position close to the net, facing the coach. Each player is assigned a number or a name.
2. **Execution**:
	- The coach randomly calls out a player's name or number.
	- The called player must "pop up" from the squatting position to quickly execute either a **forehand or backhand volley** fed by the coach.
	- After hitting the volley, the player returns to their squatting position.
3. **Format Options:**
	- **Beginner Level**:
		- Use slower, more controlled feeds to allow players to adjust and focus on form.
		- Call out names/numbers at a slower pace to give players time to react.
	- **Advanced Level**:
		- Increase the speed of feeds and call out names/numbers faster to improve reaction time.
		- Occasionally feed balls to players' non-dominant side to challenge their ability to adjust.
4. **Footwork**:
	- Emphasize quick, explosive movement when popping up from the squatting position.
	- Encourage players to use a split-step before hitting the volley to maintain balance and control.
5. **Coaching Tips**:
	- Ensure players maintain the **continental grip** and keep a **short, controlled swing**.
	- Encourage quick reactions and proper foot positioning when transitioning from squatting to hitting.
	- Provide feedback on racket face angle and body positioning.
6. **Purpose**:
	- This game helps players develop quick reflexes, improve volley accuracy, and build leg strength for explosive movements at the net. It also reinforces the importance of staying alert and ready during play.

### HANGMAN

1. **Setup**:
	- Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
	- Players will take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (e.g., backhand) or previously learned skills.
2. **Game Rules**:
	- **Objective**: Players must hit the ball **over the net** and within the **court boundaries**.
	- **Body Parts**: If a player succeeds, they "lose" a body part (start with one arm, then a leg, etc.). The progression continues until they are hitting from the ground.
	- If a player **misses** their last attempt (after all body parts are "lost"), they go to the other side of the net, also known as **jail** (or become a shark or go into detention).
3. **Escaping Jail**:
	- To escape from jail, players must catch a ball, either after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
	- Players continue until only one player remains, or the coach decides to end the game. Those who avoid missing shots and "losing body parts" will keep going.

### BATTLESHIP

1. **Setup**:
	- **Divide players** into two teams.
	- Each team positions themselves on **opposite sides** of the net.
2. **Cone Placement**:
	- Each team sets up **cones** (representing ships) on their side of the court, strategically placing them in various locations.
3. **Game Rules**:
	- Players will take turns using the **skill of the day** (e.g., forehand, backhand) to try and hit the cones on the opposite side of the net.
	- The goal is to **hit and "sink"** the opposing team's ships (cones) by knocking them down.
4. **Winning Condition**:
	- The first team to destroy all of the other team's ships (cones) **wins** the game.

### JAILBREAK

1. **Setup**:
	- Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
	- Players take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (or previously learned skills if it's past Week 1).
2. **Game Rules**:
	- **Objective**: Players aim to hit the ball over the net and into the court.
	- **Losing Lives**: If successful, the player **loses a life** (starting with a body part).
	- If they **miss** their shot, they go to the **other side of the net** (jail, become a shark, or detention).
3. **Escaping Jail**:
	- To escape from jail, players must **catch a ball** from a rally after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
	- Players continue hitting until only one player remains, or until the coach ends the game. Players who successfully keep the ball in play without missing stay in the game longer.
5. **Purpose**:
	- This game helps players practice their consistency and recovery while keeping things fun and competitive.

### SKITTLES

1. **Setup**:
	- Line all players up on **one side** of the court.
2. **Game Rules**:
	- When the coach calls out the word **"skittles"**, all players must **run** from one side of the court to the other.
	- As they run, the coach will **throw balls** at the players.
	- If a player is **hit by a ball**, they are eliminated and must step aside to help the coach throw balls at the remaining players.
3. **Winning Condition**:
	- The game continues until only **one player** is left standing, and that player is the **winner.**
4. **Purpose:**
	- Improve agility, quick reflexes, and dodging skills. It encourages players to stay alert, react quickly, and move with speed

### FIREBALL

1. **Setup**:
	- Players spread out across the court, ready for the coach to hit balls at random spots.
2. **Game Rules**:
	- The coach will hit **fewer balls** than the number of players to **random locations** on the court.
	- Players must quickly move to **catch or hit** the balls.
	- Any player who **fails to get a ball** will be **eliminated** and must step aside to help the coach toss balls for the remaining players.
3. **Progression**:
	- Each round, the number of balls decreases, ensuring that there are always **fewer balls than players**.
4. **Winning Condition**:
	- The game continues until only **one player** remains, who is declared the **winner**.
5. **Purpose:**
	- The purpose of this game is to enhance players' **speed**, **anticipation**, and **court coverage**.

### FULL SET (ADVANCED)

1. **Set Up:**
	- Divide players into two groups.
	- Players from each group face each other one-on-one.
2. **Match Play:**
	- Play in a standard scoring format (15, 30, 40, game) for 6 games, which equals one set.
	- Ensure serves are diagonal and must land in the serving box.
3. **Winning Condition:**
	- The first player to win 6 games wins the set.
	- Accurate ball calls and proper court boundaries must be followed.
4. **Repeat:**
	- Continue the match format until all players have faced each other or time ends.

### KING / QUEEN OF THE COURT (ADVANCED)

1. **Set Up**:
	- Choose **one or two players** to be the **king/queen** on one side of the net.
	- The rest of the players line up on the **opposite side** of the court.
2. **Challenger Rotation**:
	- Players from the opposite side challenge the king/queen **one at a time** by playing a point against them.
3. **Winning Conditions**:
	- **First Point**: If the challenger wins the first point, they earn the right to play a second point against the king/queen.
	- **Second Point**: If the challenger wins **two consecutive points**, they become the **new king/queen**.
4. **Automatic Victory**:
	- If a challenger hits a **winner** (a shot that the king/queen cannot reach or return) during the **first point**, they **immediately** become the new king/queen, skipping the need for a second point.
5. **Repeat**:
	- Continue the drill, with challengers rotating through the line to take on the reigning king/queen.
	- The game keeps going until a new king/queen is crowned or the drill time ends.

### RALLY (ADVANCED)

1. **Set Up:**
	- Choose one player to be on one side of the net.
	- The rest of the players line up on the opposite side.
2. **Challenger Rotation:**
	- Players take turns rallying with the player on the other side of the net, one at a time.
3. **Objective:**
	- Each player attempts to keep the rally going for as long as possible with the player on the opposite side.
4. **Winning Condition:**
	- The player who manages to sustain the rally for the longest number of hits wins the round.
5. **Repeat:**
	- Continue the drill, rotating through the line of challengers. Players can switch sides after each rally.

## Life Lesson (Ongoing)

**Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.

1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
	- Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
2. **Explain the Meaning**:
	- Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
		- **Word**: **Integrity**
		- **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it's about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
	- Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
		- Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
		- Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
4. **Assign a Task**:
