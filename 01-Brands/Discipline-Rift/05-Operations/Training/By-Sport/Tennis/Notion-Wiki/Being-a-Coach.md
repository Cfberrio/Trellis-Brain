## Origin of the word "coach"
The word comes from a Hungarian town called **Kocs**. Hungarians from Kocs made wagons named *kocsi szekér* (wagon of Kocs) for transportation — an enclosed four-wheeled vehicle pulled by horses, in which people used to travel.

1. Kocsis were imported to other countries and called "coach" in English.
2. Today the word coach is commonly used as a synonym for cars and buses as well.
3. Nowadays a "coach" is known as a bus that carries passengers.

## You are the coach
From the origin of the word coach, just like the wagons and buses, you have the responsibility of taking the players:

- To where they want to go
- To where you know they can go
- When they can't get there themselves

## Moral responsibility
1. To take your player somewhere
2. You need to go
3. When you can't get there yourself

## Take action
- Only 25% of kids aged 6–12 consistently stick with golf (National Golf Foundation).
- In a group of 20 young golfers, only 5 may continue playing regularly.
- What are we going to do about the 15 players who might never return?

By being passionate for sport and making the sport enjoyable, we connect players to the sport. **Being a coach is teaching love.**
