## Warm-Up Game — THE LINES GAME

1. **Court Overview**: Explain key court lines (kitchen, baseline, sidelines). *(Diagram lives in Notion only — S3 image not imported.)*
2. **Instructions**:
   - **Call Out a Line**: The coach randomly calls out one of the court lines (e.g. "Baseline!") and players race to it.
   - **Race to the Line**: All players run as quickly as possible to the line called.
   - **Elimination**: The last player to reach the line is eliminated from the round.
   - **Practice Rounds**: Run a few rounds without eliminating players, so everyone understands the game.
   - **Elimination Rounds**: After practice rounds, start eliminating the last player to reach the line until one remains.
   - **Calling the Next Line**: Eliminated players can help by calling out the next line.
   - **Winner**: The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, like choosing the next game or earning extra points.
3. **Purpose**: Familiarize students with court dimensions and terminology, and improve footwork.

## Warm-Up Game — PICKLEBALL SIMON SAYS

1. **Setup**
   - Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
   - Feet **shoulder-width apart**.
   - **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the paddle.
   - Players start upright and relaxed, holding their paddles.
2. **Game Rules**
   - The coach ("Simon") gives commands; players only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says…"**.
   - If a player follows a command without "Simon says," that player is **eliminated**.
3. **Commands**
   - **"Simon says, Ready!"** — move into ready position (feet wide, knees bent, paddle prepared).
   - **"Simon says, Back!"** — return to upright, relaxed position.
   - **"Simon says, Left!" / "Right!"** — shuffle laterally while holding ready position.
   - **"Simon says, Ball!"** — coach simulates hitting to one side; players move that direction as if preparing to hit.
   - **"Simon says, Split Step!"** — players perform a small jump / split step.
   - **"Simon says, Forehand!"** — turn body toward dominant-hand side, paddle drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball, swing forward.
   - **"Simon says, Backhand!"** — turn toward non-dominant side, both hands on the grip, draw back and swing forward.
4. **Trick Commands** — commands given without "Simon says." Players who follow are eliminated.
5. **Elimination** — continue until one player remains.
6. **Winner's Reward** — choose the next drill or earn extra points.

## Warm-Up Game — GRIP: THE HANDSHAKE METHOD *(Week 3 only)*

**Handshake Method**
- Have students imagine shaking hands with the paddle.
- Fingers wrap the handle naturally; thumb and index finger create a "V" pointing toward the paddle's edge.
- Grip firmly enough for control but relaxed enough to avoid stiffness.

**Alternate Grip Practice**
- Students run a hand down the edge of the paddle from the top to the handle and grip naturally at the base. Reinforces hand positioning while staying simple for beginners.

### Game: Frying Pan Bounce
**Setup**: Each student gets a paddle and ball. Demonstrate holding the paddle flat, like a frying pan, with the correct grip.

1. **Bounce Practice**: Students gently drop the ball onto the paddle and keep it bouncing steadily. Encourage controlled wrist motion, not large arm swings.
2. **Set Targets**: Start at 10 consecutive bounces, build to 20 or 30.
3. **Challenge Variations**
   - *Individual*: most consecutive bounces.
   - *Partner*: pairs alternate turns to reach a set number.
   - *Class*: highest total, or fastest to a set target (e.g. 50 bounces).
4. **Switch to Backhand**: Rotate the paddle to practice backhand bounces, keeping the same grip with a slight wrist adjustment.

**Coaching Tips**
- **Monitor Grip**: "Hold the paddle like you're holding a frying pan — firm but not too tight!"
- **Encourage Controlled Movements**: If students struggle, ask for smaller, softer bounces.
- **Provide Feedback**: Celebrate consistency ("Great job keeping the ball steady for 15 bounces!") and offer adjustments ("Try to keep the paddle flat and use gentle taps.").

**Wrap-Up and Progression**
- After mastering bounces, add movement: walk in a straight line or around cones while keeping the ball bouncing. Builds paddle control in dynamic situations.
- Reinforce correct grip for both forehand and backhand before progressing to other drills.

### Advanced: Reverse Frying Pan
Once students are proficient at bouncing upward, flip the challenge — bounce the ball downward into the ground and keep it going.

1. **Demonstrate**: Gently tap the ball into the ground with the paddle flat or angled slightly downward. Emphasize controlled, soft bounces and finding the sweet spot.
2. **Practice Steps**
   - Drop the ball, catch it on the paddle to feel the rebound.
   - Attempt 3–5 controlled downward bounces.
   - Build to continuous bounces at a steady tempo.
3. **Progressive Goals**: 5 in a row → 10 → 15+.
4. **Challenges**: individual (most consecutive), partner (first to 20), class (highest total in a time limit).
