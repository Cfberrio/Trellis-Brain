# Being a Coach

## Origin of the word
The word comes from the Hungarian town of **Kocs**. Hungarians from Kocs made wagons called *kocsi szekér* (wagon of Kocs) for transportation — an enclosed four-wheeled vehicle pulled by horses, in which people used to travel.

1. Kocsis were exported to other countries and called "coach" in English.
2. Today the word is commonly used for cars and buses as well.
3. A "coach" is now known as a bus that carries passengers.

## You are the coach
From the origin of the word — just like the wagons and buses — you have the responsibility of taking the players:
- To where they want to go
- To where you know they can go
- When they can't get there themselves

## Moral responsibility
1. To take your player somewhere
2. You need to go
3. When you can't get there yourself

## Take action
- Only 25% of kids aged 6–12 consistently stick with golf (National Golf Foundation).
- In a group of 20 young golfers, only 5 may continue playing regularly.
- What are we going to do about the 15 players who might never return? By being passionate about the sport and making it enjoyable, we connect players to the sport. `Being a coach is teaching love.`

> Import note: the retention stat is stated for golf in the source wiki. It is used as the general case for youth sport dropout, not as a pickleball figure.
