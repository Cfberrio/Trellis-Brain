# Week 1 — Forehands

## INTRODUCTION (10 MIN)

**WELCOME**
- Greet all students and introduce them to the pickleball season.
- Coaches introduce themselves.
- Briefly explain the mission of DR Sports & Athletics:
  - **DR = Discipline Rift**.
  - **Discipline Rift**: introduction and development in pickleball, and introduction to the life lessons we can learn from the sport.
  - Ask: What is discipline? Players may think discipline means receiving punishment — clarify that discipline is the power to commit to a decision and follow through.

**TEAM EXPECTATIONS**
- Arrive on time for class, held every (insert day) of each week.
- Be respectful and supportive of fellow players by helping each other improve.
- Listen to your coaches and follow instructions.

**ACTIONS & CONSEQUENCES**
- Every action has consequences:
  - Positive actions lead to good consequences.
  - Negative actions lead to bad consequences.
- 3 Call-Out Rule:
  - **First Callout**: Coaches will speak to the player.
  - **Second Callout**: Player will be placed in timeout during class.
  - **Third Callout**: Parent talk.
- Program Overview:
  - The program includes 6 weeks of classes, held on (day of practice) each week.
  - Remind students to remember and attend each week.

### ICE BREAKER / TEAM BONDING (OPTIONAL)
1. **Pass Your Name Around** — Circle up and pass a pickleball while saying names. *Goal*: memorize names.
2. **Team Building Circles** — Players take turns sharing something positive about a teammate, such as a skill. *Goal*: affirmation and encouragement.
3. **Team Handshake** — Create a unique team handshake or high-five routine. *Goal*: bonding and unity.
4. **Team Chants** — Create simple, energetic chants for before practice or during drills. *Goal*: energy and momentum.
5. **Clap Challenge** — In a circle, players throw a ball up and clap a set number of times (start at 5) before catching. Increase to 10, 15, and up. *Goal*: coordination and focus.

## SKILL (5 MIN)

**BREAKDOWN**

1. **Introduction (introduce all pickleball skills)**
   - **Forehand Drive**: basic groundstroke hit with the dominant hand, emphasizing control and direction.
   - **Backhand Drive**: stroke hit from the non-dominant side, requiring precision and balance.
   - **Dinks**: soft shots played close to the net, promoting finesse and touch.
   - **Serving**: underhand stroke used to start a rally.
   - Explain that today's focus is the **Forehand Drive**.
2. **Demonstration (Forehand Drive breakdown)**
   - **Stance and Alignment**: start in ready position — feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, paddle in front. Rotate toward your dominant side, keeping the paddle low.
   - **Grip and Paddle Position**: use the continental grip. Hold the paddle with the dominant hand; the other hand lightly balances the paddle's neck.
   - **Swing Mechanics**: bring the paddle back, pointing slightly downward. Step forward with the non-dominant foot. Swing forward with the paddle face slightly angled upward for clean contact.
   - **Contact Point**: make contact at waist height, slightly in front of the body. Aim the paddle face toward the target area.
   - **Follow-Through**: finish with the paddle pointing upward, ready to reset for the next shot.
3. **Imitation** — players mimic the coach step-by-step: stance, paddle position, swing, contact point, follow-through.
4. **Correction**
   - **Stance and Alignment**: knees bent, feet balanced.
   - **Grip**: firm yet relaxed.
   - **Contact Point**: reinforce contact in front of the body.
   - **Follow-Through**: encourage full completion of the swing for control and accuracy.

## FOREHANDS (30 MIN)

**FORM**
- **Start in Ready Position**: feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, paddle in front.
- **Turn Toward Dominant Hand**: rotate the torso to the dominant side, preparing for the swing.
- **Paddle Alignment**: bring the paddle back, pointing slightly downward.
- **Point with the Non-Dominant Hand**: use the free hand to point at the ball, aiding balance and direction.
- **Swing Mechanics**: swing forward, meeting the ball at waist level, with a slight upward motion for consistent control.
- **Recovery**: return to ready position quickly after the swing.

### ADVANCED PLAYER CUES
- **Incomplete Swing**: remind players to follow through completely for better control.
- **Incorrect Paddle Angle**: correct overly open or closed paddle faces to improve shot placement.
- **Footwork**: stay light on the feet, always ready for the next shot.
- **Recovery**: return to the center of the court after each shot.
- **Anticipation**: read the opponent's movements and position effectively.

### DRILL — ACCURACY CHALLENGE
- **Setup**: place cones or targets (hoops, buckets) on the opposite side of the net in strategic locations.
- **How to Play**: players stand at the baseline and take turns hitting balls to land inside the target zones.
- **Scoring**: 1 point for landing inside the zone, 2 points for hitting a target directly.
- **Advanced Variation**: players alternate between cross-court and down-the-line shots.
- **Coaching Tips**: emphasize follow-through and paddle control; keep eyes on the ball and aim consistently.

### DRILL — CONE TARGET CHALLENGE
**Objective**: improve forehand accuracy and precision.
**Setup**: arrange cones or targets marking specific zones (left, right, deep). Players line up at the baseline.
**Instructions**:
1. Players hit forehand strokes aiming for the cones in sequence.
2. Score 3 points for knocking down a cone, 2 for hitting the marked zone, 1 for landing the ball in bounds.
3. Adjust distance or target size for varied skill levels.
**Variation**: add time pressure to increase focus and intensity.

### DRILL — PARTNER DRILL CHALLENGE
**Objective**: develop consistency in forehand rallies.
**Setup**: pairs rally using forehands, aiming for 10 consecutive hits without errors. Use a mini-net or jump rope as the dividing line if court space is limited.
**Instructions**:
1. Start with slow-paced rallies to warm up.
2. Progress to rallies where each partner moves the other side-to-side.
3. Introduce scoring for accuracy (points for hitting specific court areas).
**Coaching Cues**: keep eyes on the ball; maintain proper weight transfer during strokes.

### DRILL — AROUND THE CLOCK
**Objective**: enhance forehand versatility and court coverage.
**Setup**: set cones around the court representing clock positions (12 o'clock is deep center). Players start at the baseline.
**Instructions**:
1. A coach or partner feeds balls to different clock positions.
2. Players hit forehands aiming to place the ball in the respective zone.
3. Rotate positions to ensure varied court coverage.
**Variation**: use a lob shot or backhand return if players miss the forehand.

### DRILL — ZIGZAG JAG
**Objective**: combine forehand skill with agility.
**Setup**: place cones along a zigzag path. A partner or coach stands at the end with a basket of balls.
**Instructions**:
1. Players move through the zigzag cones before receiving a ball.
2. At the end, players execute a forehand shot toward a target.
3. Encourage quick recovery to the starting position.
**Coaching Points**: stay low and balanced; make clean contact with the ball.

## GAME OF THE DAY (10 MIN)

### FOREHAND FRENZY
**Setup**
- Set up three target zones on the opposite side of the court:
  1. **Deep Center** (for drives)
  2. **Sidelines** (for placement shots)
  3. **Near the Kitchen Line** (for controlled drop shots)
- Use cones, hoops, or chalk to mark these areas.

**Equipment**: pickleball paddles and balls (1 per player); a net dividing the play area.

**How to Play**
1. **Game Start**: players line up behind the baseline. A coach or feeder tosses or hits balls to the players.
2. **Objective**: players hit forehands to land the ball in one of the designated target zones. Rotate players quickly to keep the line moving.
3. **Scoring**: 3 points deep center, 2 points sidelines, 1 point near the kitchen.
4. **Progression**: add movement challenges (shuffle left or right before hitting). Introduce strategic play — players call out their intended target before hitting.

**Winning Condition**: highest score after a set number of rounds wins a small prize or picks the next group activity.
**Purpose**: reinforces forehand fundamentals like accuracy and shot selection.
