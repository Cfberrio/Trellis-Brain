INTRODUCTION

Welcome to the world of volleyball coaching! Stepping into this role is an exciting adventure, whether you're here to help out at your child's school team, support a local club, or just share your love of the game. Coaching can feel a little overwhelming at first-there's a lot to think about, from teaching skills to keeping practices fun and organized-but you don't need to have all the answers on day one. This guide is here to give you a solid starting point, some simple tools, and a bit of confidence as you begin your journey.

Coaching is about much more than running drills or explaining the rules. You'll quickly discover that it's also about encouraging young players, creating a positive team environment, and helping kids fall in love with volleyball.

Need further support: coachforce@volleyballbop.co.nz (Stacy Percy, CoachForce Officer, Volleyball Bay of Plenty).

COACHING VOLLEYBALL

Coaching is one of the most rewarding ways to make a positive impact on children's lives. Every session is an opportunity to help young players discover the joy of volleyball, build confidence, and develop skills that will stay with them for life.

KiwiVolley is designed to make volleyball accessible and enjoyable for all players.

TIPS TO BE AN EFFECTIVE COACH

**Focus on Participation and Fun** - ensure every player gets plenty of touches on the ball and chances to be involved. Encourage teamwork, communication, and celebrating each other's successes. Keep activities game-like and enjoyable - long rallies and active play are the goal.

**Develop Skills Step by Step** - introduce fundamental volleyball skills (set, pass, serve, spike, block) in simple, age-appropriate ways. Progress skills gradually through age groups; focus on fundamental movement skills like balance, agility, speed, and coordination alongside volleyball skills.

**Teach Through Participation** - children learn best by doing, not just listening. Use clear goals, principles, and performance keys for each skill, and provide constructive feedback. Give them plenty of opportunities to practice in game-like situations.

**Effective Comms** - be a good role model. Use simple, age-appropriate language and clear demonstrations. Give instructions clearly, slowly, and with appropriate volume and expression. Listen to players' responses and observe their actions to ensure they understand and are engaged.

**Organize and Manage Efficiently** - keep groups small where possible, and plan drills so every player is active. Prepare equipment and space in advance to maximize practice time. Maintain a high activity level, minimize downtime, and transition smoothly between activities.

**Build a Positive Learning Environment** - set clear boundaries and expectations for behaviour. Be firm but relaxed, providing structure while allowing players to have fun. Adapt activities to suit individual abilities and make adjustments if a drill isn't working.

**Model the standards you expect** - be on time, prepared, and organized. Dress appropriately and present yourself confidently. Always give your best effort and expect the same from players.

WHAT IS KIWIVOLLEY?

KiwiVolley is volleyball made simple and fun for kids! It introduces young players to the game in an active, exciting, and easy-to-learn way. With smaller teams, softer balls, and simple rules, every child gets more touches, more chances to play, and plenty of opportunities to build confidence while learning the basics.

KiwiVolley also helps kids progress step by step through age groups. Younger players start with catching and throwing games, then move into rallies with three touches, basic serving, and rotation rules as they grow. The focus is always on fun, teamwork, and skill development-not just winning.

GENERAL RULES OF KIWIVOLLEY

- Played on a badminton court with a slightly lower net.
- 4 players play on the court at a time.
- Player in the back right position is the server. When your team wins the serve, everyone rotates one spot clockwise so all players get a turn.
- Games are played up to 25 points. Teams swap ends for a new set.
- Up to 3 touches on your side before the ball goes over the net.
- Every rally counts as 1 point for the team that wins it (Rally Point system).
- Substitutions roll into the serving position so everyone gets a fair chance to serve and play.
- On a serve, the ball can touch the net as long as it still goes over. Touching the net during play is a fault (point to the other team).
- You can't spike or attack the very first ball that comes over the net.
- A ball landing on the line counts as in.
- Servers must wait for the referee's whistle before serving.
- You can't hit the ball twice in a row (unless it's a block).
- It's okay to step under the net a little, but you can't interfere with the other team.
- All participants are expected to show good sportsmanship - encourage teammates, respect opponents, and listen to the refs.

KIWIVOLLEY - YEARS 3 & 4

For most players in Years 3 and 4, this is their first real experience with volleyball, so the focus is on having fun, learning basic skills, and getting active - not winning every game. Goals: getting comfortable with the ball through catching, passing, and hitting; learning to work together and take turns serving; practicing simple rules like rotating and rallying; enjoying the game so kids want to keep playing.

Modifications for Years 3 & 4: each team has up to 3 touches to get the ball over the net (1 catch, 1 pass, 1 hit) - aim for a full 3-hit rally whenever possible. Serves must be underarm. After the same player serves 5 times in a row, the team rotates to the next server. No overarm serves at this level.

KIWIVOLLEY - YEARS 5 & 6

Focus is on improving control, coordination, teamwork, and understanding game flow. Players start practicing setting and hitting, using more structured rallies, and gradually learning to serve overarm, preparing them for full volleyball rules.

Modifications for Years 5 & 6: encourage players to move from catch/pass/hit to the standard pass/set/hit 3-hit sequence. A player using catch/throw must stay in one spot and pass it on quickly. Aim for 3 hits, but the ball can still go over in one, two, or three hits. After 5 serves in a row, the team rotates to the next server. Serving: 2 overarm serves (from the back line if competent, or back area if still learning), followed by 3 underarm serves. Progression depends on each team - coaches should trust their judgment on when to introduce more advanced skills.

INTERMEDIATE VOLLEYBALL - YEARS 7 & 8

As players move into intermediate levels, transition from modified small-sided games (4v4) to full 6v6 format, with a larger court, higher nets, and official rules structure to develop positioning, rotations, and teamwork.

Modifications for Years 7 & 8: full FIVB volleyball rules on a full volleyball court. Encourage underhand serve closer to the net (a meter in from the baseline at most) for athletes who struggle; overhand servers must serve from the baseline and avoid a foot fault. Any player who serves 5 in a row causes their team to rotate to a new server (retaining serve) to encourage rallies. Substitutions happen at server to give playing time to teams with more than 6 players. Encourage all athletes to experience different positions during games and practices to build a broader skill range and confidence.

RUNNING A GOOD TRAINING

Running a good volleyball practice doesn't require years of coaching experience or complicated drills - what matters is that kids are active, learning, and having fun, with lots of touches on the ball and a balance of skill development and games. Structure each practice as: warm up and get excited, focus on one or two skills, let kids try those skills in game-like activities, finish with play and reflection.

CREATING A TRAINING PLAN
- Set clear goals: pick one or two skills or themes per session (e.g., passing, serving, teamwork).
- Keep it age-appropriate: younger kids include catching/throwing; older groups build toward 3-hit rallies.
- Maximize activity: avoid long lines - use small groups or stations so everyone gets lots of turns.
- Balance structure and fun: mix skill drills with games and challenges to keep energy high.
- Be flexible: if something isn't working, change it on the spot.
- End on a positive: wrap up with encouragement, high-fives, and something fun.

Practices should be aligned with age level and flexible - anywhere from 30-60 minutes depending on the group's age, energy, and attention span. Shorter sessions are often better for younger players.

SIMPLE TRAINING TEMPLATE (30-60 MINUTES)

**Warm-Up (5-10 mins)** - fun movement games (tag, relay races, balance games); add a ball if possible (dribble, throw and catch, partner toss).

**Skill Focus (10-20 mins)** - choose one or two skills (e.g., passing, serving, setting); teach briefly, then let kids practice in pairs or small groups. Example: pass back and forth with a partner, or serve into a target.

**Game-Like Activity (10-20 mins)** - small-sided games or KiwiVolley variations; encourage rallies, teamwork, and trying the skill from earlier. Example: 2v2 or 3v3 with a soft ball and simple rules. (Ideas for skill drills and game-like drills at www.volleyballbop.co.nz/coach-development.)

**Play & Cool Down (5-10 mins)** - let kids play a short game with modified rules (rolling subs); end with light stretching and a team chat asking "What did we learn today?" "What was fun?"

WHAT YOU NEED

Each session: a couple of volleyballs; a net (badminton net or volleyball net depending on age/skill level); whistle (optional); cones (optional); session plan.

If you don't have a net: all volleyball skills can be practiced without one. Alternatives: run a rope between two solid objects at approximately 1.6m height (KiwiVolley) or ~2m (intermediate/junior volley); or use any object of the right height as a practice "net" for skills like serving.

VOLLEYBALL SKILLS

Progression of skills by level:
- **Year 3/4**: Catch/throw format. Key skills: hit (2 hands, 1 hand), underarm serve, rotation and movement to ball.
- **Year 5/6**: 4 vs 4. Key focus: movement, teamwork, confidence; pass progression to pass/set/hit. Key skills: pass and set, hit, underarm/overarm serve, rotation and movement to ball.
- **Year 7/8**: 6 vs 6, full rules. Key focus: volleyball skills, advanced skills, structure. Key skills: pass and set, advanced hitting (with approach), underarm/overarm serve, rotation and movement to ball.

HOW TO TEACH VOLLEYBALL SKILLS

Three principles summarise the KiwiVolley teaching/training method:

1. **Take Part** - children learn skills best through taking part.
2. **Goals and Purpose** - children learn best when they have a clear goal of what the skill is trying to achieve, understand the principles and performance keys for each skill, and receive feedback on their performance. (Demonstrating a skill is more impactful than explaining with words - make sure all group members can see the demonstration and hear your explanation.)
3. **Play the Game** - specificity is an underlying principle for learning motor skills: "the game teaches the game." If we want to get good at playing volleyball, we should play volleyball.

BREAKDOWN OF KEY SKILLS

**Ready Position** - Performance Keys: 1. Relaxed and ready to move. 2. Face the ball, arms straight. 3. Forearm contact. 4. Platform to target.

**Forearm Pass (Progression from catch/throw)** - Performance Keys: 1. Hands & wrists together. 2. Arms straight and down. 3. See the server, see the ball. 4. Shuffle to move.

**Underarm Serve** - Performance Keys: 1. Ball forward, arm back. 2. Low throw, small step. 3. Middle of hand, middle of ball. 4. Move onto court.

**Setting (Progression from catch/throw)** - Performance Keys: 1. Move behind ball. 2. Hands up early. 3. Face your target. 4. Extend legs/elbows towards the target.

ADVANCED SKILLS

**Overarm Serve (standing float)** - Performance Keys: 1. Bow and arrow. 2. Low throw. 3. Small step. 4. Heel of the hand to back of the ball.

**Blocking** - Performance Keys: 1. Hands and elbows in front of body and perpendicular to net. 2. Knees and ankles flexed. 3. Stand in front of hitter's shoulder. 4. Watch the hitter's arm swing. 5. Reach over the net.

**Spiking** - Performance Keys: 1. Four-step approach R-L-R-L (L-R-L-R for left-hand hitters). 2. Swing arms to jump. 3. Bow and arrow to prepare, twisting at hips. 4. Swing and reach to hit.

**Base Defense** - Performance Keys: 1. Position in an arrow formation in back row with front row at net. 2. Watch ball continuously when in play. 3. Be ready to move to ball.

GAME LIKE DRILLS (GRILLS)

**Step Back and Serve** - underarm serve to a partner; if the partner catches it within one step, the server takes a step back, then the partner serves. Miss a serve and take a step forward. Aim: make it back to the baseline. Variation for Years 5/6 and 7/8: have players pass the ball, or use an overhand serve.

**Volley Battles** - players catch and throw the ball over the net trying to get opponents to misplay or ground it. Keep score; winner after two minutes moves to the winner's side (draw = rock/paper/scissors). Variation for Years 5/6 and 7/8: use forearm passing or setting.

**Partner Patterns** - pairs cooperatively count net crossings using a skill pattern (e.g. pass-set), trying to extend the streak. Variations: 1. underarm throw > pass up > catch. 2. underarm throw > pass up > set over. 3. move to 2v2, 4v4.

**King/Queen of the Court** - teams of 2, 3, or 4. Challengers serve to the Champs; if Champs win they score and stay; if challengers win they become the new Champs (crossing to that side); the losing team goes to the back of the line and a new team serves to start the next rally. Three compulsory passes required, with the last directed over the net; the second contact must be a compulsory catch-then-throw to another player.
