## Purpose of this module
This module defines **how we teach passing to beginners**.
The focus is **not perfect passes**. The focus is building **correct habits** through:
- sequence
- movement
- simple, shared cues

Every coach teaches passing the **same way**, using the **same language**, so players build habits faster and with less confusion.

## Teaching philosophy
Beginner players:
- Cannot process long technical explanations
- Learn best through **imagery + repetition**
- Need **one correction at a time**

Because of this, we teach passing through:
- Numbered sequences
- Short verbal cues
- Consistent language across all coaches

Cues are **corrections**, not explanations.

## Core concept: passing is a sequence
Passing is **not one action**. It is a **chain of actions**, done in order.
We teach this order using numbers so players:
- Move before passing
- Use their legs
- Stay balanced

## Passing by numbers (foundation)

### Position 1 — Ready
**Cue:** "Ready"
- Athletic stance
- Knees bent
- Back straight
- Arms out and relaxed
- Ready to move
- **No platform yet**

**Important clarification:** Arms stay relaxed until the player has **moved into the ball's path**.

**Why:** Players must react first. Building a platform too early locks the body and reduces balance and adjustment.

### Position 2 — Platform
**Cue:** "Pancakes"
- Hands together (hand over hand)
- Thumbs together = **"Syrup"**
- Forearms form **one surface**

**Additional cue:** "Surfboards"
- Arms stay flat and firm
- No bending at the elbows

**Why:** A bent platform creates unpredictable passes. A flat, quiet platform allows the ball to rebound cleanly.

> [!note] Diagram in Notion source (not mirrored — expiring URL)

### Position 3 — Low
**Cue:** "Low"
- Knees bend
- Hips drop
- Chest stays tall

**Important clarification:** Hips go down — **not chest forward**.

**Why:** Power comes from the legs, not collapsing posture. Staying tall preserves balance, vision, and platform control.

> [!note] Diagram in Notion source (not mirrored — expiring URL)

### Position 4 — Up
**Cue:** "Up"
- Legs extend
- Finish tall

**Why:** The ball goes up because the **body goes up**. Arms **never add power**. They only provide direction.

## Core passing pattern
**1 → 2 → 3 → 4**
Ready → Platform → Low → Up

This sequence is **non-negotiable**.

## Key teaching cues (coach logic)

### "Pancakes & Syrup"
Used when:
- Hands separate
- Thumbs are not together
- Platform is uneven

Creates:
- Wide, stable platform
- Fast visual correction

### "Surfboards"
Used when:
- Elbows bend
- Arms swing
- Platform collapses

A surfboard is: long, flat, never bent.
This cue reminds players: the platform must stay **straight and firm** through contact.

## How passing progresses over time
The **technique never changes**. Only the **movement before the pass** changes.

### Early phase (Week 1 focus)
**Goal:** Shape and confidence

**Teaching emphasis:**
- Ready position
- Correct platform shape
- Leg usage

**How coaches should teach:**
- Mostly stationary or very light movement
- Slow, controlled reps
- Stop drills **only if the same error appears repeatedly across players**

**Primary cues:** "Ready" · "Pancakes" · "Surfboards" · "Low → Up"

### Application phase (around Week 4)
**Goal:** Passing while moving and defending space
Movement patterns are added **before** the pass. The passing technique stays the same.

### Pattern A — Step & Pass
Used when the ball is slightly off center.

**Sequence:** Ready → Step → Platform → Low → Up

**Coaching focus:**
- Step first
- Balance before contact

### Pattern B — Step–Shuffle–Pass
Used when the ball is farther away.

**Sequence:** Ready → Step → Shuffle → Platform → Low → Up

**Coaching focus:**
- Move first
- Same platform as Week 1
- Same Low → Up mechanics

## Non-negotiables at all levels
These never change — even as players improve:
- Move before platform
- Flat, quiet arms
- Legs create the pass
- Same Ready → Platform → Low → Up sequence

## Coaching priorities to remember
- Movement comes before platform
- Legs create the pass
- Arms stay flat and quiet
- Cues are corrections, not explanations
- Same language across all coaches

## Coach reminder
If a pass is off, ask yourself:
1. Did they move first?
2. Was the platform flat?
3. Did they use their legs?

Fix **one thing at a time**.
