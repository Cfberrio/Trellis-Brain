TENNIS IN SCHOOLS RESOURCE

A Comprehensive Guide to Teaching Tennis in Schools

## Introduction

Welcome to the First Set - Tennis in Schools Resource, a dynamic and structured resource for teachers designed to introduce students between the ages of 4 and 13 (Grades 1 to 8) to tennis through engaging, play-based lessons. This resource is designed to help teachers deliver effective lessons tied to physical and health education curriculum outcomes. It has been informed by Tennis Canada's Whole Player Development Pathway, focusing on tactical play, fundamental movement skills, and social-emotional learning.

This resource provides 36 lesson plans for use in the classroom for the following age groups:
- Grade 1 - Grade 3 (6-8 years old)
- Grade 4 - Grade 6 (9-11 years old)
- Grade 7 - Grade 8 (12-13 years old)

Each grade level includes 12 developmentally appropriate lessons, designed to meet students where they are in their tennis journey:
- 4 lessons for beginners who are just being introduced to tennis
- 4 lessons for students with developing or emerging tennis skills
- 4 lessons for students demonstrating competence and confidence in their tennis abilities

All the lessons reference the First Set - Tennis in Schools Activity Cards.

## Program Philosophy & Alignment

**Why Teach Tennis in Schools?**
Tennis can be a lifelong sport that promotes physical literacy, teamwork, problem-solving, and resilience. This program is designed to ensure easy implementation for teachers with or without a tennis background, a progression-based approach to develop core tennis skills through play-based learning, and inclusive and adaptive activities for diverse student needs.

**Physical Education Curriculum Connections**
The skills, concepts, and strategies presented in this resource have been designed to align with your provincial Physical Education Curriculum. This resource follows Tennis Canada's Whole Player Development Pathway, emphasizing physical, mental, and social development; the 5Cs of positive youth development (Confidence, Connection, Character, Culture, and Competence); and the "Play-Practice-Play" instructional model, ensuring game-based learning from start to finish.

## Activity Card Integration & Lesson Structure

**The Four Tactical Themes** - each lesson plan is built around one of:
- Outlast the Opponent - extending rallies and maintaining consistency.
- Move the Opponent - using placement and angles to control play.
- Take Time Away - applying pressure with early contact and attacking shots.
- Gain Time - using defensive positioning to reset the point.

**Activity Card categories:**
- Minds-On (Warm-up, Coordination, & Sending and Receiving) - engages students by connecting to prior knowledge, asking questions, and setting the stage for new learning.
- Action (I Can Rally, I Can Play with Net, & I Can Serve) - the core of the lesson where students actively engage with the new concept through activities, problem-solving, or exploration.
- Consolidation (Point Play & Mini events) - reviews key points, summarizes learning, and provides opportunities to reflect through discussion, practice questions, or application.

Each lesson follows: Minds-On Activity -> Action Activity -> Consolidation Activity, and references specific activity cards for consistency across sessions.

## Lesson Plans by Grade Level

## Connecting with Local Tennis Coaches & Clubs

**Building Pathways for Students to Continue Playing Tennis** - a key objective is helping schools build relationships with local tennis coaches, clubs, and community programs so students who start at school can keep playing locally.

**Why Connect with a Local Club or Coach?**
- Smoother Transition for Students - a clear pathway to keep playing.
- Expert Support - certified coaches can assist with programming, demonstrations, joint sessions.
- Access to Facilities & Equipment - partnerships help schools with limited space/gear.
- Community Engagement - stronger school-club relationships encourage activity outside school hours.

**How to Find & Connect with a Local Coach or Club**
Tennis Canada and the Provincial and Territorial Tennis Associations (PTTA) provide a national database of clubs and certified coaches. Finding a club: use the Tennis Canada Club Locator, or contact your PTTA.

**Ways to Engage with Local Clubs & Coaches**
1. Invite a Tennis Coach to Your School - guest coaching sessions, skill demonstrations, "Try Tennis" portable-equipment sessions.
2. Organize a School Club Visit - field trips to play on proper courts; many clubs offer free introductory sessions for school groups.
3. Provide Students with Community Tennis Opportunities - encourage joining local junior programs; distribute flyers with club details and beginner-friendly programs.
4. Explore Club-School Partnership Programs - partnership grants, equipment loans, shared coaching resources, community tennis days.

**Bringing Tennis to the Community: Opening School Courts** - schools with courts can open them for after-school or weekend community use, giving students a familiar place to keep playing and strengthening school-community ties.

**Need Help Getting Started?** Tennis Canada and your local PTTA can help find the right coach or club, offer training/resources for teachers, and provide grants and funding for school-club partnerships.
