## Purpose

DR practices are developmental practices.

Players are not expected to arrive already knowing the skills, positions, or structure of the sport.

Our job is to teach them.

The coach is not running a high school practice for players who already understand the game.

The coach is building the player from the beginning and progressing them quickly through the DR method.

---

## 1. Teach Before You Test

A player should not be tested on a skill that has not been taught.

Do not ask:

> “Who knows how to serve?”

and then use the players who raise their hands as the teaching method.

Do not ask:

> “Do you know your positions?”

and then move directly into play because players answered yes.

The coach teaches first.

The sequence is:

1. Teach the skill.
2. Demonstrate the form.
3. Give players controlled repetitions.
4. Correct.
5. Progress.
6. Apply.

A demonstration by a player can be useful after instruction, but it does not replace instruction.

---

## 2. Verify, Do Not Assume

Players may say they understand because they do not want to look lost.

That does not mean they understand.

The coach needs to verify through what players actually do.

Understanding is shown through execution.

If players cannot perform the skill, the coach should teach or regress the activity.

---

## 3. Move Fast, But Do Not Skip Teaching

Efficient practice does not mean rushing past instruction.

It means teaching clearly and moving quickly through useful repetitions and progressions.

The coach should maintain energy and pace.

There should not be long periods where players are standing, waiting, or repeatedly failing without instruction.

A fast practice can still be deeply instructional.

The goal is:

**Teach quickly. Rehearse. Progress. Keep moving.**

---

## 4. Practice Should Be Serial, Not Pure Block Practice

Players need repetitions, but we are not trying to create endless repetition of the exact same task.

If an activity stays the same for too long, it becomes block practice.

For example:

- Same toss
- Same target
- Same position
- Same response
- Repeated over and over

That may help a player initially understand the movement, but the practice needs to keep progressing.

We want serial practice.

The skill stays connected across the practice, but the player continues encountering new layers.

---

## 5. Progress Through Layers of Variability

Variability does not mean changing everything.

If we constantly replace one activity with a completely different activity, players spend too much time learning the new setup instead of progressing the skill.

Players need enough repetitions to learn the skill.

Then the coach adds one layer.

Then another.

Then another.

Example:

### Passing

1. Learn passing form.
2. Pass a predictable toss.
3. Pass toward a target.
4. Change the target.
5. Move before passing.
6. Receive a less predictable ball.
7. Pass to a setter.
8. Connect pass → set.
9. Connect pass → set → hit.
10. Apply in a game or decision environment.

The base skill remains recognizable while the player is challenged in a new way.

---

## 6. The Coach Should Know the Next Layer

The coach should not reach the middle of practice and then decide what comes next.

Before practice, know:

- What skill is being taught.
- What the starting level is.
- What success looks like.
- What the next layer will be.
- What variability can be added.
- What regression will be used if players struggle.
- What the connected skill is.
- How the skill will eventually be applied.

The progression should be planned.

---

## 7. Do Not Stay in Failure

If none of the balls are going over the net, repeating the same failed serve is not progression.

Stop.

Teach.

Regress.

Correct the form.

Build the skill.

Then try again.

Failure gives information to the coach.

It should trigger an instructional response.

---

## 8. Use Strong Players to Progress the Team

When players show that they can reliably execute the current layer, progress them.

Do not keep capable players trapped at an easier level simply because the original activity is still running.

When appropriate:

- Give them a harder target.
- Move them farther.
- Add movement.
- Add a connected skill.
- Give them a special role in the progression.
- Use them to build toward the next team sequence.

Players can progress at different speeds inside the same practice.

---

## 9. Connect Skills Quickly When Players Are Ready

Players do not need to master one isolated skill for weeks before seeing how it connects to the sport.

Once the individual pieces are functional enough, connect them.

Example:

- Pass
- Pass to target
- Pass to setter
- Setter sets
- Add hitter
- Run the connected sequence

A developmental team can move quickly when the coach teaches clearly and keeps the progression moving.

---

## 10. Coaching Requires Active Instruction

The coach should be:

- Talking
- Teaching
- Correcting
- Demonstrating
- Observing
- Moving players
- Adjusting the activity
- Adding layers

Practice should not become players performing a task while the coach waits.

The coach drives the progression.

---

## 11. The DR Method Is the Starting Point

Coaches may bring previous experience, but DR practices should use the DR method.

The coach does not need to invent a new system.

The method already exists.

The responsibility is to learn it and apply it.

Previous coaching experience can help execution, but it should not replace the structure we have already developed.

---

# DR Practice Sequence

**Teach → Repetition → Target → Variability → Connection → Application**

The exact activity may change, but the developmental logic should remain.

---

# Core Philosophy

**We do not test players before we teach them.**

**We do not repeat the exact same task until practice becomes block practice.**

**We do not change everything just for the sake of variety.**

**We keep the skill recognizable and add layers of variability so the player continues progressing.**

**We teach quickly, progress intentionally, and apply the DR method.**
