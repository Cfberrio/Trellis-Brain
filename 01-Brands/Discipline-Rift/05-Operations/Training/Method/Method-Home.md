## What this is

The **source philosophy** for how Discipline Rift teaches, across every sport. Three documents written by Luis in September 2026 after watching practices drift (serving before serving was taught; one drill run as block practice for the whole hour; coaches importing a high-school approach). They state the standard; the live Notion Coach Hub is the coach-facing surface that has to *carry* the standard.

Rule of precedence when notes disagree:

1. These Method notes (philosophy).
2. Live Notion `DR | COACH HUB` and its vault mirror under `Notion/COACH-HUB/` (operational pages coaches actually open).

## The method on one screen

Three formulations exist in DR's own writing. They are the same method at three scales, not rivals (proposal C02):

| Scale | Sequence | Where it is written |
|---|---|---|
| Whole session | **Open → Retrieve / Show → Build the Ladder → Test Transfer → Close** | [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/Notion/COACH-HUB/02-Every-Session/PRACTICE-DESIGN|PRACTICE DESIGN]] |
| One learning sequence inside the session | **Teach → Repetition → Target → Variability → Connection → Application** | [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/05-Operations/Training/Method/DR-Training-Philosophy|Training Philosophy]] |
| The short version you say to a coach | **Teach → Rep → Challenge → Apply → Connect** | [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/05-Operations/Training/Method/DR-Coach-Philosophy-Methodology|Coach Philosophy §9]] |

Application and connection recur; nothing here is a one-pass checklist.

**Non-negotiables** (each one is a section in the child notes):

- **Teach before you test.** A hand raised or a "yes" is not evidence. New skill, new position, new role → instruction first, then controlled reps, then verify by what they *do*.
- **Serial, not block.** Enough identical reps to learn the movement, then keep the skill recognizable and add one layer: target, distance, movement, entry, teammate, decision. Not a new drill every five minutes; not the same toss for 30.
- **Know the next layer before you arrive** — and the regression, the success criterion, the bridge, and how you will check it.
- **Do not stay in failure.** Repeated failed serves trigger teaching and scaling, not more attempts.
- **Progress capable players inside the same activity.** Harder target, farther, add movement, add the connected skill, give a role.
- **Connect skills when they are functional enough,** not after weeks of isolation. "Functional enough for the next supported task" beats undefined mastery.
- **Depth within a skill + bridge to the next.** Passing alone is Platform → Control → Targeting → Movement → Angles → Seam Responsibility → Situational Application; then Pass → Set. This is why a second season is not a repeat.
- **Passing angle ≠ seam responsibility.** Angle = *where the ball goes* (face the ball, angle the platform, do not swing the arms sideways). Seam = *who takes the ball* between two passers. Teach them separately.
- **Coach actively.** Teaching, correcting, demonstrating, moving players — during learning reps. During an independent check, let players act before prompting (proposal C06 resolves "don't stop speaking" vs. CFU silence).
- **Environment before technique.** Attention, credibility, energy, standards. "This is how we practice here."
- **Fun is the method, not the reward.** Games, targets, scores, small-sided play carry the reps. CFU / scrimmage is not withheld as a prize.
- **Progress players, not drills.** Finishing the week page is not the goal. "Can they do it?" decides the next step.
- **Communicate the progress.** What we worked on → what improved → what comes next. Parents cannot see practice; the message is the evidence.
