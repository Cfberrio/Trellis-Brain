## Stage 1 — Ads Feedback

> Questions: (1) What is this ad? (2) What was confusing? (3) Trust/legit? (4) For someone like you? (5) Click/submit?

### Student 1
A job opportunity for someone interested in sports and looking to build leadership skills. Unclear which sports are available. Not completely trustworthy — lacking details in video, captions, bio. Could feel like a scam. Interested as a college student looking for community + enjoyment job. Probably wouldn't submit until I saw more information.

### Student 2
Job offer from a sports organization. Asking to comment name + sport background. Unclear which sports the organization has. Clips were wholesome but no distinction between sports. Fun + friendly vibe but needs more info. Would consider clicking, but would look up the organization first.

### Student 3
A job offering — provide info to apply. Unclear which sports are offered (clips cut in too quickly). Trustworthy-looking but lacks detail — feels like the company is hiding something. More interested in general business management than sports specifically. Wouldn't submit; would research first.

### Student 4
Vague ad, specific to youth sports. Maybe a franchise or coach role. Too much repetition. Eye line jumps with text. Too many sound effects. Recommends: keep vague hook at start, reel into specific information, add a few-minute video at the top of the form landing page explaining what they're signing up for. They'll be more invested if they take all the steps. Off-feeling — basic editing trying to be more than it is. Would click the form but not submit; not enough information established.

### Student 5
Targeting people interested in sports + growth, getting involved with the company. Has to assume what position. Too much visual stimulation (font sizes change, sound effects, kids running in background). More trustworthy if origin was clearer. Felt like spam. Personally felt like it was for them given sports background. Wouldn't click — couldn't gauge trustworthiness. Recommendations:
- Capture location at form submission
- Three different videos after application: (1) philosophy and mission (2) commitment (3) financials
- Day-in-the-life ad
- First video: pre-application, 1 min 30 sec max
- Post-application video: 3–4 min, 5 min max
- Bullet points before they apply to get rough idea
- Optional sport selection: volleyball / tennis / pickleball / flag football + **other** for committed candidates with other sports backgrounds

## Stage 2 — Lead Funnel Feedback (reminder preferences)

> Questions: preferred reminder channel? Want reminders? Reasonable cadence? What makes a reminder helpful?

| Student | Channel | Want reminders? | Cadence | Most likely to respond to | Helpful when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email | Maybe one or none | 2 max | Email | "Add insight, not just hey, your form is not finished" |
| 2 | Text | Yes | 1/day | Text | Clear short message + link |
| 3 | Email | Yes | 2–3 max | Both | Clear link, short message, few reminders |
| 4 | Email | Yes, just one | 2 max | Email | Short, not too often |
| 5 | Email | No | 2 emails enough | Email | Addressed by name |

**Synthesis:** Email-leaning preference. 1–2 reminders is the cap before "feels like spam". Short, clear, name personalization is the unlock.

## Stage 3 — Website Feedback

> Questions: What's the opportunity? Wording changes? Want to keep going? First change to make?

### Student 1
"Someone who loves sports and wants to expand that love to others." All straightforward. Continued the application. Would change color scheme — more pleasing, easier to read.

### Student 2
"The opportunity to valuable for the right person, but you do not convey that value enough or in a short enough timespan." Make things clearer early. Would have stopped at sign-up — didn't want to give that info away. Recommend: landing page with videos first, then move to application.

### Student 3
"The opportunity to branch out and help kids throughout the country by expanding DR." Time commitment unclear (full-time / part-time). Yes, would continue. Maybe add more color, like the layout.

### Student 4
"This is an opportunity to coach and commit to a team." Page was very clear. Format made me scroll to bottom. Wouldn't change anything.

## Stage 4 — Pre-Application Video Feedback

### Student 1
"Use DR playbook in your own community, on your own." Day-to-day duties unclear. Felt real (more info offered). For graduating college students with sports background.

### Student 2
"Work with the DR program and teach sports to young children." Unclear exactly what day-to-day looks like. Felt real. For college students with sports background.

### Student 3
"It is a valuable opportunity. It is inviting you to fill out something for a licensee." Add deliverable + value before asking anything. Felt real but giving info first makes it untrustworthy. Edit: add text + graphics in front of Luis, don't have Luis reach for the camera at the end. Want to know what the blueprint is.

### Student 4
"It is a licensee opportunity. They want you to fill out a form to see if you are a good fit." Not very confusing. Felt real. For people getting into sports + building on existing company. What does the license entail? What values/practices align with mine?

### Student 5
"Job opportunity to work for a sports organization and maybe coach kids." Confusing: actual work part / what the job is like. Felt more trustworthy (more info). For graduating college students. What are the requirements? Positions? Schedule?

## Stage 5 — Application Feedback

### Student 1
What experience / what motivates / what kind of person at work. Nothing confusing. Simple, understood. Wouldn't include the post-application page.

### Student 2
How you handle situations at DR. Some swipe questions worded unclearly — answered inaccurately as a result. Awkward in spots. Short-answer was right amount. Add optional resume upload.

### Student 3
Trying to figure out if good fit for licensee. Pretty straightforward. Swipe questions felt awkward — swiped the wrong response a few times. Short-answer felt like too much (people can opt for less). Make swipe questions a 1–5 likelihood scale; bigger buttons.

### Student 4
Trying to see if good fit / would be successful member. Some swipe questions made me think more. Felt natural on phone — unclear how it would change on laptop. Brought out good questions. Missing: past experience contributions, qualifications, special skills, performance history.

### Student 5
Trying to know me as a person + my experiences. Application very straightforward. Confusing: no question about specific sports the org provides. Swipe questions engaging. Short-answer fine — enjoys sharing sports experiences. Add an optional resume upload.

## Stage 6 — Post-Application Feedback

### Student 1
"Sharing love for sports with young children by operating an organization." Clearest part: "who is this for?" Q+A. Want to know the beginning process. Make the last video info accessible BEFORE they apply, so the job is transparent.

### Student 2
"Running practices, planning seasons, supporting families and athletes." Clearest: who the job is for + what you'll gain. Unclear: training process. Want to know: training onboarding + pay. Provide as much detail as possible.

### Student 3
"Build a pre-planned business through mentorship." Very clear video. End differently — "we'll be in contact, contact us if questions, let's make an impact together." Include option for those who don't want to franchise / aren't right fit. Give more insight into business plan + why it's valuable. State why your business won't fail like most franchises do. Make this video FIRST, before they sign up, so they're invested.

### Student 4
"Running part of DR in their local community. Since this is branching to franchise, new licensees oversee day to day." Clearest: mission statement, what kind of person succeeds. Where is the opportunity? What responsibilities? What if they're too great? Want to know day-to-day, location, why apply, local vs move. Would say basic job requirements + why it matters + mission statement + expectations + challenges + needs to succeed.

### Student 5
"Make their own schedule, organize practices and tournaments." Clearest: team behind the org, coaching role. Unclear: internship/PT/FT? License? Will org provide training? Job position? Just coaching or more? Would explain as "an opportunity to create lasting impact."
