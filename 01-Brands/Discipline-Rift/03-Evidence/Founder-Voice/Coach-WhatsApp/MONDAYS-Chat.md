### 2025-10-13 — Luis Torres (no practice — reflection)
*The Third Circle Theory* — framework by Pejman Ghadimi on how people grow in purpose and awareness:
1. First Circle: Survival — focused on self. "How do I get by?" / "What's in it for me?" We all start here… it's human.
2. Second Circle: Success & Recognition — we develop skills, confidence, and start leading. We want to be good coaches, win games, be respected. This stage builds our capability and voice.
3. Third Circle: Purpose & Legacy — we move beyond ego. We focus on impact, planting seeds in others. The goal isn't just to lead players, but to shape them. Every word, correction, and moment of care becomes a seed that grows long after the season ends.

Why this matters to us: every day on court, we get to operate in that third circle. Planting a seed in the life of a human being — that's always a win. With kids, that seed is even more fruitful, because they're listening… especially when it doesn't look like it. We are role models. Players see us as "cool coaches." That influence is the fertile soil where those seeds take root.

### 2025-11-10 — Luis Torres (layers of difficulty)
Today: how to add layers of difficulty? This means keeping the same games you already run and adding small, intentional constraints: 1. goals 2. cues 3. micro-skill tweaks that pull better habits out of our players.

This is desirable difficulty: challenging enough to stretch them, not so hard it breaks flow. Why it matters: beginners get conditioned to wait for a perfect feed. We want go-getters, not statues. Teach them to move to the ball, arrive balanced, and play the point.

Example with Jailbreak: start with your normal feed. Then layer it. Require a split-step before contact. Place the ball a step or two off their body so they must move through it. Add a recovery cue back to ready position. Explain the "why" in plain friendly language: "We chase the ball because arriving early lets us choose our shot."

Your job today: for each group you lead, pick two specific layers and coach the why. Keep the core game; upgrade the demand: footwork, spacing, decision speed, target, consequence. After practice, drop a quick note in the recap: the layers you used and one change you saw.
