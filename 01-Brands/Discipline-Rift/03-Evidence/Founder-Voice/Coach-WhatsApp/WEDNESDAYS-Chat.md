### 2025-11-12 — Luis Torres (Good cop / Bad cop)
Adapt your coaching to the group and switch intentionally between "good cop" and "bad cop."

Good cop means connection on purpose — laugh with them, high-five, learn names, share appropriate jokes. Use friendliness to set the tone. You're their ally and friend.

Bad cop means standard and clarity: set the tone, project your voice so everyone hears you, and make it unmistakable that we're here to learn and play the sport well. When the group drifts (talking over instructions, bouncing balls while others listen), stop it fast and reset the standard.

Ask the questions that sharpen attention: 1. Are we being exemplary players? 2. Are we being helpful teammates? 3. Can we have fun if we don't know the rules or can't hear the plan? So we lock in, then we earn the fun.

Make the switch visible. Call it: "Lock in." Balls down. Eyes up. Three-second rule to silence. Deliver the cue once, then run. If standards slip, escalate tone and consequence and have them do a physical activity.

Your job today: read your group and choose the mode that serves the moment. Build trust with good cop; protect learning with bad cop.

### 2026-02-04 — Luis Torres (teach the skill, don't just run the plan)
A drill isn't teaching by itself. If we just rotate kids through reps without emphasizing the key habit, they'll get activity… but not improvement.

1. Say the target out loud (1 sentence). "Today we're working on ______."
2. Coach ONE cue per block (and repeat it every rep). If the cue is important, it has to show up more than once.
3. Stop for 15–20 seconds when the same mistake repeats. Don't push through. Quick teach → quick demo → right back to reps.
4. One correction at a time. Over-correcting = kids shut down. Pick the #1 thing that will move the rep.
5. Prove learning with a quick check. Freeze and show it, or ask one player: "What's the cue?" then immediately rep again.

Our standard: kids should leave practice knowing exactly what they improved today — not just that they "played."
