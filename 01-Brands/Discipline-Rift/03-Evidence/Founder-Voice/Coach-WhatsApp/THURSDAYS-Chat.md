### 2025-11-13 — Luis Torres (Teach the sport)
Here's the truth: our job is to teach the sport. Awesome coaches start by being awesome teachers.

Every great practice starts with teaching. Before the first ball, name the skill, state the why, and show the model. Then get them moving: quick demo at game speed, two clear cues, immediate shadow reps, then into a live game or drill that rewards the behavior. Keep it engaging and dynamic. Don't lecture. Move, model, and make them mirror.

Use illustrations, comparisons, and live examples. Ask for a "show me" instead of a long answer. Shadow is non-negotiable — players learn by seeing first, then doing, then competing.

Hold a tight standard on clarity. If you've talked for more than 30 seconds, reset and demo. If a player can't answer "What are we learning? Why does it matter? What does it look like?" we haven't taught it yet — teach it again, simpler, faster, clearer.

Teach it, have them shadow it, score it, and layer difficulty as they succeed.
