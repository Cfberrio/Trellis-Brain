# WARM UPS

# WARM-UP BLOCK
**Movement Preparation & Practice Primer**
---
## Time Allocation
- **Attendance + Purpose Talk:** 3–5 minutes
- **Movement Warm-Up:** 3–5 minutes
Total Opening Block: **5–10 minutes maximum**
Warm-ups should be efficient, intentional, and directly connected to the day’s teaching focus.
This is not conditioning time.
This is preparation time.
---
# Purpose of the Warm-Up Page
The warm-up block exists to:
- Set tone and expectations
- Introduce movement vocabulary
- Prime the body for the day’s skill focus
- Build transferable habits
- Increase focus and energy
We are preparing:
- The body
- The brain
- The language
- The mechanics
---
# Who Leads the Warm-Up?
**Head Coach**
- Leads attendance and purpose.
- Sets tone.
- Introduces vocabulary cues.
**Assistant Coaches**
- Demonstrate movements.
- Correct technique.
- Reinforce cues.
Early in the season, coaches lead.
Later in the season, players may help lead for ownership and leadership development.
---
# Warm-Up Structure (Standard Format)
Every warm-up follows this sequence:
### 1. Attendance & Purpose (3–5 Minutes)
- Take attendance.
- State the objective of practice.
- Connect today’s focus to previous learning.
Example:
> “Today we’re working on cutting and changing direction. That means we need fast feet and strong hips.”
Keep it short and clear.
---
### 2. General Movement Activation (1–2 Minutes)
Light locomotor movements:
- High knees
- Karaoke
- Lateral shuffle
- Quick jog
Goal:
- Raise heart rate
- Activate lower body
- Build rhythm
---
### 3. Dynamic Stretching (1–2 Minutes)
Keep stretches dynamic and brief:
- Reach one arm overhead
- Cross arm across body
- Switch sides
- Overhead elbow pull
- Shoulder rolls
Purpose:
- Loosen shoulders and hips
- Prepare joints for change of direction
Not static stretching. Not extended.
---
### 4. Skill Primer Movement (2–3 Minutes)
This is the most important part.
Movements must connect directly to the day’s drills.
---
# Core Movement Progression
When teaching cutting, defending, or route running:
Players move through:
1. **Quick Feet**
2. **Step**
3. **Pivot**
Sequence:
> “Quick feet!”
	“Step!”
	“Pivot!”
Both directions.
Repeat 4–6 reps each side.
---
## What Each Step Teaches
**Quick Feet**
- Deceleration
- Balance
- Body control
**Step**
- Planting the outside foot
- Weight transfer
**Pivot**
- Hip rotation
- Direction change
This becomes automatic language during drills and games.
---
# Vocabulary Transfer Rule
In practice or games, instead of explaining mechanics, use cue words.
Example:
> “FOR!”
Players immediately execute:
Quick feet → Step → Pivot
Because it has been repeated in warm-up.
This builds fast reaction and shared language.
---
# Connecting Warm-Up to Drill Theme
If practice focus is:
### Route Running
Warm-Up Focus:
- Hip turn
- Plant and drive
- Break mechanics
### Defensive Containment
Warm-Up Focus:
- Breakdown steps
- Lateral shuffle
- Controlled pivot
### Flag Pulling
Warm-Up Focus:
- Low hips
- Short choppy feet
- Controlled stop
Warm-ups must preview drills.
---
# Coaching Language During Warm-Up
Use short cues only:
- “Quick feet.”
- “Low hips.”
- “Step.”
- “Pivot.”
- “Explode.”
Do not stack instructions.
Repetition builds clarity.
---
# Optional Warm-Up Variations
Warm-up may rotate between:
- Movement progression block
- Lines Game (field awareness)
- 4-Corner movement variation
But the structure remains:
Activate → Stretch → Skill Primer
---
# Curriculum Note
Drills listed in your curriculum system are tools, not scripts.
Coaches should:
1. Identify skill goal.
2. Identify movement breakdown.
3. Select or modify drill to address it.
4. Keep warm-up aligned with that skill.
Add creativity — but maintain purpose.
---
# Example 5-Minute Warm-Up Flow
Minute 0–3
Attendance + Purpose
Minute 3–4
High knees → Karaoke → Quick feet
Minute 4–5
Quick feet → Step → Pivot
Repeat both directions
Done.
Efficient. Focused. Connected.
---
# Final Philosophy
Warm-ups are not filler.
They:
- Set culture
- Build vocabulary
- Prime mechanics
- Reinforce fundamentals
- Save teaching time later
You are not just warming up muscles.
You are warming up habits.
