## THE TWELVE
1. **Show first.** Demonstrate the movement or activity. Narrate what players should see. Keep setup talk short.
2. **Run 3 lines per coach.** Three lines for each coach whenever roster, space and safety allow. High reps beat long lines.
3. **Build a ready position into every line.** One player performs. The next is already in the ready position with clear separation from the active rep.
4. **Keep every player ready.** Eyes up. Equipment ready. Know the next action. No dead lines.
5. **Make players communicate.** Use the sport language every rep: call the ball, name, target, route, score, cue or role.
6. **Coach every rep.** Keep talking to players while they work. Praise what to repeat, correct what to change.
7. **Make feedback actionable.** Tell the player what to do on the next rep: get lower, move first, look at target, beat the ball, step to target.
8. **Teach one new skill at a time.** A practice may touch several skills, but new technical instruction enters one at a time. Keep the coaching focus on 1–2.
9. **Add variation after success.** Change the task once players begin solving it. Difficulty follows understanding.
10. **Check with evidence.** Look for the skill or decision under changed conditions. Use CFU. Do not rely on "Got it?"
11. **Keep waiting low.** Design for time-on-task. Reset quickly, use multiple lines, keep eliminated players involved if a game has outs.
12. **Fun first. Learning always.** The challenge should feel like play while still solving a real developmental problem.
## THE 3-LINE STANDARD
Each coach owns three lines. Every line has an active rep and a player already prepared for the next rep.
