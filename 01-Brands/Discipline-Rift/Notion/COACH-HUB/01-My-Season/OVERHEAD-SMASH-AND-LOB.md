# OVERHEAD SMASH & LOB
*(Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
The overhead is the serve motion applied to a moving ball — it only makes sense once the serve trophy position is solid. The lob is its natural pair: one is the answer to a short ball at the net, the other is the answer to an opponent crowding the net. USTA QuickStart's scaled-net, low-compression-ball approach matters most here — a full-speed overhead on a full net is a advanced-only skill; slower balls give beginners time to track and set up.
---
## SHOW IT — OVERHEAD SMASH BY NUMBERS
**Grip:** Continental — same grip as the serve. Shake hands with the racket, don't switch grips between the two.
### Position 1 — Track
**Cue:** "Point and track"
- Non-racket hand points up at the ball the whole flight
- Small adjustment steps to get under it, not in front of it
### Position 2 — Trophy
**Cue:** "Trophy pose, like a serve"
- Same trophy position as the serve
- Weight on the back foot
### Position 3 — Swing
**Cue:** "Reach and hit"
- Full extension at contact, above and slightly in front of the head
- Hit down and out, not flat
### Position 4 — Finish
**Cue:** "Land and reset"
- Land balanced, racket finishes across the body
- Recover to ready position
---
## SHOW IT — LOB
**Cue:** "Low to high, high finish"
- Same low-to-high swing path as a groundstroke, longer finish
- Aim for height and depth over the opponent, not pace

## Progression Connections
Beginner: fed overheads close to the net, isolated from live play; lob taught as a shape, not under pressure. Intermediate: overhead off a lob feed, lob used to reset a rally. Advanced: overhead and lob combined live — smash a short lob, lob to escape net pressure.
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Tracks the ball, gets the overhead over the net with a recognizable trophy position; lob clears the net with height.
- **Intermediate:** Overhead lands in the court off a fed lob; lob resets a rally and lands deep.
- **Advanced:** Overhead finishes points with pace and placement; lob used tactically to escape pressure or draw an error.
