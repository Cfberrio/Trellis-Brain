# FOREHAND GROUNDSTROKE
*(Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
The forehand is the first stroke every player owns. USTA QuickStart research shows players learn strokes faster on scaled courts with low-compression balls and lower nets — slower ball flight gives more time to find the Ready → Turn → Swing → Finish pattern before adding pace or a full court. We teach the forehand the same way every time, with the same words, so habits form fast and hold up under pressure.
---
## SHOW IT — FOREHAND BY NUMBERS
### Position 1 — Ready
**Cue:** "Ready"
- Continental/semi-western grip, racket up
- Knees bent, weight forward
- Split step as the ball is struck by the feeder
**Why:** Players must react before they swing. A locked-in grip and stance too early kills adjustment.
### Position 2 — Turn
**Cue:** "Shoulder to the fence"
- Non-dominant hand points at the ball
- Hips and shoulders turn sideways early
- Racket takes back low
**Why:** Turning early is what creates time and power. Late turn = rushed, arm-only swing.
### Position 3 — Swing
**Cue:** "Low to high"
- Racket drops below the ball
- Swing path brushes up and through contact
- Contact out in front of the lead hip
**Why:** Low-to-high swing path is what puts the ball in the court with margin — it is the difference between a controlled shot and a swing that sails.
### Position 4 — Finish
**Cue:** "Finish on the shoulder"
- Racket finishes up near the opposite shoulder
- Chest faces the target
- Weight transfers into the front foot
**Why:** A full finish means a full swing happened — a short finish is the first visible sign of a scared or rushed stroke.
---
## Core Forehand Pattern
**1 → 2 → 3 → 4** — Ready → Turn → Swing → Finish. Non-negotiable at every level.
---
## ADVANCED EXTENSION — APPROACH SHOT
**Cue:** "Deep, then in"
- Hit deep down the opponent's court with enough topspin to land inside the baseline, not fly long
- Move forward toward the net immediately after contact — the shot buys the position, not the point
- Can go down the line or crosscourt depending on where the opponent is standing
**Why:** This is the same Position 1-4 pattern turned offensive — the swing doesn't change, the intent and the follow-up movement do. Advanced tier only; requires the core pattern to already be reliable under rally pressure.

## Progression Connections
Beginner: stationary, fed forehands, short court, low-compression ball, oversized target. Intermediate: forehand off a bounce with lateral movement, standard court with lower net. Advanced: forehand under rally pressure, full court, directional targets (crosscourt/down the line).
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Gets the ball over the net into the court 2 of 3 tries with a visible turn and low-to-high swing.
- **Intermediate:** Controls direction and moderate topspin, lands inside a defined target zone consistently.
- **Advanced:** Places the ball in a target zone with depth, spin, and pace under rally pressure; varies crosscourt/down the line on command.
