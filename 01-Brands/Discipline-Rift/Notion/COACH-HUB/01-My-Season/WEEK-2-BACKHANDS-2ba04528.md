# WEEK 2 - BACKHANDS
# INTRODUCTION (10 MIN)
> **WELCOME**
	- Greet all students and welcome them to the second week of the pickleball season.
	- Reinforce the program’s mission:
		- **Pickleball Pathways**: Using pickleball to teach life skills like discipline and teamwork.
	- Recap: “What did we learn last week about teamwork?”
		- Highlight positive examples shared by players.
	<empty-block/>
	**LIFE LESSON RECAP**
	- Word of the Week: Consistency.
	- Ask players to share one way they demonstrated teamwork during the week (e.g., in school, with friends, or at home).
	- Discuss briefly how teamwork applies to pickleball, such as working together during doubles play.
	<empty-block/>
	**TEAM EXPECTATIONS **
	- Reinforce rules:
		- Be on time, respect teammates, and listen to coaches.
	- Add a fun twist:
		- Let players suggest one team rule for the week that everyone follows, like “cheer for your partner.”

		<summary>**PICKLEBALL SIMON SAYS**</summary>
			1. **Setup:**
				- Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
				- Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
				- **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
				- Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
			2. **Game Rules:**
				- The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
				- If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
			3. **Commands:**
				- **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
				- **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
				- **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
				- **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
				- **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
				- **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
					- **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
					- Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
				- **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
					- Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
			4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
				- The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
			5. **Elimination:**
				- Any player who follows a command that doesn’t begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
				- Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
			6. **Winner's Reward:**
				- The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.
		</details>
	</callout>

# SKILL - BACKHANDS + FOREHANDS (5 MIN)
> **DEMONSTRATION**
	**Stance and Ready Position**:
		- Begin with a balanced stance: feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, and paddle held in front with both hands (for two-handed backhand) or the dominant hand (for one-handed backhand).
		- Emphasize the importance of staying low and keeping weight evenly distributed.
	<empty-block/>
	**IMITATION**
	**Breakdown Steps for Players**:
	- **Stance**: Stand in the ready position and practice rotating the shoulders.
	- **Grip Practice**: Let players experiment with both grips to find what feels natural.
	- **Swing Practice**: Use shadow swings to guide the low-to-high motion without hitting a ball.
	1. **Controlled Repetition**:
		- Use slow feeds, allowing players to focus on technique.
		- Provide immediate corrections on grip, swing path, or foot positioning.
	<empty-block/>
	**CORRECTION**
	1. **Stance and Alignment**:
		- Feet too close together: Adjust to shoulder-width apart for better balance.
		- Knees locked: Encourage a slight bend for mobility.
	2. **Grip Adjustments**:
		- Ensure a secure grip without excessive tightness.
		- Adjust non-dominant hand placement for control (for two-handed backhand).
	3. **Swing Issues**:
		- Swing too flat: Emphasize the upward brushing motion for proper lift.
		- Late contact: Encourage earlier paddle preparation and swing.
	4. **Follow-Through**:
		- Short or abrupt finish: Remind players to extend and finish high for better control and power.
# BACKHANDS (30 MIN)
> **Coach Demonstration**
	1. **Stance and Ready Position**:
		- Begin with a balanced stance: feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, and paddle held in front with both hands (for two-handed backhand) or the dominant hand (for one-handed backhand).
		- Emphasize the importance of staying low and keeping weight evenly distributed.
	2. **Grip Explanation**:
		- **Two-Handed Backhand**:
			- Dominant hand on the paddle handle, non-dominant hand above, gripping lightly for support.
			- Use a neutral or slightly rotated grip for better control.
		- **One-Handed Backhand**:
			- Use a continental grip (like a “handshake” with the paddle).
	3. **Body Rotation**:
		- Pivot on the back foot while turning the shoulders so the paddle aligns with the side of the body.
		- Highlight how the non-dominant hand (in a two-handed backhand) leads the initial motion.
	4. **Swing Path**:
		- Start the paddle low, below the level of the ball.
		- Use a low-to-high motion, brushing the paddle up and forward to lift the ball over the net.
	5. **Contact Point**:
		- Strike the ball at waist height, slightly in front of the body.
		- Keep eyes on the ball until contact is made.
	6. **Follow-Through**:
		- Finish with the paddle above the opposite shoulder, pointing toward the target zone.
		- Demonstrate a smooth recovery to the ready position.
		<empty-block/>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**ADVANCED PLAYER CUES**</summary>
			1. **Footwork**:
				- Move to the ball with small, quick steps, ensuring balanced positioning before the swing.
				- Recover quickly to the ready position after each shot.
			2. **Topspin Focus**:
				- Emphasize brushing up on the ball to create topspin, keeping it within bounds.
				- Practice “brushing” drills with a coach feeding balls at a controlled pace.
			3. **Anticipation**:
				- Teach players to read the incoming ball’s height and speed, preparing for the backhand well in advance.
		</details>
	</callout>
	**<br>DRILLS**
		- **Set Cones as Targets: **Place cones on the opposite side of the net, in different spots for players to target.
		- **Baseline Position: **Line the players up behind (or) at the baseline and feed balls to them one by one.
		- **Hit Targets: **Players aim to hit the ball towards the cones with their **forehand stroke**.
		- **Scoring: **Players who hit the targets earn **points**, which can lead to rewards.
		- **Vary the Target Locations: **Place some cones down the line and others cross-court to improve accuracy and simulate match scenarios.<br>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>BACKHAND TARGET CHALLENGE</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Place 5-7 cones on the opposite side of the net, spaced strategically in different zones:
				1. Near the baseline.
				2. Mid-court.
				3. Near the NVZ (Non-Volley Zone).
			### **Instructions:**
			1. Players line up behind the baseline on one side of the net.
			2. Coach feeds the ball to each player, one at a time.
			3. Players hit backhands, aiming for specific cones.
			4. **Scoring Options:**
				- 3 points for hitting a cone directly.
				- 2 points for landing within 1 foot of a cone.
				- 1 point for a successful hit into the correct zone.
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Use larger, brightly colored cones and slower feeds.
			- Intermediate: Introduce alternating targets and medium-paced feeds.
			- Advanced: Require specific types of backhands (e.g., cross-court, topspin) or faster feeds.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- Emphasize low-to-high motion for controlled lift.
			- Encourage players to keep their eyes on the cone until they strike the ball.
			- Focus on consistent foot positioning before each shot.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>PARTNER BACKHAND RALLIES</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Pair up players and position each partner on opposite sides of the net, about mid-court distance apart.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Warm-Up Phase:**
				- Partners rally using only backhands, keeping the ball in play.
				- Players aim to achieve 10 continuous shots without an error.
			2. **Scoring Phase:**
				- Each successful rally earns the pair a point.
				- Introduce "combo points" for reaching specific rally counts (e.g., 2 points for 15 hits, 3 points for 20 hits).
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Allow players to stand closer together with slower-paced rallies.
			- Intermediate: Increase the distance and require shots to clear the NVZ.
			- Advanced: Add cross-court backhand rallies or targets for more precision.
			### **Variation Twist:**
			- Time Challenge: See how many rallies the pair can complete in 3 minutes.
			- Moving Rally: Partners shuffle laterally after each shot to practice movement.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- Highlight proper grip and follow-through.
			- Encourage communication between partners for better rally flow.
			- Reinforce controlled, consistent strokes rather than power.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>ZIGZAG BACK</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Place cones in a zigzag pattern on one side of the court, from the baseline to the NVZ line.
			- Space cones about 5-7 feet apart to encourage lateral movement.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Movement Phase:**
				- Players start at the baseline, moving laterally around each cone in a zigzag pattern.
				- After reaching the cone, they hit a backhand toward a target area.
			2. **Shooting Phase:**
				- Coach or partner feeds balls as players reach each cone.
				- Players return to the ready position after each shot and continue moving.
			### **Scoring Options:**
			- 1 point for landing in the correct zone.
			- Bonus points for hitting cones in the target area.
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Allow stationary backhand shots after each cone.
			- Intermediate: Introduce faster feeds and alternate cone placement.
			- Advanced: Require topspin backhands and quick recovery to the middle after each shot.
			### **Variation Twist:**
			- Relay Race: Set up two zigzag lanes and have players compete in teams for speed and accuracy.
			- Directional Focus: Players alternate between down-the-line and cross-court shots after each cone.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- Focus on quick recovery to the ready position.
			- Encourage smooth, controlled movement rather than rushing.
			- Reinforce proper alignment to the ball before each shot.
		</details>
	</callout>
# GAME OF THE DAY (10 MIN)
> In the **Game of the Day** for forehands, players will compete in a fun, competitive format that emphasizes backhand + forehand stroke development.<br>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_orange.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>KITCHEN BATTLE ROYALE</summary>
			**Objective:** Build confidence in forehands and backhands while emphasizing court positioning.
			**Setup:**
			- Divide players into two teams.
			- Players rally from the NVZ line (kitchen) using forehands and backhands.
			**Rules:**
			1. Each team starts with a set number of points.
			2. Players earn points for successful, controlled rallies and lose points for unforced errors.
			3. A team wins when they reach a pre-set score (e.g., 10 points) or maintain the highest score after time runs out.
			**Variation:**
			- Advanced players must alternate between forehands and backhands.
			- Add a bonus rule: Hit into marked zones to earn extra points.
		</details>
	</callout>
# LIFE LESSON (ONGOING)
> **Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.<br>
	1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
		- Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
	2. **Explain the Meaning**:
		- Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
			- **Word**: **Integrity**
			- **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it’s about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
	3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
		- Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
			- Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
			- Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
	4. **Assign a Task**:
		- Ask the players to think about the word during the week and find examples in their own lives or in the stories they read or hear about. They should come back next week ready to:
			- Share their understanding of the word.
			- Give an example of how they or someone they know demonstrated this life lesson in sports or everyday life.
	5. **Wrap-Up**:
		- Encourage players to practice the life lesson throughout the week, both in their sports activities and in their personal lives. Remind them that these lessons help them become better athletes and better people.
	This activity emphasizes the importance of applying sports values to real-life situations, fostering personal growth and character development in young athletes.
