# KING/QUEEN OF THE COURT
**Setup:** One pair (or player, for singles groups) holds the court as "king/queen." Challengers line up behind the baseline on the other side.
**Base Game:** Challenger starts the point with a toss or serve, coach's call. Play the point out. Winner stays; on the king/queen side, challengers who win cross over and become the new king/queen. Losing challengers go to the back of the line.
**Use It To Check:** Whether a stroke holds up under live, competitive pressure with a line of players watching — not just in a fed drill.
**Constraint Menu:** Max stay on the king/queen side: 2 or 3 consecutive points, then rotate out regardless of the score (everyone gets a turn). Toss-start instead of serve-start for beginners so the serve doesn't decide every point. Singles or pairs depending on group size.
**Observable Evidence:** Turn, swing path, and footwork stay recognizable when a point is on the line, not just in cooperative rallies.
**If It Breaks:** If serve strength is deciding every point, switch the whole round to toss starts. If one player dominates and nobody else gets a rep, shrink the max stay to 2 points.
**Progress The Check:** Once rallies hold up, require a named target (crosscourt/down the line) to win the point instead of any ball in.
## MATCH ETIQUETTE & SCORING — ADD ONCE RALLIES HOLD UP
**Scoring:** 15-30-40-game, first to 6 games wins a set (win by 2). Serve is diagonal, into the correct service box, alternating server each game.
**Etiquette:** Call your own line honestly, ball goes to the server between points, wait for the returner to be ready before serving, shake hands or racket-tap at the net after the match.
**Coach watches for:** Players calling their own score out loud before the next point — that's the observable evidence this has transferred, not just correct shot-making.
