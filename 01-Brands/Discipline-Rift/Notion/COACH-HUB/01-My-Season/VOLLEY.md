# VOLLEY
*(Forehand + Backhand, Net Play — Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
The volley is a reaction skill, not a swing skill — the single biggest technical break from the groundstrokes players just learned. USTA QuickStart research backs low-compression balls and a lower net for this skill specifically: reaction time at the net is short, and slower balls are what let beginners build the "punch, don't swing" habit instead of flinching.
---
## SHOW IT — VOLLEY BY NUMBERS
### Position 1 — Ready
**Cue:** "Racket up"
- Continental grip
- Racket head above the wrist, out in front, where you can see it
- Split step as the ball is struck
### Position 2 — Punch
**Cue:** "Punch, don't swing"
- Short backswing, barely past the shoulder
- Racket face stays open, firm wrist
**Why:** A big backswing at the net is a ball in the net or a missed reaction — there isn't time for a full swing.
### Position 3 — Contact
**Cue:** "Catch it out front"
- Contact in front of the body, racket face leads
- Forehand volley: step in with the foot opposite the racket hand. Backhand volley: step in with the same foot as the racket hand.
**Why:** The wrong foot forward on either side pulls the shoulder out of position and turns the punch into a lunge.
### Position 4 — Finish
**Cue:** "Stick it"
- Racket stops just past contact, no big follow-through
- Recover to ready position immediately
---
## Core Volley Pattern
**1 → 2 → 3 → 4** — Ready → Punch → Contact → Stick.

## Progression Connections
Beginner: fed volleys close to the net, stationary, low-compression ball. Intermediate: forehand and backhand volleys with lateral movement, moving to target areas. Advanced: volleys with pace and precision, reflex volleys, connects to overhead when the ball is lobbed.
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Clean contact, ball in the court 2 of 3 tries, proper grip and minimal swing.
- **Intermediate:** Forehand and backhand volleys to target areas with controlled, short swing.
- **Advanced:** Firm volleys placed deep with minimal error, handles fast-paced feeds and reflex exchanges.
