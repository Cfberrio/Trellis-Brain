# INTRODUCTION (10 MIN)
> **WELCOME**
	- Greet the players
		- *Example:* “Good to see everyone! Welcome to Week 3 of our program. We’re making great progress, and it’s been exciting to see how much you’ve grown already!”
	- Reinforce the mission of the program’s mission**:** “We’re here to grow as athletes and individuals, using sports to build confidence, adaptability, and discipline.”
	- Recap last week’s life lessons:
		- **Consistency:** “Who can share an example of how you practiced consistency, maybe in a drill or at home?”
		- **Humility:** “Did anyone have a moment where they stayed humble even when things were going well—or when they weren’t?”
			- Highlight positive examples shared by players.

		<summary>**PICKLEBALL SIMON SAYS**</summary>
			1. **Setup:**
				- Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
				- Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
				- **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
				- Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
			2. **Game Rules:**
				- The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
				- If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
			3. **Commands:**
				- **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
				- **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
				- **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
				- **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
				- **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
				- **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
					- **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
					- Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
				- **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
					- Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
			4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
				- The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
			5. **Elimination:**
				- Any player who follows a command that doesn’t begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
				- Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
			6. **Winner's Reward:**
				- The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.

		<summary>**GRIP: THE HANDSHAKE METHOD**</summary>
			### **Handshake Method:**
			- Instruct students to imagine shaking hands with the paddle.
			- They should wrap their fingers around the handle naturally, with their thumb and index finger creating a "V" pointing toward the paddle’s edge.
			- Encourage them to grip firmly enough for control but relaxed to avoid stiffness.
			1. **Alternate Grip Practice:**
				- Have students run their hand down the edge of the paddle (from the top) to the handle and grip it naturally at the base.
				- This reinforces proper hand positioning while keeping it simple for beginners.
			### **Game: Frying Pan Bounce**
			### **Setup:**
			- Give each student a paddle and a ball.
			- Demonstrate how to hold the paddle flat, like a frying pan, with the correct grip.
			### **Steps:**
			1. **Bounce Practice:**
				- Students gently drop the ball onto the paddle and try to keep it bouncing steadily in the air.
				- Encourage a controlled wrist motion rather than large arm swings.
			2. **Set Targets:**
				- Start with a goal of 10 consecutive bounces.
				- Gradually increase to 20 or 30 as students gain control.
			3. **Challenge Variations:**
				- **Individual Challenge:** Most consecutive bounces in a row.
				- **Partner Challenge:** Students pair up, taking turns to reach a set number of bounces.
				- **Class Challenge:** See who can reach the highest total or who gets to a set target (e.g., 50 bounces) the fastest.
			4. **Switch to Backhand:**
				- Instruct students to rotate the paddle to practice backhand bounces.
				- Focus on maintaining the same grip while adjusting the wrist slightly for the backhand position.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- **Monitor Grip:** Ensure students maintain the correct grip during the exercise. *Tip:* “Hold the paddle like you’re holding a frying pan—firm but not too tight!”
			- **Encourage Controlled Movements:**
				- If students are struggling, ask them to focus on smaller, softer bounces.
			- **Provide Feedback:**
				- Celebrate consistency: *“Great job keeping the ball steady for 15 bounces!”*
				- Offer adjustments: *“Try to keep the paddle flat and use gentle taps for better control.”*
			### **Wrap-Up and Progression:**
			- After mastering bounces, suggest integrating movement:
				- Have students walk in a straight line or around cones while keeping the ball bouncing.
				- This helps build paddle control in dynamic situations.
			- Reinforce the importance of using the correct grip for both forehand and backhand as they progress to other drills.
			Breaking the repetition into these small, engaging activities keeps students focused, encourages skill-building, and adds an element of fun competition.
			### Advanced: Reverse Frying Pan
			**Transition to Reverse Frying Pan:**
			After students demonstrate proficiency with the regular frying pan drill (bouncing the ball upward on the paddle), introduce the reverse variation: *“Now that you’ve mastered keeping the ball in the air, let’s flip the challenge. This time, you’ll bounce the ball downward into the ground and keep it going!”*
			1. **Demonstrate the Drill:**
				- Show how to gently tap the ball into the ground with the paddle angled flat or slightly downward.
				- Emphasize controlled, soft bounces and finding the paddle’s sweet spot for consistent rebounds.
			2. **Practice Steps:**
				- **Step 1:** Drop the ball onto the ground and catch it on the paddle to get a feel for the rebound.
				- **Step 2:** Attempt 3–5 controlled downward bounces.
				- **Step 3:** Gradually build up to continuous bounces, focusing on a steady tempo.
			3. **Set Progressive Goals:**
				- Start with 5 downward bounces in a row.
				- Increase targets to 10, 15, or more as players gain confidence.
			4. **Integrate Challenges:**
				- **Individual:** Most consecutive reverse bounces without losing control.
				- **Partner:** Alternate turns to see who can reach a goal first (e.g., 20 bounces).
				- **Class Challenge:** Set a time limit and see who achieves the highest total.

# SKILL - DINKS (5 MIN)
> **DEMONSTRATION**
	**Why Dinks?**
	- **Purpose of Dinks**: Dinks are soft, controlled shots designed to land in the opponent’s kitchen (non-volley zone), forcing them to play low and preventing aggressive returns. They are critical for slowing the pace of play, maintaining precision, and creating opportunities to outmaneuver your opponent.
	- **Strategy**: Emphasize patience and control during dink rallies. Highlight how dinks can transition a defensive situation into an offensive advantage.
	<empty-block/>
	**Stance and Ready Position**
	- **Balanced Stance**:
		- Feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, and body weight distributed evenly for stability.
		- Paddle held at chest height, angled slightly forward for quick reactions.
	- **Staying Low**:
		- Stress the importance of bending at the knees to stay low, aiding in controlled, upward motion on dinks.
	<empty-block/>
	**IMITATION**
	**Breakdown Steps for Players**
	1. **Stance**:
		- Start in the ready position and practice keeping the paddle in a neutral position.
	2. **Grip Practice**:
		- Encourage players to use a relaxed grip for better control and finesse.
	3. **Swing Practice**:
		- Demonstrate a short, upward stroke from below the ball, ensuring a soft touch.
	4. **Follow-Through**:
		- Guide players to finish with the paddle pointing toward their target.
	<empty-block/>
	**Controlled Repetition**
	- **Shadow Practice**:
		- Use shadow swings without a ball to reinforce the low-to-high motion.
	- Provide immediate feedback on paddle angle, swing length, and posture.
	<empty-block/>
	**CORRECTION**
	**Common Errors**
	1. **Stance and Balance**:
		- **Error**: Feet too close together or knees locked.
		- **Fix**: Adjust to shoulder-width stance and keep a slight knee bend.
	2. **Grip**:
		- **Error**: Grip too tight or paddle angle too flat.
		- **Fix**: Encourage a relaxed grip and slight paddle tilt for better control.
	3. **Swing Technique**:
		- **Error**: Swinging too flat or hard.
		- **Fix**: Focus on brushing the ball softly upward with minimal motion.
	4. **Follow-Through**:
		- **Error**: Stopping the paddle abruptly.
		- **Fix**: Extend through the shot toward the target.

			### **Objective:**
			Simulate a "Dink Rally" to reinforce soft, controlled shots into the opponent's kitchen. This drill lays the foundation for dinking in live gameplay and strengthens precision and consistency.
			### **Setup:**
			- **Players:** 4 participants on the court, 2 on each side of the net.
			- **Positioning:** Players start just behind the kitchen line.
			- **Ball:** 1 ball per rally.
			- **Rotation:** Additional players wait on the sidelines for their turn to swap in.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Start the Rally:**
				- Players dink the ball softly over the net, aiming for the kitchen area on the opponent’s side.
			2. **Maintain Control:**
				- Emphasize smooth, upward strokes and keeping the ball low.
				- Shots must bounce in the kitchen to count.
			3. **Rotation:**
				- When the rally ends, players on the sidelines swap in, maintaining continuous play.
				- Encourage active observation and learning from the sidelines.

		<summary>**AROUND THE WORLD**</summary>
			### **Objective:**
			Enhance coordination, teamwork, and awareness by directing the ball across the court in a rotating pattern.
			### **Setup:**
			- **Players:** 4 participants: Player A and B on one side, Player C and D on the other.
			- **Positioning:** Players begin just behind the kitchen line.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Clockwise Rotation:**
				- Players follow this sequence: A → C → B → D.
				- Each player dinks the ball to the next in sequence.
			2. **Reverse Rotation:**
				- Players follow this sequence: D → B → C → A.
				- This adds variety and challenges players’ directional control.
			3. **Goal:**
				- See how many successful rotations the group can achieve without losing control.
				- Encourage steady communication among players to anticipate the next shot.
			### **Extension:**
			- **Volley Dinks:**
				- Players behind the Non-Volley Line (NVL) can take the ball on the full (volley) if reachable, but the shot must still bounce in the opponent’s kitchen.
				- This adds an element of quick decision-making and control.

		<summary>BACKHAND TARGET CHALLENGE</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Place 5-7 cones on the opposite side of the net, spaced strategically in different zones:
				1. Near the baseline.
				2. Mid-court.
				3. Near the NVZ (Non-Volley Zone).
			### **Instructions:**
			1. Players line up behind the baseline on one side of the net.
			2. Coach feeds the ball to each player, one at a time.
			3. Players hit backhands, aiming for specific cones.
			4. **Scoring Options:**
				- 3 points for hitting a cone directly.
				- 2 points for landing within 1 foot of a cone.
				- 1 point for a successful hit into the correct zone.
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Use larger, brightly colored cones and slower feeds.
			- Intermediate: Introduce alternating targets and medium-paced feeds.
			- Advanced: Require specific types of backhands (e.g., cross-court, topspin) or faster feeds.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- Emphasize low-to-high motion for controlled lift.
			- Encourage players to keep their eyes on the cone until they strike the ball.
			- Focus on consistent foot positioning before each shot.

		<summary>PARTNER BACKHAND RALLIES</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Pair up players and position each partner on opposite sides of the net, about mid-court distance apart.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Warm-Up Phase:**
				- Partners rally using only backhands, keeping the ball in play.
				- Players aim to achieve 10 continuous shots without an error.
			2. **Scoring Phase:**
				- Each successful rally earns the pair a point.
				- Introduce "combo points" for reaching specific rally counts (e.g., 2 points for 15 hits, 3 points for 20 hits).
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Allow players to stand closer together with slower-paced rallies.
			- Intermediate: Increase the distance and require shots to clear the NVZ.
			- Advanced: Add cross-court backhand rallies or targets for more precision.
			### **Variation Twist:**
			- Time Challenge: See how many rallies the pair can complete in 3 minutes.
			- Moving Rally: Partners shuffle laterally after each shot to practice movement.
			### **Coaching Tips:**
			- Highlight proper grip and follow-through.
			- Encourage communication between partners for better rally flow.
			- Reinforce controlled, consistent strokes rather than power.

		<summary>ZIGZAG BACK</summary>
			### **Setup:**
			- Place cones in a zigzag pattern on one side of the court, from the baseline to the NVZ line.
			- Space cones about 5-7 feet apart to encourage lateral movement.
			### **Instructions:**
			1. **Movement Phase:**
				- Players start at the baseline, moving laterally around each cone in a zigzag pattern.
				- After reaching the cone, they hit a backhand toward a target area.
			2. **Shooting Phase:**
				- Coach or partner feeds balls as players reach each cone.
				- Players return to the ready position after each shot and continue moving.
			### **Scoring Options:**
			- 1 point for landing in the correct zone.
			- Bonus points for hitting cones in the target area.
			### **Progression Options:**
			- Beginner: Allow stationary backhand shots after each cone.
			- Intermediate: Introduce faster feeds and alternate cone placement.
			- Advanced: Require topspin backhands and quick recovery to the middle after each shot.
			### **Variation Twist:**
			- Relay Race: Set up two zigzag lanes and have players compete in teams for speed and accuracy.

			- Focus on quick recovery to the ready position.
			- Encourage smooth, controlled movement rather than rushing.

# LIFE LESSON (ONGOING)
> **Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.<br>
	1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
		- Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
	2. **Explain the Meaning**:
		- Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
			- **Word**: **Integrity**
			- **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it’s about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
	3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
		- Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
			- Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
			- Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
	4. **Assign a Task**:
		- Ask the players to think about the word during the week and find examples in their own lives or in the stories they read or hear about. They should come back next week ready to:
			- Share their understanding of the word.
			- Give an example of how they or someone they know demonstrated this life lesson in sports or everyday life.
	5. **Wrap-Up**:
		- Encourage players to practice the life lesson throughout the week, both in their sports activities and in their personal lives. Remind them that these lessons help them become better athletes and better people.
	This activity emphasizes the importance of applying sports values to real-life situations, fostering personal growth and character development in young athletes.
