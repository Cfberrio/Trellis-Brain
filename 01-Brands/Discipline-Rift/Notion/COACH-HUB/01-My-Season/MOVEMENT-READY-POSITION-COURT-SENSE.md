# MOVEMENT, READY POSITION & COURT SENSE
*(Non-Stroke Connective Skill — Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
Every stroke on this curriculum depends on the player already being in the right place, balanced, before the swing starts. This is the connective skill — like Moving/Communicating in volleyball — that makes every other skill page work. USTA QuickStart research is explicit that scaled courts and lower-compression balls exist partly to give beginners the extra time this skill needs to develop: split step, read, move, arrive balanced.
---
## SHOW IT — MOVEMENT BY NUMBERS
### Position 1 — Ready
**Cue:** "Ready position"
- Feet shoulder-width, knees bent, weight on the balls of the feet
- Racket up, eyes on the opponent/feeder
### Position 2 — Split Step
**Cue:** "Split as they hit"
- Small hop timed to the opponent's or feeder's contact
- Land with feet apart, ready to push off in any direction
**Why:** The split step is what turns reaction into a fast first move — skip it and every shot starts a half-step late.
### Position 3 — Move
**Cue:** "First step explosive, then adjust"
- Explosive first step toward the ball
- Shuffle/adjust steps to fine-tune positioning, not a sprint the whole way
### Position 4 — Arrive & Recover
**Cue:** "Balanced in, balanced out"
- Last step sets up a balanced base for the stroke
- Recover back toward the center/ready position after every shot
---
## Core Movement Pattern
**1 → 2 → 3 → 4** — Ready → Split → Move → Arrive & Recover. Runs underneath every stroke on this curriculum.
---
## RACKET & COURT VOCABULARY
Week 1 basics, taught once and referenced everywhere after: **Head, Frame, Face, Neck, Grip, Handle, Butt, Sweet Spot** (racket parts) and **Baseline, Service Line, Net, Singles Sideline, Doubles Sideline** (court lines). Use Court Compass in the warm-up bank to drill the court vocabulary live.
---
## TIMING & RHYTHM
Named here because it's the thread through every cue above, not a separate skill: "split as they hit" (Position 2) and "bounce-hit" (Forehand's coach cue) are both timing cues. A player who's technically correct but always early or late on contact has a timing problem, not a technique problem — fix the rhythm before re-teaching the stroke.

## Progression Connections
Beginner: ready position and split step in isolation, short-court spacing. Intermediate: movement added before every fed stroke, step patterns (step-and-swing, shuffle-and-swing). Advanced: full-court movement and recovery under rally pressure, connects directly into every stroke's Position 1.
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Shows a recognizable ready position and attempts a split step on cue.
- **Intermediate:** Splits and moves before the stroke consistently, arrives balanced most reps.
- **Advanced:** Split step and recovery are automatic under rally pressure; court coverage supports every stroke without a technical breakdown.
