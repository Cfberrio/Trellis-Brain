# WEEK 1 - FOREHANDS
# INTRODUCTION (10 MIN)
> **WELCOME**
	- Greet all students and introduce them to the pickleball season.
	- Coaches introduce themselves.
	- Briefly explain the mission of DR Sports & Athletics:
		- **DR = Discipline Rift**.
		- **Discipline Rift**: Introduction and development in pickleball and introduction to the life lessons we can learn from the sport.
		- Ask: What is discipline?<br>Players may think discipline receiving punishment, clarify that discipline is the power to commit to a decision and follow through.<br>
	**TEAM EXPECTATIONS **
		- Arrive on time for tennis class, held every (insert day) of each week.
		- Be respectful and supportive of fellow players by helping each other improve.
		- Listen to your coaches and follow instructions.<br>
	**ACTIONS & CONSEQUENCES:**
		- Every action has consequences:
			- Positive actions lead to good consequences.
			- Negative actions lead to bad consequences.
		- 3 Call-Out Rule:
			- **First Callout**: Coaches will speak to the player.
			- **Second Callout**: Player will be placed in timeout during class.
			- **Third Callout**: Parent talk.
		- Program Overview:
			- The program includes 6 weeks of tennis classes, held on (day of practice) each week.
			- Remind students to remember and attend each week.

		<summary>**PICKLEBALL SIMON SAYS**</summary>
			1. **Setup:**
				- Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
				- Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
				- **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
				- Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
			2. **Game Rules:**
				- The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
				- If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
			3. **Commands:**
				- **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
				- **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
				- **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
				- **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
				- **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
				- **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
					- **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
					- Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
				- **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
					- Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
			4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
				- The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
			5. **Elimination:**
				- Any player who follows a command that doesn’t begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
				- Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
			6. **Winner's Reward:**
				- The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.
		</details>

# SKILL (5 MIN)
> **BREAKDOWN**
	1. **Introduction (Introduce all pickleball skills)**:
		- **Forehand Drive**: A basic groundstroke hit with the dominant hand, emphasizing control and direction.
		- **Backhand Drive**: A stroke hit from the non-dominant side, requiring precision and balance.
		- **Dinks**: Soft shots played close to the net, promoting finesse and touch.
		- **Serving**: An underhand stroke used to start a rally.
		- **Explain that today’s focus will be on** the **Forehand Drive.**
	2. **Demonstration (Forehand Drive Breakdown)**:
		- **Stance and Alignment**:
			- Start in the ready position: feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, paddle in front.
			- Rotate toward your dominant side, keeping the paddle low.
		- **Grip and Paddle Position**:
			- Use the continental grip.
			- Hold the paddle with the dominant hand; keep the other hand lightly balancing the paddle's neck.
		- **Swing Mechanics**:
			- Bring the paddle back, pointing it slightly downward.
			- Step forward with the non-dominant foot.
			- Swing forward with the paddle face slightly angled upward to ensure clean contact.
		- **Contact Point**:
			- Make contact at waist height, slightly in front of the body.
			- Aim the paddle face toward the target area.
		- **Follow-Through**:
			- Finish with the paddle pointing upward, ready to reset for the next shot.
	3. **Imitation (Players Imitate the Coach)**:
		- Players mimic the coach’s demonstration step-by-step:
			- Stance, paddle position, swing, contact point, and follow-through.
	4. **Correction (Coach Adjusts Form)**:
		- **Stance and Alignment**: Ensure knees are bent, and feet are balanced.
		- **Grip**: Ensure players maintain a firm yet relaxed grip.
		- **Contact Point**: Reinforce making contact in front of the body.
		- **Follow-Through**: Encourage full completion of the swing for control and accuracy.

# FOREHANDS (30 MIN)
> **FORM**
	- **Start in Ready Position**:
		- Feet shoulder-width apart, knees bent, paddle positioned in front.
	- **Turn Toward Dominant Hand**:
		- Rotate the torso to the dominant side, preparing for the swing.
	- **Paddle Alignment**:
		- Bring the paddle back, pointing slightly downward.
	- **Point with the Non-Dominant Hand**:
		- Use the free hand to point at the ball, aiding balance and direction.
	- **Swing Mechanics**:
		- Swing the paddle forward, meeting the ball at waist level.
		- Ensure a slight upward motion for consistent control.
	- **Recovery**:
		- Return to the ready position quickly after the swing.

# GAME OF THE DAY (10 MIN)
> In the **Game of the Day** for forehands, players will compete in a fun, competitive format that emphasizes forehand stroke development.<br>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_orange.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>FOREHAND FRENZY</summary>
			**Setup**
			- Set up three target zones on the opposite side of the court:
				1. **Deep Center** (for drives).
				2. **Sidelines** (for placement shots).
				3. **Near the Kitchen Line** (for controlled drop shots).
			- Use cones, hoops, or chalk to mark these areas.
			**Equipment:**
			- Pickleball paddles and balls (1 per player).
			- A net dividing the play area.
			**How to Play**
			1. **Game Start**:
				- Players line up behind the baseline.
				- A coach or feeder tosses or hits balls to the players.
			2. **Objective**:
				- Players hit forehands to land the ball in one of the designated target zones.
				- Rotate players quickly to keep the line moving and maintain engagement.
			3. **Scoring**:
				- **3 points**: Deep center.
				- **2 points**: Sidelines.
				- **1 point**: Near the kitchen.
			4. **Progression**:
				- Add movement challenges by requiring players to shuffle left or right before hitting.
				- Introduce strategic play: players must call out their intended target before hitting.
			**Winning Condition**
			- The player with the highest score after a set number of rounds wins a small prize or gets to choose the next group activity.
			**Purpose:**
			- Reinforces forehand fundamentals like accuracy and shot selection.
		</details>

# LIFE LESSON (ONGOING)
> **Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.<br>
	1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
		- Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
	2. **Explain the Meaning**:
		- Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
			- **Word**: **Integrity**
			- **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it’s about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
	3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
		- Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
			- Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
			- Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
	4. **Assign a Task**:
		- Ask the players to think about the word during the week and find examples in their own lives or in the stories they read or hear about. They should come back next week ready to:
			- Share their understanding of the word.
			- Give an example of how they or someone they know demonstrated this life lesson in sports or everyday life.
	5. **Wrap-Up**:
		- Encourage players to practice the life lesson throughout the week, both in their sports activities and in their personal lives. Remind them that these lessons help them become better athletes and better people.
	This activity emphasizes the importance of applying sports values to real-life situations, fostering personal growth and character development in young athletes.
