# RACKET & BALL CONTROL
*(Foundation Skill — Pre-Stroke, Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
Before any stroke, a player needs to own the racket and predict how a ball behaves off it. Every purpose-built youth tennis curriculum opens here — grip, balance, bounce, tap-up — before teaching a single swing. Skipping it doesn't save time; it shows up later as a forehand that can't find the ball. USTA QuickStart's low-compression ball and lower net exist partly to make this stage possible at all — a full-pressure ball off a full-size racket is too fast to control at this stage.
---
## SHOW IT — RACKET & BALL CONTROL BY NUMBERS
### Position 1 — Grip
**Cue:** "Shake hands with the racket"
- Handshake grip, racket face flat
- Thumb and fingers wrapped, not choked up on the throat
**Why:** Every stroke that follows starts from this grip. Get it automatic now, before there's a ball in motion to think about too.
### Position 2 — Balance
**Cue:** "Flat plate, not a spoon"
- Ball balanced on the strings, palm up
- Racket held steady, arm relaxed
### Position 3 — Bounce
**Cue:** "Stiff wrist, quiet elbow"
- Drop the ball, let it bounce once, catch it back on the strings
- No slapping or stabbing at the ball on the catch
### Position 4 — Tap-Up
**Cue:** "Soft hands, control it"
- Palm up, tap the ball to about eye height, catch
- Build toward continuous tap-ups without a body assist
---
## Core Racket & Ball Control Pattern
**1 → 2 → 3 → 4** — Grip → Balance → Bounce → Tap-Up. The gate every true beginner passes through before Forehand Position 1 means anything.

## Progression Connections
This skill sits underneath every stroke on the curriculum — it isn't retired once Week 1 ends. A player who loses racket control under pressure later in the season (rushed serve toss, mishandled volley) is showing a Position 1-4 breakdown, not a new problem.
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Balances a ball on the strings while standing still; completes 3+ consecutive bungee drops (see Bungee Jumps).
- **Intermediate:** Walks while balancing the ball; completes 5+ consecutive tap-ups ("Fivesies" in Jacks).
- **Advanced:** Controls the ball on the racket while moving and changing direction; transitions smoothly into a stroke from a tap-up.
