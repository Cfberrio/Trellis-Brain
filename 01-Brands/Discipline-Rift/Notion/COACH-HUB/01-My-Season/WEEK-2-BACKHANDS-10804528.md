		<summary>**TENNIS SIMON SAYS**</summary>
			1. **Setup:**
				- Players stand on one side of the court along the **baseline**.
				- Feet are **shoulder-width apart**.
				- **Dominant hand** on the grip, **secondary hand** on the neck of the racket.
				- Players start upright and relaxed, holding their rackets.
			2. **Game Rules:**
				- The coach (acting as "Simon") gives commands, but players should only follow if the command starts with **"Simon says..."**.
				- If the coach gives a command without saying "Simon says" and a player follows it, that player is **eliminated**.
			3. **Commands:**
				- **"Simon says, Ready!"**: Players quickly move into the **ready position** (feet wide, knees bent, racket prepared).
				- **"Simon says, Back!"**: Players return to their **upright, relaxed position**.
				- **"Simon says, Left!" or "Right!"**: Players shuffle laterally to the left or right while maintaining the ready position.
				- **"Simon says, Ball!"**: Coach simulates hitting a ball to one side, and players move in that direction as if preparing to hit it.
				- **"Simon says, Split Step!"**: Players perform a small jump or **split step**, preparing for a shot.
				- **"Simon says, Forehand!"**: Players move into **forehand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **dominant hand side**.
					- **Racket** is drawn back, non-dominant hand pointing at the ball.
					- Swing forward, mimicking a forehand stroke.
				- **"Simon says, Backhand!"**: Players switch to **backhand position**:
					- Turn the body toward the **non-dominant hand side**, both hands on the racket grip.
					- Draw the **racket** back and swing forward, mimicking a backhand stroke.
			4. **Trick Commands (No "Simon says")**:
				- The coach can give commands such as "Forehand!" or "Backhand!" without saying "Simon says." Players who follow this command are **eliminated**.
			5. **Elimination:**
				- Any player who follows a command that doesn’t begin with "Simon says" is **eliminated**.
				- Continue playing until only one player remains, who is the **winner**.
			6. **Winner's Reward:**
				- The last player standing wins and can receive a reward, such as choosing the next drill or earning extra points.

			1. **Setup**:
				- Players line up in a single horizontal line along the baseline.

			3. **Elimination**:
				- If a player is touched by a ball, they are out and join the coach in the middle.
				- Eliminated players can help the coach by calling out the candy names but cannot toss the balls for safety reasons.
			4. **Winning**:
				- The game continues until only one player remains untagged. This player is the winner.
			5. **Purpose**: 
				- Improve players' agility, reaction time, and dodging skills in a fun and engaging way.

			1. **Explain:**
				- Define the boundaries on the court within which the game will be played.
				- Select one player to start as "it," holding a tennis ball.
			2. **Gameplay**:
				- The player who is "it" must tag other players by touching them with the volleyball (no throwing, just a soft touch).

				- The tagged player then becomes "it" and continues the game.

				- If there are many players, divide them into two teams:

					- **Runners**: The other team, without volleyballs, must avoid being tagged.

				- Develop agility, quickness, and spatial awareness while adding an element of fun and teamwork.

		- **Follow-Through** (Correct follow-through by ensuring the racquet finishes high)
# BACKHANDS (30 MIN)
> **FORM**
	- **Start in the Ready Position:** Feet shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, racquet held with both hands, facing the net.
	- **Turn Foot Positioning to the Secondary Hand’s Side:** Rotate your body so that your non-dominant hand leads the motion.
	- **Grip:** Place both hands on the handle of the racquet, ensuring proper grip (Eastern or Semi-Western for two-handed, Continental for one-handed).
	- **Racquet Alignment:** Align the racquet with your body, keeping it close to your torso.
	- **Swing:** Use a low-to-high motion to swing the racquet towards the net, aiming to make contact with the ball around waist height.
	- **Follow Through:** Finish the swing above the opposite shoulder to ensure proper mechanics.
	- **Return to Ready Position:** After the swing, return to the ready position to prepare for the next shot.

		<details>
		<summary>**PLAYER CUES**</summary>
			**<br>BEGINNER/INTERMEDIATE**
			- **Racquet too far back:** Ensure players don’t overextend their racquet behind their body.
			- **Improper grip:** Check that players are placing their hands on the racquet handle correctly.
			- **Moving incorrectly towards the ball:** Emphasize staying balanced and swinging properly without lunging towards the ball.
			- **Not finishing through the shot:** Ensure players finish with the racquet above the opposite shoulder to complete the stroke.<br>
			**ADVANCED: TOPSPIN**
			- **Brushing the Ball:** Emphasize that players need to brush the ball with their racquet using a **low-to-high swing path** to generate topspin.
			- **"Pet the Dog" Motion:**
				- Instruct players to visualize the motion as if they are "petting a dog."
				- Start with the racquet face slightly open and bring it down toward the ground.
				- Once the ball bounces, they should swing upwards, brushing against the ball with the racquet to create the desired spin.
				- They should hear a slight brushing sound as the strings make contact with the ball.
			**STEPS**
			1. **Ready Position:** Players should start in the ready position, knees bent, and racquet held with both hands for backhand topspin.
			2. **Grip:** Ensure players have the right grip (Eastern or Semi-Western for two-handed, Continental for one-handed).
			3. **Swing Path:**
				- Have players begin the swing low, bringing the racquet below the level of the ball.
				- As they make contact with the ball, they should use an upward brushing motion, swinging from **low to high**.
			4. **Contact Point:** Emphasize that contact with the ball should be around waist height, and the racquet should finish high after brushing the ball.
			<empty-block/>
			**COMMON CUES**
			- **Not Brushing Enough:** 
				- If the player isn't brushing enough, the ball won’t spin properly and may fly flat or long.
			- **Brushing Too Much:**
				- If the player brushes too much and doesn’t make enough forward contact, the ball may not get enough forward momentum and will fall short.
			- **Early Preparation:**
				- Turn your shoulders early and get into position quickly for more power and control.
			- **Racquet Positioning:**
				- Keep the racquet face slightly closed for control. Use a compact swing to maintain efficiency.
			- **Topspin Focus:**
				- Brush up on the ball with a low-to-high motion for topspin. Steeper swing = more spin.
			- **Footwork:**
				- Drive off the back leg for power. Recover quickly to the center of the court.
			- **Follow Through:**
				- Finish high above the opposite shoulder. Loosen grip slightly after contact for fluidity.
			- **Power Generation:**
				- Use core rotation and shift weight from back to front foot for maximum power.
			- **Spin and Precision:**
				- Vary spin to control depth and use your non-dominant hand for direction.
		</details>

		- **Baseline Position: **Line the players up behind (or) at the baseline and feed balls to them one by one.

		- **Vary the Target Locations: **Place some cones down the line and others cross-court to improve accuracy and simulate match scenarios.<br>

			### **Exercise:**
			1. **Net Balance Topspin Practice:**
				- Have players stand near the net and balance a ball against it using their racquets.
				- Instruct them to push the ball over the net by brushing up on the ball with a low-to-high motion.

			### **Drill:**
			1. **Topspin Practice (Advanced):**
				- **Set-Up:**
					- Line players up behind the baseline or service line.
					- Feed balls to each player one by one.
			2. **Execution:**
				- Players practice generating topspin using a low-to-high swing path.
				- Focus on brushing the ball to create spin and control the ball's flight.
			3. **Objective:**
				- Ensure the ball spins and lands inside the court boundaries on the opposite side of the net.
				- If topspin is executed properly, the ball will dip into the court. Incorrect topspin will cause a flat ball flight and may send it out of bounds.

		<summary>**BACKHAND FOOTWORK DRILL**</summary>
			1. **Instructions:**
				- **Set a Cone as a Target:**

				- Emphasize constant foot movement and the importance of recovering to the middle of the court.
				- Provide feedback on improving balance, positioning, and shot execution.

# GAME OF THE DAY (10 MIN)
> In the **Game of the Day** for forehands, players will compete in a fun, competitive format that emphasizes backhand + forehand stroke development.<br>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**HANGMAN**</summary>
			1. **Setup**:
				- Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
				- Players will take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (e.g., backhand) or previously learned skills.
			2. **Game Rules**:
				- **Objective**: Players must hit the ball **over the net** and within the **court boundaries**.
				- **Body Parts**: If a player succeeds, they "lose" a body part (start with one arm, then a leg, etc.). The progression continues until they are hitting from the ground.
				- If a player **misses** their last attempt (after all body parts are "lost"), they go to the other side of the net, also known as **jail** (or become a shark or go into detention).
			3. **Escaping Jail**:
				- To escape from jail, players must catch a ball, either after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
			4. **Winning Condition**:
				- Players continue until only one player remains, or the coach decides to end the game. Those who avoid missing shots and "losing body parts" will keep going.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**BATTLESHIP**</summary>
			1. **Setup**:
				- **Divide players** into two teams.
				- Each team positions themselves on **opposite sides** of the net.
			2. **Cone Placement**:
				- Each team sets up **cones** (representing ships) on their side of the court, strategically placing them in various locations.
			3. **Game Rules**:
				- Players will take turns using the **skill of the day** (e.g., forehand, backhand) to try and hit the cones on the opposite side of the net.
				- The goal is to **hit and "sink"** the opposing team’s ships (cones) by knocking them down.
			4. **Winning Condition**:
				- The first team to destroy all of the other team’s ships (cones) **wins** the game.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**JAILBREAK**</summary>
			1. **Setup**:
				- Line players up behind the **baseline** on one side of the court.
				- Players take turns hitting the **skill of the day** (or previously learned skills if it's past Week 1).
			2. **Game Rules**:
				- **Objective**: Players aim to hit the ball over the net and into the court.
				- **Losing Lives**: If successful, the player **loses a life** (starting with a body part).
				- If they **miss** their shot, they go to the **other side of the net** (jail, become a shark, or detention).
			3. **Escaping Jail**:
				- To escape from jail, players must **catch a ball** from a rally after **one bounce** or **in the air**.
			4. **Winning Condition**:
				- Players continue hitting until only one player remains, or until the coach ends the game. Players who successfully keep the ball in play without missing stay in the game longer.
			5. Purpose:
				- This game helps players practice their consistency and recovery while keeping things fun and competitive.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**SKITTLES**</summary>
			1. **Setup**:
				- Line all players up on **one side** of the court.
			2. **Game Rules**:
				- When the coach calls out the word **“skittles”**, all players must **run** from one side of the court to the other.
				- As they run, the coach will **throw balls** at the players.
				- If a player is **hit by a ball**, they are eliminated and must step aside to help the coach throw balls at the remaining players.
			3. **Winning Condition**:
				- The game continues until only **one player** is left standing, and that player is the **winner.**
			4. **Purpose:**
				- Improve agility, quick reflexes, and dodging skills. It encourages players to stay alert, react quickly, and move with speed
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**FIREBALL**</summary>
			1. **Setup**:
				- Players spread out across the court, ready for the coach to hit balls at random spots.
			2. **Game Rules**:
				- The coach will hit **fewer balls** than the number of players to **random locations** on the court.
				- Players must quickly move to **catch or hit** the balls.
				- Any player who **fails to get a ball** will be **eliminated** and must step aside to help the coach toss balls for the remaining players.
			3. **Progression**:
				- Each round, the number of balls decreases, ensuring that there are always **fewer balls than players**.
			4. **Winning Condition**:
				- The game continues until only **one player** remains, who is declared the **winner**.
			5. **Purpose:**
				- The purpose of this game is to enhance players' **speed**, **anticipation**, and **court coverage**.
		</details>
	</callout>

		<summary>**FULL SET (ADVANCED)**</summary>
			1. **Set Up:**
				- Divide players into two groups.
				- Players from each group face each other one-on-one.
			2. **Match Play:**
				- Play in a standard scoring format (15, 30, 40, game) for 6 games, which equals one set.
				- Ensure serves are diagonal and must land in the serving box.
			3. **Winning Condition:**
				- The first player to win 6 games wins the set.
				- Accurate ball calls and proper court boundaries must be followed.
			4. **Repeat:**
				- Continue the match format until all players have faced each other or time ends.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**KING / QUEEN OF THE COURT (ADVANCED)**</summary>
			1. **Set Up**:
			- Choose **one or two players** to be the **king/queen** on one side of the net.
			- The rest of the players line up on the **opposite side** of the court.
			2. **Challenger Rotation**:
			- Players from the opposite side challenge the king/queen **one at a time** by playing a point against them.
			3. **Winning Conditions**:
			- **First Point**: If the challenger wins the first point, they earn the right to play a second point against the king/queen.
			- **Second Point**: If the challenger wins **two consecutive points**, they become the **new king/queen**.
			4. **Automatic Victory**:
			- If a challenger hits a **winner** (a shot that the king/queen cannot reach or return) during the **first point**, they **immediately** become the new king/queen, skipping the need for a second point.
			5. **Repeat**:
			- Continue the drill, with challengers rotating through the line to take on the reigning king/queen.
			- The game keeps going until a new king/queen is crowned or the drill time ends.
		</details>
	</callout>
	<callout icon="/icons/baseball_yellow.svg" color="gray_bg">
		<details>
		<summary>**RALLY (ADVANCED)**</summary>
			1. **Set Up:**
				- Choose one player to be on one side of the net.
				- The rest of the players line up on the opposite side.
			2. **Challenger Rotation:**
				- Players take turns rallying with the player on the other side of the net, one at a time.
			3. **Objective:**
				- Each player attempts to keep the rally going for as long as possible with the player on the opposite side.
			4. **Winning Condition:**
				- The player who manages to sustain the rally for the longest number of hits wins the round.
			5. **Repeat:**
				- Continue the drill, rotating through the line of challengers. Players can switch sides after each rally.
		</details>

# LIFE LESSON (ONGOING)
> **Objective**: Connect life skills and values to the practice and sport, helping players understand and apply these lessons both on and off the field.<br>
	1. **Introduce the Word of the Week**:
		- Begin by introducing a key life lesson or value as the "Word of the Week." Choose a word that is relevant to sports and personal development, such as "Integrity," "Resilience," or "Responsibility."
	2. **Explain the Meaning**:
		- Provide a brief explanation of what the word means and how it applies to both sports and life. For example:
			- **Word**: **Integrity**
			- **Explanation**: "Integrity means doing the right thing, even when no one is watching. In sports, it’s about being honest, playing by the rules, and respecting your opponents. In life, integrity is about being true to your values and making good choices, even when it's hard."
	3. **Discuss the Life Lesson**:
		- Engage the players in a brief discussion to help them connect with the concept:
			- Ask: "Why is integrity important in sports? Can anyone give an example of a situation where they had to show integrity?"
			- Share a personal or famous example that demonstrates the importance of the word.
	4. **Assign a Task**:
		- Ask the players to think about the word during the week and find examples in their own lives or in the stories they read or hear about. They should come back next week ready to:
			- Share their understanding of the word.
			- Give an example of how they or someone they know demonstrated this life lesson in sports or everyday life.
	5. **Wrap-Up**:
		- Encourage players to practice the life lesson throughout the week, both in their sports activities and in their personal lives. Remind them that these lessons help them become better athletes and better people.
	This activity emphasizes the importance of applying sports values to real-life situations, fostering personal growth and character development in young athletes.
