# SERVE & RETURN
*(Underhand Progression → Overhand, Developmental Tennis)*
---
## Why We Use It
The serve is the only stroke a player fully controls — it's also the most complex motion in the sport. USTA QuickStart's underhand-first progression exists for a reason: it lets beginners own the toss, the target, and the rally start before layering in the overhand motion. Lower nets and scaled service boxes keep the box makeable while the motion is still new; return of serve gets the same slow-ball, short-court treatment so players can track it.
---
## SHOW IT — SERVE BY NUMBERS (Underhand → Overhand)
### Stage 1 — Underhand Serve
**Cue:** "Step and push"
- Ball held out in front at waist height
- Step with the opposite foot, swing the racket forward and up like a bowling motion
- Contact below the waist, push through to the target box
**Why:** Underhand isolates toss, target, and rules — no overhand motion to fight yet.
### Stage 2 — Overhand Serve, Position 1: Stance & Toss
**Cue:** "Toss to 1 o'clock"
- Sideways stance: front foot points diagonally at the net post, back foot sideways to the court, back foot's toe lined up with the front foot's heel
- Toss with a straight arm, releases at eye level, slightly in front
### Position 2 — Trophy
**Cue:** "Trophy pose"
- Racket arm bent behind the head, elbow up
- Tossing arm still reaching up
**Why:** The trophy position is what turns a toss into a full-motion swing instead of a slap.
### Position 3 — Swing
**Cue:** "Reach and hit"
- Racket extends up to full reach, contact above the head
- Hit up and out, not down
### Position 4 — Finish
**Cue:** "Finish at the hip"
- Racket finishes across the body near the opposite hip
- Back foot steps into the court
---
## Return of Serve — Show It
**Cue:** "Split, turn, block"
- Split step as the server makes contact
- Turn shoulders to the ball side
- Short, blocked swing — return is about placement, not power

## Progression Connections
Beginner: underhand serve, short/scaled box. Intermediate: overhand serve introduced, 3 target zones, moderate pace. Advanced: overhand serve with placement and spin (slice/topspin), live return of serve under match pressure.
---
## Observable Evidence (Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced)
- **Beginner:** Ball over the net into the service box 2 of 3 tries with proper stance, toss, and follow-through (underhand or basic overhand).
- **Intermediate:** Lands serve in specific target areas with moderate pace and consistent toss/rotation.
- **Advanced:** Consistent high-velocity serves with control, uses spin or placement; returns with two clean returns per point under pressure.
