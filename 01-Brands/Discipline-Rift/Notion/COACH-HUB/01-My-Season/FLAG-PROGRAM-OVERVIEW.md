# FLAG PROGRAM OVERVIEW

## Overview
**Flatfootbook Flag Football Curriculum**
A progressive, decision-based flag football program built on intentional teaching and skill development.
## Core Pillars
- **Decide** — Teach kids what to look at so they choose better actions
- **Plan** — Practices and units are intentionally designed, not improvised
- **Feedback** — Coaches give short, specific corrections that change the next rep
- **CFU** — Coaches actively check that learning happened
- **Culture** — Routines create safety, belonging, and effort
- **Growth** — Everything is age-appropriate and progressive
## Teaching Philosophy
Flag football is taught as a **space-and-decision game**, not a playbook sport. Players learn to move, protect space, create space, and make simple decisions under pressure—without tackling or complex schemes.
**Core Decisions Players Make:**
- Find open space
- Protect the ball while moving
- Choose who is open
- Stop space on defense by angles and flag pulls
## Age Groups
### Funny Joes (K–2)
**Coaching Approach:**
- Movement-heavy, playful, highly structured practices
- One cue at a time
- Short, fast reps
- Focus on basic movement, confidence, and effort
**Player Development:**
- Run, stop, change direction, play simple chase games
- Carry the ball safely
- Pull flags with control
- Listen, start on signal, reset quickly
### Cool Jess (3–5)
**Coaching Approach:**
- Introduce decision-making and responsibility
- Ask players what they saw
- Add simple constraints that force choices
- Begin tracking progress through tiers
**Player Development:**
- Throw and catch on the move
- Learn when to run vs. pass
- Create space without being told exactly where to go
- Work in small groups with shared responsibility
## Skill Development System
### Six Core Skill Domains
**1. Ball Handling + Carrying**
- Hold the ball securely
- Keep eyes up while running
- Change direction without stopping
- Protect the ball when defenders are close
**2. Throwing**
*Focus: Consistency and decision timing, not distance*
- Step toward a target
- Release the ball smoothly
- Throw to open teammates
- Throw at the right moment
**3. Catching**
*Focus: Readiness and confidence*
- Watch the ball all the way in
- Catch with hands, not trapping
- Secure the ball before running
- Progress from standing to moving
**4. Getting Open**
*Create space without routes or plays*
- Move away from defenders
- Vary speed to get open
- Show hands to the thrower
- Find empty space naturally
**5. Defense + Flag Pulling**
*Focus: Control and angles, not aggression*
- Stay balanced and face the ball carrier
- Take good pursuit angles
- Pull flags safely
- Work together to stop space
**6. Team Play + Communication**
- Talk to teammates
- Reset quickly after plays
- Encourage effort
- Rotate roles so everyone participates
### Sub-Skill Breakdown
Each skill is broken into small, teachable pieces. Coaches focus on **one sub-skill at a time**.
**Examples:**
- Throwing → grip, step, release, follow-through
- Catching → eyes on ball, hands ready, secure then run
- Defense → stance, angle, reach, pull
### Tier Progression System
Players progress through 3–4 tiers per skill based on what the coach observes.
**Each tier shows:**
- What a player can currently do
- What comes next
- Clear signs they're ready to move up
## Practice Structure (60 Minutes)
**1. Arrival & Routine**
- Kids get equipment and start moving immediately
- Sets focus and eliminates wasted time
**2. Skill Focus Block**
- One main skill is introduced or reinforced
- Quick demonstration and cueing
- Fast, high-rep practice
**3. Decision Block**
- Add constraints that force choices: who to pass to, where to run, how to defend space
**4. Game Segment**
- Modified game reinforces the learning goal
- Scoring rewards good decisions, not just points
**5. Feedback & CFU**
- Coach pauses briefly to ask: "What did you see?" and "Why did that work?"
- Kids show or explain the answer
**6. Wrap-Up**
- Highlight effort, progress, and one takeaway
- Give parents one clear message at pickup
## Supporting Systems
### Drill Library
Every drill includes:
- What skill it teaches
- How to keep reps high
- Specific cues to use
- Age-appropriate adjustments
### Game Library
Games designed to force learning through:
- Limited space
- Extra points for spacing or passing
- Everyone must touch the ball
*No playbooks. No scrimmage chaos.*
### CFU (Check for Understanding) System
Learning is checked constantly through:
- Asking kids to explain decisions
- Watching for repeated behaviors
- Quick demonstrations as proof
If many kids miss the same thing, the coach adjusts teaching immediately.
### Coach Scripts
Consistent language across all locations:
- Short cues
- Quick corrections
- Simple questions
### Culture Routines
Intentional culture-building in every practice:
- Clear start routines
- Fast transitions
- Effort praise
- Safety reminders
## Parent & Home Connection
### Parent Updates
After each practice, parents receive:
- What their child worked on
- Where they're progressing
- One simple thing to practice at home
### Home Playbook
Optional at-home activities:
- 10–15 minutes
- Minimal equipment
- Reinforces the week's skill
## Program Quality Systems
### Coach Training
Coaches are trained on:
- How to run practices
- How to give feedback
- How to check learning
- How to manage energy and transitions
### Metrics
Each week, coaches record:
- Attendance
- Skill progress
- Engagement
- Effort
This keeps quality consistent across the program.
