# COMMUNICATING
## Purpose of this module
The focus is building **comfort, habit, and shared language**: starting with **coach-led call-and-response**, then progressing into **player-to-player communication**.
Every coach uses the same call phrases so players learn to respond **automatically and without hesitation**.
## Teaching philosophy
Beginner players:
- feel awkward speaking
- don't know what to say
- stay silent to avoid being wrong
Because of this, we teach communication through:
- **group responses first** (low pressure)
- short, simple words
- repetition
- waiting for athletes to speak before moving on
Communication cues are **actions**, not conversations.
## Core concept: communication is a habit before it's a skill
Before athletes can communicate in play, they must get comfortable **making sound** with teammates.
We progress communication in this order:
1. **Coach → Team response**
2. **Player → Player names**
3. **In-play responsibility calls**
This order is non-negotiable.
## Communication with coach by numbers (foundation)
### Communication 1: Team response
**Cue:** "Mine on three!"
- Coach says: "Mine on three. One, two, three…"
- Team says: "**MINE!**"
Repeat as needed.
**Why:** Everyone speaks together = no fear. This builds volume, timing, confidence, and togetherness.
### Communication 2: Call-and-response trigger word
**Cue:** "Volley!"
- Coach says: "**VOLLEY!**"
- Team responds (standardized): "**ATTENTION!**"
*(If your program uses "Attention, volley!" that is fine. The wording must stay identical across coaches.)*
**Why:** This creates an instant, predictable **lock-in moment** that refocuses the group immediately.
### Communication 3: Energy phrase (short + unified)
**Cue:** "Ball!"
**Sequence:**
- Coach: "**VOLLEY!**"
- Team: "**ATTENTION!**"
- Coach: "**BALL!**"
- Team: "**BALL!**"
**Why:** Short words build voice without needing volleyball knowledge. Players learn to respond **without thinking**.
### Communication 4: Name loud (comfort builder)
**Cue:** "Name!"
**Coach instruction:** "Say one teammate's name out loud. Go." Then **wait** until names are spoken.
Ways to prompt:
- Coach points: "Say her name." *(wait)*
- "One, two, three: **NAME!**" *(players say names)*
**Why:** This bridges group speaking into **individual speaking** with very low pressure.
## Key coaching moves (non-negotiable)
### 1. Wait for them to speak
If you talk over silence, you teach players that silence is acceptable.
**Coach rule:** Give the cue → **pause** → do not continue until you hear the response.
### 2. Correct silence like a skill error
Silence is **not personality**. It is a habit, and habits are trained.
Treat silence the same way you treat poor posture, a late platform, or wrong footwork.
### 3. Keep the words identical across coaches
Confidence comes from predictability. If the language changes, hesitation increases.
## How communication progresses over time
The goal is to move from: **group voice → individual voice → in-play voice**
### Early phase (Week 1 focus)
**Goal:** Break silence and normalize speaking
Primary communication used:
- "Mine on three"
- "Volley → Attention"
- "Ball"
- teammate names
Coaching emphasis:
- repetition
- waiting for responses
- praise for participation (not volume perfection)
### Application phase (later weeks)
**Goal:** Transfer voice into volleyball actions
Now we begin adding **simple in-play language**:
- "Mine"
- "Help"
- "Free"
- "Up"
- "Next"
Coach-led triggers remain in use to maintain team voice and energy.
## Non-negotiables at all levels
These never change:
- Everyone responds to the coach
- Everyone says **something** every practice
- Coach waits for the response
- Short words only
- Same language across all coaches
## Coach reminder
If communication is weak, ask yourself:
1. Did I give them exact words?
2. Did I **wait** for the response?
3. Did I require names out loud?
Fix **one expectation at a time**.
## Recommended coach script (standardized)
- "Let's communicate."
- "**Mine on three. One, two, three… MINE!**"
- "**VOLLEY!**" → team: "**ATTENTION!**"
- "**BALL!**" → team: "**BALL!**"
- "Say a teammate's name. Go." *(pause until you hear it)*
- "Mine on three. One, two, three… **MINE!**"
