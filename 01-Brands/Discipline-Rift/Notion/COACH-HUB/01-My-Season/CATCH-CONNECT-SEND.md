	Coach! A complete game for beginners. Catches carry the game at the start and get replaced by real volleyball contacts as the group is ready. Pick **one version per round**.
</callout>
# CATCH → CONNECT → SEND
<callout icon="🏐" color="gray_bg">
	**SETUP FOR 20 PLAYERS**
	Two coaches, ten players each, one 5v5 game per coach.
	This needs two separated playable spaces with a net or an approved lower divider, clear boundaries, and room between courts. One court only? Use the swap plan on the large-group page.
	Light ball they can catch comfortably. Mark five starting spots per side: three deeper, two nearer the net. These are learning locations, not the six official positions.
</callout>
## Base rules
1. Start each rally with a gentle toss over the net from an agreed marker.
2. Catch the incoming ball. The holder stays where they caught it. Teammates move to offer a receiving option.
3. Toss to a teammate before sending over. At least two different players must touch it. Maximum three team contacts. A catch and release counts as one contact.
4. Release within about three seconds. Demonstrate controlled tosses. Nobody throws hard at an opponent.
5. A dropped ball, an out-of-bounds send, or a missed contact rule ends the rally. One point to the other team.
6. Short rounds to five points, or about three minutes. Reset fast with a new starter.
## Rotation and participation
After each rally every player moves to the next numbered home spot in a fixed loop you demonstrate. Restart only when all ten are ready. This is a DR teaching rotation, not official volleyball rotation.
If rotating every rally kills the play, rotate after three rallies. Change the starting thrower every rally regardless.
Watch who actually gets contacts. Equal home-spot rotation does not mean equal ball involvement.
<callout icon="⚠️" color="yellow_bg">
	**DO NOT RENAME THIS AS THE REAL SYSTEM** — a five-player loop is not a six-position rotation lesson. When you teach standard rotation later, demonstrate it separately with six spots.
</callout>

## Scoring without overload
Start with ordinary rally points. For your one learning focus, add at most **one** process bonus per rally, for example a playable first pass to a teammate. The bonus counts even if a later contact loses the rally. Do not add bonuses for passing, setting, talking and rotation all at once.
## Observe before intervening
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Look for</td>
<td>Evidence</td>
<td>What not to conclude</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>First-ball ownership</td>
<td>One player claims before contact; others respond.</td>
<td>Noise alone is not coordination.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Connection</td>
<td>A teammate offers a reachable next target.</td>
<td>A catch does not show passing technique.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Recovery</td>
<td>Players spread into useful space as the ball crosses.</td>
<td>Standing on a cone is not reading play.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Contact skill</td>
<td>The required real pass or set stays playable.</td>
<td>A team win does not show every child learned.</td>
</tr>
</table>
<callout icon="💡" color="gray_bg">
	**FREEZE → CUE → REPLAY**
	Two players chase the same ball. Freeze after the rally, show the original starting positions, say "One claims, one helps," and toss the same kind of ball again. Then run two more entries without telling them who takes it. Keep the correction short enough that the next rep is immediate.
</callout>

## Adjust to the group
**Easier:** catch every contact, shrink the court, slow the entries, or temporarily allow an extra team contact.
**Harder:** change one contact rule, one entry location, or one target.
Different courts may run different versions. Inside one court, an agreed catch allowance for selected learners is fine if it stays simple and visible.
Rotate partners or starters if dominant players take every contact. Do not require all five players to touch every rally: it fights the three-contact connection this game teaches.
