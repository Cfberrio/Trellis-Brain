# Why DR Practice?

Sister page → [[DR-Practice]]

## What DR Practice is NOT
- A specific drill
- A "game-only" practice
- Letting players scrimmage and figure it out

## What DR Practice IS
- Sequenced learning structure: **know what** → **recognize when** → **execute under pressure**
- Built on **See → Decide → Act**
- Reason practices **transfer** to matches instead of stalling at execution-only reps

## Core structural idea
Decision-making is **layered on top of** skill, not substituted.

Three truths:
1. Players can't decide well about skills they don't control
2. Execution breaks when decisions enter too early
3. Game performance requires decisions under constraint

DR Practice is **not random** — it is **progressive**.

## Where DR Practice lives in a session
DR learning lives in **2 blocks** of [[DR-Practice|DR-UPS]]:
- **Block 5** — Teaching Acquisition (Guided Decisions)
- **Block 6** — Application (Constraint Game)

Blocks 1–4 = prep nervous system + skill base.

### Why Blocks 1–4 exist
> "Can they execute this skill reliably *without* decision stress?"

| Block | Function |
|-------|----------|
| 1 — Culture + Belonging | Psychological safety → risk-taking later |
| 2 — Retrieval Warm-Up | Reactivate prior learning, no cold starts |
| 3 — Teaching | Clarify what right looks like (shared mental model) |
| 4 — Skill Acquisition Reps | Stabilize mechanics, free attention for decisions |

Without these → DR becomes guessing, not learning.

## Block 5 — Guided Decision–Response (Teaching Bridge)
Teach **how to decide**, not just what happened.
- Decisions limited
- Cues named + coached
- Feedback between reps
- Environment manipulated, not chaotic

Sample prompts:
- *"If the pass is off the net, what changes?"*
- *"When the blocker commits, what option opens?"*

## Block 6 — Application (Constraint Game)
Test whether decisions survive speed, pressure, emotion.
- Fewer stoppages
- Real scoring consequences
- Constraints replace verbal reminders
- Players self-regulate

Real question:
> "Can they still choose the right action when it feels like a match?"

## Failure modes (without structure)
- Jumping to games too early
- Labelling everything "game-like"
- Adding decisions before skills are stable
- Coaching outcomes instead of cues

## Structural payoff
- Skills transfer
- Players adapt instead of freeze
- Teams improve decision quality without over-coaching
- Coaches talk less, players solve more

## One-sentence summary
> **DR Practice works because it deliberately delays decision-making until skills are stable, then introduces decisions in controlled layers before testing them under game pressure.**
