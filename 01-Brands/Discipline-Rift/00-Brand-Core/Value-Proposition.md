Discipline Rift creates value by solving the biggest blocker to youth sports participation: **friction**. It removes parent logistics, reduces beginner fear, and packages sports into a predictable, low-pressure seasonal commitment that fits normal family life.

## The three strongest sources of value
1. **On-campus convenience** — the child is already at school, so the parent avoids the off-campus sports burden.
2. **Beginner-safe environment** — kids can start without feeling behind or embarrassed.
3. **Coach-led visible progress** — the season has structure, skill progression, and repeatable sessions that create proof of improvement.

## Why parents say yes
Parents do not need to say yes to “sports” in general. They need to say yes to a sports option that feels manageable, safe, and worth the fixed price.

## Why a second season is not a repeat (added 2026-09-17)
Source of value #3 has a mechanism now. Every skill has **depth within it** and a **bridge to the next skill**. Passing alone runs platform → control → targeting → movement → angles → seam responsibility → situational application, and only then bridges into pass → set → attack. A returning player works on the *next layer* of the same skill, not the same drill again. That is the retention argument: the parent can see the child learning more each season, not just attending again. Coach communication makes it visible ("what we worked on → what improved → what comes next"). Sources: [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/02-Communication/DR-Parent-Communication-Philosophy|DR Parent Communication Philosophy]] §2, §13–§14; [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/05-Operations/Training/Method/DR-Coach-Philosophy-Methodology|DR Coach Philosophy & Methodology]] §2.

Corollary for the "worth the fixed price" question: DR is where a family finds out whether the child loves the sport *before* club dues, uniforms, tournaments and travel.

## Why schools say yes
Schools say yes when participation goes up without creating supervision headaches, staff burden, or parent confusion.
