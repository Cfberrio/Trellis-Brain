## Purpose

Help parents feel welcomed, understand what is happening, and know exactly what to do next. This guide draws communication principles from Luis’s call. Quoted excerpts preserve his wording; the guidance turns those examples into a reusable approach.

## 1. Open warmly and state the purpose immediately

> “I just wanted to give you a quick call to clarify what I've been texting you for volleyball today.”

Make the conversation feel personal and easy to enter. Tell the parent why you are reaching out in the first sentence or two. When a text exchange needs clarification, a brief call can help you explain and hear their questions.

Keep the opening conversational. Get to the reason for the call without a long introduction.

## 2. Explain one piece at a time

> “What's happening is that registrations for next season are open.”

Start with the fact the parent needs to understand first. Then explain what it means for their family and what happens next. Use everyday language and short sentences.

Give only enough background to make the instruction understandable. Parents should be able to follow the explanation without knowing our internal processes.

## 3. Check understanding before moving forward

> “Does that make sense?”

Pause after the part most likely to cause confusion. Give the parent room to answer or ask a question. Their response helps you decide whether to continue or explain it another way.

Make this a real pause, especially when several steps or dates are involved.

## 4. Make the family feel considered

> “We added a coach just for your daughters to be a part of the team.”

Connect what we are doing to the family’s experience. When we have made a specific arrangement, explain how it helps their children participate.

Use personal language such as “your daughters,” “your child,” and “you guys” when it fits the relationship. Describe the actual effort made without overstating it or making the parent feel indebted.

## 5. Anticipate what could confuse them

> “Once you complete the registration online, you receive a schedule and whatever that is for next season.”

Prepare parents for anything they may see or receive that appears inconsistent with your explanation. Tell them what to expect, what it means, and which information applies to them.

For clarity, name the specific item they should disregard and what will replace it. This makes reassurance actionable.

## 6. Take ownership of our part

> “Once you do it, we'll manually switch you.”

Separate the parent’s action from our responsibility. Use direct language: “Once you complete this, we’ll take care of that.”

If they need to wait for us, say so. Explain what we will confirm before they take the next step. Give them a clear sequence they can follow.

## 7. Answer questions directly and respond naturally

> “For sure.”

> “Anytime.”

> “Awesome, we'll keep you updated.”

Use brief, warm acknowledgments. Answer the parent’s question first, then add any condition or timing detail they need.

Keep the exchange responsive. Familiar phrases work when they sound natural and accompany a useful answer.

## 8. Be encouraging and honest about availability

> “We're very thankful for that, but it might not happen next season.”

Express enthusiasm for the opportunity while being clear about what is confirmed and what remains uncertain. If there is a real reason to act promptly, explain it plainly.

Tie urgency to actual availability. Do not turn a special arrangement into a promise that we can always repeat it.

## 9. Summarize the practical details

> “Just to summarize…”

After the explanation, bring the parent back to the few details they need to remember. Confirm dates, quantities, and the order of actions before closing.

If a detail is corrected during the conversation, restate the final confirmed information clearly so the parent leaves with one answer.

## 10. Close with a specific follow-up

> “We'll send you a practice schedule once you're in, okay?”

End by naming what they can expect from us and when or after which step it will happen. Thank them and close warmly.

The parent should leave knowing:

1. What they need to do.
2. What we will handle.
3. What confirmation or information comes next.

## Reusable conversation flow

1. **Welcome:** Greet the parent warmly and explain why you are reaching out.
2. **Clarify:** Explain the situation in small, understandable steps.
3. **Check:** Pause and confirm that the explanation makes sense.
4. **Guide:** Tell them what to do and what to expect.
5. **Own:** State what we will take care of and any required waiting step.
6. **Respond:** Answer their questions directly and clarify any limitations.
7. **Recap:** Confirm the important details and next actions.
8. **Close:** Promise a specific follow-up that we can deliver.

## Tone to carry across calls and messages

Warm, personal, conversational, and confident. Show care through specific attention to the family. Build trust by explaining clearly, acknowledging uncertainty, and following through on what we say we will do.

Adapt the answer to the family’s actual situation. Keep the same care and clarity whether the next step is immediate participation, a future opportunity, or waiting for confirmation.
