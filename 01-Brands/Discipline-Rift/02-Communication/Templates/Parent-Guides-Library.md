## Purpose

Public-facing parent guides used as **lead magnets + content** on the DR website + social. Each guide reinforces DR's positioning by giving away high-trust, actionable parent advice while reframing what to look for in a youth sports program.

## Guide 1 — 5 Questions Every Parent Should Ask Before Signing Up for Any Youth Sports Program

1. **What's the method behind your practice?**
"Drills" isn't a method. Ask the coach to describe how a typical practice is structured. If they can't name what they're doing or why, the practice will run on improvisation.

2. **How do you separate players by age and stage?**
A 6-year-old and a 12-year-old learn differently. If everyone trains together "for community," dig deeper. The right answer involves stage, not just age.

3. **Who supervises your coaches?**
A great coach without a system is luck. A team of coaches with shared standards is consistency. Ask if there's a head of coaching, what they look for, and how often they show up.

4. **What does week 1 look like vs week 12?**
A program with a real plan can describe progression. A program without one will say "we adapt as we go" — meaning they don't know yet.

5. **How will I know my player is actually growing?**
Trophies aren't growth. Ask what the program shares with parents: weekly notes, photos, progress markers, parent dashboard access. If they can't tell you, you'll be guessing for 12 weeks.

> *We don't expect you to like every answer we give. We expect you to ask the questions.*

## Guide 2 — The One Question to Ask Your Player After Every Practice

The question is:

> **"What's one thing that felt easier today than it did last week?"**

**Why it works:**
- It's specific, so they can't say "fine."
- It's framed around progress, not performance.
- It works on a bad day too. Even after a tough practice, *something* usually felt easier — even if it's "remembering my water bottle."
- It invites them to notice their own growth instead of waiting for a coach to validate it.

**Avoid these:**
- "How was practice?" — too vague
- "Did you have fun?" — binary, no signal
- "Did you score?" / "Did you win?" — trains them to value the wrong thing

**What to do with their answer:** Don't fix it. Don't add advice. Just say: *"That's growth. Let's see what feels easier next week."*

3 weeks = a player who tracks their own progress. 12 weeks = a player who self-motivates.

> *Coaching happens at practice. Belief happens at home.*

## Guide 3 — 4 Red Flags a Youth Program Is Overpromising

🚩 **1. They mention "scholarship pipeline" without numbers.**
Real numbers are checkable. Programs that pitch college outcomes without naming a single scholarship recipient by name and school are selling hope, not pathway.

🚩 **2. The coach is also the salesperson.**
The same person who pitches you on price shouldn't be the one teaching on Tuesday. If they are — there's no system, just a personality.

🚩 **3. There's no parent communication system.**
If "we'll text you if there's a problem" is the entire comms plan, you'll know nothing for 12 weeks. A real program shows you what's happening, proactively.

🚩 **4. They brag more about wins than progress.**
Watch their Instagram. If the only highlight is trophy photos, the development is incidental, not intentional. Find a program that posts about practice — not just podiums.

> *We'd rather lose your business than oversell.*

## Guide 4 — Is This Program Built for Your Player's Age? (6-Question Checklist)

- [ ] **1. Are players grouped by age, not just availability?** Or are 8-year-olds and 13-year-olds in the same drill because that's who showed up?

- [ ] **2. Is the language age-appropriate during practice?** Watch a practice. A coach speaking to a 6-year-old should sound different from a coach speaking to a 12-year-old.

- [ ] **3. Do drills change format based on the age group?** Same skill — different drill format, time, complexity.

- [ ] **4. Is there a clear "fun-first" floor for the youngest players?** Under-8s should leave practice tired and smiling. Bored or stressed = built for older kids.

- [ ] **5. Is there a clear "challenge ceiling" for the oldest players?** Over-10s need to be pushed. If they're phoning it in because the drill is too simple, you'll lose them by week 4.

- [ ] **6. Can the coach explain — out loud — why a drill is matched to that age?** If the coach hesitates, the drill is matched to convenience, not to development.

> *5 or more checked → this program is built around your player's stage.*
> *3 or fewer → it's built around the coach's schedule.*

## Guide 5 — 5 Life Lessons Volleyball Teaches Better Than Any Other Sport

1. **You can't win alone — by rule.**
The ball has to touch three players before it crosses the net. Every point is a built thing. Every player's role is required.

2. **Apologize fast, recover faster.**
A bad pass is followed instantly by your teammate's set. You don't get to sulk. You reset. Real life works the same way.

3. **No hero ball.**
Volleyball doesn't reward the player who tries to do it all — it punishes them. So players learn to trust someone else's hands.

4. **Communication is non-negotiable.**
You have to call the ball. Out loud. Every time. Quiet players get exposed fast — and grow fast — in volleyball.

5. **Mistakes belong to the team, not the individual.**
A missed point isn't "your fault." The team owns it. That's the closest a sport gets to teaching emotional fairness.
