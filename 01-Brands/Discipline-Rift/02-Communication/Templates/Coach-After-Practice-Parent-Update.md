## Why it exists

Parents do not watch after-school practice. The coach's message is the only evidence they get that development is happening. "Practice went great today!" gives them nothing to see, nothing to ask their child about, and no reason to believe season two is different from season one. Retention should come from *my child is learning more*, not from discounts (Parent Philosophy §14).

## When and how

- **After every practice**, before leaving campus if possible — same moment the session is logged.
- **Channel:** Coach Dashboard → **Message All**. It is delivered to each family individually; parents do not see each other. Do not use WhatsApp, personal SMS, or email for this.
- **One message per team per practice.** Individual concerns about one child are a separate, private conversation — never inside the team update.
- **Length:** phone-readable. Four to six short lines. No essay.

## Structure

> **Warm opening → What we worked on → What improved / what we observed → What comes next → Logistics (only if needed)**

| Part | What goes there | What does not |
|---|---|---|
| Warm opening | "Hi families!" / the team's name | Generic praise as the whole message |
| What we worked on | The skill and the version of it actually run today (e.g. "directing passes toward a teammate", not just "passing") | Curriculum jargon: "rung 3", "CFU", "serial practice" |
| What improved / observed | One true, visible thing. If it did not improve yet, say what is still hard and how you will teach it next | Claiming every player reached the same point |
| What comes next | The next layer or bridge you already planned (Training Philosophy §6) | Promises about outcomes or dates |
| Logistics | Only if something changes: location, weather, a form, current registration window | Refunds, pricing, roster decisions — those go through the office (MESSAGING PARENTS) |

## Rules

1. **Real evidence only.** Write what you saw. If the group stayed on the first layer all practice, that is the update — "we kept working on controlled contact; most players are now getting the ball up consistently; next we add a target."
2. **Both progress and next step, every time.** One without the other is half a message.
3. **Mixed levels are normal — say so plainly.** "Some players worked on a controlled first contact, while others added a set after the pass. We adjusted the challenge so each player had a useful next step."
4. **Plain words.** Explain layers the way you would to a neighbor: target, move first, less predictable ball, pass to the setter, connect two skills.
5. **Never name a child in the team message,** positively or negatively.
6. **No invented facts.** No prices, no next-season dates or schedules you have not been given, no comparisons to clubs, no offers of clinics or camps that are not confirmed (Parent Philosophy §8–§9 are options, not promises).
7. **Office boundary.** Registration, refunds, payments, transfers → "the DR office will follow up" and nothing more. See [[01-Brands/Discipline-Rift/AI-Customer-Service-Instructions/06-escalation-rules|Escalation Rules]].
8. **Safety and incidents are not this message.** Injury, behavior, missing player follow their own procedures and go to the specific family and DR leadership first.

## Examples (use only when true)

**Single layer, whole group**

> Hi families! Today we worked on directing passes toward a teammate. Players began sending more balls to a reachable target. Our next step is helping the setter move under that pass so we can connect the two skills.

**Mixed levels**

> Hi families! Today was passing again, at two levels. Some players worked on a controlled first contact, while others added a set after the pass. We adjusted the challenge so each player had a useful next step. Next practice we bring both groups toward pass → set together.

**When it did not click yet**

> Hi families! We introduced underhand serving today. Getting the ball over the net from full distance is still hard for most of the group, so we shortened the distance and worked on the contact. Next practice we keep the shorter distance, add a target, and move back as it becomes consistent.

**Progress into a connection**

> Today we progressed from passing into setting. Players are beginning to reliably target their passes, which allows us to start connecting the pass into the next action.

**What to avoid**

> ~~Practice went great today! The kids had a lot of fun. See you next week!~~ — nothing to see, nothing to ask about.
> ~~We are still working on passing.~~ — sounds like a repeat. Say *which layer* of passing.

## How it fits the other parent touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Owner | Timing | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly email (Getting Started → Bringing It Together) | Automation (n8n) | Day before practice | Sets the expectation for the week |
| **This message** | Coach | Right after practice | Proves it with evidence and names the next step |
| Dashboard unread-message reminders | System (GHL) | Business hours, max 2/cycle | Makes sure the parent sees it |
| Individual conversation | Coach, then office if needed | As needed | One child, one family, private |
