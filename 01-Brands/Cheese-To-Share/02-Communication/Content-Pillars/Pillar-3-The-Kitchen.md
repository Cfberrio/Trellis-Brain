## What this pillar is

Storytelling pillar in **skit format**. Converts real situations from the CTS universe into **dramatized micro-episodes** using a logic of **series + skit + narrative editing**.

The intent isn't to over-explain. It's to **represent day-to-day moments of the brand in a way that feels entertaining, exaggerated, and recognizable**.

## How it works

Structure per piece:

```
Hook → Base situation → Improvisable development → Escalation → Punchline
```

Each piece feels part of the same universe even when situations, characters, or tone change.

## Key of the format

A piece does **not need a closed script**. It needs a **clear base** that allows for shooting with direction but also natural delivery.

Each piece defined by:

- **Hook** — how the piece opens
- **Base situation** — what real moment is being dramatized
- **Main idea** — what's actually happening
- **Style** — how the episode feels
- **Roles** — who enters the scene
- **Escalation** — what grows the tension, humor, or absurdity
- **Punchline** — how it closes

## What makes it a "skit"

Doesn't depend on perfect acting or exact dialogue. Strength is in:

- a recognizable situation
- a clear dynamic
- natural reactions
- controlled exaggeration
- improvisation
- editing that elevates the moment

The skit works because it starts from something real but presents it with dramatic intent, rhythm, and personality.

## Why this pillar is flexible

No word-by-word scripting. Planned **in layers**, not by exact dialogue.

Per piece, only define:
- the base situation
- the hook
- the tone
- who participates
- what it should feel like
- how it escalates
- how it closes

Leaves room for natural improvisation during recording. Improvisation here doesn't mean chaos — the piece already has clear intent, but allows gestures, silences, reactions, and spontaneous dialogue to give authenticity.

## Principle of the pillar

**Take a real situation from Cheese To Share and turn it into a dramatized skit that feels like part of a series.**

## Idea set

### 1) Kitch1n universe intro
- Hook: entry like a series intro presenting the CTS universe with team shots, random moments, looks, mini-chaos, restaurant details with dramatic / comedic tone.
- Style: series intro / character montage / iconic recognizable tone.
- Scale: starts normal → cuts get faster → reactions → absurd / dramatic mini-moments.
- Close: pillar name reveal or final shot that says "this is just the beginning."

### 2) Slice of life of Cheese To Share
- Hook: a phrase or moment that seems small, random, or unnecessarily dramatic for something very mundane.
- Style: slice of life / dramatized everyday / observational.
- Scale: starts tiny and fills with small moments → ends feeling like "a normal day at CTS."
- Close: a small but memorable detail.

### 3) A boring day at CTS
- Hook: an opening that feels like an important episode starts… reveals nothing is happening.
- Style: absurd drama / dry humor / "nothing is happening but we're treating it like it is."
- Scale: calm → each gesture / silence dramatized further → boredom becomes the central conflict.
- Close: mini-rupture of calm, or a punchline where it seemed something would finally happen… but didn't.

### 4) The rush / kitchen in war mode
- Hook: announces calm is over.
- Style: kitchen chaos / war tone / comedic horror.
- Roles: kitchen, register, people assembling orders, someone losing control.
- Close: "after battle" — silence, exhaustion, defeated faces.

### 5) The customer who came to order… and ended up sharing her life story
- Hook: customer talking too much while the team is clearly busy.
- Style: interaction comedy / conversational skit / observational humor.
- Idea: dramatize the customer who can't read context and wants to socialize mid-chaos.

## Short doc version

**The Kitchen / Kitch1n** is a dramatized skit-format storytelling pillar that transforms real day-to-day moments of CTS into entertaining micro-episodes. Each piece starts from a simple base situation, opens with a quick hook, develops with room for improvisation, escalates through acting + editing, and closes with a punchline. Its strength isn't in a rigid script but in a flexible structure that keeps universe consistency and shooting freedom.
