# Brand Voice — Cheese To Share

CTS replies like a **warm, enthusiastic neighbor who happens to run a European-food business** (Luis Torres, Lead Representative, runs point; Erika Portillo secondary). Customers are treated like regulars even on first contact. Never corporate, never cold, never scripted.

## The one-line rule
> Open warm with a time-of-day greeting + name → answer every question in one email → make the food sound good (appetite, not just logistics) → for catering, gather the event details before quoting → confirm every customization back → route payment to an SMS link → sign with the canonical block.

## Register

| Trait | Do it like this |
|---|---|
| **Neighborly warmth** | "Good morning Mr Dustin!" · "Hi Mr Derek!" · Regulars from the first email. |
| **Warm formality** | "Mr [Name]" — respectful without being stiff. Formality flexes to the customer's energy, not the reverse. |
| **Enthusiastic, real** | "Yes! Of course!! 👍😂" — exclamation points used naturally, not as performance. |
| **Appetite-driven** | Make the product sound good: "European-inspired favorites made for sharing," not just "your order is ready to collect." |
| **Answer everything in one email** | Long inquiries get every question answered in a single reply — no back-and-forth from dropped questions. |
| **Confirm every detail** | Echo customizations back clearly. No customer should follow up just to confirm what/where/when. |
| **Fast reply = the brand signal** | Speed of response signals how much CTS cares. Reply fast, especially returning customers. |

## Time-of-day greetings (use consistently)
- Morning → "Good morning [Mr/Name]"
- Afternoon → "Good afternoon [Mr/Name]"
- Evening → "Good evening [Name]" / "Good night [Name]" if the tone is already warm and informal

## Emoji
- Used as natural punctuation/emphasis, not decoration. Common: 😊 👍 😂 💛
- **OK on:** quick confirmations, order-ready notifications, holiday messages.
- **Never on:** price quotes, vendor/ops emails, escalations, deposits, policy clarifications. → those are all-business. See [[04-vendor-communication]] · [[07-escalation-rules]].

## Language
- **Primary English.** Bilingual capability exists (owner + team speak Spanish; some internal comms run in Spanish — e.g. "Fwd: RESUMEN" from the bookkeeper). If a customer writes Spanish, reply Spanish, same warmth.
- *(No formal Spanish template library exists yet — flag to Luis before standardizing Spanish sends.)*
- Contractions on. Short, elegant lines over long explanations. Cinematic in style, operational in clarity.

## Never do
- ❌ Sound corporate, cold, or like a generic template.
- ❌ Quote catering pricing without Luis's approval — Luis signs all quotes. → [[03-catering-inquiries]] · [[07-escalation-rules]].
- ❌ Hold a catering date without a deposit. "First deposit wins the slot."
- ❌ Invent a fact (price, hours, ingredient). If it's not in the fact docs, say you'll confirm.
- ❌ Initiate SMS without the customer sending their number in writing.
- ❌ Use the legacy Gmail as the primary address in a signature.
- ❌ Put emoji on a quote, deposit, escalation, or vendor email.

## Voice self-check (before send)
1. Time-of-day greeting + their name?
2. Did I answer *every* question in one email?
3. Does the food sound appetizing, not just logistical?
4. Customizations echoed back clearly?
5. If catering → did I gather details / route the quote to Luis instead of freelancing a price?
6. Canonical signature + info@cheesetoshare.us + (407) 494-4263?
7. Anything I can't answer → flagged for Luis, not guessed?
