## How a lead becomes a booking

CTS catering conversion runs through a clear, repeatable path:

```
Inquiry → Discovery call → Quote → 50% deposit → Confirmation → Event → Balance
```

### Step 1 — Inquiry capture
Lead arrives via DM, comment, link in bio, or paid ad. Sales captures:
- event date
- guest count
- event type (private / corporate / brand / birthday / wedding)
- desired setup tier (or "no idea, recommend")

### Step 2 — Discovery call
Client calls in. Sales:
- confirms availability for the date
- walks through base + setup tiers + station options
- asks for reference photos (their references or aesthetic goal)
- communicates 1-week (standard) or 15-day (themed) advance requirement

### Step 3 — Quote
Quote sent by **Luis Torres** (centralized quote authority). Quote includes:
- guest count × $25
- setup tier line
- staff line (if applicable)
- stations line (if applicable)
- 50% deposit instruction
- 24-hour balance instruction

### Step 4 — Deposit
**50% deposit reserves the date.** No deposit = no date held. Deposit is the conversion event.

### Step 5 — Confirmation
On deposit:
- date locked
- menu confirmed
- setup style confirmed
- final headcount deadline communicated
- pre-event reminder scheduled

### Step 6 — Event
Day-of execution per booked package.

### Step 7 — Balance
50% balance settled 24 hours before the event.

## Core conversion levers

### Lever 1 — Speed to quote
A same-day quote dramatically beats a 3-day quote. CTS doesn't compete on price floor, so the next-best advantage is responsiveness.

### Lever 2 — Visual proof
Reference photos from past events + reels carry more weight than a written description. Catering buyers buy what they can imagine.

### Lever 3 — Live dessert anchor
Catering pitches that include a live station component close at higher tickets because the "phones come out" moment is felt, not just heard.

### Lever 4 — Setup tier framing
Always present 3 tiers (drop-off / standard / premium). Tier framing makes premium feel reasonable instead of expensive.

### Lever 5 — Deposit-first framing
"50% holds the date" is a clean, fair, non-pushy commitment device. Use it directly — don't soften it.

## Common objections + default move

| Objection | Default move |
|---|---|
| "Too expensive" | Reframe by ticket math: per-guest cost vs. food + setup + experience |
| "We have a smaller group" | Confirm 30-person minimum + offer drop-off only |
| "Less than a week away" | Be honest: standard catering needs 1 week; themed needs 15 days |
| "Can you do liquor?" | No — clarify scope; partner option as needed |
| "Can we pay all at the end?" | No — 50% deposit holds the date; balance 24h before |
| "Can we compare to other quotes?" | Let them; emphasize experience differentiator (live station, presentation) |

See [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/00-Brand-Core/Objections|CTS Objections]] for the full list.

## What kills conversion

- Slow quote turnaround.
- Verbal-only quote (no written summary).
- Pricing creep mid-conversation.
- Promising customizations without confirming feasibility.
- Skipping deposit "just this once."
