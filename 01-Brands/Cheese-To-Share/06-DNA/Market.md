## Who CTS serves

CTS sells into **two demand modes** anchored to one Mediterranean / European food identity.

### Mode 1 — Everyday eaters
Local people who walk in, order in, or pick up from Avalon Park. Bought on:
- visual presentation
- shareable format (boards, focaccia, cheesecake)
- "feels like an occasion" vibe in a casual cost range

### Mode 2 — Event hosts
People planning private, corporate, or celebration events. Bought on:
- visual wow factor (grazing tables, live dessert)
- low-coordination (CTS handles catering + setup + staff)
- price clarity ($25/person base, transparent setup tiers)

## Geographic core — Avalon Park

CTS center of gravity is **Avalon Park, Orlando**. Why this matters strategically:

- Avalon is a tight community with strong **events calendar** — farmer markets, street events, Christmas, 4th of July, Latin Night.
- Communities surrounding Avalon **come to it** for events, expanding effective reach beyond local population.
- Avalon's character is calm, safe, relaxed — matches CTS's "moments to share" identity better than a high-volume downtown location would.
- Proximity to the airport adds residual catering demand from short-stay corporate / family events.
- Downtown Avalon is the convergence point — commerce, restaurants, parks, markets — making CTS naturally embedded in community flow.

CTS participates **actively** in the Avalon events calendar. The community presence is part of the brand's market position, not just a side activity.

## Demand signals that fit CTS

- "We need catering for an event" (private, corporate, brand)
- "Looking for somewhere different to take a date / friends"
- "Want a live element at our event"
- "Need 30+ guests served without managing logistics ourselves"
- "Want food that looks good on camera"

## Demand CTS should *not* chase

- Pure low-cost catering (CTS does not compete on price floor).
- Fast-food convenience speed.
- Generic "drop a tray and leave" catering.
- High-volume cafeteria-style food service.

## Cultural / language layer

The CTS audience splits across English-first and Spanish-first speakers. Communications need to land naturally in both. The brand DNA — "We love to share" — is intentionally translatable across cultures, but the **execution language** (ads, captions, scripts) should adapt to channel.

## Implication for offers + content

- Lead with **visual experience**, not specs.
- Talk to events through "the moment guests stop, phones come out, everyone watches."
- Talk to everyday demand through shareable plates, family moments, date nights.
- Anchor brand to Avalon community when local trust matters; expand outward when scaling Meta ads.
