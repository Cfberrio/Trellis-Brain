## What "scale" means for CTS

Scaling CTS is **not** opening more locations first. It is **stretching the existing kitchen + catering capacity** until that stretch breaks, then deciding what to expand and how.

The growth ladder is operational, not geographic.

## Growth ladder

### Stage 1 — Lock the conversion engine
Before pushing more leads, ensure:
- quote turnaround is fast (same-day where possible)
- 50% deposit instruction is consistent
- setup tier framing is the default sales motion
- station pricing is fully published (see [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/01-Systems/Finance/Pricing-Logic|Pricing Logic]])

This is the foundation. Spending on ads before this stage burns leads.

### Stage 2 — Push qualified leads
Once conversion is locked:
- scale Meta ad spend on the live-dessert hook family
- add a second hook family targeting birthdays / private events
- expand organic posting across all 6 content pillars (see [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/02-Communication/Content-Pillars/Pillars-Home|Pillars Home]])

### Stage 3 — Expand product surface inside same kitchen
Add revenue streams that share existing kitchen capacity:
- corporate quarterly catering retainers
- recurring weekly drop-off (offices, co-working)
- holiday menu (Christmas, 4th of July, etc.) — tied to Avalon community events
- branded board kits for pickup (smaller version of catering for at-home hosting)

### Stage 4 — Add capacity layer
When existing kitchen + crew capacity is repeatedly maxed:
- second event staff team (allows two events per night)
- dedicated catering kitchen prep window
- live-station specialist (focus role for the wow stream)

### Stage 5 — Multi-location or franchise pattern
Only after the operating model is documented, replicable, and trained. Indicators that this stage is ready:
- catering pipeline is stable and oversubscribed
- documented SOPs for every catering phase
- training curriculum for new event staff
- proven event-day execution score above quality bar

## Constraints to manage as CTS scales

- **Kitchen throughput** — boards + cheesecakes + station prep all compete for prep space.
- **Quote authority bottleneck** — Luis Torres as sole quoter is fine at current scale; becomes the bottleneck at Stage 2–3.
- **Live-station specialty skill** — the "wow" depends on one person knowing how to perform the live moment well.
- **Brand voice drift** — if multiple people start writing reels, voice quality drops. Stay anchored to [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/00-Brand-Core/Voice-and-Tone|Voice and Tone]].

## What CTS should *not* do to scale

- Drop the 30-person minimum to win more small bookings.
- Cut setup tiers to compete on price.
- Open a second location before Stage 4 capacity is hit.
- Add a "cheaper grazing" SKU that dilutes the visual standard.

## Open questions

- Catering kitchen separation: at what booking volume does it pay for itself?
- Live-station specialist as W2 role vs. event-staff rotation?
- Direct-to-consumer board kit (Stage 3) — pricing model still open.
