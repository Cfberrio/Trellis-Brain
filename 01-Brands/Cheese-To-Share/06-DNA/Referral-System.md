## Why CTS has a referral edge

CTS already builds referrals **incidentally** through:
- visual presentation (boards photograph themselves at events)
- live-dessert moments (guests film and share)
- Avalon community presence (CTS shows up at neighborhood events)

The strategic move is converting incidental referral into **a structured referral system** with a tracked payout / discount mechanic.

## Three referral sources

### Source 1 — Past catering clients
Highest-quality referral. Their event happened, guests saw the product, they vouch with credibility. Trigger:
- post-event nurture (see [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/06-DNA/Retention|Retention]])
- explicit referral ask 30–60 days after event
- offer client a credit for next event when they refer

### Source 2 — Event guests (UGC referral)
Guests at an event who didn't book it but want their own. Trigger:
- visible CTS branding at event (board labels, station signage)
- shareable food formats (cheesecake cuts, live dessert)
- a clear way to inquire ("scan to ask about your event")

### Source 3 — Community / business partners
Local Avalon businesses, venue partners, event planners, photographers. Trigger:
- structured partner outreach (planners, photographers, real estate listing agents who host open-house events)
- referral fee or commission per booked event
- joint visibility (mutual social tagging, partner sections in marketing)

## Referral payout structure (proposed)

Two referral payouts that should be operationalized:

### A) Past client → new client
- Referring client receives a credit toward next catering booking (e.g., a fixed dollar amount applied to setup or stations).
- New client receives a small first-booking incentive (e.g., a complimentary station upgrade or discount on a setup tier).

### B) Partner → client
- Partner receives a percentage / fixed referral fee per booked event.
- Tracked via a partner-specific code or capture field in GHL.

> Exact dollar values to be locked in [[01-Brands/Cheese-To-Share/01-Systems/Finance/Pricing-Logic|CTS Pricing Logic]].

## Tracking requirements

For referral attribution to work:
- every inquiry captures a "how did you hear about us" field
- referral codes are unique per source / partner
- GHL pipeline shows referral source on each lead
- monthly reporting includes referral-attributable revenue

## What kills a referral program

- Manual tracking that nobody updates → attribution loss → trust loss with partners.
- Inconsistent payout timing → partners stop referring.
- Discount fatigue (every customer gets a discount) → erodes the price floor.

## Avalon community referral lever

Avalon's events calendar (farmer markets, Christmas, 4th of July, Latin Night) is a structural referral source. Strategic moves:
- show up consistently at community events
- bring branded boards / stations as visible demo
- collect contact for follow-up (event next month? need catering?)

This is **earned referral** — no payout needed, just consistent community presence.
